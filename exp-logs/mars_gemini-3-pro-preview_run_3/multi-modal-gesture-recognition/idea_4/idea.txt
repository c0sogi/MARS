Based on the comprehensive analysis of the "Lessons Learned" and the progression of "Previous Ideas," I propose the **Feature-Informed Cascaded Refinement Network**.

This solution structurally evolves the successful "Cascaded Refinement" architecture (Idea 3/Lesson 00015) by addressing its primary weakness: the information bottleneck between the encoder and the refinement stage. By introducing a **Feature-Forwarding Skip Connection**, the refinement stage gains access to the rich latent representations of the data, allowing it to correct false positives/negatives based on signal content rather than just smoothing probability distributions. Additionally, we extend the feature engineering paradigm validated in Lesson 00005 to include higher-order dynamics.

### Model
The architecture is a **Two-Stage Feature-Informed Network**:

1.  **Stage 1: Contextual Encoder (Bi-GRU)**
    *   **Backbone:** A multi-layer **Bi-directional Gated Recurrent Unit (Bi-GRU)** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002). This component processes the frame-wise input vectors to capture local temporal dynamics and generates two outputs for every frame:
        1.  **Initial Logits:** The raw class scores (21 dimensions).
        2.  **Latent Features:** The high-dimensional hidden states from the final GRU layer, representing the learned "motion context."
2.  **Stage 2: Holo-Refinement Module (Residual TCN)**
    *   **Input Fusion:** Unlike previous iterations where the refinement stage only saw the noisy probabilities, this module takes a **concatenation** of the **Initial Logits** and the **Latent Features** from Stage 1. This allows the network to distinguish between "uncertainty due to noise" (which should be smoothed) and "rapid transitions supported by data" (which should be preserved).
    *   **Architecture:** A stack of **Dilated Temporal Convolutional Layers** (TCN) with residual connections. The dilation factors (e.g., 1, 2, 4, 8) provide a wide temporal receptive field to enforce consistency.
    *   **Output:** Refined frame-wise class probabilities.

### Data
The data pipeline synthesizes the feature engineering success of Lesson 00005 with the windowing calibration of Lesson 00017.

1.  **Higher-Order Motion Features:**
    *   **Skeleton:** Extract 3D joint coordinates ($P_t$).
    *   **Velocity:** Compute 1st-order derivatives ($V_t = P_t - P_{t-1}$) (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).
    *   **Acceleration:** Compute **2nd-order derivatives** ($A_t = V_t - V_{t-1}$). Since velocity proved critical for dynamic modeling, acceleration provides explicit signals for the "stops" and "starts" characteristic of gesture boundaries.
    *   **Normalization:** Root-relative normalization for positions; zero-centering for derivatives.
2.  **Audio:** MFCC vectors synchronized to the video frame rate.
3.  **Windowing Strategy:**
    *   Generate training samples using **Sliding Windows of 64 frames** (approx. 3.2 seconds). This size aligns with the average atomic gesture duration (~45 frames), preventing the "noise" and degradation observed when using larger 128-frame windows (Cite solution_lesson_node_00017).

### Training
The training process is designed to enforce accuracy, stability, and class balance simultaneously.

1.  **Loss Function:**
    *   **Weighted Cross-Entropy (Stage 1 & 2):** Applied to both stages. A weight of **0.2** is assigned to the "background" class (Index 0) to counteract its dominance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).
    *   **Log-Space Smoothing Loss (Stage 2):** A Truncated Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss applied to the log-probabilities of adjacent frames in the Stage 2 output. This explicitly penalizes high-frequency "jitter" (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015).
    *   **Total Loss:** $L_{total} = L_{CE1} + L_{CE2} + \lambda L_{smooth}$.
2.  **Optimizer:** Adam optimizer.
3.  **Strategy:** End-to-end training. The gradient flow from Stage 2 back to Stage 1 via the feature skip connection encourages the Bi-GRU to learn features that are not just discriminative, but also "refinable."

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** Process the full, unsegmented test sequences. The input features are passed through Stage 1, and the resulting logits and hidden states are fused and passed through Stage 2.
2.  **Prediction:** Extract the final predictions from **Stage 2**.
3.  **Decoding:**
    *   Apply `argmax` to the refined probabilities.
    *   Perform **Run-Length Encoding (RLE)** to collapse consecutive identical labels.
    *   Remove the "background" class and any segments shorter than a minimal threshold (e.g., 5 frames) to produce the final ordered list of Gesture IDs.
