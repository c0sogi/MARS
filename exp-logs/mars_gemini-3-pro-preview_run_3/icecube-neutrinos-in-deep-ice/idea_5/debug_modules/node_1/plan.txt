Specifically, the implementation adds a small epsilon (`1e-8`) to the denominator `r` (`z / (r + 1e-8)`) to prevent division by zero. For a unit vector where $z=1$ and $r=1$, this results in an argument slightly less than 1 (approx. $0.99999999$). Because the derivative of $\arccos(x)$ approaches infinity as $x \to 1$, this tiny deviation results in a zenith angle of $\approx 0.00014$ radians instead of $0.0$, which exceeds the strict assertion tolerance of `1e-6`.

To fix this, I will replace the addition of epsilon with a clamping operation (`np.maximum` for NumPy and `torch.clamp` for PyTorch) on the denominator. This ensures that we avoid division by zero when $r \approx 0$ while maintaining exact division (e.g., $1/1 = 1$) for valid vectors, thus eliminating the bias.
