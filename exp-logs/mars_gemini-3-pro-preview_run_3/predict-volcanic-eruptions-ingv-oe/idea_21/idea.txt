Based on the comprehensive analysis of the "Lessons" and the empirical history of "Previous Ideas," I propose the **Hybrid-Transform Orthogonal Decomposition with Loss-Metric Decoupling** strategy.

This approach structurally evolves the "Current Best" Idea 15 by refining the signal decomposition paradigm. It replaces the simple "Residuals" texture view with a **Discrete Wavelet Transform** (Structural Innovation) to better capture multi-resolution signal complexity, while strictly adhering to the **Loss-Metric Decoupling** principle (Correction) to ensure convergence stability—addressing the specific failure modes of Ideas 19 (L1 Loss instability) and 20 (Overfitting).

# Model
The predictive core will be a **Single, Highly-Optimized LightGBM Regressor**.
*   **Rationale:** Lessons 00008 and 00046 confirmed that a single, well-tuned Gradient Boosted Decision Tree (GBDT) consistently outperforms complex Stacking Ensembles of under-optimized base learners.
*   **Regularization:** The model must be configured with explicit **L1 (`reg_alpha`) and L2 (`reg_lambda`) regularization** and a **low learning rate** (e.g., < 0.01). This is mandatory to prevent the overfitting observed in Idea 20 when using dense feature sets (Cite solution_lesson_node_00064).

# Data
The data processing pipeline implements a **Hybrid-Transform** paradigm that assigns the optimal signal processing technique to each physical component of the seismic event.

1.  **Preprocessing:**
    *   **Imputation:** Fill missing values with the segment-wise column mean to preserve DC offsets (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).

2.  **Feature Engineering (The Hybrid Paradigm):**
    *   **View 1: Kinematic Trend (via Savitzky-Golay):**
        *   Apply a **Savitzky-Golay filter** (window > 20) to the raw data.
        *   Compute **First (Velocity)** and **Second (Acceleration)** derivatives.
        *   Extract a **Dense Grid of Quantiles** (e.g., 1%, 5%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 95%, 99%) from these derivatives.
        *   *Rationale:* SG filters provide the smooth differentiability required for stable kinematics (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006), while dense quantiles capture the exact shape of the motion better than moments (Cite solution_lesson_node_00026, solution_lesson_node_00059).
    *   **View 2: Multi-Resolution Texture (via Wavelets):**
        *   Apply **Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT)** (e.g., `db4`) to the raw data.
        *   Extract **Energy**, **Entropy**, and **Quantiles** specifically from the **Detail Coefficients** (High-Frequency).
        *   *Rationale:* Wavelets preserve multi-resolution time-frequency structures that simple "Raw minus Trend" residuals miss (Cite solution_lesson_node_00060), providing a richer representation of the signal's "roughness" (Cite solution_lesson_node_00049).
    *   **View 3: Absolute Intensity (via Raw Data):**
        *   Extract **Global Minimum**, **Global Maximum**, and **Peak-to-Peak Range**.
        *   *Rationale:* Strictly preserves the absolute intensity of the event, correcting the failure of robust statistics to capture critical outliers (Cite solution_lesson_node_00031).
    *   **View 4: Structural Spectral Features (via PSD):**
        *   Compute **PSD Band Power** using **Welch's Method** with **Closed Intervals** (Low/Mid/High).
        *   *Rationale:* Avoids the noise of Raw Periodograms (Cite solution_lesson_node_00054) and the artifacts of open-ended filters (Cite solution_lesson_node_00058).
    *   **View 5: Temporal Evolution (via Flattening):**
        *   Divide signal into non-overlapping windows. Compute **RMS** and **Mean**. **Flatten** these into explicit features (e.g., `w1_rms`, `w2_rms`).
        *   *Rationale:* Preserves the temporal "arrow of time" essential for prediction (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).

3.  **Feature Organization:**
    *   **Spatial Concatenation:** Concatenate the feature vectors of all 10 sensors side-by-side. Do **not** aggregate features across sensors (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).

# Training
*   **Loss Function (Structural Innovation):** Train using **L2 Loss (Mean Squared Error)** or **Huber Loss**.
    *   *Rationale:* Even though the evaluation metric is MAE, Lesson 00060 demonstrated that optimizing L1 Loss directly leads to poor gradient dynamics (constant gradients). L2/Huber provides informative gradients that scale with error magnitude, leading to better convergence and lower final MAE.
*   **Strategy:** Use **Stratified K-Fold Cross-Validation** (e.g., 5 folds) based on the provided metadata bins.
*   **Optimization:** Use **Early Stopping** based on the *Validation MAE* (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
