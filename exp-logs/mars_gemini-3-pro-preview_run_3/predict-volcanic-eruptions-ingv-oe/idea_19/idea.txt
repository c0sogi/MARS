Based on the comprehensive analysis of the "Lessons" and the progression of "Previous Ideas," I propose the **Dense-Quantile Wavelet Decomposition Strategy with Loss-Metric Decoupling**.

This approach structurally evolves the "Current Best" Idea 15 by replacing the Savitzky-Golay filter with a **Multi-Resolution Wavelet Transform** (Structural Innovation) to better capture signal trend and texture. Crucially, it corrects the failure of previous Wavelet attempts (Idea 18) by rigorously applying the **Dense Quantile** feature extraction paradigm (Exploitation) to the wavelet coefficients, ensuring the model retains the high-fidelity distributional information that was lost in prior iterations.

# Model
The predictive core will be a **Single, Highly-Optimized LightGBM Regressor**.
*   **Rationale:** Lessons 00008 and 00046 demonstrated that a single, well-tuned Gradient Boosted Decision Tree (GBDT) consistently outperforms complex Stacking Ensembles of under-optimized base learners or non-convergent Deep Learning models.
*   **Architecture:** A standard GBDT, but with hyperparameters (specifically `num_leaves` and `min_data_in_leaf`) tuned to handle the high dimensionality of the dense quantile feature set.

# Data
The data processing pipeline synthesizes the **Orthogonal Decomposition** logic with **Multi-Resolution Analysis**, strictly adhering to noise-suppression and peak-preservation protocols.

1.  **Preprocessing:**
    *   **Imputation:** Fill missing values with the segment-wise column mean to preserve DC offsets (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
    *   **Multi-Resolution Decomposition (Structural Innovation):** Instead of a single Savitzky-Golay filter, apply a **Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT)** (using the `db4` wavelet at level 3 or 4) to each sensor.
        *   **Approximation Coefficients (cA):** Represent the robust low-frequency "Trend" without the parameter tuning fragility of SG filters (Cite solution_lesson_node_00060).
        *   **Detail Coefficients (cD):** Represent the high-frequency "Texture" and noise structure.

2.  **Feature Engineering:**
    *   **View 1: Kinematic Quantiles (From Wavelet Approximation):**
        *   Compute **First (Velocity)** and **Second (Acceleration)** derivatives of the *Approximation Coefficients*.
        *   Extract a **Dense Grid of Quantiles** (e.g., 1%, 5%, 10%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 90%, 95%, 99%) for these derivatives.
        *   *Rationale:* This combines the noise-suppression of the wavelet transform (Cite solution_lesson_node_00060) with the distributional richness of quantiles, correcting the failure of Idea 18 which lost performance by using abstract metrics instead of granular shapes (Cite solution_lesson_node_00059).
    *   **View 2: Texture Energy (From Wavelet Details):**
        *   Compute **Energy (RMS)** and **Entropy** of the *Detail Coefficients*.
        *   *Rationale:* Captures the roughness/timbre of the signal (Cite solution_lesson_node_00049).
    *   **View 3: Raw Intensity (From Raw Signal):**
        *   Extract **Global Minimum**, **Global Maximum**, and **Peak-to-Peak Range** from the original raw data.
        *   *Rationale:* Strictly preserves the absolute intensity of the event, adhering to the lesson that "outliers are the signal" (Cite solution_lesson_node_00031).
    *   **View 4: Structural Spectral Features (From Raw Signal):**
        *   Compute **PSD Band Power** using **Welch's Method** and **Closed Intervals** (Low/Mid/High).
        *   *Rationale:* Avoids the noise of Raw Periodograms (Cite solution_lesson_node_00054) and the artifacts of open-ended filters (Cite solution_lesson_node_00058).
    *   **View 5: Flattened Temporal Windows (From Raw Signal):**
        *   Divide the signal into non-overlapping windows. Compute **RMS** and **Mean**. **Flatten** these into explicit features (e.g., `w1_rms`, `w2_rms`).
        *   *Rationale:* Preserves the temporal "arrow of time" essential for prediction (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).

3.  **Feature Organization:**
    *   **Spatial Concatenation:** Concatenate features for all 10 sensors side-by-side. Do **not** aggregate across sensors (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).
    *   **No Selection:** Feed the full feature set to the model (Cite solution_lesson_node_00017).

# Training
*   **Strategy:** Use **Stratified K-Fold Cross-Validation** (e.g., 5 folds) based on the provided metadata bins.
*   **Loss Function (Optimization Innovation):** Train using the **L2 Loss (Mean Squared Error)** or **Huber Loss**, even though the evaluation metric is MAE.
    *   *Rationale:* Lesson 00060 demonstrated that optimizing L1 Loss directly leads to degradation due to poor gradient dynamics (constant gradients). L2 provides informative gradients that scale with error magnitude, leading to better convergence and lower final MAE.
*   **Hyperparameters:** Set a high `n_estimators` (e.g., 5,000+) and use **Early Stopping** based on the *Validation MAE* (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).

3.  **Submission:** Format the output as `segment_id,time_to_eruption`.
