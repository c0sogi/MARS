Based on the rigorous analysis of the "Lessons" and the empirical progression of "Previous Ideas," I propose the **Homogeneous Bagging Ensemble with Hybrid-Transform Decomposition and Trend-Shape Profiling**.

This strategy structurally evolves the "Current Best" Idea 15 and the high-potential Idea 31. It resolves the specific conflict between "Feature Granularity" (Lesson 00059) and "Noise Overfitting" (Lesson 00077) by applying a **Split-Granularity Feature Engineering** paradigm: using **Dense Quantiles** to capture the robust shape of the low-frequency Trend, while restricting high-frequency Kinematics (Derivatives) to **Statistical Moments**. This is wrapped in a **High-Capacity Homogeneous Ensemble** to maximize generalization without artificial constraints.

# Model
The predictive architecture will be a **Homogeneous Ensemble of High-Capacity LightGBM Regressors**.

*   **Architecture:** Five independent instances of a **High-Capacity LightGBM** (e.g., `num_leaves=128`, `n_estimators=10,000`, `learning_rate` < 0.05) trained on different data folds (Bagging).
*   **Rationale:** Lesson 00075 demonstrated that **Cross-Validation Ensembling (Bagging)** is the superior regularization strategy for this dataset, outperforming artificial capacity constraints (e.g., shallow trees). High-capacity trees are required to capture the complex non-linear interactions in the decomposed signal, while averaging across folds mitigates the variance that plagued single-model solutions (Cite solution_lesson_node_00075).
*   **Objective Function:** The models will be trained using **L2 Loss (Mean Squared Error)**.
    *   **Rationale:** Lesson 00060 proved that optimizing L2 Loss yields better convergence and lower final MAE than L1 Loss due to superior gradient dynamics (informative gradients vs. constant sign gradients).

# Data
The data processing pipeline implements a **Hybrid-Transform Orthogonal Decomposition** to extract physically distinct signal components using the most effective transform for each domain.

1.  **Preprocessing:**
    *   **Imputation:** Fill missing values with the segment-wise column mean to preserve DC offsets (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
    *   **Decomposition:** For each sensor, generate three distinct signal views:
        *   **View A (Trend):** Apply a **Savitzky-Golay filter** with a **large window size (e.g., 51)** and a **Polynomial Order of 2 (Quadratic)**.
            *   *Correction:* Strictly use **Order 2** to prevent fitting local noise artifacts (Cite solution_lesson_node_00094) and a large window to isolate the baseline drift (Cite solution_lesson_node_00070).
        *   **View B (Texture):** Apply a **Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT)** (e.g., `db4`) to the **Residuals** ($Raw - Trend$) and extract Detail Coefficients (Cite solution_lesson_node_00060).
        *   **View C (Raw):** Retain the original **Raw** signal.

2.  **Feature Engineering (The Split-Granularity Paradigm):**
    *   **From View A (Trend Shape - Innovation):** Extract a **Dense Grid of Quantiles** (e.g., 1%, 5%, 10%, ..., 90%, 95%, 99%) directly from the **Trend** signal.
        *   *Rationale:* The Trend is smooth and noise-free; dense quantiles here accurately describe the "shape" of the deformation without overfitting, recovering the information lost when abstracting to moments (Cite solution_lesson_node_00059).
    *   **From View A (Robust Kinematics):** Compute **First (Velocity)** and **Second (Acceleration)** derivatives of the Trend. Extract **Higher-Order Moments (Skewness, Kurtosis)**, Mean, and Std.
        *   *Correction:* Do **not** use dense quantiles on derivatives. Lesson 00077 proved that moments capture signal "impulsiveness" better than quantiles for derivatives, which otherwise overfit to noise artifacts (Cite solution_lesson_node_00077).
    *   **From View B (Wavelet Texture):** Extract **Energy**, **Entropy**, **Skewness**, and **Kurtosis** from the DWT Detail Coefficients (Cite solution_lesson_node_00049).
    *   **From View C (Absolute Intensity):** Extract **Global Minimum**, **Global Maximum**, and **Peak-to-Peak Range**.
        *   *Rationale:* Strictly preserves the absolute intensity of the event, adhering to the lesson that "outliers are the signal" (Cite solution_lesson_node_00031).
    *   **From View C (High-Resolution Spectral Structure):** Compute **PSD Band Power** using **Welch's Method** with **Closed Intervals** (Low: 0.1-3Hz, Mid: 3-10Hz, High: 10-45Hz).
        *   *Constraint:* Explicitly set `nperseg=1024` to ensure high resolution in the 0.1-3Hz band (Cite solution_lesson_node_00082).
    *   **From View C (Differential Temporal Profiling):**
        *   Divide the signal into $N$ non-overlapping windows. Compute RMS and Mean for each.
        *   **Flatten** these into explicit features (e.g., `w1_rms`) to preserve the "arrow of time" (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).
        *   **Augment** with **First-Order Differences** between consecutive windows to explicitly encode the gradient of the energy envelope.

3.  **Feature Organization:**
    *   **Spatial Concatenation:** Concatenate features for all 10 sensors side-by-side. Do **not** aggregate across sensors (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).
    *   **No Selection:** Feed the full feature set to the models (Cite solution_lesson_node_00017).

# Training
*   **Strategy:** Use **Stratified K-Fold Cross-Validation** (5 folds) based on the provided metadata bins. Train one high-capacity LightGBM per fold.
*   **Optimization:** Use **Early Stopping** based on the *Validation MAE* (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
*   **Hyperparameters:** Set `num_leaves=128` to leverage the high-capacity ensemble strategy (Cite solution_lesson_node_00075).
