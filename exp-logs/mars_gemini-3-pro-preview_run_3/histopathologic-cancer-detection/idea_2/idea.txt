To solve the digital pathology tumor detection task more effectively, I propose a structural evolution from a single-model baseline to a **Heterogeneous Ensemble Pipeline**. This strategy leverages the computational budget to minimize variance and maximize generalization by combining diverse architectural inductive biases.

# Model
I propose replacing the single ResNet-18 architecture with a **Heterogeneous Ensemble** consisting of three distinct neural network backbones. This structural innovation aims to capture different feature representations of the tumor tissue:
1.  **ResNet-18**: Retained as the anchor model due to its proven efficiency and strong performance on this specific patch size.
2.  **EfficientNetV2-Small**: Introduces Fused-MBConv blocks and squeeze-and-excitation mechanisms, which focus on channel-wise feature recalibration and offer a different texture-extraction capability than standard ResNets.
3.  **ConvNeXt-Tiny**: Represents a "modernized" CNN architecture with larger kernel sizes (7x7) and LayerNorm, mimicking the hierarchical representation of Vision Transformers while maintaining convolutional inductive biases.

The final output will be a soft-voting ensemble (arithmetic mean) of the probabilities generated by these three independent models.

# Data
- **Preprocessing (Cropping):** We will strictly maintain the **Center Crop of 64x64 pixels** for all models. This focuses the network on the labeled 32x32 pixel Region of Interest (ROI) while retaining a necessary 16-pixel context buffer, mitigating label noise from the outer periphery (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
- **Augmentation:** We will continue to apply **RandomRotate90** in addition to horizontal and vertical flips. This ensures the models learn features that are robust to the rotational invariance inherent in histopathology images (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).

# Training
- **Dataset Usage:** All three models will be trained on the **full dataset** (approx. 140,000 images) to maximize feature exposure and convergence stability (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
- **Optimization Strategy:** Each model will be trained independently using the AdamW optimizer and a Cosine Annealing scheduler.
- **Checkpointing:** We will implement **TTA-Integrated Checkpointing** for every model. Instead of saving the model with the best single-view validation score, we will run Test Time Augmentation (TTA) during the validation phase of each epoch. The checkpoint will be saved based on the highest **TTA-averaged AUC**, ensuring the stored weights are explicitly optimized for the inference-time aggregation strategy (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).

# Evaluation
- **Inference Strategy:** We will apply **Test Time Augmentation (TTA)** for every image in the test set. Specifically, we will generate predictions for the original image and its geometric transformations (horizontal flip, vertical flip, and combined flip) (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).
- **Aggregation:** The final prediction for each ID will be the average of 12 probability scores (3 models × 4 TTA views per model). This multi-level aggregation reduces both the stochastic variance of individual training runs and the architectural bias of any single network.
