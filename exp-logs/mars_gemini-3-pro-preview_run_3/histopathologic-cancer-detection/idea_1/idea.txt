To establish a robust and highly efficient baseline for this digital pathology task, I propose an **End-to-End ResNet-18 with Spatial Cropping**.

This approach synthesizes the convolutional foundations of the provided architectures (specifically the ResNet backbone mentioned in the hybrid models) but simplifies the pipeline into a standard, single-stage deep learning workflow. It prioritizes speed and direct optimization of the target metric over complex feature extraction or ensemble methods.

### Model
The architecture will be a standard **ResNet-18** (Residual Network with 18 layers).
*   **Backbone**: We will use a ResNet-18 backbone initialized with pre-trained ImageNet weights. This leverages learned feature extractors (edges, textures) that are transferable to histopathology, significantly accelerating convergence compared to training from scratch.
*   **Head**: The standard 1000-class classification head will be replaced with a simple linear layer mapping the final 512-dimensional feature vector to a single scalar output.
*   **Activation**: A Sigmoid activation will be applied to the output during inference to produce a probability score between 0 and 1.
*   **Reasoning**: ResNet-18 is lightweight and computationally efficient, fitting easily within the 24-hour runtime limit while providing sufficient capacity for this binary classification task. Unlike the frozen feature extractors in the "Model Architectures" list, this model will be fine-tuned end-to-end, allowing the filters to adapt specifically to the visual characteristics of tumor tissue.

### Data
The data processing strategy is designed to address the specific spatial constraint of the task (prediction based on the center 32x32px region).
*   **Preprocessing (Cropping)**: Although the input images are 96x96px, the label is determined solely by the center 32x32px region. Training on the full image risks introducing label noise (e.g., a tumor in the corner causing a False Positive). Conversely, cropping strictly to 32x32px eliminates valuable context. We will apply a **Center Crop of 64x64px**. This captures the entire target region plus a 16px context border on all sides, balancing signal integrity with contextual information.
*   **Normalization**: Images will be normalized using the standard ImageNet mean and standard deviation values.
*   **Augmentation**: To prevent overfitting, we will apply lightweight augmentations suitable for pathology images, specifically random horizontal flips and random vertical flips. Rotations are computationally more expensive and will be omitted for this baseline.

### Training
*   **Loss Function**: We will use **BCEWithLogitsLoss** (Binary Cross Entropy combined with a sigmoid layer), which is numerically stable and standard for binary classification.
*   **Optimizer**: The **AdamW** optimizer will be used for its efficiency in handling weight decay and convergence speed.
*   **Scheduler**: A **Cosine Annealing** learning rate scheduler will be employed to gradually reduce the learning rate, helping the model settle into a robust minimum.
*   **Validation**: The provided validation split (from the metadata generation step) will be used to monitor the Area Under the ROC Curve (AUC) after each epoch. The model with the highest validation AUC will be saved.

### Evaluation
*   **Inference**: For the test set, we will load the images and apply the same **Center Crop (64x64px)** and normalization used in training.
*   **Prediction**: The model will output a logit, which will be converted to a probability using the Sigmoid function.
*   **Submission**: These probabilities will be paired with their corresponding IDs and written to the submission CSV file.
