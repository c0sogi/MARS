Based on the comprehensive analysis of the previous attempts and the lessons learned, I propose a **Heterogeneous Cross-Validation Ensemble**. This strategy addresses the variance issues of single-split training and the convergence failures of the previous ensemble attempt by combining architectural diversity with a robust K-Fold validation scheme, all within the ample 24-hour compute budget.

# Model
I propose an ensemble of two distinct backbone architectures to capture diverse feature representations:
1.  **ResNet-18:** Retained as the anchor model due to its proven efficiency and high performance on this specific patch size.
2.  **ConvNeXt-Tiny:** Introduced to provide a modern, Vision Transformer-inspired inductive bias (e.g., larger 7x7 kernels, LayerNorm) which contrasts with the standard CNN approach of ResNet.

Crucially, instead of training a single instance of each, we will implement **5-Fold Cross-Validation**. This means we will train 5 instances of ResNet-18 and 5 instances of ConvNeXt-Tiny (10 models total), each on a different 80% subset of the data. This structural change minimizes the risk of overfitting to a specific data split and maximizes the utilization of the training set.

# Data
- **Preprocessing (Cropping):** We will strictly apply the **Center Crop of 64x64 pixels** for all models. This isolates the 32x32px target ROI while preserving the optimal 16px context buffer found to maximize signal-to-noise ratio (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
- **Augmentation:** We will employ a comprehensive geometric augmentation suite including **RandomRotate90**, HorizontalFlip, and VerticalFlip. This ensures the models learn the rotational invariance inherent in histopathology images (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).

# Training
- **Convergence Priority:** Unlike the failed ensemble attempt which limited training to 2 epochs, we will train every fold of every model to **full convergence (20 epochs)**. This ensures that the ensemble aggregates high-quality predictions rather than under-trained noise (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008).
- **Optimization:**
    - **Batch Size:** We will maximize the batch size (e.g., 512 for ResNet, and the maximum fitting into VRAM for ConvNeXt) to ensure stable Batch Normalization statistics and gradient updates (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).
    - **Regularization:** We will use moderate regularization (Weight Decay ~1e-4) to avoid over-constraining the model's discriminative power, prioritizing ranking performance (AUC) over probability calibration (Loss) (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
- **Checkpointing:** We will utilize **TTA-Integrated Checkpointing**. For each fold, the best model weights will be saved based on the validation AUC calculated using Test Time Augmentation, ensuring the saved state is optimized for the inference strategy (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).

# Evaluation
- **Inference Strategy:** We will apply **Test Time Augmentation (TTA)** with 4 views (Original, Horizontal Flip, Vertical Flip, Combined Flip) for every test image (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).
- **Aggregation:** The final submission will be the **soft voting (arithmetic mean)** of the probabilities from all 10 models (2 architectures × 5 folds), with each model's contribution being the average of its 4 TTA views. This multi-level aggregation (Architecture + Data Split + Geometric View) will significantly reduce variance and boost the final AUC.
