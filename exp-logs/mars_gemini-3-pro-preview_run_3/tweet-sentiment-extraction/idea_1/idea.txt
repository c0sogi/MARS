To establish a robust, high-efficiency baseline that prioritizes speed and simplicity while remaining distinct from the provided deep learning and ensemble architectures, I propose the following approach:

### Model: Statistical Sentiment-Relevance Model
The proposed model is a probabilistic look-up architecture that relies on the statistical "selectability" of words rather than complex feature extraction or neural representations. It operates on the hypothesis that certain words (e.g., "love", "hate", "worst") have a consistently high probability of being included in the `selected_text` when a specific sentiment is present, while others (e.g., "the", "to") have a low probability.

The model consists of two learned dictionaries (one for 'positive' sentiment, one for 'negative' sentiment) where keys are vocabulary tokens and values are **Selection Probabilities**—scores representing the likelihood that a token is part of the ground truth span given the sentiment.

### Data
1.  **Preprocessing**:
    *   Convert all text to lowercase to reduce vocabulary size.
    *   Tokenize the `text` and `selected_text` using a simple whitespace splitter. This preserves punctuation attached to words (e.g., "good!" remains "good!"), aligning with the submission requirement to predict complete, quoted strings.
2.  **Partitioning**:
    *   Separate the training data into three subsets based on the `sentiment` label: Neutral, Positive, and Negative.
    *   The "Neutral" subset is not used for training, as the EDA indicates a 97% overlap between `text` and `selected_text` for this class.

### Training
The training process is a single-pass counting algorithm with no iterative optimization (no gradient descent):

1.  **Count Aggregation**:
    *   For the **Positive** subset: Iterate through all tweets. For every unique token in the `text`, increment a `Total_Count`. If that token also appears in the `selected_text`, increment a `Selected_Count`.
    *   Repeat the same process for the **Negative** subset.
2.  **Probability Estimation**:
    *   Calculate the Selection Probability for each token $w$ given sentiment $S$:
        $$P(selected | w, S) = \frac{\text{Selected\_Count}(w, S)}{\text{Total\_Count}(w, S)}$$
    *   Apply Laplace smoothing or a frequency threshold to handle rare words and prevent overfitting to noise.
    *   This results in two static look-up tables (Positive Weights and Negative Weights).

### Evaluation
Inference is performed using a "Shifted-Score Span Search" to determine the optimal substring:

1.  **Neutral Handling**: If the test tweet's sentiment is "neutral", predict the entire `text` (exploiting the high Jaccard overlap observed in the data).
2.  **Sentiment Handling**: If the sentiment is "positive" or "negative":
    *   Tokenize the test tweet.
    *   Map each token to its learned Selection Probability from the corresponding dictionary. Tokens not found in the training vocabulary are assigned a low default probability.
    *   **Score Transformation**: Subtract a hyperparameter $\tau$ (e.g., 0.4) from each probability. This penalizes irrelevant words (low probability) and rewards relevant words (high probability).
    *   **Span Search**: Identify the contiguous sub-sequence of tokens that maximizes the sum of these transformed scores (effectively applying Kadane’s algorithm or a sliding window search).
    *   Reconstruct the string from the selected tokens to form the final prediction.
