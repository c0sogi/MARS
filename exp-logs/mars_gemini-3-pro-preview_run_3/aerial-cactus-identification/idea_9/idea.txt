This approach synthesizes the superior generalization of ImageNet-pretrained models `Cite solution_lesson_node_00026` with the necessary structural adaptations for low-resolution imagery `Cite solution_lesson_node_00008`, while explicitly excluding architectures proven to be brittle (EfficientNet) or unstable (Transformers) in this specific domain `Cite solution_lesson_node_00022` `Cite solution_lesson_node_00027`.

# Model
I propose a **Heterogeneous Ensemble** of three robust, ImageNet-pretrained architectures. This selection maximizes "orthogonal inductive biases"—combining residual learning, channel-wise attention, and dense feature reuse—to ensure error decorrelation `Cite solution_lesson_node_00012`.

1.  **Base Architectures (The "Robust Triad"):**
    *   **SE-ResNeXt50 (32x4d):** Selected for its high capacity and **Squeeze-and-Excitation** blocks, which effectively model channel interdependencies. This replaces the weaker ResNet34 used in previous iterations, addressing the capacity gap identified in comparative analyses `Cite solution_lesson_node_00027`.
    *   **DenseNet121:** Selected for its **concatenative connectivity**, which maximizes feature reuse and gradient flow. It has proven highly stable under stem modification `Cite solution_lesson_node_00027`.
    *   **ResNet34:** Retained as a stable, lightweight anchor to complement the heavier models.
    *   *Exclusion:* We explicitly exclude **EfficientNet** (brittle to stem surgery) and **Transformers** (unstable convergence on small data) `Cite solution_lesson_node_00022` `Cite solution_lesson_node_00027`.

2.  **Stem Surgery & Hybrid Initialization:**
    *   **Structural Adaptation:** For all three models, the standard ImageNet "stem" (7x7 convolution, stride 2, max pooling) will be replaced with a **single 3x3 convolution with stride 1**. This preserves the full 32x32 spatial resolution, preventing the premature loss of fine-grained details `Cite solution_lesson_node_00008`.
    *   **Initialization Protocol:** We will employ a **Hybrid Initialization** strategy. The new stride-1 stem will be **randomly initialized**, while the rest of the network loads **pretrained ImageNet weights**. This avoids the distribution shift caused by forcing stride-2 weights into a stride-1 context `Cite solution_lesson_node_00023`, while retaining the powerful feature extractors learned from massive data.

3.  **Stacking Meta-Learner:**
    *   Predictions will be fused using a **Logistic Regression Meta-Learner** trained on **Out-Of-Fold (OOF)** predictions. This mitigates "ensemble dilution" by learning optimal weights based on validated reliability rather than simple averaging `Cite solution_lesson_node_00013` `Cite solution_lesson_node_00015`.

# Data
1.  **Preprocessing:**
    *   Images will be converted to floating-point tensors and normalized to [0, 1].
    *   The dataset will be split into **5 Stratified Folds** to ensure consistent class distribution `Cite solution_lesson_node_00015`.

2.  **Augmentation Strategy:**
    *   **Geometric (Lossless Only):** We will strictly limit geometric transformations to **RandomHorizontalFlip** and **RandomVerticalFlip**. We will exclude affine transformations (rotation/shear) to avoid interpolation artifacts that degrade 32x32 images `Cite solution_lesson_node_00014`.
    *   **Photometric:** We will apply **ColorJitter** (brightness and contrast) to resolve the model's proven sensitivity to lighting variations `Cite solution_lesson_node_00002`.
    *   **Regularization:** We will apply **Mixup augmentation** ($\alpha=1.0$) to the training batches. This encourages the models to learn smoother decision boundaries and prevents overfitting `Cite solution_lesson_node_00010`.

# Training
1.  **Optimization:**
    *   **Optimizer:** We will use **AdamW** with weight decay. This decouples weight decay from gradient updates, providing the stable regularization needed for fine-tuning pretrained weights near metric saturation `Cite solution_lesson_node_00016`.
    *   **Hardware Optimization:** We will explicitly set `torch.backends.cudnn.benchmark = True`. Since input sizes are fixed (32x32), this enables the cuDNN autotuner to select the most efficient convolution kernels `Cite solution_lesson_node_00017`.

2.  **Procedure:**
    *   We will train 15 models total (3 architectures $\times$ 5 folds).
    *   **Extended Convergence:** We will employ a **relaxed Early Stopping patience** (e.g., 20+ epochs) and a higher total epoch limit. This accommodates the slower convergence caused by Mixup regularization `Cite solution_lesson_node_00011`.
    *   **Checkpointing:** We will strictly use **`copy.deepcopy(model.state_dict())`** when saving the best model weights to ensure the stored state is immutable `Cite solution_lesson_node_00001`.

# Evaluation
1.  **Inference (Base Models):**
    *   We will employ **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA)**. Predictions will be generated for the original, horizontally flipped, and vertically flipped versions of each test image, then averaged. This enforces the rotational invariance required for aerial imagery `Cite solution_lesson_node_00008`.

2.  **Fusion (Stacking):**
    *   The OOF predictions generated during the training phase will be used to train the Logistic Regression meta-learner.
    *   The TTA-averaged test predictions from all 15 base models will be fed into this meta-learner to produce the final `has_cactus` probability.
