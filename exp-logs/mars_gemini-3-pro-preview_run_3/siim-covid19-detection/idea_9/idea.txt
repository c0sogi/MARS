# Idea 9: Swin Transformer with Dynamic Head (DyHead) and Query-Based Global Classification

## Model
I propose a **Multi-Task Dynamic Head (DyHead) Detector**. This architecture introduces a structural evolution in feature fusion and representation learning, addressing the limitations of standard Feature Pyramid Networks (FPN) used in previous ideas (Idea 1, 5, 6) by unifying scale, spatial, and task awareness.

-   **Backbone**: I will utilize a **Swin Transformer** backbone. Its hierarchical design and shifted-window attention mechanism are effective at capturing both local fine-grained details (for small opacities) and long-range dependencies (for diffuse viral patterns).
-   **Neck (Structural Innovation)**: I will replace the standard FPN/BiFPN with a **Dynamic Head (DyHead)** module. The DyHead block sequentially applies three orthogonal attention mechanisms to the feature pyramid:
    1.  **Scale-aware Attention**: Dynamically fuses features across different pyramid levels, addressing the extreme scale variation of COVID-19 findings (from tiny focal consolidations to massive ground-glass opacities).
    2.  **Spatial-aware Attention**: Focuses on discriminative spatial locations (e.g., lung fields) while suppressing background noise.
    3.  **Task-aware Attention**: Dynamically re-calibrates feature channels to favor either classification or localization tasks.
-   **Detection Head**: I will employ an **ATSS (Adaptive Training Sample Selection)** head on top of the DyHead features. ATSS is an anchor-free mechanism that automatically selects positive and negative samples based on the statistical characteristics of the object, eliminating the need for manual anchor box tuning required in R-CNN approaches.
-   **Study Classification Head (Structural Innovation)**: Instead of simple pooling, I will implement a **Query-Based Global Classifier**. A Transformer Decoder layer will take the refined multi-scale feature maps from the DyHead as keys/values and use **4 learnable "Diagnosis Queries"** (representing the four study categories) as queries. This allows the model to actively "scan" the entire radiograph for class-specific evidence (e.g., searching specifically for "Atypical" textures) rather than relying on a globally averaged vector.

## Data
-   **Preprocessing (Strategic Retention)**: I will strictly implement **Letterbox Resizing** for all input images. By resizing the longest dimension to the target size (e.g., 800 pixels) and padding the shorter dimension with zeros, I ensure that the original aspect ratio is preserved. This prevents the geometric distortion of lung opacities that was identified as a critical failure mode **Cite solution_lesson_node_00010**.
-   **Augmentation**: I will employ a pipeline consisting of **Random Horizontal Flip**, **RandomBrightnessContrast**, and **Multi-Scale Training** (resizing the input within a range of +/- 20% during training). I will avoid Mosaic augmentation to preserve the global anatomical structure required for the Query-Based Classifier.

## Training
-   **Loss Function**: The model will be trained end-to-end with a multi-task objective:
    -   **Detection Loss**: Generalized Focal Loss (GFL) for classification and GIoU Loss for bounding box regression.
    -   **Study Loss**: Cross-Entropy Loss applied to the output of the Diagnosis Queries.
-   **Optimizer**: I will use **AdamW** with a cosine annealing learning rate schedule, which is standard for stabilizing Transformer-based architectures.

## Evaluation
-   **Inference**: A single forward pass generates the study-level probabilities and image-level bounding boxes.
-   **Test Time Augmentation (TTA)**: Inference will be run on original and horizontally flipped images.
-   **Fusion**: The bounding boxes from TTA will be merged using **Weighted Boxes Fusion (WBF)**, which averages the coordinates of clustered predictions to improve localization precision.
-   **Prediction Logic**:
    1.  **Study Level**: The label is determined by the argmax of the Diagnosis Queries' output.
    2.  **Image Level**:
        -   If the Study Label is "Negative for Pneumonia", the image prediction is forced to `none 1 0 0 1 1`.
        -   Otherwise, the WBF-fused bounding boxes are formatted as the "opacity" prediction string.
