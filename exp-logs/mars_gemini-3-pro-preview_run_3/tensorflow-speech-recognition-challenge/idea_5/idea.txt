Based on the progression of experiments and the specific lessons learned, I propose a strategy that evolves the architecture to a **Multi-Resolution ConvNeXt-CRNN**.

This approach upgrades the backbone from the standard ResNet to a modern **ConvNeXt** architecture, which integrates the design principles of Vision Transformers (large kernels, inverted bottlenecks) into a Convolutional Network. This structural change aims to capture wider time-frequency contexts more efficiently while retaining the successful sequence modeling and multi-resolution input strategies from previous iterations.

### Model
I propose the **Multi-Resolution ConvNeXt-CRNN**, a hybrid architecture designed to maximize feature extraction fidelity and temporal modeling.

*   **Input Representation (Retained):** The model will accept a **3-Channel Multi-Resolution Log-Mel Spectrogram**.
    *   We map the time-frequency trade-off to the channel dimension by generating three spectrograms with varying STFT window sizes (e.g., 20ms, 40ms, 60ms) but a fixed hop length. These are stacked to form a 3-channel image (RGB equivalent) **Cite solution_lesson_node_00020**.
*   **Backbone (Structural Innovation):** I will replace the ResNet34 backbone with **ConvNeXt-Tiny** (pretrained on ImageNet).
    *   **Why ConvNeXt:** Unlike ResNet's 3x3 kernels, ConvNeXt utilizes **7x7 depthwise convolutions**. This significantly expands the receptive field in early layers, allowing the model to capture larger spectral patterns (like formants) and longer temporal contexts immediately. Its "inverted bottleneck" design and Layer Normalization provide a more robust feature space for audio than standard ResNet blocks.
    *   **Adaptation:** The 3-channel multi-resolution input will be mapped directly to the pretrained RGB input weights **Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**.
    *   **Temporal Preservation:** To ensure the output feature map retains sufficient length for the recurrent layer, I will modify the strides of the final ConvNeXt stages to `(1, 1)` instead of `(2, 2)` **Cite solution_lesson_node_00017**.
*   **Neck (Retained):** The output features will be averaged over the frequency dimension and passed to a **Bidirectional GRU (BiGRU)**. This explicitly models the sequential order of phonemes, which is critical for distinguishing commands like "stop" vs "top" **Cite solution_lesson_node_00017**.
*   **Head (Retained):** An **Attention Pooling** layer will aggregate the recurrent states, allowing the model to dynamically focus on the active speech segment and ignore the variable-length silence inherent in the uncropped test data **Cite solution_lesson_node_00017**.

### Data
*   **Preprocessing:** Audio will be resampled to 16kHz and padded/cropped to 1 second. The 3-channel spectrogram stack will be generated on-the-fly with `n_mels=80` to match the increased capacity of the ConvNeXt backbone **Cite solution_lesson_node_00022**.
*   **Sampling Strategy (Retained):** I will use **Weighted Random Sampling** with the **Full Unknown Dataset**.
    *   Instead of undersampling the `unknown` class (which discards data), we will keep all ~29k `unknown` samples to maximize negative class diversity. The sampler will ensure balanced batches by assigning weights inversely proportional to class frequency **Cite solution_lesson_node_00022**.
*   **Augmentation:**
    *   **SpecAugment Only:** I will apply Frequency and Time Masking.
    *   **Configuration:** Masked regions will be filled with the **minimum value** of the spectrogram **Cite solution_lesson_node_00010**. Random masks will be generated using **inclusive bounds** **Cite solution_lesson_node_00011**. Time masks will be strictly limited to **<20%** of the duration to preserve short command context **Cite solution_lesson_node_00008**.
    *   **Exclusion:** I will **NOT** use MixUp or Label Smoothing, as these have been shown to cause underfitting by over-regularizing the model given the small generalization gap **Cite solution_lesson_node_00016** **Cite solution_lesson_node_00019**.

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** Standard **Cross-Entropy Loss**.
*   **Optimizer:** **AdamW** with weight decay.
*   **Scheduler:** **Cosine Annealing** scheduler to ensure smooth convergence to a robust minima.
*   **Validation:** Performance will be monitored on the speaker-disjoint validation set defined in the metadata.

### Evaluation
*   **Inference:** For each test file, the pipeline will generate the same 3-channel Multi-Resolution Spectrogram (without augmentation).
*   **Prediction:** The model will process the input, and the Attention Pooling head will automatically handle alignment and silence. The class with the highest probability will be the final prediction.
