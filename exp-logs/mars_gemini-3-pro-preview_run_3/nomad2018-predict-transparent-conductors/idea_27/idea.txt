Based on the accumulated lessons, particularly the success of **Distributional Aggregation (Percentiles)** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00054) and **Continuous Geometric Descriptors** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00057), I propose a **Chemo-Structural Dual-Sublattice Distributional Fingerprinting** strategy.

This approach addresses a critical gap in previous iterations: while cation (Metal) environments have been well-modeled, the **Anion (Oxygen) Sublattice** has been treated purely geometrically. In complex oxides ($Al_x Ga_y In_z O_3$), the electronic properties are heavily influenced by the specific chemical coordination of Oxygen atoms (e.g., an Oxygen bonded to In-In-In vs. Al-Ga-Al). This strategy explicitly models the distribution of these *chemical* environments alongside the *geometric* distortions, using the robust percentile-based aggregation method to avoid feature dilution.

# Model
-   **Architecture**: **XGBoost Regressor** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003). Tree ensembles remain the most effective architecture for this dataset when provided with dense, high-signal descriptors.
-   **Input Features**: A concatenated feature vector representing the material from multiple physical perspectives:
    1.  **Global Physical Descriptors**: Unit Cell Volume and Mass Density.
    2.  **Radial Fingerprints (RDF)**: Element-resolved pairwise distance histograms.
    3.  **Cation Geometric Fingerprints**: Element-resolved percentiles of local distortion metrics (ECoN, Angle Variance).
    4.  **Anion Chemo-Structural Fingerprints (The Innovation)**: Percentiles of Oxygen-centric chemical and topological properties.
    5.  **Tabular Metadata**: Atomic composition and Spacegroup.

# Data
1.  **Geometric Feature Extraction (Raw Geometry)**:
    -   Load crystal structures from raw `.xyz` files using the `ase` library to ensure fidelity (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).
    -   **Global**: Calculate **Unit Cell Volume** and **Mass Density** directly from the atomic objects (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).
    -   **Radial (2-Body)**: Compute **Element-Resolved RDFs** (e.g., Al-O, Ga-O, In-O) using a moderate cutoff (e.g., 6.0 Å) and normalize by **atom count** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00033).
    -   **Cation Sublattice (Metal-Centric)**:
        -   For each Metal atom (Al, Ga, In), calculate continuous geometric metrics: **Effective Coordination Number (ECoN)** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00057) and **Bond Angle Variance** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00037).
        -   Group by element type and compute **Percentiles** (Min, 25%, 50%, 75%, Max). This captures the distribution of site distortions (Cite solution_lesson_node_00054).
    -   **Anion Sublattice (Oxygen-Centric)**:
        -   For each Oxygen atom, identify its Metal neighbors.
        -   Calculate **Local Chemical Descriptors**:
            *   **Mean Neighbor Electronegativity**: The average electronegativity of the bonded metals.
            *   **Mean Neighbor Ionic Radius**: The average radius of the bonded metals (proxy for local steric strain).
        -   Calculate **Local Topological Descriptors**:
            *   **Mean Metal-Oxygen-Metal Angle**: The average bridging angle centered at the oxygen (Cite solution_lesson_node_00055).
        -   **Aggregation**: Collect these values for *all* Oxygen atoms in the crystal and compute **Percentiles** (Min, 25%, 50%, 75%, Max). This creates a dense fingerprint of the *chemical heterogeneity* and *connectivity* of the oxygen network without sparse histograms.

2.  **Preprocessing**:
    -   **Target Transformation**: Apply the natural logarithm transformation $z = \log(1 + y)$ to the target variables (`formation_energy_ev_natom` and `bandgap_energy_ev`) (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
    -   **Feature Cleaning**: Drop constant columns to prevent feature dilution (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016).
    -   **Metadata**: Include explicit Lattice Vectors, Angles, and Composition from the CSV (Cite solution_lesson_node_00058).

# Training
-   **Algorithm**: Train separate XGBoost regressors for formation energy and bandgap energy.
-   **Optimization Strategy**:
    -   **Shrinkage**: Use a **low learning rate** (e.g., $\eta \approx 0.01$) and a **high number of estimators** (e.g., 3,000) to maximize generalization (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).
    -   **Stochastic Subsampling**: Set row (`subsample`) and column (`colsample_bytree`) subsampling to $\approx 0.6 - 0.7$. This forces the model to utilize the diverse structural views (Radial, Cation-Geometric, Anion-Chemical) rather than overfitting to specific features (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).
-   **Loss Function**: Mean Squared Error (MSE) on the log-transformed targets.

# Evaluation
-   **Inference**:
    1.  Process the test set `.xyz` files to generate Physical, RDF, Cation Geometric, and Anion Chemo-Structural features.
    2.  Generate predictions in the log-transformed space using the trained XGBoost models.
    3.  Apply the inverse transformation $\hat{y} = \exp(z) - 1$ to obtain the final energy predictions.
