Based on the comprehensive analysis of previous attempts and the accumulated lessons, I propose a **Distributional Multi-Order Geometric Fingerprinting (DMGF)** strategy.

This approach synthesizes the most successful feature engineering paradigms identified in the lessons—**Radial Distribution Functions (RDF)** for global structure and **Percentile Aggregation** for local distributions—into a unified pipeline. It addresses the "feature dilution" of sparse histograms (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00016}) and the "information loss" of mean/variance statistics (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00054}) by rigorously characterizing the *shape* of geometric distributions. Crucially, it introduces **Steinhardt Order Parameters ($Q_l$)** aggregated via percentiles, correcting the failure of Idea 15 (which used means, Cite {solution_lesson_node_00040}) to explicitly capture the distribution of local symmetries (e.g., distorted tetrahedra vs. octahedra) alongside packing and topology.

# Model
-   **Architecture**: **XGBoost Regressor** (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00003}). Tree ensembles have consistently proven to be the most robust predictor for this dataset when provided with dense, high-signal descriptors.
-   **Input Features**: A concatenated feature vector representing the material at multiple geometric orders:
    1.  **Global Descriptors**: Unit Cell Volume and Mass Density.
    2.  **2-Body Fingerprints**: Element-resolved Radial Distribution Functions (RDF) and Bond Length Percentiles.
    3.  **3-Body Fingerprints**: Chemically resolved Bond Angle Percentiles (both Intra- and Inter-polyhedral).
    4.  **Many-Body Fingerprints (The Innovation)**: Chemically resolved percentiles of **Effective Coordination Number (ECoN)** (Packing) and **Steinhardt Order Parameters** (Symmetry).
    5.  **Tabular Metadata**: Atomic composition and Spacegroup.

# Data
1.  **Geometric Feature Extraction (Raw Geometry)**:
    -   Load crystal structures from raw `.xyz` files using the `ase` library to ensure fidelity (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00018}).
    -   **Global**: Calculate Unit Cell Volume and Mass Density directly from the atomic objects.
    -   **2-Body (Radial)**:
        -   Compute **Element-Resolved RDFs** (e.g., Al-O, Ga-O) using a moderate cutoff (e.g., 6.0 Å) and normalize by **atom count** (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00033}).
        -   Compute **Bond Length Percentiles**: Collect all bond lengths for specific pairs (e.g., Al-O) and calculate percentiles (Min, 10%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 90%, Max).
    -   **3-Body (Angular)**:
        -   Compute **Bond Angle Percentiles**: Collect bond angles grouped by the central atom type.
            -   **O-Metal-O**: Captures polyhedral distortion.
            -   **Metal-Oxygen-Metal**: Captures framework connectivity/tilting (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00022}).
    -   **Many-Body (Local Environment)**:
        -   For every atom, calculate continuous local metrics:
            -   **Effective Coordination Number (ECoN)**: Captures local packing density (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00057}).
            -   **Steinhardt Order Parameters ($Q_4, Q_6$)**: Captures local rotational symmetry (e.g., distinguishing tetrahedral vs. octahedral environments).
        -   **Distributional Aggregation**: Group these metrics by element type (Al, Ga, In, O) and compute **Percentiles**. This explicitly resolves the population of distinct crystallographic sites (e.g., 30% distorted surface sites vs. 70% bulk) without the smoothing of averages (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00054}).

2.  **Preprocessing**:
    -   **Target Transformation**: Apply the natural logarithm transformation $z = \log(1 + y)$ to the target variables (`formation_energy_ev_natom` and `bandgap_energy_ev`) (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00002}).
    -   **Feature Cleaning**: Drop constant columns to prevent feature dilution (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00016}).

# Training
-   **Algorithm**: Train separate XGBoost regressors for formation energy and bandgap energy.
-   **Optimization Strategy**:
    -   **Shrinkage**: Use a **low learning rate** (e.g., $\eta \approx 0.01$) and a **high number of estimators** (e.g., 3,000) to maximize generalization (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00004}).
    -   **Stochastic Subsampling**: Set row (`subsample`) and column (`colsample_bytree`) subsampling to $\approx 0.6 - 0.7$. This forces the model to utilize the diverse multi-order information rather than overfitting to specific dominant features (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00004}).
-   **Loss Function**: Mean Squared Error (MSE) on the log-transformed targets.

# Evaluation
-   **Inference**:
    1.  Process the test set `.xyz` files to generate the Multi-Order Distributional Fingerprints.
    2.  Generate predictions in the log-transformed space using the trained XGBoost models.
    3.  Apply the inverse transformation $\hat{y} = \exp(z) - 1$ to obtain the final energy predictions.
