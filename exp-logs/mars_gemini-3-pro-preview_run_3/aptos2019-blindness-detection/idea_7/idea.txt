Based on the analysis of the provided lessons and the progression of previous ideas, I propose the **Progressive High-Resolution GeM-ConvNeXt Ensemble** strategy.

This approach introduces a **Progressive Resizing** training pipeline to structurally evolve the robust ConvNeXt baseline. By training on increasing resolutions, we can effectively target the remaining resolution-dependent errors identified in the failure analysis while managing the computational constraints that previously limited input size.

### Model
I propose using **ConvNeXt-Base** as the backbone, enhanced with a **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling** head.
*   **Backbone:** We utilize **ConvNeXt-Base** specifically for its **Layer Normalization** design. This is critical because it decouples the model's statistical stability from the physical batch size, enabling us to train on extremely high-resolution inputs (e.g., 1024x1024) using micro-batches without the feature collapse observed in Batch Norm-based architectures like EfficientNet (**Cite Lesson 00012**, **Cite Lesson 00015**).
*   **Aggregation Head:** We replace the standard Global Average Pooling with **GeM Pooling**. This trainable pooling layer focuses on high-activation spatial regions, allowing the model to better isolate sparse, fine-grained lesions (microaneurysms) from the healthy background retina compared to standard averaging (**Cite Lesson 00019**).
*   **Regression Output:** The model terminates in a single linear neuron trained to predict a continuous severity score, maintaining the regression formulation that aligns optimally with the ordinal Quadratic Weighted Kappa metric (**Cite Lesson 00001**).

### Data
The data pipeline is designed to maximize information density and geometric invariance while enabling the progressive training strategy.
*   **Preprocessing (ROI Cropping):** We apply an algorithm to detect the circular fundus mask and crop the image to this Region of Interest (ROI) before resizing. This ensures the input tensor is dedicated entirely to retinal tissue, removing uninformative black borders (**Cite Lesson 00007**).
*   **Resolution Strategy:** We employ a **Progressive Resizing** approach. We prepare the dataset at two resolutions: **512x512** for the initial training phase and **1024x1024** for the fine-tuning phase. The push to 1024x1024 directly targets the persistent correlation between prediction error and image dimensions, minimizing information loss for large images (**Cite Lesson 00006**).
*   **Augmentation:** We strictly limit augmentation to **Geometric Transformations** (Random Horizontal Flip, Random Vertical Flip, Random 90-degree Rotation). This enforces rotational invariance (**Cite Lesson 00008**) while avoiding Photometric Augmentations (e.g., brightness/contrast), which have been shown to degrade performance by washing out subtle lesion signals (**Cite Lesson 00016**).

### Training
We implement a **5-Fold Stratified Cross-Validation** pipeline with a two-stage progressive training schedule.
*   **Cross-Validation:** Training on 5 folds is essential to mitigate the high variance observed in the dataset and provide a reliable estimate of performance (**Cite Lesson 00020**).
*   **Stage 1 (Structure Learning):** Train the model from scratch on **512x512** images. This allows for faster epoch times and larger effective batch sizes, letting the model learn high-level structural features efficiently.
*   **Stage 2 (Fine-Grained Adaptation):** Fine-tune the model on **1024x1024** images. This phase adapts the feature extractors to the specific pixel-scale of lesions at high resolution, preventing the "Scale Sensitivity" failure mode (**Cite Lesson 00009**).
*   **Optimization:**
    *   **Loss Function:** Mean Squared Error (MSE) (**Cite Lesson 00001**).
    *   **Gradient Accumulation:** During Stage 2, we use a physical batch size of 1 or 2 (to fit 1024px images in GPU memory) and accumulate gradients to achieve a stable effective batch size. This is only feasible due to the ConvNeXt backbone (**Cite Lesson 00015**).
    *   **Learning Rate:** We use a **Constant Learning Rate** (e.g., 1e-4 for Stage 1, 1e-5 for Stage 2) to act as implicit regularization and prevent overfitting to sharp minima (**Cite Lesson 00018**).

### Evaluation
*   **Inference:** Predictions are generated using the ensemble of 5 models. Each model processes test images at **1024x1024**, matching the final training resolution to ensure optimal feature detection (**Cite Lesson 00009**).
*   **Post-Processing:** The continuous outputs from the ensemble are averaged, **clipped** to [0, 4], and **rounded** to the nearest integer to produce the final diagnosis (**Cite Lesson 00001**).
