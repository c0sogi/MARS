My optimization strategy focuses on two key areas derived from the lessons learned: improving the robustness of the primary interaction feature (`closure_rate`) and enabling model convergence by training on the full dataset.

1.  **Feature Engineering (Stream A)**: I will replace the vector-projection based calculation of `closure_rate` with a **Finite Difference** approach (Cite Lesson 00080). The previous method compounded noise from four sensor readings (speed/direction for two players), whereas calculating the change in Euclidean distance over time (`-(d_t - d_{t-1}) / dt`) provides a more robust, direct measurement of approach velocity, which is critical for the interaction model.

2.  **Training Configuration**: I will remove the debug overrides in `runfile.py` that limited the dataset to 150,000 samples and estimators to 150. By reverting to the default configuration (`DEBUG_SAMPLE_SIZE = None`, `n_estimators = 2000`), I allow the model to train on the full dataset. This addresses the "Hierarchy of Feature Convergence" (Cite Lesson 00104), ensuring the model learns fundamental kinematic boundaries (distance, speed) before attempting to leverage complex features.
