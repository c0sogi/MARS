Based on the analysis of the previous solution (Idea 32) and the provided lessons, the regression in performance was primarily caused by the use of "Scale-Aligned Residuals" (Z-scores) which removed critical absolute magnitude information (Cite Lesson 107) and the use of sparse lags which failed to capture the dense immediate context required for transient impact detection (Cite Lesson 107).

I will implement the following optimizations:
1.  **Revert to Raw Physical Features:** In Stream A, I will remove the Z-score normalization and the composite `scale_aligned_residual` feature, reverting to raw `closure_rate`, `looming_rate` (IoU diff), and `dist`. This aligns with Lesson 107 ("Raw absolute magnitudes are superior") and Lesson 105 (avoiding complex linear combinations in GBDTs).
2.  **Implement Dense Immediate Temporal Context:** I will replace the sparse lag schedule (`[0, 4, 8, 15]`) with a dense, centered lag schedule that includes immediate past and future steps (e.g., `t +/- 1, 2`).
    -   **Stream A (Interaction):** `[-8, -4, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 4, 8]` to capture both approach and departure dynamics.
    -   **Stream B (Impact):** `[-5, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 5]` to capture the sharp, transient deceleration profile of ground impacts (Cite Lesson 86, Lesson 107).
3.  **Preserve Hybrid Context in Stream B:** I will ensure Stream B retains both ego-centric features (Surge/Sway) and raw magnitudes (Speed/Accel) to maintain robustness (Cite Lesson 55, Lesson 91).
