To optimize the solution, I will implement a strategy based on "Hybrid Context with Dense Lags" and "Explicit Directional Kinematics," reverting the "Scale-Aligned Residuals" approach which degraded performance.

**Strategy:**
1.  **Feature Engineering (Stream A - Interaction):**
    *   **Remove Z-Score Normalization:** Revert to raw physical magnitudes for `closure_rate` and `looming_rate`. *Cite Lesson 00107 (Raw absolute magnitudes are superior to locally normalized statistics).*
    *   **Dense Temporal Lags:** Expand the lag schedule to `[0, 1, 2, 4, 8, 15]` to capture immediate contact dynamics ($t\pm1, t\pm2$) which were lost in the sparse schedule. *Cite Lesson 00042 (Exponentially spaced lags).*
    *   **Feature Set:** Use explicit `dist`, `closure_rate` (finite diff), `max_iou`, `looming_rate` (finite diff), and individual kinematics (`speed`, `acceleration`). *Cite Lesson 00080 (Prioritize Positional Differentials).*

2.  **Feature Engineering (Stream B - Impact):**
    *   **Explicit Directional Jerk:** Calculate `jerk_surge` and `jerk_sway` (derivatives of ego-centric velocities) to capture the specific mechanics of ground impact (e.g., sudden stop vs. lateral tumble). *Cite Lesson 00070 (Explicitly engineering higher-order derivatives).*
    *   **Context Preservation:** Retain raw `speed` and `acceleration` alongside ego-centric features. *Cite Lesson 00091 (Do not strip contextual features).*
    *   **Dense Lags:** Apply the same dense lag schedule to these kinematic features to capture the impact profile.

3.  **Hyperparameters:**
    *   **Stream A:** Increase `max_depth` to 8 to allow the model to learn complex interaction boundaries given the richer feature set. *Cite Lesson 00045 (Allow higher capacity).*
    *   **Stream B:** Set `max_depth` to 6 to prevent overfitting on noisy high-order derivatives. *Cite Lesson 00070 (Keep model shallow for noisy sensor data).*
