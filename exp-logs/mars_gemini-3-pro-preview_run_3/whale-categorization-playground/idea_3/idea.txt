Based on the empirical insights from the provided lessons, I propose the following optimized strategy. This approach evolves the solution from a standard Margin-based Classification model to a **Curriculum-Guided Metric Learning Pipeline with Contextual Re-ranking**.

### 1. Model: EfficientNet-B2 with CurricularFace
**Architecture:**
*   **Backbone:** I will retain the **EfficientNet-B2** backbone.
    *   *Reasoning:* This architecture was identified as the "sweet spot" in **Lesson 00010** (outperforming DenseNet) and **Lesson 00014** (where larger models like B4 failed to converge due to data sparsity). Its channel-wise attention (SE blocks) is critical for extracting fine-grained features from whale flukes.
*   **Pooling:** I will use **Global Average Pooling (GAP)**.
    *   *Reasoning:* I will explicitly avoid learnable pooling layers (like GeM), which were shown to induce instability and early plateaus in this low-data regime (**Cite Lesson 00007**).
*   **Head (Structural Innovation):** I will replace the ArcFace head with a **CurricularFace** (Curriculum Learning for Face Recognition) head.
    *   *Reasoning:* While **Lesson 00008** proved that Margin-based Softmax (ArcFace) is superior to Triplet Loss, **Lesson 00005** highlighted the model's sensitivity to the margin hyperparameter. CurricularFace structurally evolves this by introducing **Adaptive Curriculum Learning** into the loss function. It dynamically adjusts the importance of samples during training—initially focusing on easy samples and gradually shifting focus to hard samples—addressing the convergence difficulties observed with static margins.

### 2. Data: Geometric Consistency
*   **Preprocessing:** Images will be resized to a standard resolution (e.g., 448x448) compatible with the B2 backbone.
*   **Augmentation:** I will strictly employ **Geometric Augmentations** (rotations, flips, slight affine shifts).
    *   *Reasoning:* I will explicitly exclude occlusion-based augmentations (like Cutout/Random Erasing). **Lesson 00014** demonstrated that these techniques, while popular, are detrimental in few-shot scenarios because they risk obscuring the *only* discriminative features (e.g., a specific scar) present in singleton classes.

### 3. Training: Adaptive Margin Optimization
*   **Loss Function:** The model will be trained using **CurricularFace Loss**. Unlike ArcFace's fixed angular margin ($m=0.5$), CurricularFace modulates the margin based on the sample's difficulty, preventing the "lazy convergence" seen in **Lesson 00005** while ensuring robust separation.
*   **Strategy:**
    *   **Class Handling:** Training will be conducted on the set of known `Id`s. `new_whale` images will be excluded from the classification training to prevent clustering distinct unknown individuals into a single prototype (**Cite Lesson 00001**).
    *   **Optimizer:** AdamW with a cosine annealing scheduler, consistent with the successful convergence of the B2 baseline.

### 4. Evaluation: k-Reciprocal Re-ranking
**Structural Innovation (Pipeline):**
Instead of relying solely on pairwise Cosine Similarity for the final ranking (as done in **Lesson 00008**), I will implement **k-Reciprocal Encoding Re-ranking** during the inference phase.
*   **Concept:** This technique, standard in Person Re-Identification but novel here, refines the distance between a query (Test Image) and a gallery (Train Image) by considering their **mutual nearest neighbors**.
*   **Mechanism:**
    1.  Compute the initial Cosine Similarity rank list.
    2.  Check the "k-reciprocal" condition: If Image A is in Image B's top-k neighbors *and* Image B is in Image A's top-k neighbors, they are a strong match.
    3.  Calculate a new Jaccard-based distance metric based on the overlap of their neighbor sets.
*   **Benefit:** This directly addresses the **Open-Set** and **Singleton** nature of the task. A `new_whale` query will likely have a sparse, non-reciprocal set of neighbors in the gallery, resulting in a high re-ranked distance that makes it easier to threshold correctly as `new_whale`. Conversely, true matches will have their similarity boosted by the density of their shared context.
