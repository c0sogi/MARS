Based on the empirical insights from the provided lessons, I propose the following optimized strategy. This approach synthesizes the structural strengths of **Idea 3** (which failed due to flawed validation, not architecture) with the optimization rigor established in **Lesson 00019** and the architectural "sweet spot" identified in **Lesson 00010**.

### 1. Model: EfficientNet-B2 with CurricularFace
**Architecture:**
*   **Backbone:** I will utilize the **EfficientNet-B2** architecture.
    *   *Reasoning:* This backbone provides the optimal trade-off between feature extraction capacity and convergence speed (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00010**). It avoids the underfitting and optimization difficulties observed with larger backbones like B4 in this low-data regime (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00014**).
*   **Pooling:** I will employ **Global Average Pooling (GAP)**.
    *   *Reasoning:* I will strictly avoid learnable pooling layers (like GeM), which were shown to introduce instability and early plateaus (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00007**).
*   **Head (Structural Innovation):** I will implement a **CurricularFace** head.
    *   *Reasoning:* While ArcFace (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00008**) is superior to Triplet Loss, its fixed margin is sensitive to hyperparameters (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**). Adaptive margins like AdaFace failed because they rely on batch statistics, which are unstable at small batch sizes (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00020**). CurricularFace adapts the margin based on *sample difficulty* (the cosine angle of the sample itself) rather than batch statistics. This allows the model to dynamically focus on hard samples without the instability of batch-dependent normalization.

### 2. Data: Geometric Consistency
*   **Preprocessing:** Images will be resized to **448x448**, the native resolution for EfficientNet-B2.
*   **Augmentation:** I will strictly apply **Geometric Augmentations** (rotation, scaling, flips).
    *   *Reasoning:* I will explicitly exclude occlusion-based augmentations (Cutout/Random Erasing). As demonstrated in **Lesson 00014**, these techniques are detrimental in few-shot scenarios because they risk obscuring the sole discriminative features (e.g., scars) present in singleton classes.
*   **Class Handling:** Training will be restricted to known `Id`s. `new_whale` images will be excluded from the training loop to prevent clustering distinct unknown individuals into a single prototype (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00001**).

### 3. Training: Aggressive Optimization Strategy
*   **Loss Function:** The model will be trained using **CurricularFace Loss**.
*   **Optimizer:** I will use **AdamW** with a **High Learning Rate (e.g., 1e-3)** and a **Small Batch Size (e.g., 16)**.
    *   *Reasoning:* Margin-based losses on hyperspheres require significant gradient energy to cluster embeddings. **Lesson 00019** proved that conservative learning rates (1e-4) lead to severe underfitting. A smaller batch size increases the frequency of gradient updates per epoch, accelerating the convergence of the complex geometric objective.

### 4. Evaluation: Retrieval-Based Validation & Contextual Re-ranking
*   **Validation Protocol (Crucial Correction):** I will implement a **Retrieval-based Validation Loop**.
    *   *Reasoning:* I will not validate using the classification head's proxies. Instead, I will extract embeddings for the Train set (Gallery) and Validation set (Query) and compute MAP@5 using k-Nearest Neighbors on *known identities only*. This corrects the "Proxy vs. Exemplar" mismatch and the "Open-Set Penalty" that caused the failure in **Lesson 00016**.
*   **Inference (Structural Innovation):** I will apply **k-Reciprocal Encoding Re-ranking** to the test predictions.
    *   *Reasoning:* Standard Cosine Similarity considers only pairwise distances. Re-ranking refines these distances by checking if the nearest neighbors are *reciprocal* (i.e., do they share a common set of neighbors?). This is particularly effective for the Open-Set problem: `new_whale` images will likely have sparse, non-reciprocal matches in the gallery, resulting in lower re-ranked scores that are easier to filter out with a threshold compared to raw cosine similarity.
