Based on the progression of empirical findings and the lessons learned, I propose the following optimized strategy. This approach structurally evolves the architecture by transitioning to a **ConvNeXt** backbone to address receptive field and width limitations identified in previous lessons, while introducing **Frequency-Wise Normalization** to specifically target the stationary noise characteristics of the dataset.

# Model

**Backbone: ConvNeXt-Small with Coordinate Attention**
I propose replacing the EfficientNet architecture with **ConvNeXt-Small**.
*   **Rationale (Receptive Field):** Previous lessons established that matching the model's receptive field to the signal duration is critical (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002). ConvNeXt utilizes large **7x7 kernels**, which significantly expands the effective receptive field in early layers compared to EfficientNet's 3x3 convolutions. This allows the model to capture the long-range temporal dynamics of whale calls more efficiently.
*   **Rationale (Width):** ConvNeXt-Small offers significantly wider channels compared to the EfficientNet-B2 used previously. This aligns with the finding that increasing channel width is highly effective for extracting fine-grained texture features from spectrograms (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
*   **Structural Innovation (Coordinate Attention):** I will inject **Coordinate Attention** blocks into the backbone. While standard Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006) pools spatial information globally, Coordinate Attention factorizes attention into two 1D encoding processes (along time and frequency axes). This preserves precise positional information, enabling the model to distinguish the specific time-frequency morphology of a call from random noise.

**Head: Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling**
I will retain **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling** to aggregate the feature maps.
*   **Rationale:** Whale calls are transient events localized in time and frequency. GeM pooling allows the model to dynamically learn to focus on high-activation regions (the call) while suppressing the dominant background noise, a critical mechanism for this weakly supervised task (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).

# Data

**Input Representation: Frequency-Standardized High-Resolution Log-Mel Spectrograms**
*   **Resolution Scaling:** I will use **320 Mel bands** with a short hop length (<15ms). This implements the **Compound Scaling** principle (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010), ensuring the input fidelity matches the increased capacity of the ConvNeXt-Small backbone.
*   **Normalization (Structural Innovation):** Instead of global or instance-level Min-Max scaling, I will implement **Per-Instance Frequency-Wise Standardization**. For each spectrogram, I will calculate the mean and standard deviation *per frequency bin* across the time dimension and normalize ($x' = (x - \mu_f) / \sigma_f$).
    *   **Rationale:** Underwater noise (e.g., ship engines, water flow) often manifests as stationary energy in specific frequency bands. Normalizing per frequency bin effectively performs "spectral subtraction," flattening the background noise floor and enhancing the contrast of transient events (whale calls) that deviate from the stationary mean.

# Training

**Loss Function: Binary Cross-Entropy with Inverse Class Frequency Weighting**
*   I will use **BCE Loss weighted by the inverse class frequency**. This remains the most robust and efficient method for handling the ~9:1 class imbalance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).

**Regularization & Augmentation**
*   **Mixup with Mixed Losses:** I will apply Mixup augmentation. Crucially, I will compute the loss by mixing the **weighted scalar losses** of the input pair rather than mixing the labels or weights. This ensures that the gradients from the minority class are not diluted by the majority class weights (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).
*   **SpecAugment:** I will apply random time and frequency masking to the spectrograms to force the model to learn robust partial features (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
*   **Stochastic Depth:** To prevent overfitting in the deeper ConvNeXt-Small model, I will apply Stochastic Depth (DropPath) regularization.

**Optimization**
*   **Optimizer:** Use **AdamW** combined with a **Cosine Annealing Learning Rate Scheduler**. This schedule allows for aggressive initial learning followed by stable convergence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).

# Evaluation

**Inference Strategy**
*   **Checkpoint Reloading:** The training pipeline will explicitly monitor Validation AUC and save the model state with the highest score. Before generating the final submission, the system will **reload these best-saved weights**, discarding the state from the final training epoch. This prevents the use of a potentially degraded model caused by overfitting in the late stages of training (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008).
