Based on the progression of empirical findings and the lessons learned, I propose the following optimized strategy. This approach structurally evolves the architecture by integrating the modern design principles of **ConvNeXt** with specialized attention mechanisms and pooling strategies tailored for weakly supervised acoustic event detection.

# Model

**Backbone: ConvNeXt-Small with Coordinate Attention**
I propose upgrading the backbone from the previously suggested ConvNeXt-Tiny to **ConvNeXt-Small**. This structural evolution addresses two key lessons:
1.  **Capacity Scaling:** The "Small" variant significantly increases network width and depth compared to "Tiny". This aligns with the finding that increasing channel width is highly effective for extracting fine-grained texture from spectrograms (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
2.  **Attention Integration:** Unlike EfficientNet (used in Lesson 9), standard ConvNeXt blocks lack explicit attention mechanisms. I propose injecting **Coordinate Attention** blocks into the backbone. While standard Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) pools spatial information globally (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006), Coordinate Attention factorizes attention into two 1D encoding processes (along the time and frequency axes). This allows the model to capture long-range dependencies in the time domain (call duration) and frequency domain (pitch contours) separately, preserving the precise positional information required for detecting localized whale calls.

**Head: Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling with Multi-Sample Dropout**
*   **GeM Pooling:** I will replace the standard Global Average Pooling with **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling**. As established, this trainable pooling layer allows the model to dynamically focus on the high-activation regions (the transient call) while suppressing the dominant background noise, which is critical for this weakly supervised task (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
*   **Multi-Sample Dropout:** To prevent overfitting given the increased capacity of the "Small" backbone, I will implement Multi-Sample Dropout in the classification head. This technique computes the loss across multiple dropout masks for the same sample, accelerating convergence and improving generalization.

# Data

**Input Representation: Ultra-High-Resolution Log-Mel Spectrograms**
*   **Resolution Scaling:** Implementing the principle of **Compound Scaling** for audio (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010), I will increase the vertical resolution to **320 Mel bands**. This high-dimensional input is necessary to fully utilize the increased parameter count of the ConvNeXt-Small backbone, ensuring the deeper network has sufficient signal detail to process.
*   **Temporal Fidelity:** I will use a short hop length (approx. 10ms) to generate the spectrograms. This preserves the rapid temporal dynamics of the whale chirps, ensuring that the "image" fed to the CNN accurately reflects the signal's morphology (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
*   **Normalization:** Inputs will be standardized using instance-level Min-Max normalization (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).

# Training

**Loss Function: Binary Cross-Entropy with Inverse Class Frequency Weighting**
*   I will continue to use **BCE Loss weighted by the inverse class frequency**. This remains the most robust method for handling the ~9:1 class imbalance without the computational overhead or potential information loss of resampling (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).

**Regularization & Augmentation**
*   **Mixup with Mixed Losses:** I will apply Mixup augmentation to scale the model's capacity. Crucially, I will compute the loss by mixing the **weighted scalar losses** of the input pair rather than mixing the labels or weights. This ensures that the gradients from the minority class are not diluted by the majority class weights during the mixing process (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).
*   **SpecAugment:** I will apply random time and frequency masking to the spectrograms. This forces the model to learn robust, partial spectral features rather than relying on specific noise artifacts (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).

**Optimization**
*   **Optimizer:** Use **AdamW** combined with a **Cosine Annealing Learning Rate Scheduler**. This schedule enables aggressive initial learning to escape local minima, followed by a stable decay phase for fine-tuning the weights of the deep network (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).

# Evaluation

**Inference Strategy**
*   **Checkpoint Reloading:** The training pipeline will explicitly monitor Validation AUC and save the model state with the highest score. Before generating the final submission, the system will **reload these best-saved weights**, discarding the state from the final training epoch. This prevents the use of a potentially degraded model caused by overfitting in the late stages of training (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008).
