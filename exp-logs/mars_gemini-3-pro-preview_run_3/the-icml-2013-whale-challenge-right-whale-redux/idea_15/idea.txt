Based on the comprehensive failure analysis of previous semi-supervised attempts and the optimization of the supervised baseline, I propose a **Calibrated Noisy Student Self-Training Pipeline with Differential Structural Regularization**.

This strategy structurally evolves the solution from a single-stage supervised paradigm to a multi-stage self-training framework. It specifically addresses the root causes of previous failures: it corrects the Teacher's batch size bottleneck (Cite solution_lesson_node_00022), calibrates the Mixup intensity to preserve signal structure (Cite solution_lesson_node_00030), and implements the required "Student Noise" through structural regularization rather than destructive augmentation (Cite solution_lesson_node_00033, Cite solution_lesson_node_00035).

# Model

**Backbone: EfficientNetV2-Medium**
I will utilize the **EfficientNetV2-Medium** architecture for both the Teacher and Student models.
*   **Rationale:** This backbone has consistently outperformed Transformer-based alternatives like ConvNeXt for this spectrogram task (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016). I will strictly adhere to the "Medium" variant to ensure the model fits within GPU memory while maintaining the critical batch size (Cite solution_lesson_node_00034).

**Head: Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling**
Both models will replace Global Average Pooling with **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling**.
*   **Rationale:** GeM pooling allows the model to dynamically focus on the high-activation time-frequency regions (the transient whale call) while suppressing the dominant background noise, a mechanism proven superior for weakly supervised detection (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).

# Data

**Input Representation: High-Fidelity Log-Mel Spectrograms**
I will generate a single, high-resolution Log-Mel Spectrogram for each clip using the optimal signal processing parameters:
*   **Frequency Resolution:** **384 Mel bands** to match the Compound Scaling capacity of the V2-Medium backbone (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010) and preserve fine-grained spectral texture (Cite solution_lesson_node_00017).
*   **Temporal/Spectral Fidelity:** Use a **large FFT window (N_FFT=1024)** to maximize frequency resolution, paired with a **short hop length (approx. 10ms)** to simultaneously capture rapid temporal transients. This resolves the resolution trade-off without requiring multi-stream architectures (Cite solution_lesson_node_00019).
*   **Normalization:** **Instance-level Min-Max Normalization** will be used to preserve spectral contrast, avoiding the signal degradation caused by frequency-wise standardization (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015).

# Training

**Strategy: Two-Stage Calibrated Noisy Student**
The training will proceed in two distinct stages to leverage the unlabeled test data without compromising the baseline's stability.

**Stage 1: The Optimized Teacher**
*   **Procedure:** Train the EfficientNetV2-Medium on the labeled training set.
*   **Batch Size:** Explicitly enforce a **Batch Size of 64** (using gradient accumulation if necessary) to ensure robust Batch Normalization statistics, correcting the flaw in previous semi-supervised attempts (Cite solution_lesson_node_00022).
*   **Augmentation:** Use **Mixup with $\alpha=0.4$** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00030) and **SpecAugment** with conservative masking (TimeMask=20, FreqMask=20) to avoid occluding the short transient calls (Cite solution_lesson_node_00035).
*   **Regularization:** Apply moderate **Stochastic Depth (DropPath)** and **Classifier Dropout** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00024).

**Stage 2: The Robust Student**
*   **Pseudo-Labeling:** Use the converged Teacher to generate **soft probability predictions** for the entire unlabeled `test2` dataset.
*   **Procedure:** Train a fresh EfficientNetV2-Medium on the combined dataset (Labeled Train + Pseudo-Labeled Test).
*   **Differential Noise Injection:** To ensure the Student learns a more robust boundary than the Teacher (Cite solution_lesson_node_00033), I will apply **stronger regularization**, but implemented structurally to avoid data destruction:
    *   **Augmentation:** Maintain Mixup ($\alpha=0.4$) and SpecAugment (20/20) to preserve signal integrity (Cite solution_lesson_node_00030, Cite solution_lesson_node_00035).
    *   **Structure:** Significantly increase the **Stochastic Depth (DropPath) rate** and **Classifier Dropout rate** compared to the Teacher. This forces the Student to rely on a wider variety of features without risking the "silence" mapping caused by excessive masking.
*   **Loss Function:** Use **Binary Cross-Entropy**. For labeled data, apply **Inverse Class Frequency Weighting** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001). For pseudo-labeled data, calculate loss against the soft targets. Mixup losses will be calculated by mixing the **weighted scalar losses** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).

**Optimizer**
*   Use **AdamW** with a **Cosine Annealing Learning Rate Scheduler** for stable convergence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).

# Evaluation

**Inference Strategy**
*   **Checkpoint Reloading:** During both Teacher and Student training, the pipeline will monitor Validation AUC and save the best model state. Before generating pseudo-labels or the final submission, the system will **explicitly reload these best-saved weights**, discarding the state from the final training epoch (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008).
