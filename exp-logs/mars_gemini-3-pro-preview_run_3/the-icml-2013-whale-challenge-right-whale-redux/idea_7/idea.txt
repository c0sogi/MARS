Based on the empirical insights from the provided lessons, I propose the following optimized strategy. This approach structurally evolves the architecture by transitioning from the EfficientNet paradigm (used in Lesson 10) to a **ConvNeXt** backbone, which offers superior receptive field modeling for spectral textures. It further integrates specialized attention and pooling mechanisms to address the specific challenges of weakly supervised acoustic event detection.

# Model

**Backbone: ConvNeXt-Small with Coordinate Attention**
I propose replacing the EfficientNet backbone with **ConvNeXt-Small**.
*   **Rationale (Receptive Field):** Previous lessons established that matching the model's receptive field to the signal duration is critical (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002). Unlike EfficientNet's 3x3 convolutions, ConvNeXt utilizes large **7x7 kernels**. This significantly expands the effective receptive field in early layers, allowing the model to capture the long-range temporal dynamics and continuous spectral contours of whale calls more efficiently than stacking small kernels.
*   **Rationale (Width):** ConvNeXt-Small increases the channel width compared to the EfficientNet-B2 used in Lesson 10. Increasing network width has been proven to be highly effective for extracting fine-grained texture features from spectrograms (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
*   **Structural Innovation (Coordinate Attention):** I will inject **Coordinate Attention** blocks into the backbone. While standard Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006) pools spatial information globally, Coordinate Attention factorizes attention into two 1D encoding processes (along time and frequency axes). This allows the model to preserve precise positional information, which is essential for distinguishing the specific "up-sweep" morphology of a right whale call from random noise, addressing the localization need inherent in event detection.

**Head: Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling**
I will retain **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling** instead of Global Average Pooling.
*   **Rationale:** Whale calls are transient events (sparse in time) within the clip. GeM pooling allows the model to dynamically learn to focus on high-activation regions (the call) while suppressing the dominant background noise, a critical mechanism for weakly supervised detection that outperformed GAP in previous iterations (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).

# Data

**Input Representation: High-Resolution Log-Mel Spectrograms**
*   **Resolution Scaling:** Following the **Compound Scaling** principle (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010), I will increase the vertical resolution to **320 Mel bands**. This ensures that the increased capacity of the ConvNeXt-Small backbone is fed with sufficient signal detail, preventing an information bottleneck.
*   **Temporal Fidelity:** I will use a short hop length (approx. 10ms) to generate the spectrograms. This preserves the rapid temporal dynamics of the whale chirps (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
*   **Normalization:** Inputs will be converted to Log-Mel scale and standardized using instance-level Min-Max normalization (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).

# Training

**Loss Function: Binary Cross-Entropy with Inverse Class Frequency Weighting**
*   I will use **BCE Loss weighted by the inverse class frequency**. This remains the most robust and efficient method for handling the ~9:1 class imbalance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).

**Regularization & Augmentation**
*   **Mixup with Mixed Losses:** I will apply Mixup augmentation. Crucially, I will compute the loss by mixing the **weighted scalar losses** of the input pair rather than mixing the labels or weights. This ensures that the gradients from the minority class are not diluted by the majority class weights (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).
*   **SpecAugment:** I will apply random time and frequency masking to the spectrograms to force the model to learn robust partial features (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
*   **Stochastic Depth:** To prevent overfitting in the deeper ConvNeXt-Small model, I will apply Stochastic Depth (DropPath) regularization.

**Optimization**
*   **Optimizer:** Use **AdamW** combined with a **Cosine Annealing Learning Rate Scheduler**. This schedule allows for aggressive initial learning followed by stable convergence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).

# Evaluation

**Inference Strategy**
*   **Checkpoint Reloading:** The training pipeline will explicitly monitor Validation AUC and save the model state with the highest score. Before generating the final submission, the system will **reload these best-saved weights**, discarding the state from the final training epoch. This prevents the use of a potentially degraded model caused by overfitting in the late stages of training (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008).
