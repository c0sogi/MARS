Based on the progression of empirical findings and the lessons learned, I propose the following optimized strategy. This approach structurally evolves the architecture by integrating the **Compound Scaling** principles (Lesson 10) with a **ConvNeXt** backbone enhanced by **Coordinate Attention**, and introduces a domain-specific **Frequency-Wise Normalization** scheme to address stationary underwater noise.

# Model

**Backbone: ConvNeXt-Small with Coordinate Attention**
I propose upgrading the backbone to **ConvNeXt-Small**, integrating **Coordinate Attention** modules.
*   **Rationale (Receptive Field & Width):** Previous lessons established that matching the receptive field to the signal duration is critical (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002) and that increasing channel width improves texture extraction (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007). ConvNeXt utilizes large **7x7 kernels** (superior receptive field to EfficientNet's 3x3) and the "Small" variant significantly increases width compared to the "Tiny" or "B2" models used previously.
*   **Structural Innovation (Coordinate Attention):** I will inject **Coordinate Attention** blocks into the backbone. While GeM pooling (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009) handles localization at the *end* of the network, Coordinate Attention factorizes attention into two 1D encoding processes (along time and frequency axes) *within* the feature extractor. This preserves precise positional information, helping the model distinguish the specific time-frequency morphology of a whale call from random noise.

**Head: Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling**
I will retain **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling** to aggregate the feature maps.
*   **Rationale:** Whale calls are transient events localized in time and frequency. GeM pooling allows the model to dynamically learn to focus on high-activation regions (the call) while suppressing the dominant background noise, a critical mechanism for this weakly supervised task (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).

# Data

**Input Representation: Frequency-Standardized High-Resolution Log-Mel Spectrograms**
*   **Resolution Scaling:** Implementing **Compound Scaling** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010), I will increase the vertical resolution to **320 Mel bands**. This ensures the input fidelity matches the increased capacity of the ConvNeXt-Small backbone, preventing an information bottleneck.
*   **Temporal Fidelity:** I will use a short hop length (<15ms) to preserve the rapid temporal dynamics of the whale chirps (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
*   **Normalization (Structural Innovation):** Instead of global Min-Max scaling, I will implement **Frequency-Wise Standardization**. For each spectrogram, I will calculate the mean and standard deviation *per frequency bin* across the time dimension and normalize ($x' = (x - \mu_f) / \sigma_f$).
    *   **Rationale:** Underwater noise (e.g., ship engines) often manifests as stationary energy in specific frequency bands. Normalizing per frequency bin effectively performs "spectral subtraction," flattening the stationary noise floor and enhancing the contrast of transient events (whale calls) that deviate from the mean.

# Training

**Loss Function: Binary Cross-Entropy with Inverse Class Frequency Weighting**
*   I will use **BCE Loss weighted by the inverse class frequency**. This remains the most robust and efficient method for handling the ~9:1 class imbalance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).

**Regularization & Augmentation**
*   **Mixup with Mixed Losses:** I will apply Mixup augmentation. Crucially, I will compute the loss by mixing the **weighted scalar losses** of the input pair rather than mixing the labels or weights. This ensures that the gradients from the minority class are not diluted by the majority class weights (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).
*   **SpecAugment:** I will apply random time and frequency masking to the spectrograms to force the model to learn robust partial features (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).

**Optimization**
*   **Optimizer:** Use **AdamW** combined with a **Cosine Annealing Learning Rate Scheduler**. This schedule allows for aggressive initial learning followed by stable convergence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).

# Evaluation

**Inference Strategy**
*   **Checkpoint Reloading:** The training pipeline will explicitly monitor Validation AUC and save the model state with the highest score. Before generating the final submission, the system will **reload these best-saved weights**, discarding the state from the final training epoch. This prevents the use of a potentially degraded model caused by overfitting in the late stages of training (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008).
