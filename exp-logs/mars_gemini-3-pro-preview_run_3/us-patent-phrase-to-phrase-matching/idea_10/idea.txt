Based on the comprehensive analysis of the previous experiments, particularly the success of the Hierarchical Context approach (Idea 6) and the catastrophic failure of Domain-Adaptive Pre-training (Idea 7), I propose the **"Stratified Ensemble with Dynamic Layer Mixing"**.

This strategy structurally evolves the solution by integrating **Dynamic Layer Mixing** to capture the full spectrum of lexical and semantic features and wrapping the model in a **Stratified K-Fold Ensemble** for robustness. Crucially, it explicitly removes the DAPT phase to prevent the catastrophic forgetting observed in Idea 7.

### Model
*   **Backbone:** We will utilize **`microsoft/deberta-v3-large`** as the core encoder, leveraging its superior disentangled attention mechanism (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
*   **Architecture:** We will persist with the **Cross-Encoder** design, feeding the concatenated hierarchical context and phrase pairs as a single sequence to enable deep token-level interaction (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008).
*   **Dynamic Layer Mixing (Structural Innovation):** Instead of relying solely on the final hidden state (which emphasizes abstract semantics), we will implement **Scalar Mixing (Weighted Layer Pooling)**. We will learn a trainable scalar weight $w_i$ for every layer $i$ of the transformer. The output representation will be the weighted sum of all layer outputs: $H_{mix} = \sum \frac{\exp(w_i)}{\sum \exp(w_j)} H_i$.
    *   *Rationale:* The scoring rubric distinguishes between "Exact Match" (1.0) and "Close Synonym" (0.75), a difference that is often lexical rather than purely semantic. Lower layers of the transformer retain these lexical details, while higher layers capture broad meaning. Dynamic mixing allows the model to utilize the appropriate level of abstraction for each pair.
*   **Head:** The mixed representation will be passed through an **Attention Pooling** layer (Cite solution_lesson_node_00011) to aggregate token embeddings, followed by **Multi-Sample Dropout** and the **Dual-Head** structure (Regression + Classification) to leverage multi-task learning (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014).

### Data
*   **Hierarchical Context Expansion:** We will map every `context` code to its full lineage of descriptions (Section > Class > Subclass) and inject this into the input sequence (e.g., `[CLS] Section Desc [SEP] Class Desc [SEP] Subclass Desc [SEP] Anchor [SEP] Target [SEP]`). This aligns the model with the specific technical domain (Cite solution_lesson_node_00021).
*   **Stratified K-Fold Split:** We will implement a **5-Fold Stratified Cross-Validation** scheme. We will split the training data into 5 folds, stratifying by the `score` distribution. This ensures that each fold maintains the class imbalance profile of the full dataset.

### Training
*   **No DAPT:** We will **strictly skip Domain-Adaptive Pre-training**. As evidenced by the failure of Idea 7, DAPT on this specific corpus led to catastrophic forgetting (Cite solution_lesson_node_00023). We will rely on the robust pre-trained weights of DeBERTa-v3-Large directly.
*   **Loss Function:** We will optimize the **Hybrid Pearson Loss**: $L_{Total} = L_{MSE} + \lambda_1 L_{CE} + \lambda_2 (1 - \rho)$. This combines geometric precision, decision-boundary sharpness, and direct metric optimization (Cite solution_lesson_node_00021).
*   **Regularization:** We will apply **Adversarial Weight Perturbation (AWP)** during training to flatten the loss landscape and improve generalization (Cite solution_lesson_node_00012).
*   **Stabilization:** We will employ **Exponential Moving Average (EMA)** of the model weights. This acts as a continuous temporal ensemble, smoothing out the high-frequency noise introduced by AWP.
*   **Optimization:** We will use **AdamW** with **Layer-wise Learning Rate Decay (LLRD)** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00011) to preserve the pre-trained backbone features while aggressively training the new mixing and head layers.

### Evaluation
*   **Inference:** For each sample in the test set, we will generate predictions using the EMA weights from all 5 trained fold models.
*   **Ensemble Aggregation:** The final similarity score will be the **arithmetic mean** of the 5 predictions. This averaging reduces the variance associated with any single model's initialization or data split.
*   **Post-Processing:** The aggregated scores will be clipped to the [0, 1] range.
