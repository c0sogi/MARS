Based on the comprehensive analysis of previous attempts and the specific lessons learned, I propose a **Triple Heterogeneous Ensemble with Inductive Bias Diversity**.

This strategy structurally evolves the solution by rejecting the complex Stacking/Cross-Validation pipeline (which failed in Idea 5) in favor of maximizing architectural diversity (which succeeded in the "Current Best" referenced in Lesson 00023). It combines three distinct neural network paradigms to cover different feature extraction hierarchies.

### Model
**Architecture:** Implement a **Triple Heterogeneous Ensemble** consisting of three backbones with distinct inductive biases:
1.  **ResNet-50:** Represents the "Standard CNN" paradigm. It provides robust, edge-and-texture-based feature extraction and serves as a stable anchor for the ensemble (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
2.  **ConvNeXt-Small:** Represents the "Modernized CNN" paradigm. Its transformer-inspired design (large kernels, inverted bottlenecks) captures long-range dependencies and semantic context better than standard ResNets (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016).
3.  **EfficientNetV2-Small:** Represents the "Depthwise Separable / NAS" paradigm. This adds a third axis of diversity, utilizing Fused-MBConv blocks that process spatial information differently from the other two. This restores the diversity lost in previous stacking attempts (Cite solution_lesson_node_00023).

**Initialization:** All models must be initialized with the highest-quality pre-trained weights available—specifically **`IMAGENET1K_V2`** for ResNet/EfficientNet and the best available for ConvNeXt. This ensures the starting point is superior to legacy V1 weights (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).

### Data
**Preprocessing:** Resize all images to **256x256**.
**Rationale:** This resolution strikes the optimal balance, allowing the models to utilize finer spatial details than the standard 224x224 (improving accuracy) without the diminishing returns and batch-size constraints of 320x320 (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).

**Augmentation:** Apply the **Context-Preserving Augmentation** strategy.
**Rationale:** Use `RandomResizedCrop` with a restricted scale of `(0.8, 1.0)` to strictly prevent cropping out the subject. Combine this with `ColorJitter` and `RandomHorizontalFlip`. This ensures the model sees the whole animal while still receiving regularization, preventing the noise introduced by aggressive geometric crops (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).

### Training
**Optimization Strategy:** Train each of the three models independently.
*   **Optimizer:** Use **AdamW** combined with a **Cosine Annealing** learning rate scheduler. This combination consistently outperforms standard Adam by smoothing the optimization path (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
*   **Loss Function:** Use **Binary Cross-Entropy with Logits Loss** (`BCEWithLogitsLoss`) **without Label Smoothing**. We strictly avoid Label Smoothing because it creates a "loss floor" (approx 0.05) that prevents the model from minimizing Log Loss for high-confidence predictions (Cite solution_lesson_node_00012).

**Resource Management:**
*   **Batch Size & Epochs:** Use a batch size of 64 or 128 to utilize the GPU, but scale the training duration to **8-10 epochs**. This ensures the total number of gradient update steps remains sufficient for convergence, compensating for the reduced updates per epoch caused by larger batch sizes (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
*   **Early Stopping:** Monitor validation loss to prevent overfitting, though the conservative augmentation and scheduler should mitigate this (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).

### Evaluation
**Inference & Aggregation:**
1.  **Test Time Augmentation (TTA):** For every test image, generate predictions using both the original and a horizontally flipped version for *each* of the three models. Average these pairs to stabilize individual model predictions (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
2.  **Simple Averaging:** Calculate the final probability by taking the **arithmetic mean** of the outputs from the ResNet-50, ConvNeXt-Small, and EfficientNetV2-Small.
    *   **Rationale:** We explicitly choose simple averaging over complex meta-learning (stacking). As proven in previous lessons, the error reduction from averaging diverse inductive biases exceeds the marginal gains from learning aggregation weights, which often leads to overfitting on small validation sets (Cite solution_lesson_node_00023).
