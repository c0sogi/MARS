Based on the comprehensive analysis of the "Lessons Learned" and the trajectory of previous experiments, I propose the following optimized strategy. This solution synthesizes the structural innovation of **Task-Adaptive Pretraining (TAPT)** with a rigorous engineering pipeline that resolves the specific conflicts (e.g., batch size vs. updates, containment logic) that caused previous iterations to fail.

# Model
**Architecture:**
I will utilize the **`xlm-roberta-base`** model (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004) as the backbone. This model is chosen for its superior capacity to handle the morphological complexity of Hindi and Tamil compared to distilled variants. The architecture will be a **Token Classifier**, projecting hidden states into 3 classes (`B-ANS`, `I-ANS`, `O`).

**Structural Innovation: Corrected Task-Adaptive Pretraining (TAPT)**
To address the domain gap between generic pre-training and the specific dataset, I will implement a **Two-Stage Training Pipeline**:
1.  **Stage 1 (Adaptation):** I will fine-tune the `xlm-roberta-base` model using a **Masked Language Modeling (MLM)** objective on the concatenation of the raw `context` text from both the Training and Test sets. This aligns the embeddings with the target vocabulary.
2.  **Stage 2 (Supervised Initialization):** The TAPT-adapted weights will serve as the initialization for the Question Answering model.
*Note: Previous attempts at TAPT failed due to incorrect batch sizing and metadata handling. This proposal retains the structural benefit of TAPT while fixing those engineering flaws.*

# Data
**Preprocessing:**
*   **Sliding Window:** The text will be tokenized and split into overlapping windows of **384 tokens** with a **stride of 128** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
*   **Labeling (Strict Containment):** I will strictly employ **Strict Containment** for label generation. A window will only be labeled with `B-ANS`/`I-ANS` if it contains the **entire** ground truth span. Partial matches will be labeled as `O`. This prevents the "structural noise" (e.g., `I` tags without `B` tags) that caused model collapse in previous "Soft Overlap" experiments (Cite solution_lesson_node_00023).
*   **Full Distribution:** I will retain **all** generated windows for training. I will **not** perform any negative downsampling, ensuring the model learns to suppress false positives by seeing the full background distribution (Cite solution_lesson_node_00011).
*   **Coupled Metadata:** I will implement a custom `collate_fn` to bundle necessary metadata (e.g., `offset_mapping`, `input_ids`) directly into the training batches. This prevents the inference failures caused by decoupled data streams in previous attempts (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).

# Training
**Strategy:**
*   **Batch Size:** I will strictly utilize a **Batch Size of 16**. This is the empirical "sweet spot" identified in the lessons: it is large enough to avoid the "gradient starvation" observed at batch size 8 (Cite solution_lesson_node_00020) but small enough to provide the high frequency of gradient updates required for convergence on this small dataset, unlike batch size 32 (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015).
*   **Loss Function:** I will use **Standard Cross-Entropy Loss**. I will avoid aggressive manual class weighting (e.g., 10:1), as this was shown to degrade Jaccard scores by prioritizing recall over precision (Cite solution_lesson_node_00022).
*   **Duration:** The model will be fine-tuned for **10 epochs** to accommodate the "delayed onset" of convergence characteristic of this dataset (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).
*   **Ensembling:** I will train **3 independent model instances** using different random seeds to mitigate the high variance inherent in low-resource training (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008).

# Evaluation
**Inference Pipeline:**
1.  **Global Confidence Aggregation (Per Model):** For a given document, the model will process **all** overlapping windows. It will collect all candidate spans and select the single span with the **highest confidence score** across the entire document. This filters out low-confidence false positives often generated by greedy "first-match" strategies in partial contexts (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016).
2.  **Self-Contained Decoding:** Answer strings will be decoded directly from the predicted token IDs using the tokenizer, ensuring robustness against offset mapping errors (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).
3.  **Majority Voting (Ensemble):** The final prediction will be determined by a **Majority Vote** on the exact string outputs from the 3 models. This avoids the "smearing" artifacts caused by averaging raw token probabilities (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
