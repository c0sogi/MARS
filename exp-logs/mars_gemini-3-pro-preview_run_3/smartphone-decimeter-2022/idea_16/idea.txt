Based on the accumulated lessons, specifically the superiority of **Gradient Boosting** for sensor residuals (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010), the effectiveness of **Geometry-Projected Features** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00043), and the failure of standard smoothing due to heavy-tailed outliers (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014), I propose the **Geometry-Projected Boosting with Carrier-Phase Trajectory Alignment** strategy.

This approach structurally separates the problem into two distinct tasks: 1) Estimating absolute position bias using Machine Learning, and 2) Reconstructing the precise trajectory shape using Differential GNSS Physics (Carrier Phase). This replaces the noisy, heuristic smoothing of previous attempts with a rigorous physical constraint derived from the most precise measurement available in the logs.

### 1. Model: Geometry-Projected LightGBM Ensemble
The predictive core remains an ensemble of **LightGBM Regressors**, chosen for their proven ability to map instantaneous signal properties to error vectors without overfitting to trajectory history (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010, solution_lesson_node_00011).

*   **Architecture:** Independent models for **East** and **North** error components.
*   **Target:** The model predicts the **ENU (East-North-Up) Residuals** ($P_{GT} - P_{WLS}$) in meters. This leverages the WLS baseline as a robust prior while focusing the model on correcting non-linear biases like multipath (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).

### 2. Data: Point-Wise Physics Features
The feature engineering pipeline strictly adheres to a **Point-Wise** paradigm to ensure generalization across disjoint drives (Cite solution_lesson_node_00011), utilizing the **Geometric Projection** method (Cite solution_lesson_node_00043) which outperformed manual sector binning.

*   **Coordinate Transformation:** Convert all positions to a local ENU frame.
*   **Projected Residual Features:**
    For each timestamp, calculate the pseudorange residual ($r_i$) and Line-of-Sight vector ($\vec{u}_i$) for every satellite. Compute the "Net Error Force" vector and Geometry Matrix elements:
    $$ \vec{F} = \sum w_i r_i \vec{u}_i, \quad \mathbf{G} = \sum w_i \vec{u}_i \vec{u}_i^T $$
    These features explicitly describe the magnitude and direction of the force pulling the WLS solution away from the truth (Cite solution_lesson_node_00033).
*   **Global Context:** Aggregated signal quality (Mean Cn0) and IMU dynamics (Accel Std) to capture environmental state.

### 3. Training
*   **Loss Function:** **Mean Absolute Error (MAE)**. This L1 loss minimizes the median error, ensuring the predicted "anchors" are robust to the massive outliers inherent in urban GNSS data (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
*   **Validation:** **GroupKFold** cross-validation grouped by `drive_id` to strictly enforce evaluation on unseen routes.

### 4. Evaluation: Carrier-Phase Trajectory Alignment
The inference pipeline introduces a **Structural Innovation** in post-processing. Instead of smoothing the ML predictions directly (which smears outliers, Cite solution_lesson_node_00014), we use the ML predictions as "noisy anchors" to position a rigid trajectory shape derived from **Time-Differenced Carrier Phase (TDCP)**.

1.  **Base Prediction:** Generate point-wise corrected positions $\hat{P}_{ML}$ using the LightGBM ensemble.
2.  **TDCP Odometry (Physics):**
    *   Extract `AccumulatedDeltaRangeMeters` (Carrier Phase) from the raw logs.
    *   Filter for satellites with valid phase states (checking `AccumulatedDeltaRangeState` for cycle slips/resets).
    *   For consecutive epochs $t-1, t$, compute the **delta-phase** for common satellites.
    *   Solve a precise Least Squares problem to find the **relative displacement vector** $\Delta \vec{x}_{t}$ of the receiver.
    *   *Benefit:* TDCP provides mm-to-cm level relative accuracy, far superior to the noisy Doppler velocities used in previous attempts (Cite solution_lesson_node_00027).
3.  **Global Robust Optimization:**
    Solve for the final trajectory $X = \{x_0, \dots, x_N\}$ by minimizing a composite global objective:
    $$ \min_X \sum_{t} \rho(x_t - \hat{P}_{ML, t}) + \lambda \sum_{t} ||(x_t - x_{t-1}) - \Delta \vec{x}_{TDCP, t}||^2 $$
    *   **Anchor Term:** $\rho$ is a **Robust Loss** (e.g., Huber or L1) that pulls the trajectory towards the ML predictions but allows it to ignore large outliers (fixing the failure of Lesson 00014).
    *   **Shape Term:** The L2 penalty enforces the precise relative shape computed by TDCP.

This strategy effectively uses Machine Learning to find the "Global Offset" (Bias) and Carrier Phase Physics to determine the "Local Shape" (Path), combining the strengths of both domains.
