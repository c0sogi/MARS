Based on the lessons learned, I propose a solution that evolves the architecture from a flat Fully Convolutional Network (FCN) to a **Lean 2.5D U-Net with a Dilated Bottleneck**. This approach introduces multi-scale feature aggregation to better resolve complex ink structures while strictly adhering to the efficiency constraints and stabilization needs identified in the previous experiments.

### Model: Lean 2.5D U-Net with Dilated Bottleneck
The architecture transitions from a single-scale model to an Encoder-Decoder structure to capture both fine details and global context, specifically designed to be lightweight and depth-aware.

1.  **Learnable Depth Compression (Input Stage):** The network begins with a `1x1` convolution that projects the input tensor `(Batch, 65, H, W)` to `(Batch, 32, H, W)`. This retains the highly effective, learnable "soft" slice selection mechanism **Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**.
2.  **Lean Encoder:** The encoder consists of 3 downsampling stages. To prevent overfitting and maintain generalization, the channel dimensions are kept "lean" (e.g., 32 $\to$ 64 $\to$ 64), avoiding the massive parameter explosion typical of standard U-Nets **Cite solution_lesson_node_00004**.
3.  **Dilated Bottleneck:** At the lowest resolution (the "bottom" of the U-Net), the model employs a block of **Dilated Convolutions** (rates 2, 4, 8). This expands the receptive field exponentially to capture large-scale geometric continuity of ink strokes without losing resolution or adding excessive parameters **Cite solution_lesson_node_00003**.
4.  **Decoder with Skip Connections:** The decoder upsamples the features and concatenates them with the corresponding high-resolution maps from the encoder. This recovers the spatial precision lost during downsampling, which is critical for defining sharp character boundaries.
5.  **Output Head:** A final `1x1` convolution maps the features to a single probability map, followed by a Sigmoid activation.

### Data: Deterministic Validation and Geometric Consistency
1.  **Preprocessing:** Apply Z-score normalization to the input volumes using global statistics (mean/std) calculated from the training set.
2.  **Geometric Augmentation:** During training, apply random horizontal/vertical flips and discrete 90-degree rotations to the input patches. This forces the model to learn rotationally invariant features, which is essential for this dataset **Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**.
3.  **Deterministic Validation Grid (Crucial Change):** To address the high metric volatility and unreliable evaluation caused by stochastic sampling **Cite solution_lesson_node_00007**, the validation process will no longer use random patches. Instead, we will generate a **fixed, deterministic grid of overlapping tiles** covering the validation fragment. These exact same tiles will be evaluated at every epoch, ensuring that changes in the validation score reflect actual model progress rather than sampling noise.

### Training: Metric-Aligned Loss and Stabilization
1.  **Tversky Loss (F0.5 Alignment):** Since the competition metric is the F0.5 score (which weights precision higher than recall), standard Dice loss (F1) is suboptimal. We will use the **Tversky Loss** with $\beta < 0.5$ (e.g., $\alpha=0.3, \beta=0.7$) to penalize False Positives more heavily than False Negatives, directly aligning the optimization objective with the evaluation metric.
2.  **Exponential Moving Average (EMA):** To further counter the training instability and variance **Cite solution_lesson_node_00007**, we will maintain an EMA of the model weights during training. The final model used for inference will be the EMA version, which typically yields smoother decision boundaries and better generalization than the raw weights from the final epoch.

### Evaluation: Tiled Inference with Test-Time Augmentation (TTA)
1.  **Tiled Inference:** The large test images will be processed in overlapping tiles (e.g., $256 \times 256$ with 50% overlap). The predictions in overlapping regions will be averaged to prevent edge artifacts.
2.  **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):** Leveraging the model's training on geometric transformations **Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**, inference will be performed on the original tile as well as its rotated and flipped versions (e.g., 4 rotations $\times$ 2 flips). The results will be averaged to produce a robust final probability map, reducing false positives caused by specific fiber orientations.
3.  **Submission:** The final probability map is thresholded and converted to Run-Length Encoding (RLE) for submission.
