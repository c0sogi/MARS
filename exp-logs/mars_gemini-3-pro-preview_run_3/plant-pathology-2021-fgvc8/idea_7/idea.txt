Based on the empirical evidence gathered from the previous lessons, here is a proposal for an optimized strategy. This approach addresses the limitations of previous high-capacity attempts (Idea 4) and regularization failures (Idea 2) by synthesizing a **High-Resolution, Long-Horizon** training paradigm.

### Model
We will advance the architecture to **ConvNeXt-Small** equipped with **Generalized Mean Pooling (GeM)**.
*   **Backbone**: We will utilize **ConvNeXt-Small**. **Cite Lesson 14** demonstrated that the "Transformer-style" ConvNeXt architecture significantly outperforms ResNet-style baselines. We scale up from "Tiny" to "Small" to provide the necessary capacity to capture complex feature hierarchies, addressing the potential underfitting of complex disease patterns.
*   **Structural Innovation (Pooling)**: We will replace the standard Global Average Pooling (GAP) with **Generalized Mean Pooling (GeM)**. Standard GAP averages feature activations across the entire spatial map, which can dilute the signal of small, localized disease lesions (like early Rust or Frog Eye Leaf Spot) against the healthy leaf background. GeM introduces a learnable parameter $p$ (where $p \to \infty$ approximates Max Pooling and $p \to 1$ approximates Average Pooling), allowing the model to dynamically focus on high-activation salient regions (the spots) while suppressing noise.

### Data
We will implement a **High-Fidelity, Strong-Regularization** pipeline designed to prevent the "excess capacity" overfitting risks noted in **Cite Lesson 1**.
*   **Resolution**: Input images will be resized to **384x384**. **Cite Lesson 9** emphasized the importance of local feature discrimination; increasing the native resolution provides the GeM pooling layer with higher fidelity spatial maps, ensuring small spots are not lost during downsampling.
*   **Augmentation Strategy**:
    *   **MixUp and CutMix**: We will re-introduce these strong regularizers. **Cite Lesson 6** attributed their previous failure to an insufficient training horizon (15 epochs). By extending the schedule (see Training), we can leverage their ability to enforce smooth decision boundaries without underfitting.
    *   **RandomResizedCrop**: We will strictly maintain the aggressive minimum scale of **0.5**. **Cite Lesson 9** proved this is the most effective geometric regularizer for forcing the model to learn from partial views.
    *   **Orientation**: We will apply both **HorizontalFlip** and **VerticalFlip**. **Cite Lesson 11** confirmed that leaf images lack a canonical vertical orientation, and removing vertical flips degrades generalization.
    *   **Color Jitter**: We will **exclude** Color Jitter. Since MixUp and CutMix already introduce significant pixel-level variance and intensity shifting, adding Jitter on top risks destroying color signatures, a danger highlighted in **Cite Lesson 12**.

### Training
We will fundamentally alter the training dynamics to accommodate the increased model complexity and regularization strength.
*   **Schedule**: We will extend the training duration to **50 epochs**. This directly addresses the findings of **Cite Lesson 6**, which showed that strong regularizers like MixUp require longer convergence horizons to be effective.
*   **Stabilization (Corrected EMA)**: We will utilize **Model Exponential Moving Average (EMA)** with a tuned decay rate of **0.999**. **Cite Lesson 21** demonstrated that a decay of 0.9999 is too slow for shorter runs, causing the EMA model to lag. A decay of 0.999 is appropriate for a 50-epoch schedule, balancing stability with the ability to update weights effectively.
*   **Loss Function**: We will use **BCEWithLogitsLoss** accepting **soft targets** to accommodate the continuous labels generated by MixUp/CutMix.

### Evaluation
*   **Test Time Augmentation (TTA)**: Inference will be performed on the original image, a horizontally flipped version, and a vertically flipped version. The logits will be averaged before sigmoid activation. This leverages the rotational invariance confirmed in **Cite Lesson 11** to minimize prediction variance.
*   **Thresholding**: A fixed threshold of **0.5** will be applied to the averaged probabilities.
