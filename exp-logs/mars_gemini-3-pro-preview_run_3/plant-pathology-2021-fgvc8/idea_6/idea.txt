Based on the comprehensive analysis of the previous experiments and the specific lessons learned, here is a proposal for an optimized strategy. This approach evolves the "Previous Ideas" by shifting from a short-schedule/light-regularization paradigm to a **long-schedule/heavy-regularization** paradigm, which is necessary to fully exploit the capacity of modern architectures like ConvNeXt-Small.

### Model
We will implement a **ConvNeXt-Small** backbone modified for fine-grained multi-label classification.
*   **Architecture**: We retain the **ConvNeXt** family, as **Cite Lesson 14** demonstrated its superiority over ResNet-style architectures for this task. We scale up to the **Small** variant (as proposed in Idea 4/5) to provide the necessary capacity for capturing complex disease patterns, which the "Tiny" variant might under-represent.
*   **Head Design**: We will replace the standard Global Average Pooling (GAP) with **Generalized Mean Pooling (GeM)**. While GAP dilutes high-frequency signals (like small rust spots) by averaging them with the healthy background, GeM (with a learnable parameter $p$) can focus on high-activation spatial regions, effectively highlighting the disease symptoms.

### Data
We will adopt a **High-Fidelity, Strong-Regularization** strategy.
*   **Resolution**: Input images will be resized to **384x384**. **Cite Lesson 9** emphasized the importance of local feature discrimination; increasing resolution provides the model with higher-fidelity pixels for small lesions, complementing the GeM pooling.
*   **Augmentation**:
    *   **MixUp and CutMix**: We will re-introduce these strong regularizers. **Cite Lesson 6** attributed their previous failure to insufficient training horizons (15 epochs). By significantly extending the training schedule (see below), we can leverage their ability to enforce smooth decision boundaries and robustness without underfitting.
    *   **RandomResizedCrop**: We will maintain the aggressive minimum scale of **0.5**. **Cite Lesson 9** proved this is the most effective geometric regularizer for forcing the model to learn from partial views.
    *   **Orientation**: We will apply both **HorizontalFlip** and **VerticalFlip**, as **Cite Lesson 11** confirmed that leaf images are rotationally invariant.
    *   **Color Jitter**: We will **remove** Color Jitter. Since MixUp/CutMix already introduces significant pixel-level variance and intensity shifting, adding Jitter on top risks destroying color signatures as warned in **Cite Lesson 12**.

### Training
We will fundamentally alter the training dynamics to accommodate the increased model capacity and regularization strength.
*   **Schedule**: We will extend the training duration to **50 epochs**. This directly addresses the "Generalization Gap" and underfitting issues observed in **Cite Lesson 6** when using strong regularization. The A100 GPU allows this extended runtime within the 24-hour limit.
*   **Loss Function**: We will use **BCEWithLogitsLoss** that accepts **soft targets**. This allows the model to learn from the continuous labels generated by MixUp/CutMix (e.g., 0.8 Scab, 0.2 Rust) rather than forcing hard binary classifications.
*   **Stabilization (EMA)**: We will use **Model Exponential Moving Average (EMA)** with a decay rate of **0.999**. **Cite Lesson 21** showed that 0.9999 was too slow for short schedules, but with the extension to 50 epochs, a decay of 0.999 provides the optimal balance between stability and convergence speed.
*   **Optimizer**: **AdamW** with a **Cosine Annealing** scheduler will be used to manage the weight decay and learning rate trajectory.

### Evaluation
*   **Test Time Augmentation (TTA)**: Inference will be performed on the original image, a horizontally flipped version, and a vertically flipped version. The logits will be averaged before sigmoid activation. This leverages the rotational invariance confirmed in **Cite Lesson 11** to minimize prediction variance.
*   **Thresholding**: A fixed threshold of **0.5** will be applied to the averaged probabilities to generate the final labels.
