Here is a proposal for an optimized strategy that introduces a modern architectural paradigm while strictly adhering to the empirical constraints and successful components identified in previous experiments.

### Model
We will transition the backbone from the standard ResNet-18 to **ConvNeXt-Tiny**.
*   **Structural Innovation**: Unlike the ResNet architecture used in **Idea 1**, ConvNeXt modernizes the Convolutional Neural Network design by incorporating "Transformer-style" blocks. It utilizes **depthwise convolutions**, **inverted bottlenecks**, and significantly **larger kernel sizes (7x7)**. This structural change increases the effective receptive field, allowing the model to better capture the interplay between local disease spots and global leaf structure, addressing the "excess capacity" and feature brittleness issues noted in **Cite Lesson 1**.
*   **Regularization**: We will implement **Stochastic Depth (DropPath)** within the ConvNeXt blocks. This structural regularization randomly drops entire residual paths during training, preventing the co-adaptation of feature detectors. This serves as a more efficient alternative to the heavy data-mixing regularizers (MixUp/CutMix) that failed due to short training horizons in **Cite Lesson 6**.

### Data
*   **Preprocessing**: Images will be resized to a standard **224x224** resolution.
*   **Augmentation Strategy**: We will construct a pipeline based on the specific "sweet spots" identified in prior lessons:
    *   **RandomResizedCrop**: We will strictly maintain the aggressive lower scale limit of **0.5**. **Cite Lesson 9** demonstrated that this forces the model to learn from local textures (disease spots) rather than global geometry, and **Cite Lesson 4** confirmed this is superior to deterministic resizing with affine transforms.
    *   **Orientation**: We will apply both **HorizontalFlip** and **VerticalFlip**. **Cite Lesson 11** established that leaf images lack a canonical "up" orientation, and removing vertical flips causes a regression in generalization.
    *   **Color Jitter**: We will apply mild brightness and contrast perturbations (strength **0.2**). **Cite Lesson 1** showed error correlation with lighting conditions, but **Cite Lesson 12** warned that increasing intensity to 0.3 degrades performance by destroying color signatures.

### Training
*   **Loss Function**: We will utilize **BCEWithLogitsLoss**. We avoid the Asymmetric Loss (ASL) from Idea 2, as the previous failure was linked to the incompatibility of complex losses/regularizers with short training schedules (**Cite Lesson 6**).
*   **Optimizer**: **AdamW** will be used to effectively handle the weight decay required for the ConvNeXt architecture.
*   **Schedule**: We will extend the training duration to **20 epochs** using a **Cosine Annealing** scheduler. **Cite Lesson 9** noted that aggressive cropping and structural complexity increase the difficulty of the training task, necessitating a longer convergence horizon than the original 12 epochs.
*   **Precision**: Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP) will be used to maximize throughput on the A100 GPU.

### Evaluation
*   **Test Time Augmentation (TTA)**: During inference, we will generate predictions for the original image, a horizontally flipped version, and a vertically flipped version. These logits will be averaged before thresholding. This directly leverages the rotational invariance confirmed in **Cite Lesson 11** to smooth out prediction noise.
*   **Thresholding**: A fixed threshold of **0.5** will be applied to the averaged probabilities to generate the final space-delimited labels.
