# Proposed Solution: Progressive Resolution Fine-Tuning with ConvNeXt-Base

## Model
We will advance the architecture from ConvNeXt-Small to **ConvNeXt-Base**.
*   **Architecture:** ConvNeXt-Base offers approximately 88M parameters, providing a middle ground between the successful Small variant (50M) and the overfitting Large variant (198M). This addresses the finding that model capacity is the primary bottleneck for this fine-grained task (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009) while respecting the dataset size constraints that caused the Large model to fail (Cite solution_lesson_node_00013).
*   **Head:** A simple linear layer mapping the 1024-dimensional features to the 120 breed classes.

## Data
We will implement a strict augmentation pipeline that enforces geometric diversity at all resolutions.
*   **Preprocessing Pipeline:** The training data will undergo **`RandomResizedCrop`** followed by **`RandomHorizontalFlip`** and then **`RandAugment`**.
    *   **Critical Retention:** We will explicitly retain `RandomResizedCrop` throughout all training stages. Previous attempts to scale to high resolutions failed because this transformation was removed, leading to overfitting (Cite solution_lesson_node_00012).
    *   **Augmentation Ordering:** We will ensure `RandomHorizontalFlip` is applied before `RandAugment` to guarantee basic spatial invariance is learned (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014).
*   **Target Formatting:** We will use **Hard Labels** (0 or 1) and strictly avoid Label Smoothing to ensure the training objective maximizes the Log Loss metric (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).

## Training
We will employ a **Progressive Resolution** training strategy to maximize feature extraction while maintaining stability.
*   **Loss Function:** Standard **Cross-Entropy Loss** on hard labels. We will ensure the validation metric is also standard Cross-Entropy to prevent decoupling from the training objective (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
*   **Phase 1: Warm-up (224x224):**
    *   Freeze the backbone and train only the head for 1 epoch to align weights (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
*   **Phase 2: Standard Resolution Training (224x224):**
    *   Unfreeze the backbone and train for approximately 15 epochs at standard resolution. This allows the model to learn structural features and converge quickly without the high computational cost of larger images.
*   **Phase 3: High-Resolution Fine-Tuning (384x384):**
    *   Increase the input resolution to **384x384**. Continue training for another 15-20 epochs with a reduced learning rate. This phase allows the higher-capacity Base model to resolve fine-grained texture details (fur patterns) that are compressed at 224x224, without starting from scratch.
*   **Scheduler:** We will use **Cosine Annealing** with a `T_max` covering the cumulative epochs (approx 30-35) to ensure a smooth, long-horizon decay (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007, Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).

## Evaluation
*   **Inference Preprocessing:** Images will be resized to slightly larger than the target resolution (e.g., 400px) and **Center Cropped to 384x384**.
*   **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):** We will predict probabilities for the center crop and a **horizontally flipped** version of the image. The results will be averaged to reduce variance and penalize overconfidence.
