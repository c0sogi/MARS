# Proposed Solution: High-Resolution ConvNeXt-Base with Test-Time Augmentation

## Model
We will structurally evolve the solution by scaling the backbone architecture from ConvNeXt-Small to **ConvNeXt-Base**.
*   **Architecture:** ConvNeXt-Base provides a significant increase in parameter count (~88M) and feature extraction capacity compared to the Small variant (~50M). This directly addresses the finding that model capacity is the primary bottleneck for this fine-grained classification task (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
*   **Input Adaptation:** We will modify the network to accept **384x384** pixel inputs instead of the standard 224x224. Fine-grained dog breed classification relies heavily on subtle texture details (fur patterns, snout shape) that are preserved at higher resolutions.
*   **Head:** The classification head will remain a simple linear layer mapping to the 120 classes, initialized randomly.

## Data
*   **Training Preprocessing:** Images will be resized to **384x384** pixels. We will employ **RandAugment** (magnitude 9, 2 layers) to provide robust regularization, which is essential for preventing the larger model from memorizing the training data over the extended training schedule (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).
*   **Validation/Test Preprocessing:** Images will be resized to roughly 400 pixels and then **Center Cropped to 384x384** to ensure the model evaluates the same high-resolution field of view seen during training.
*   **Target Formatting:** We will strictly use **Hard Labels** (0 or 1) for targets. We will explicitly **exclude Label Smoothing** to avoid artificially capping the Log Loss performance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).

## Training
*   **Loss Function:** We will use standard **Cross-Entropy Loss**. This aligns the training objective perfectly with the evaluation metric (Log Loss) and ensures the validation metric is interpretable (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
*   **Strategy:** We will continue the **Two-Phase Transfer Learning** approach:
    1.  **Warm-up:** Freeze the backbone and train the head for 1 epoch to stabilize gradients (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
    2.  **Fine-tuning:** Unfreeze the entire network and train with a low learning rate (e.g., `1e-5`) (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).
*   **Scheduler:** We will use a **Cosine Annealing** scheduler with a `T_max` of **30 epochs**. This extended horizon allows the optimizer to decay the learning rate more gradually, facilitating the discovery of deeper, more robust minima (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).

## Evaluation
*   **Inference:** We will implement **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA)**. For each test image, the model will predict probabilities for the standard center crop and a horizontally flipped version.
*   **Aggregation:** The predictions will be averaged to produce the final output. This structural addition reduces prediction variance and penalizes overconfidence, which is critical for minimizing Log Loss.
