Based on the comprehensive analysis of the accumulated lessons, I propose the **Max-Preserving ConvNeXt MIL Network**.

This solution represents a structural evolution focused on **Signal Preservation**. It addresses the core contradiction identified in the lessons: fractures are sparse, high-frequency anomalies (Lesson 00042) that are easily "washed out" by standard averaging operations (Lesson 00047) or destructive augmentations (Lesson 00037), yet the computational constraints (Lesson 00022) and small dataset size (Lesson 00032) prevent the use of high-resolution inputs or heavy backbones.

### 1. Model
**Architecture:** 2.5D ConvNeXt-Tiny with Spatial-Temporal Max Pooling
*   **Backbone:** **ConvNeXt-Tiny** (pretrained on ImageNet).
    *   *Strategic Retention:* We utilize ConvNeXt because it natively employs **Layer Normalization**. This eliminates the "Micro-Batch Instability" observed with ResNet, allowing us to train a stable model with a Batch Size of 8 (Cite solution_lesson_node_00076, Cite solution_lesson_node_00022). We strictly avoid replacing normalization layers manually to preserve pretrained weights (Cite solution_lesson_node_00048).
*   **Input:** **2.5D Stacks** (3 channels: slices $z-1, z, z+1$) to capture immediate local volumetric context (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
*   **Structural Innovation (Spatial Max Pooling):**
    *   Replace the standard **Global Average Pooling (GAP)** layer at the end of the ConvNeXt backbone with **Global Max Pooling (GMP)**.
    *   *Justification:* Standard GAP averages the feature map (typically $7 \times 7$) into a vector. For a tiny fracture occupying a small portion of the receptive field, averaging dilutes the signal with the "healthy" background. GMP extracts the *peak* activation from the spatial map, preserving the sharpest anomaly signal before it enters the sequence module. This applies the logic of "Max Pooling for Sparse Anomalies" (Cite solution_lesson_node_00019) to the *spatial* dimension (Cite solution_lesson_node_00047).
*   **Context Module:** **Non-Linear 1D Convolution**.
    *   Structure: `Conv1d(k=3, padding=1) -> LayerNorm -> GELU`.
    *   *Strategic Retention:* We retain the 1D Convolution to leverage Z-axis spatial continuity (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014). We enforce Non-Linearity and Normalization for stability (Cite solution_lesson_node_00027) but explicitly reject Residual connections to prevent overfitting (Cite solution_lesson_node_00026) and complex Multi-Scale blocks (Cite solution_lesson_node_00028).
*   **Heads:** **Multi-Task Instance Classifiers**.
    *   The model projects the context-features to **8 distinct logits** per slice: [C1...C7, Patient_Overall].
    *   *Strategic Retention:* We use **Multi-Task Learning** (dedicated patient head) rather than deriving the patient prediction via `max()`. This stabilizes training by allowing independent gradient flow for the global label (Cite solution_lesson_node_00045). We strictly adhere to **Instance-Level Aggregation** (Classify-then-Pool) (Cite solution_lesson_node_00024).
*   **Aggregation:** **Global Max Pooling** applied per channel across the 64 slices.
    *   *Strategic Retention:* Max Pooling is superior to Attention or Average Pooling for detecting sparse anomalies like fractures (Cite solution_lesson_node_00019).

### 2. Data
*   **Ingestion Pipeline (Critical):**
    *   **Sorting:** Sort slices strictly by `ImagePositionPatient` z-coordinate to ensure physical continuity (Cite solution_lesson_node_00031).
    *   **Calibration:** Apply `RescaleSlope` and `RescaleIntercept` to convert raw pixels to Hounsfield Units (HU) (Cite solution_lesson_node_00031).
    *   **Windowing:** Apply **Standard Bone Window** (Level: 400, Width: 1800). **Do not** use High-Density windows which wash out trabecular bone signal (Cite solution_lesson_node_00029).
*   **Caching:** Preprocess and cache 2.5D stacks as **`uint8`** to maximize I/O throughput (Cite solution_lesson_node_00021).
*   **Sampling:** Uniformly sample **64 slices** per exam. Increasing to 96 increases False Positive risk (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).
*   **Augmentation:**
    *   **Volumetric-Consistent:** Use a deterministic replay mechanism to apply the *exact same* geometric transform to every slice in the bag (Cite solution_lesson_node_00013).
    *   **Single-Step Affine:** Fuse rotation, scaling, and shifting into a single affine operation (`ShiftScaleRotate`) to minimize resampling blur and preserve high-frequency fracture signals (Cite solution_lesson_node_00042).
    *   **Non-Destructive:** Explicitly avoid `Cutout` or `Dropout` on inputs, as they can erase the sparse fracture targets (Cite solution_lesson_node_00037).
    *   **Resolution:** Maintain **224x224**. Increasing to 256x256 forces batch size reduction which harms stability more than the resolution helps (Cite solution_lesson_node_00072).

### 3. Training
*   **Loss Function:** **Implicitly Weighted Multi-Task Loss**.
    *   $L = \text{mean}(\text{BCE}_{C1..C7}) + \text{BCE}_{\text{Patient}}$.
    *   *Strategic Retention:* This formulation naturally creates the **1:7 weighting ratio** required by the metric without explicit scalar multipliers that distort gradients (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008, Cite solution_lesson_node_00043).
    *   *Metric Scaling:* When evaluating, normalize the loss by the sum of weights (not the number of heads) to avoid artificial deflation of the score (Cite solution_lesson_node_00077).
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with **Batch Size 8**.
    *   *Strategic Retention:* Small batch size acts as a necessary regularizer for this small dataset (Cite solution_lesson_node_00032). The LayerNorm backbone ensures stability.
*   **Scheduler:** **Decoupled Cosine Annealing**. Set `T_max` to **1.5x the number of epochs**.
    *   *Strategic Retention:* Prevents premature learning rate decay, allowing the model to escape local minima (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
*   **Regularization:** Do **not** use Dropout in the classification heads (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).

### 4. Evaluation
*   **Inference:**
    *   Load test scans using the sorted/calibrated pipeline.
    *   Process through ConvNeXt-Tiny (Spatial Max Pool) -> Context -> Multi-Task Heads.
    *   Aggregate via Global Max Pooling to obtain 8 probabilities.
    *   Submit the 7 vertebral probabilities and the distinct `patient_overall` probability directly.
