Based on the comprehensive analysis of the provided lessons, I propose the **Dynamic-Depth ConvNeXt MIL Network**.

This solution structurally evolves the "Current Best" approach by addressing the critical limitation of static downsampling: the risk of missing a sparse fracture that lies *between* the sampled slices (Lesson 00010, Lesson 00089). It synthesizes the **LayerNorm-Native Stability** of ConvNeXt (Lesson 00076, Lesson 00022), the **Multi-Task Aggregation** strategy (Lesson 00045), and the **Signal Preservation** protocols (Lesson 00042, Lesson 00079) into a robust pipeline.

### 1. Model
**Architecture:** 2.5D ConvNeXt-Tiny with Non-Linear Context and Multi-Task Heads
*   **Backbone:** **ConvNeXt-Tiny** (pretrained on ImageNet).
    *   *Strategic Retention:* We utilize ConvNeXt because it natively employs **Layer Normalization**. This explicitly solves the "Micro-Batch Instability" observed with ResNet at Batch Size 8 (Cite solution_lesson_node_00022), allowing us to use a modern, powerful backbone without the destructive effects of manually replacing normalization layers (Cite solution_lesson_node_00048).
    *   *Spatial Pooling:* **Global Average Pooling (GAP)**. We retain GAP for spatial aggregation within the backbone, as Max Pooling (GMP) tends to overfit to high-frequency spatial noise in medical scans (Cite solution_lesson_node_00079).
*   **Input:** **2.5D Stacks** (3 channels: slices $z-1, z, z+1$).
    *   *Strategic Retention:* This captures local volumetric coherence, enabling the 2D backbone to perceive 3D continuity (Cite solution_lesson_node_00086).
*   **Context Module:** **Non-Linear 1D Convolution**.
    *   Structure: `Conv1d(k=3, padding=1) -> LayerNorm -> GELU`.
    *   *Strategic Retention:* We retain the 1D Convolution to leverage Z-axis spatial continuity (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014). We enforce Non-Linearity and Normalization for stability (Cite solution_lesson_node_00027) but explicitly reject Residual connections (Cite solution_lesson_node_00026) and complex Multi-Scale blocks (Cite solution_lesson_node_00028) to prevent overfitting.
*   **Heads:** **Multi-Task Instance Classifiers**.
    *   The model projects the contextualized features to **8 distinct logits** per slice: [C1...C7, Patient_Overall].
    *   *Strategic Retention:* We use **Multi-Task Learning** (dedicated patient head) rather than deriving the patient prediction via `max()`. This stabilizes training by allowing independent gradient flow for the global label (Cite solution_lesson_node_00045).
*   **Aggregation:** **Global Max Pooling** applied per channel across the slice sequence.
    *   *Strategic Retention:* Max Pooling is superior to Attention or Average Pooling for detecting sparse anomalies like fractures in the sequence dimension (Cite solution_lesson_node_00019, Cite solution_lesson_node_00047).

### 2. Data
*   **Ingestion Pipeline:**
    *   **Sorting:** Sort slices strictly by `ImagePositionPatient` z-coordinate (Cite solution_lesson_node_00031).
    *   **Calibration:** Apply `RescaleSlope` and `RescaleIntercept` to convert to Hounsfield Units (HU) (Cite solution_lesson_node_00031).
    *   **Windowing:** Apply **Standard Bone Window** (Level: 400, Width: 1800) (Cite solution_lesson_node_00029).
    *   **Resizing:** Resize to **224x224**. We explicitly reject cropping (Cite solution_lesson_node_00087) to preserve Field-of-View, and reject 256x256 resolution to maintain a stable Batch Size of 8 (Cite solution_lesson_node_00072).
    *   **Neighbor Selection (Critical):** Construct 2.5D stacks using neighbors ($z \pm 1$) from the **original high-resolution volume**, not the downsampled sequence, to preserve physical local coherence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00089).
*   **Structural Innovation (Dynamic Z-Jitter Sampling):**
    *   Instead of deterministically sampling 64 uniformly spaced slices, we implement **Stochastic Strided Sampling** during training.
    *   Calculate the stride $S = N_{total} / 64$. For each index $i \in [0, 63]$, select slice $z_i = (i \times S) + \delta$, where $\delta \sim \text{Uniform}(-0.5 \times S, 0.5 \times S)$.
    *   *Justification:* This exposes the model to the *entire* volume over the course of training epochs, preventing it from overfitting to specific slice indices and increasing the probability of seeing fractures that lie between the standard grid points (Addressing Lesson 00010's sampling limitation).
*   **Augmentation:**
    *   **Volumetric-Consistent:** Apply the exact same geometric transform to every slice in the bag (Cite solution_lesson_node_00013).
    *   **Single-Step Affine:** Fuse rotation, scaling, and shifting into a single affine operation (`ShiftScaleRotate`) to minimize resampling blur (Cite solution_lesson_node_00042).
    *   **Non-Destructive:** Explicitly avoid `Cutout` or `Dropout` on inputs (Cite solution_lesson_node_00037).

### 3. Training
*   **Loss Function:** **Implicitly Weighted Multi-Task Loss**.
    *   $L = \text{mean}(\text{BCE}_{C1..C7}) + \text{BCE}_{\text{Patient}}$.
    *   *Strategic Retention:* This formulation naturally creates the **1:7 weighting ratio** required by the metric without explicit scalar multipliers that distort gradients (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008, Cite solution_lesson_node_00043).
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with **Batch Size 8**.
    *   *Strategic Retention:* Small batch size acts as a necessary regularizer for this small dataset (Cite solution_lesson_node_00032). The LayerNorm backbone ensures stability.
*   **Scheduler:** **Decoupled Cosine Annealing**. Set `T_max` to **1.5x the number of epochs**.
    *   *Strategic Retention:* Prevents premature learning rate decay (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
*   **Regularization:** Do **not** use Dropout in the classification heads (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).

### 4. Evaluation
*   **Structural Innovation (Multi-Offset Test-Time Augmentation):**
    *   During inference, we address the "Sparse Anomaly" risk by running the model on **3 distinct Z-offsets**:
        1.  Standard Uniform Sampling (Offset 0).
        2.  Shifted Sampling (Offset $+0.3 \times S$).
        3.  Shifted Sampling (Offset $-0.3 \times S$).
    *   **Aggregation:** Compute the **element-wise Maximum** of the probabilities across the 3 runs.
    *   *Justification:* Since fractures are sparse and the aggregation function is Max Pooling, taking the maximum across multiple grid offsets significantly increases the recall for fractures that might otherwise fall between sampled slices, without retraining the model.
*   **Submission:** Submit the aggregated 7 vertebral probabilities and the distinct `patient_overall` probability. Ensure metric calculation normalizes by sum-of-weights, not number-of-heads (Cite solution_lesson_node_00077).
