Based on the lessons learned, I propose a structural evolution of the solution called the **Sequence-Smoothed 2.5D MIL Network**.

This approach moves beyond the "independent slice" assumption of the previous baseline. Instead of treating the CT scan as a "bag" of disconnected images, it explicitly models the **z-axis continuity** of the spine using a lightweight sequence modeling layer. This addresses the critical weakness identified in **Lesson 00010**, where the Max-Pooling aggregator was highly sensitive to single-slice noise.

### Model
The architecture is a **Weakly Supervised 2.5D CNN-RNN Hybrid** (using 1D Convolutions as the RNN component for efficiency) designed to filter noise before aggregation.

1.  **Backbone (Retained):** Use **ResNet-18** as the feature extractor. The input remains a **2.5D Stack** (3 channels: slices $z-1, z, z+1$) to capture immediate local context **(Cite Idea 1)**.
2.  **Structural Innovation: Inter-Slice Context Module:**
    *   Instead of feeding the backbone features directly to a classifier, the model collects the feature vectors from all sampled slices into a sequence tensor of shape $(Batch, \text{Slices}, \text{Features})$.
    *   Apply a **1D Convolutional Layer** (kernel size=3, padding=1) across the slice dimension.
    *   **Purpose:** This layer allows the model to "smooth" the feature representation of a specific slice $z$ using information from its neighbors $z-1$ and $z+1$. If a fracture signal is detected at slice $z$ but not in its neighbors, the 1D convolution can suppress this as noise/artifact. This directly mitigates the "False Positive Risk" of Max Pooling observed in **Cite Lesson 00010**.
3.  **Classification Head:** The smoothed features are projected to 7 logits (C1-C7) per slice.
4.  **Aggregation:** Apply **Global Max Pooling** across the slice dimension to obtain the study-level logits for each vertebra.
5.  **Patient Output:** The `patient_overall` logit is dynamically computed as the maximum of the 7 vertebrae logits **(Cite Lesson 00005)**.
6.  **Regularization:** Do **not** use Dropout in the classification head, as it destabilizes the Max-Pooling gradient signal **(Cite Lesson 00003)**.

### Data
1.  **Sampling:** Uniformly sample **64 slices** per exam. Do not increase the bag size to 96, as the increased resolution introduces more noise than signal for the Max operator **(Cite Lesson 00010)**.
2.  **Preprocessing:** Resize images to 256x256 and apply bone windowing.
3.  **Augmentation:** Apply a robust augmentation pipeline (HorizontalFlip, ShiftScaleRotate, RandomBrightnessContrast) during training. This is structurally required to prevent the catastrophic overfitting and high confidence penalties observed when augmentation was removed **(Cite Lesson 00011)**.

### Training
1.  **Loss Function (Hierarchical Compound Loss):**
    *   Compute Binary Cross Entropy (BCE) for the 7 vertebral sub-labels.
    *   Compute BCE for the `patient_overall` label using the `max(C1...C7)` aggregate derived in the forward pass.
    *   Sum these two losses. This ensures the model learns that a fracture prediction on *any* vertebra implies a fractured patient, forcing calibration **(Cite Lesson 00005)**.
2.  **Weighting:** Use **Implicit Weighting via Reduction**. Calculate the mean loss for the 7 sub-labels and add it to the scalar loss for the patient label. Do not apply explicit positive class weights, as this causes the Max-Pooling aggregator to hallucinate fractures **(Cite Lesson 00001)**. This formulation naturally balances the importance of specific vertebrae vs. the global outcome **(Cite Lesson 00008)**.
3.  **Optimizer & Scheduler:** Use AdamW with a **Cosine Annealing Scheduler**. Crucially, set the scheduler's `T_max` to **1.5x the total epochs** (e.g., 15 if training for 10). This prevents the learning rate from decaying too rapidly, allowing the model to escape suboptimal local minima in the early phase **(Cite Lesson 00007)**.

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** Process the test volume (subsampled to 64 slices) through the backbone and the **Inter-Slice Context Module**.
2.  **Prediction:**
    *   Extract the max-pooled probability for each vertebra (C1-C7).
    *   Derive the `patient_overall` probability as the maximum of the C1-C7 probabilities.
    *   This ensures the submission is logically consistent and minimizes the weighted log loss penalty.
