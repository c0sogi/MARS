# Solution Proposal: Subword-Level Dilated Wide-and-Deep Network

## Model
I propose a **Subword-Level Dilated Wide-and-Deep** architecture. This design evolves the previous "Wide-and-Deep" and "TextCNN" ideas by fundamentally changing the input representation from word-level to subword-level, addressing the high rate of out-of-vocabulary (OOV) technical terms while adjusting the convolutional backbone to maintain its effective receptive field.

*   **Input Representation (Structural Innovation)**: Instead of the standard word-level tokenization used in previous baselines, this model utilizes **Subword Tokenization** (e.g., Byte-Pair Encoding or BPE). Technical discussions frequently involve rare library names, error codes (e.g., `0x80040154`), and version numbers that are discarded as `<UNK>` in fixed-vocabulary word models. Subword tokenization decomposes these into meaningful constituent parts, preserving crucial semantic signals.
*   **Wide Stream (Memorization)**: A linear `EmbeddingBag` layer (with `mode='sum'`) operates directly on the subword sequence. This allows the model to learn direct, low-latency associations between specific subword patterns (e.g., "django") and their corresponding tags, functioning as a learnable TF-IDF mechanism.
*   **Deep Stream (Generalization)**: A **Dilated TextCNN**.
    *   **Adaptation**: Since subword sequences are significantly longer than word sequences (e.g., "TensorFlow" $\to$ ["Ten", "sor", "Flow"]), standard kernel sizes (3, 4, 5) become too local to capture phrases. I propose using **Parallel Dilated Convolutions** with a fixed kernel size (e.g., 3) but varying dilation rates (e.g., 1, 2, and 3). This expands the receptive field to cover 3, 5, and 7 subwords respectively without increasing the parameter count, effectively restoring the n-gram capture capability proven effective in the baseline (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
    *   **Aggregation**: The outputs of the dilated filters are aggregated via **Global Max Pooling** to extract the strongest feature signals regardless of their position (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
*   **Fusion**: The logits from the Wide and Deep streams are summed element-wise before the final Sigmoid activation.

## Data
*   **Preprocessing**: Concatenate the `Title` and `Body` fields into a single text sequence. This aligns with the finding that shared representations are superior for same-modality fields (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
*   **Tokenization**: Train a **BPE Tokenizer** on the training corpus with a vocabulary size of approximately 30,000 to 50,000. This ensures 100% vocabulary coverage.
*   **Sequence Standardization**: Pad or truncate sequences to a fixed length (e.g., 512 tokens). The length is increased compared to word-level models to accommodate the finer granularity of subwords.
*   **Target Encoding**: Encode the top $K$ most frequent tags using a Multi-Label Binarizer.

## Training
*   **Strategy**: Train on the **full training dataset**. The efficiency of the CNN architecture allows for processing the entire corpus, avoiding the performance collapse associated with data starvation in complex models (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008).
*   **Loss Function**: **Binary Cross-Entropy with Logits Loss** (BCEWithLogitsLoss).
*   **Optimizer**: **AdamW** with a One-Cycle learning rate schedule.
*   **Efficiency**: Utilize **Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP)** on the NVIDIA A100 GPU. This is particularly important for the Wide stream's embedding layer and the increased sequence length of the Deep stream.

## Evaluation
*   **Inference**: Process the test set using the trained BPE tokenizer and model pipeline.
*   **Thresholding**: Compute tag probabilities and apply a threshold. The threshold value will be determined by maximizing the F1-score on the validation set.
*   **Formatting**: Map predicted indices back to tag strings and join them with spaces.
