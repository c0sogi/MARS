Based on the lessons learned, specifically the success of the MLP-Mixer as an efficient encoder and the failure of the RNN decoder on long sequences (Lesson ID: `solution_lesson_node_00013`), I propose the following structural evolution.

### Proposed Solution: Isotropic Mixer-Transformer with Stoichiometric Guidance

This solution synthesizes the efficient encoding of the MLP-Mixer with the long-range sequence modeling capabilities of a Transformer, augmented by an auxiliary task to enforce chemical validity.

#### Model
- **Encoder (MLP-Mixer):** The backbone remains an **MLP-Mixer**. It divides the input image into patches and processes them using alternating layers of Token-Mixing MLPs (mixing spatial information) and Channel-Mixing MLPs (mixing feature information). This component is explicitly retained for its proven convergence and compute efficiency on chemical graph images *Cite {solution_lesson_node_00013}*.
- **Decoder (Transformer):** The Recurrent Neural Network (GRU) used in previous iterations is replaced with a **Transformer Decoder**. This decoder employs Masked Self-Attention to model the InChI sequence and Multi-Head Cross-Attention to query the MLP-Mixer's patch embeddings. This structural change is designed to overcome the information bottleneck and degradation on long sequences observed with RNN decoders *Cite {solution_lesson_node_00013}*.
- **Stoichiometric Auxiliary Head:** A dense classification head is attached to the global average pooling of the encoder's output. This head predicts the **molecular formula** (counts of atoms like C, H, N, O, etc.). This auxiliary path forces the isotropic encoder to capture precise stoichiometric information, mitigating the risk of hallucinating or dropping atoms in complex structures.

#### Data
- **Preprocessing:**
    - **Resizing:** Images are resized to a higher resolution (e.g., **384x384**) compared to previous baselines. This is necessary to alleviate the "fixed patch resolution" bottleneck identified in the failure analysis of dense molecules *Cite {solution_lesson_node_00013}*.
    - **Normalization:** Standard pixel normalization to $[0, 1]$.
    - **Auxiliary Labels:** During data loading, the ground truth InChI strings are parsed to extract atom counts, creating a target vector for the auxiliary head.
    - **Tokenization:** Standard character-level tokenization for the InChI strings.

#### Training
- **Loss Function:** A multi-task loss objective is employed: $L_{total} = L_{CE} + \lambda L_{Formula}$.
    - $L_{CE}$: Cross-Entropy loss for the predicted character sequence.
    - $L_{Formula}$: Mean Squared Error (or Smooth L1) loss for the predicted atom counts.
- **Optimizer:** **AdamW** is used to handle the Transformer's training dynamics.
- **Strategy:** The Transformer architecture allows for **Teacher Forcing** with full parallelization (unlike the sequential RNN), significantly increasing training throughput.

#### Evaluation
- **Inference Process:**
    - The test image is passed through the MLP-Mixer to generate a sequence of patch embeddings.
    - **Greedy Decoding:** The Transformer decoder is initialized with a `<SOS>` token. It autoregressively predicts the next character by attending to the encoder's patch memory, stopping when the `<EOS>` token is generated or the maximum length is reached.
