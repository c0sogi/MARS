# Proposed Strategy: Multi-Task Stylometric DAPT with Length-Adaptive Hybrid Ensembling

## Model
The solution implements a **Heterogeneous Hybrid Ensemble** that structurally integrates stylometric supervision into the neural training process and adapts the ensemble strategy based on input text length.

1.  **Neural Branch (Multi-Task Stylometric Learners):**
    *   **Backbones:** Deploy **`microsoft/deberta-v3-base`** and **`roberta-base`**. This combination preserves the architectural diversity (Disentangled Attention vs. Absolute Positional Embeddings) proven to provide orthogonal error profiles **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00008}**.
    *   **Hierarchical Pooling:** Instead of utilizing only the final layer's `[CLS]` token, the model extracts and concatenates the `[CLS]` tokens from the **last four hidden layers**. This ensures the representation captures the hierarchical progression from low-level morphology to high-level semantics **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00019}**.
    *   **Multi-Task Learning (MTL) Head (Structural Innovation):** To explicitly force the neural encoders to retain stylometric signals (addressing the "bag-of-words" degeneration risk **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00010}**), we introduce an auxiliary regression head. This head takes the concatenated embedding and predicts two scalar values: **Log-Character-Length** and **Punctuation Density**. This acts as a regularizer, ensuring the latent space encodes these critical authorial signatures.

2.  **Statistical Branch (The Surface Expert):**
    *   Retain the **TF-IDF pipeline** utilizing **Word N-grams (1-2)** and **Character N-grams (3-5)**. This captures explicit sub-word features and punctuation habits **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00002}**.
    *   The classifier is a weighted ensemble of **Logistic Regression** and **Multinomial Naive Bayes**, combining discriminative calibration with generative signal detection **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00003}**.

## Data
1.  **Domain-Adaptive Pre-training (DAPT):**
    *   Concatenate the text from `train.csv` and `test.csv`.
    *   Train both Transformer backbones on this combined corpus using the **Masked Language Modeling (MLM)** objective. This adapts the pre-trained weights to the specific 19th-century vocabulary and syntax **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00017}**.
2.  **Auxiliary Target Generation:**
    *   For the training set, pre-calculate the **Log-Character-Length** and **Punctuation Density** (ratio of punctuation characters to total characters) for every sample. These serve as the ground truth for the MTL auxiliary head.

## Training
1.  **Stratified K-Fold Cross-Validation (k=5):**
    *   Train all models using a 5-fold stratified split to ensure 100% of the training data is utilized and to generate robust Out-Of-Fold (OOF) predictions.
2.  **Multi-Task Optimization:**
    *   Train the neural models to minimize a joint loss function: $L_{total} = L_{CrossEntropy} + \lambda \cdot L_{MSE}$, where $L_{MSE}$ is the mean squared error for the auxiliary stylometric targets.
    *   Use **Adversarial Weight Perturbation (AWP)** to flatten the loss landscape and improve generalization.
3.  **Statistical Optimization:**
    *   Train the Logistic Regression and Naive Bayes models on the TF-IDF features. Optimize their internal mixing weight on the OOF predictions **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00014}**.

## Evaluation
1.  **Inference:**
    *   Generate probability predictions for the test set using the 5-fold bagged Neural models and the Statistical ensemble.
2.  **Length-Adaptive Blending (Structural Innovation):**
    *   To address the finding that model performance correlates with text length **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00002}**, we implement a piecewise ensemble strategy.
    *   Partition the validation (OOF) data into **3 bins based on character length** (e.g., Short, Medium, Long).
    *   Mathematically optimize the ensemble weights ($w_{stat}, w_{deberta}, w_{roberta}$) **independently for each bin** to minimize Log Loss. This allows the system to rely more on the Neural branch for short texts (where N-grams are sparse) or vice-versa, depending on empirical performance.
    *   Apply these bin-specific weights to the test set predictions based on their respective lengths.
3.  **Stability:**
    *   Apply numerical stability clipping ($max(min(p, 1-10^{-15}), 10^{-15})$) to the final probabilities.
