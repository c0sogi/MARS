Based on the empirical evidence and lessons learned, I propose the **Mono-Residual EfficientNet with GeM Pooling**. This strategy synthesizes the efficient input adaptation of the baseline ResNet solution with the advanced feature extraction capabilities of the EfficientNet architecture, while strictly addressing the class imbalance issue identified in the failure analysis.

# Model
**Architecture: Mono-Residual EfficientNet-B0**
The model architecture is designed to maximize sensitivity to high-frequency noise while preserving transfer learning efficiency.
1.  **Fixed Residual Preprocessing**: The network input is processed by a single, non-trainable **$5 \times 5$ High-Pass Filter (KV Kernel)**. This layer suppresses semantic image content (edges, textures) to isolate the noise residuals where steganographic signals reside. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00001**.
2.  **Single-Channel Backbone Adaptation**: The residual map is passed to an **EfficientNet-B0** backbone. To leverage ImageNet pretrained weights without the instability of expanding to a 30-channel input (as seen in previous attempts), the first convolutional layer of the EfficientNet is adapted to accept the single-channel residual input by **summing the weights** of the original RGB channels. This preserves the magnitude and spatial structure of the pretrained feature detectors. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00009**.
3.  **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling**: Standard Global Average Pooling is replaced with a trainable **GeM Pooling** layer. Since steganographic artifacts are often sparse or localized in specific textured regions, GeM pooling allows the model to focus on high-activation areas (strongest evidence of modification) rather than diluting the signal through simple averaging.

# Data
**Preprocessing**
1.  **Channel Extraction**: Extract the **Luminance (Y)** channel from the YCbCr color space for all images. This focuses the model on the channel containing the majority of the embedding signal and reduces computational overhead. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00001**.
2.  **Augmentation**: Apply Dihedral group augmentations (random 90-degree rotations and flips) during training.

**Sampling Strategy**
1.  **Class Balancing via Oversampling**: To address the 1:3 imbalance (1 Cover vs. 3 Stego algorithms), use a **Weighted Random Sampler** that oversamples the minority "Cover" class. Ensure that Cover images constitute 50% of every batch. This repeated exposure to the "negative" class tightens the decision boundary and improves specificity, which is critical for the Weighted AUC metric. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**.

# Training
**Procedure**
1.  **Loss Function**: Use **Binary Cross Entropy with Logits**.
2.  **Optimizer**: Use **AdamW** with a learning rate of 1e-3 and a **Cosine Annealing** scheduler to efficiently navigate the loss landscape within the time limit.
3.  **Regularization**: Apply minimal Label Smoothing (0.05) to prevent overfitting to the noisy labels.

# Evaluation
**Prediction Strategy**
1.  **Test Time Augmentation (TTA)**: For each test image, generate predictions for the original image and its 3 rotated versions (90, 180, 270 degrees).
2.  **Score Aggregation**: Average the sigmoid scores of the 4 views to produce the final prediction. This ensemble-in-time approach reduces the variance caused by the grid-aligned nature of JPEG artifacts.
