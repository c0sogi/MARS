### Proposed Solution: Context-Aware Transformer with Stochastic Identity Masking

I propose a structural evolution of the previous solution by transitioning from a static Neural Bag-of-Words (NBOW) architecture to a **Context-Aware Transformer Backbone (e.g., DistilBERT)**. While the NBOW model established a strong baseline for bias mitigation, it fundamentally lacks the ability to understand word order and syntactic context (e.g., distinguishing "I hate X" from "X hates me"). A Transformer backbone addresses this limitation by learning deep, contextualized representations, while retaining the successful bias mitigation strategies identified in the lessons.

#### 1. Model
*   **Backbone Architecture**: Replace the `EmbeddingBag` and MLP layers with a pre-trained **DistilBERT** transformer model. This architecture utilizes self-attention mechanisms to capture complex dependencies between words, significantly improving the model's ability to discern toxic intent from benign usage of identity terms, which NBOW could not capture.
*   **Classification Head**: The `[CLS]` token embedding from the transformer will be fed into a distinct classification head. To improve generalization and stability, we will implement **Multi-Sample Dropout** in this head, where the embedding is passed through multiple dropout masks and linear layers in parallel, and their outputs are averaged.
*   **Bias Mitigation Integration**: We explicitly retain the **Stochastic Identity Masking** strategy. By integrating this with a Transformer, the technique becomes even more potent: when an identity token is masked, the self-attention mechanism is forced to attend to the *surrounding context* (adjectives, verbs) to predict toxicity, effectively "wiring" the model to ignore identity identifiers as discriminative features. #### 2. Data
*   **Preprocessing**: We will switch from simple whitespace tokenization to **WordPiece tokenization** to match the pre-trained transformer's vocabulary. This handles rare words and subwords more effectively.
*   **Dynamic Identity Masking**: We will maintain the data augmentation pipeline where tokens corresponding to identity subgroups (e.g., "muslim", "gay", "women") are identified and replaced with the special `[MASK]` token with a probability of 0.5 during training. Cite solution_lesson_node_00001
*   **Dynamic Padding**: To maximize training throughput on the A100 GPU, we will implement dynamic padding, where batches are padded only to the length of the longest sequence in that specific batch rather than a fixed global maximum.

#### 3. Training
*   **Objective**: We will minimize the Binary Cross Entropy (BCE) loss using the soft targets provided in the dataset, preserving the nuance of rater disagreement.
*   **Optimization**: We will use the **AdamW** optimizer with a linear learning rate warmup and decay, which is standard for stabilizing transformer fine-tuning.
*   **State Management**: To address the critical failure observed in previous iterations, we will implement a robust Early Stopping mechanism. When a new best metric is achieved on the validation set, we will strictly use `copy.deepcopy(model.state_dict())` to cache the weights, ensuring that subsequent optimizer steps do not mutate the stored best state. *   **Metric Monitoring**: The model will be evaluated after every epoch on the validation set using the competition's custom weighted metric (Generalized Mean of Bias AUCs), ensuring we optimize for fairness, not just accuracy.

#### 4. Evaluation
*   **Inference**: For the final submission, the model will process the test set without any masking. The `[CLS]` token output will be passed through the classification head to generate toxicity probabilities.
*   **Validation**: We will compute the full suite of bias metrics (Subgroup AUC, BPSN, BNSP) on the validation split to verify that the structural upgrade to a Transformer has not reintroduced bias compared to the NBOW baseline.
