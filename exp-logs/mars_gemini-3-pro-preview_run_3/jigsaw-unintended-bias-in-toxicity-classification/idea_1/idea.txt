Based on the task requirements and the provided dataset, I propose a highly efficient baseline approach that prioritizes speed and simplicity while effectively addressing the bias minimization objective.

### **Proposed Solution: Neural Bag-of-Words (NBOW) with Stochastic Identity Masking**

This approach utilizes a lightweight neural architecture that averages word embeddings, making it significantly faster than Recurrent (LSTM) or Convolutional (CNN) models. The core innovation for bias mitigation is a data-level intervention called **Stochastic Identity Masking**, which prevents the model from learning simple correlations between identity terms and toxicity.

#### **1. Model**
The architecture is a simple, feed-forward neural network designed for maximum throughput and interpretability.
*   **Embedding Layer**: The model uses a trainable embedding layer (e.g., PyTorch's `nn.EmbeddingBag`) which maps each word in a comment to a dense vector and efficiently calculates the **mean** of these vectors for the entire sequence. This ignores word order, focusing purely on the presence of semantic content.
*   **Hidden Layers**: The averaged embedding vector is passed through a shallow Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP)—specifically, two fully connected layers with ReLU activation and Batch Normalization.
*   **Output Head**: A single output neuron with a Sigmoid activation function produces the toxicity probability.
*   **Distinction**: This design is distinct from the complex sequence models (LSTM, CNN, Transformer) and tree ensembles (LightGBM) listed in the architectures. It relies on the "Bag-of-Words" assumption, which is a robust baseline for text classification.

#### **2. Data**
Preprocessing focuses on preparing the text for the embedding layer and identifying terms for the bias mitigation strategy.
*   **Tokenization**: Text is lowercased and split into tokens. A vocabulary is built from the top $N$ (e.g., 50,000) most frequent words in the training set.
*   **Identity Term Mapping**: We create a specific set of vocabulary indices corresponding to the protected identity attributes found in the dataset columns (e.g., words like "muslim", "gay", "black", "female", "jewish").
*   **Soft Targets**: We utilize the original fractional values in the `target` column (e.g., 0.6, 0.2) rather than binarizing them. This preserves the nuance of human rater disagreement, which is often high in ambiguous, bias-sensitive examples.

#### **3. Training**
The training procedure incorporates a unique regularization technique to handle bias without complex loss functions.
*   **Stochastic Identity Masking**: During the training phase, we apply a dynamic transformation to the input data. For each batch, any token in the input sequence that matches our **Identity Term Mapping** is replaced with a special `<MASK>` token with a probability $p$ (e.g., 0.5).
    *   *Mechanism*: By randomly hiding the identity terms, the model is forced to predict toxicity based on the **surrounding context** (e.g., "I hate [MASK]" vs. "[MASK] are distinct") rather than overfitting to the identity word itself as a feature. This directly minimizes BPSN (Background Positive, Subgroup Negative) bias.
*   **Loss Function**: Binary Cross Entropy (BCE) Loss applied to the soft targets.
*   **Optimization**: Standard Adam optimizer. We employ **Early Stopping** based on the validation set's **Generalized Mean of Bias AUCs** (the competition metric) rather than global accuracy, ensuring the final model is selected for its fairness.

#### **4. Evaluation**
*   **Inference**: During the prediction phase on the test set, **no masking** is applied. The model sees the full text.
*   **Outcome**: Because the model was trained to rely on context rather than specific identity keywords, it will naturally produce unbiased predictions on the test data, distinguishing between toxic and non-toxic mentions of identities without requiring post-processing or ensembles.
