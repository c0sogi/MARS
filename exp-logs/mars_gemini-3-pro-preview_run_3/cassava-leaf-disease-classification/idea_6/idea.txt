Based on the comprehensive analysis of previous experiments and the derived lessons, I propose the following optimized strategy. This solution implements a **Dual-Stream Hybrid Architecture**, structurally synthesizing the strengths of the best-performing CNN (Idea 2) and Transformer (Idea 3) models into a unified system.

# Model
I propose a **Dual-Stream Late-Fusion** architecture that explicitly processes images through two parallel backbones to capture complementary feature sets:
*   **Stream A (Global Context):** **ViT-Base (patch16_384)**. This branch leverages the self-attention mechanism to model long-range dependencies and global leaf patterns, preserving the superior performance observed in Idea 3 (Cite Lesson 00015).
*   **Stream B (Local Detail):** **EfficientNet-B4**. This branch leverages the inductive bias of convolutions to capture fine-grained, high-frequency local textures (e.g., specific lesion boundaries), retaining the robustness observed in Idea 2.
*   **Fusion Head:** The feature embeddings from both streams (the CLS token from ViT and the Global Average Pooled vector from EfficientNet) will be concatenated. This combined vector will pass through a Batch Normalization layer, a Dropout layer, and a final Linear classification layer.
*   **Resolution Alignment:** Crucially, both backbones will operate at **384x384** resolution. This ensures we utilize the native resolution of the ViT and the high-fidelity capacity of the EfficientNet (resized slightly from 380 to 384), explicitly avoiding the pre-training resolution mismatch that caused the failure of the CoAtNet experiment (Cite Lesson 00022).
*   **Initialization:** Both backbones will be initialized with their respective ImageNet pre-trained weights (ImageNet-21k for ViT, NoisyStudent for EfficientNet) to ensure robust feature extraction.

# Data
*   **Preprocessing:** All images will be resized to **384x384 pixels**. This standardizes the input for the dual-stream model and maintains the high resolution required for fine-grained classification (Cite Lesson 00012).
*   **Augmentation:**
    *   **Mixing:** We will strictly employ **MixUp and CutMix** regularization. This is essential to prevent the significantly increased parameter count of the dual model (approx. 105M parameters) from overfitting and to enforce linear decision boundaries (Cite Lesson 00012).
    *   **Geometric vs. Photometric:** We will prioritize geometric transformations (flips, rotations) and strictly avoid aggressive photometric augmentations (like Hue/Saturation shifts) to preserve discriminative color signals (Cite Lesson 00004).
    *   **Determinism:** We will implement **Deterministic Data Loading** by seeding augmentation decisions within the `__getitem__` method, ensuring that the training trajectory is reproducible and immune to multi-process loader variance (Cite Lesson 00014).

# Training
*   **Loss Function:** We will use **Cross Entropy Loss with Label Smoothing (0.1)**. This remains a critical regularizer to prevent the high-capacity hybrid model from becoming overconfident (Cite Lesson 00007).
*   **Optimizer:** We will use **AdamW** with a Cosine Annealing scheduler.
*   **Batch Size & Accumulation:** Given the memory footprint of running two backbones, the physical batch size may need to be reduced (e.g., to 16). To mitigate the instability of Batch Normalization at lower batch sizes, we will implement **Gradient Accumulation** to maintain an effective batch size of at least 32 (Cite Lesson 00013).
*   **Strategy:** We will employ **Early Stopping** based on validation accuracy. We will explicitly avoid prolonged training strategies like SWA, as the high-capacity backbones are expected to converge to an optimal peak rapidly (Cite Lesson 00017).

# Evaluation
*   **Inference:** We will utilize **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA)**, averaging predictions across the original image, a horizontal flip, and a vertical flip. Given the extremely high representational capacity of the dual-stream architecture, TTA will serve as a robust performance multiplier (Cite Lesson 00010).
