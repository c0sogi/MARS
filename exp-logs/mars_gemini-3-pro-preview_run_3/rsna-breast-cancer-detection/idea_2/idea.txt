# Optimized Strategy: Decoupled Two-Stage Calibration with EfficientNetV2

This proposal introduces a structural evolution by decoupling **feature representation learning** from **probability calibration**. It addresses the critical failure identified in the previous attempt—where balanced sampling destroyed probability calibration—by implementing a dedicated calibration stage while preserving the convergence benefits of balanced training.

### Model
*   **Backbone Architecture**: Upgrade the backbone to **EfficientNetV2-Small**.
    *   *Rationale*: EfficientNetV2 offers a superior trade-off between training speed and feature extraction capability compared to ResNet-18, particularly for the fine-grained texture analysis required in mammography.
*   **Calibration Head**: Instead of a simple fully connected layer trained end-to-end, the model will utilize a **Decoupled Classification Head**. This head (a Logistic Regression layer) will be trained in a separate stage to map the backbone's feature embeddings to calibrated probabilities.

### Data
*   **Preprocessing**:
    *   **Percentile Windowing**: Instead of simple Min-Max normalization (which is sensitive to high-intensity artifacts common in DICOMs), apply **Percentile Normalization**. Clip pixel intensities to the 1st and 99th percentiles of the image before normalizing to [0, 1]. This ensures the model focuses on tissue texture rather than scanner artifacts.
    *   **Resizing**: Maintain the **512x512** resolution to balance detail and compute.
*   **Augmentation**: Incorporate **RandomBrightnessContrast** and **ShiftScaleRotate**. These are crucial for X-ray modalities where intensity and orientation can vary slightly between scans.

### Training
The training process is split into two distinct stages to reconcile the conflict between efficient learning (which needs balance) and accurate probability estimation (which needs natural distribution).

*   **Stage 1: Representation Learning (Balanced)**
    *   **Objective**: Learn robust features to distinguish cancer from normal tissue.
    *   **Sampling**: Use **Balanced Batch Sampling (1:1 ratio)** of positive and negative samples. As noted in the lessons, this prevents the model from trivially predicting the majority class and ensures strong gradient flow for the minority class. Cite {solution_lesson_node_00001}.
    *   **Procedure**: Train the EfficientNetV2 backbone and a temporary head using Binary Cross Entropy (BCE) loss.

*   **Stage 2: Probability Calibration (Natural)**
    *   **Objective**: Correct the biased priors learned in Stage 1 to maximize the **Probabilistic F1 (pF1)** score.
    *   **Sampling**: Use the **Natural Distribution** (no resampling) from the Validation Set (or a held-out portion of the train set).
    *   **Procedure**: Freeze the EfficientNetV2 backbone. Re-initialize and train *only* the final classification layer (or a Platt Scaling layer) on this natural distribution. This allows the model to adjust its decision boundary and logits to reflect the true ~2% cancer prevalence, directly fixing the calibration issue. Cite {solution_lesson_node_00001}.

### Evaluation
*   **Inference**: Pass test images through the two-stage model. The output will be a probability score that is already calibrated to the test set's expected distribution.
*   **Aggregation**: Apply **Max Pooling** over the calibrated probabilities for all images sharing a `prediction_id`. This retains the robust logic that a high probability in *any* view warrants a high risk score for the patient.
