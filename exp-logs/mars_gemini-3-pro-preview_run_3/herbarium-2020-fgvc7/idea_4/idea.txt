Based on the comprehensive analysis of previous experiments and lessons learned, I propose the following solution. This approach structurally evolves the architecture by integrating the **Hierarchical Multi-Task** concept (from Idea 3) with the **Full Data Coverage** mandate (from Lesson 00013), enabled by a **Progressive Resizing** strategy to manage the computational budget.

# Proposed Solution: Hierarchical ResNet-50 with ArcFace and Progressive Resizing

### Model
*   **Backbone Architecture:** Use **ResNet-50**. Previous experiments confirmed that the ResNet-50 backbone (Idea 2, F1 0.351) significantly outperforms the lighter ResNet-18 (Idea 1, F1 0.178) due to the high capacity required to distinguish 32,093 fine-grained classes.
*   **Primary Head (Species):** Utilize an **ArcFace (Additive Angular Margin)** head for the species classification task.
    *   *Rationale:* As demonstrated in Lesson 00012, Angular Margin loss is critical for high-cardinality datasets, enforcing intra-class compactness that standard Linear/Softmax heads fail to achieve. Cite {solution_lesson_node_00012}
*   **Auxiliary Head (Genus):** Integrate a secondary **Cross-Entropy Classification Head** to predict the **Genus** of the plant.
    *   *Rationale:* This reintroduces the structural innovation of Idea 3. By supervising the backbone with Genus labels, we force the model to learn robust, transferable botanical features shared across species. This acts as a regularizer for the thousands of "singleton" species that have insufficient data to learn effectively on their own.

### Data
*   **Dataset Coverage:** Train on the **Full Training Dataset**.
    *   *Rationale:* Lesson 00013 proved that subsampling the dataset (even to 15%) to save time is catastrophic for long-tailed distributions, as it statistically eliminates the rare classes we aim to predict. We must prioritize data coverage over all else. Cite {solution_lesson_node_00013}
*   **Sampling Strategy:** Use **Inverse Square-Root Weighted Random Sampling** ($1/\sqrt{N}$).
    *   *Rationale:* This balances the need to upsample rare classes without causing the rapid overfitting observed with aggressive inverse-frequency sampling ($1/N$). Cite {solution_lesson_node_00007}
*   **Preprocessing (Progressive Resizing):** Implement a dynamic resolution strategy.
    *   **Phase 1:** Resize images to **160x160** pixels.
    *   **Phase 2:** Resize images to **224x224** pixels.
    *   *Rationale:* This is the key enabler. Training on smaller images initially significantly increases throughput (images/second) and allows larger batch sizes. This ensures the model sees the **full dataset** multiple times (satisfying Lesson 00013) to learn coarse features and the genus hierarchy, before fine-tuning on high-resolution images to resolve fine-grained species differences.

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** $L_{total} = L_{ArcFace}(Species) + \lambda \cdot L_{CE}(Genus)$.
*   **Optimizer:** SGD with Momentum (0.9) and Weight Decay ($1e-4$).
*   **Schedule:**
    *   **Epochs 1-12:** Train at **160x160** resolution.
    *   **Epochs 13-20:** Fine-tune at **224x224** resolution.
    *   *Rationale:* This schedule provides the necessary training duration (20 epochs) to converge with strong regularization (ArcFace/Augmentation) as recommended in Lesson 00008, while fitting within the 24-hour runtime limit. Cite {solution_lesson_node_00008}
*   **Validation:** Use a **Stratified Subset** of the training data to ensure rare classes are represented during evaluation. Cite {solution_lesson_node_00004}
*   **Safety Check:** Explicitly validate that cached sampling weights match the full dataset length to prevent the silent failures observed in Idea 2/3. Cite {solution_lesson_node_00010}

### Evaluation
*   **Inference:** Resize test images to 256x256 and center crop to 224x224.
*   **Prediction:** Use only the **Species ArcFace Head**. Compute the cosine similarity between the test image embedding and the learned class centers, selecting the class with the highest similarity. The Genus head is discarded at inference time.
