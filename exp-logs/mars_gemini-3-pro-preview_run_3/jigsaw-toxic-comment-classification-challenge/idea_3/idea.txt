# Proposed Solution: DeBERTa-v3-Large with Mean Pooling and NBSVM Ensemble

## Model
I propose a structural evolution of the architecture by replacing the RoBERTa backbone with **DeBERTa-v3-Large** (Decoding-enhanced BERT with disentangled attention). While RoBERTa relies on absolute position embeddings, DeBERTa utilizes disentangled attention mechanisms that separate content and position vectors, allowing for a more nuanced understanding of the syntactic dependencies often found in complex, obfuscated toxic comments.

To further enhance the neural component, I will replace the standard `[CLS]` token classification head with a **Mean Pooling** strategy. Instead of relying on a single token to summarize the entire sequence, Mean Pooling averages the contextual embeddings of all valid tokens in the final layer. This ensures that toxic signals buried deep within longer comments contribute directly to the final representation, addressing the information bottleneck of the `[CLS]` token.

I will explicitly retain the **NBSVM (Naive Bayes - Support Vector Machine)** model as a secondary "Keyword Expert" branch. As established, this linear baseline provides orthogonal value by capturing explicit lexical triggers that deep learning models might occasionally smooth over (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001, solution_lesson_node_00006).

## Data
1.  **Neural Preprocessing:** The text will be tokenized using the DeBERTa-v3 specific SentencePiece tokenizer. Building on the finding that longer sequences improve performance by capturing the "long tail" of information (Cite solution_lesson_node_00012), and leveraging the available A100 GPU memory, I will increase the maximum sequence length to **512 tokens**. This ensures virtually no truncation occurs, even for the most verbose comments.
2.  **NBSVM Preprocessing:** For the linear branch, I will generate sparse TF-IDF matrices for word n-grams (1-2) and character n-grams (2-6). I will strictly enforce a vocabulary pruning threshold of `min_df` $\ge$ 3 to filter out noisy, rare artifacts that were shown to degrade performance when included (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).

## Training
1.  **DeBERTa Fine-tuning:**
    *   **Schedule:** I will employ a fixed training schedule of **2 to 3 epochs** without intermediate evaluation or early stopping, as this maximizes training throughput for stable convergence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008).
    *   **Optimization:** To address the instability observed when scaling to "Large" architectures (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009), I will implement **Layer-wise Learning Rate Decay (LLRD)**. This technique assigns lower learning rates to the bottom layers (closer to the pre-trained embeddings) and higher rates to the top layers. This preserves the high-quality pre-trained features while allowing the new pooling head to adapt rapidly to the toxicity task.
2.  **NBSVM Fitting:** The linear model will be trained using Logistic Regression on the Naive-Bayes-scaled features, maintaining the robust regularization settings ($C=1.0$) identified in previous successful iterations (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).

## Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** Both the DeBERTa-v3-Large model and the NBSVM model will independently predict probabilities for the test set.
2.  **Adaptive Ensembling:** I will perform a **post-hoc linear weight search** on the validation set to determine the optimal mixing coefficient $\alpha$ for the ensemble (e.g., $P_{final} = \alpha \cdot P_{DeBERTa} + (1-\alpha) \cdot P_{NBSVM}$). This ensures that the contribution of the stronger DeBERTa model is maximized while still leveraging the keyword detection capabilities of the NBSVM, rather than relying on arbitrary fixed weights (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
