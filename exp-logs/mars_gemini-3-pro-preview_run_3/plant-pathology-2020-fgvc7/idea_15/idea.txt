Based on the analysis of the previous experiments and the derived lessons, I propose the **EMA-Stabilized Heterogeneous Ensemble with Multi-Level GeM**. This strategy structurally evolves the "Current Best" approach (Idea 7/19) by upgrading the backbone architectures for better texture extraction and integrating **Model Exponential Moving Average (EMA)** to address the variance issues observed with high-capacity models on this small dataset, while strictly avoiding the complexity penalties that caused Idea 14 to fail.

# Model
I propose a **Dual-Backbone Heterogeneous Ensemble** that synthesizes the inductive biases of CNNs and Transformers, specifically optimized for fine-grained lesion detection.

1.  **Backbone 1: EfficientNetV2-M (Texture Expert)**
    *   **Evolution:** We replace the EfficientNet-B4 (used in Idea 7/19) with **EfficientNetV2-M**.
    *   **Rationale:** EfficientNetV2 utilizes **Fused-MBConv** blocks in its early stages. These blocks replace depthwise-separable convolutions with standard dense convolutions, which are empirically superior at capturing the low-level, high-frequency textural details (e.g., edges of rust spots) critical for this task **Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**.
    *   **Head:** We retain the **Multi-Level Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling** head. We extract feature maps from the last three reduction stages, apply a learnable GeM pooling layer ($p=3$) to each, and concatenate the outputs. This ensures the model retains sensitivity to small, localized lesions **Cite solution_lesson_node_00018**.

2.  **Backbone 2: Swin Transformer Small (Context Expert)**
    *   **Retention:** We retain the **Swin-Small** architecture.
    *   **Rationale:** While Lesson 16 noted high variance with this model, it also highlighted its ability to capture long-range dependencies. We address the variance not by downgrading the model, but by stabilizing the training (see "Training" section).
    *   **Head:** We apply the same **Multi-Level GeM Pooling** strategy to the hierarchical outputs of the Swin backbone.

3.  **Constraint:** We explicitly **exclude** any Feature Pyramid Networks (FPN) or Multi-Task Auxiliary Heads. As demonstrated in Lesson 59, these complex, uninitialized structures induce catastrophic overfitting on small datasets. We adhere to the "Backbone → Pooling → Linear" design pattern **Cite solution_lesson_node_00059**.

# Data
1.  **Preprocessing:**
    *   All images will be resized to **384x384**. This resolution is higher than the standard 224x224, ensuring that minute disease lesions are not lost during downsampling **Cite solution_lesson_node_00004**, while remaining computationally feasible for the V2-M/Swin-S backbones using AMP.
2.  **Augmentation:**
    *   We employ a **Strong Geometric Augmentation** strategy (`ShiftScaleRotate`, `RandomFlip`) with high intensity to act as the primary regularizer **Cite solution_lesson_node_00003**.
    *   **Strict Exclusions:**
        *   **No Spatial Occlusion:** We strictly exclude Cutout/CoarseDropout to prevent removing small disease indicators **Cite solution_lesson_node_00004**.
        *   **No Photometric Augmentation:** We strictly exclude Brightness/Contrast shifts to preserve critical color signals **Cite solution_lesson_node_00014**.

# Training
1.  **Structural Innovation: Model EMA (Exponential Moving Average)**
    *   **Implementation:** We implement **Model EMA**. During training, we maintain a shadow copy of the model parameters that is updated as a moving average of the trained weights: $\theta_{EMA} = \beta \cdot \theta_{EMA} + (1 - \beta) \cdot \theta_{current}$.
    *   **Rationale:** This directly addresses the **high variance and instability** observed when training the Swin-Small backbone on this small dataset **Cite solution_lesson_node_00016**. EMA effectively smooths the optimization trajectory, finding a flatter, more robust minimum than the final checkpoint, which is often sensitive to the noise of Early Stopping **Cite solution_lesson_node_00006**.
2.  **Loss Function:** We use **Cross-Entropy Loss with Inverse Frequency Class Weights** to align the optimization with the macro-averaged metric **Cite solution_lesson_node_00001**.
3.  **Optimization:**
    *   **Optimizer:** AdamW with **Cosine Annealing**.
    *   **Schedule:** We train for **20-25 epochs** with a **Relaxed Patience of 10 epochs**. This extended patience is critical to allow the EMA weights to converge fully without premature termination during the high-variance phase **Cite solution_lesson_node_00008**.
    *   **Precision:** We use **Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP)** to handle the memory footprint of the V2-M/Swin-S backbones at 384x384 resolution **Cite solution_lesson_node_00019**.

# Evaluation
1.  **Inference Weights:** We generate predictions using the **EMA weights**, not the final training weights.
2.  **Domain-Aware Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):**
    *   We generate predictions for the **Original** image and a **Horizontal Flip**.
    *   **Constraint:** We explicitly **exclude Vertical Flips and Transpose**. These transformations violate the natural gravity priors of leaf images (stems typically point up/down), creating "unnatural" inputs that degrade inference accuracy **Cite solution_lesson_node_00012**.
3.  **Ensemble:** The final submission is the unweighted average of the probability vectors from all trained models (5-Fold Cross-Validation).
