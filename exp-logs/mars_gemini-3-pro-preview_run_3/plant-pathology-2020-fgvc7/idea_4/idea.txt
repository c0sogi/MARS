### Model
I propose a **K-Fold Ensemble with Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling**. This strategy introduces a structural innovation in the feature aggregation mechanism and the training pipeline to specifically address the "small, localized" nature of disease lesions and the dataset size constraints.

1.  **Backbone Architectures:** We will utilize two distinct backbones to ensure diversity:
    *   **EfficientNet-B4:** We upgrade the CNN component to the B4 variant (trained on **380x380** resolution). This higher resolution is critical for resolving fine-grained disease features.
    *   **ConvNeXt-Tiny:** We retain this transformer-style CNN (trained on **224x224** resolution) to capture global context.
2.  **Structural Innovation (GeM Pooling):** Standard Global Average Pooling (GAP) dilutes the signal of small, sparse lesions (like rust spots) by averaging them with the dominant healthy leaf area. We will replace the GAP layer in both models with **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling**. GeM is a learnable pooling layer that can dynamically adjust its pooling parameter $p$. As $p \to \infty$, it approaches Max Pooling. This allows the model to focus on the high-activation regions (the disease lesions) while suppressing the background noise, directly addressing the issue where discriminative features are "small, localized, and sparse" **Cite solution_lesson_node_00004**.

### Data
1.  **Stratified K-Fold Cross-Validation:** Instead of a single train/validation split, we will implement a **4-Fold Stratified Cross-Validation** scheme. Given the small dataset size (~1300 training images), a single split is statistically fragile. K-Fold ensures that every image is used for training in some folds and for validation in others, maximizing data utility and producing a more robust ensemble.
2.  **Preprocessing:** Images for EfficientNet-B4 will be resized to **380x380**, and images for ConvNeXt-Tiny to **224x224**.
3.  **Augmentation:** We will employ a **Strong Geometric Augmentation** pipeline including `ShiftScaleRotate`, `RandomFlip`, and `Transpose`. We will maintain wide scaling limits to force the learning of invariant features **Cite solution_lesson_node_00003**. Crucially, we will **strictly exclude** any spatial occlusion augmentations (like Cutout or CoarseDropout) to prevent the accidental removal of small disease indicators **Cite solution_lesson_node_00004**.

### Training
1.  **Loss Function:** We will use **Cross-Entropy Loss with Inverse Frequency Class Weights**. This ensures the optimization objective prioritizes the minority "multiple_diseases" class, aligning with the macro-averaged evaluation metric **Cite solution_lesson_node_00001**. We will avoid Label Smoothing as it was previously shown to degrade performance on this specific dataset **Cite solution_lesson_node_00004**.
2.  **Optimization:** We will use the **AdamW** optimizer with a Cosine Annealing learning rate scheduler.
3.  **Execution:** We will train a total of 8 models (2 architectures $\times$ 4 folds). Each fold will be trained independently, saving the checkpoint with the best validation ROC AUC.

### Evaluation
1.  **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):** During inference, we will generate predictions for the original image, a horizontally flipped version, and a vertically flipped version for every model instance **Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**.
2.  **Ensemble Strategy:** The final submission will be the unweighted average of the probability vectors from all 8 models (across all folds and architectures). This "Bagging" approach significantly reduces variance and overfitting compared to a single-split model.
