Based on the lessons learned from previous experiments, I propose the **High-Resolution Heterogeneous Ensemble with Model EMA**. This strategy structurally evolves the previous best approach (Idea 10) by significantly increasing the input resolution to resolve fine-grained lesions and integrating **Model Exponential Moving Average (EMA)** to stabilize the training of high-capacity architectures on this small dataset.

### Model
I propose a dual-backbone ensemble designed to maximize the detection of "small, localized" disease features (like rust spots) while maintaining robust global context.

1.  **Backbone 1: EfficientNetV2-M (High-Res Texture Expert)**
    *   **Architecture:** We utilize **EfficientNetV2-M**, leveraging its **Fused-MBConv** blocks which are superior for capturing high-frequency textural details compared to standard depthwise convolutions.
    *   **Structural Innovation (Resolution):** We increase the input resolution to **512x512** (up from 384x384). Given the original image dimensions (~2000x1300), this higher resolution is critical to prevent the vanishing of minute disease lesions during downsampling **Cite solution_lesson_node_00004**.
    *   **Head:** We employ **Multi-Level Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling**. We extract features from the last three reduction stages, apply GeM ($p=3$), and concatenate them to preserve signal from early, high-detail layers **Cite solution_lesson_node_00005** **Cite solution_lesson_node_00018**.

2.  **Backbone 2: MaxViT-Small (High-Res Global Expert)**
    *   **Architecture:** We employ **MaxViT-Small**, which combines convolutional inductive biases with **Multi-Axis Attention** (Block + Grid).
    *   **Structural Innovation (Resolution):** We increase the input resolution to **384x384** (up from 224x224). Standard ViT resolutions (224) are often insufficient for "sparse" feature detection. Increasing resolution allows the **Grid Attention** mechanism to attend to finer spatial regions across the global context.
    *   **Head:** We apply the same **Multi-Level GeM Pooling** strategy to the hierarchical outputs of the MaxViT backbone.

### Data
1.  **Preprocessing:**
    *   **EfficientNetV2 Pipeline:** Resize images to **512x512**.
    *   **MaxViT Pipeline:** Resize images to **384x384**.
2.  **Augmentation:**
    *   We employ a **Strong Geometric Augmentation** strategy (`ShiftScaleRotate`, `RandomFlip`) with high intensity to force the learning of invariant features **Cite solution_lesson_node_00003**.
    *   **Strict Exclusions:**
        *   **No Spatial Occlusion:** We exclude Cutout/CoarseDropout to prevent removing small disease indicators **Cite solution_lesson_node_00004**.
        *   **No Photometric Augmentation:** We exclude Brightness/Contrast shifts to preserve critical color signals **Cite solution_lesson_node_00014**.

### Training
1.  **Structural Innovation (Stability):** We implement **Model Exponential Moving Average (EMA)**.
    *   **Rationale:** Previous lessons indicated that high-capacity models (like Swin/MaxViT-Small) exhibit high variance and instability on this small dataset **Cite solution_lesson_node_00016**. Instead of relying on a single "best" checkpoint (which is sensitive to the noise of Early Stopping **Cite solution_lesson_node_00006**), we maintain a moving average of the model weights during training. This effectively smooths the optimization trajectory, finding a flatter, more robust minimum.
2.  **Loss Function:** We use **Cross-Entropy Loss with Inverse Frequency Class Weights** to optimize for the macro-averaged metric **Cite solution_lesson_node_00001**.
3.  **Optimization:**
    *   **Optimizer:** AdamW with **Cosine Annealing**.
    *   **Schedule:** We train for **25 epochs** with a **Relaxed Patience of 10 epochs**. This allows the EMA model to converge fully without premature termination during the high-variance phase **Cite solution_lesson_node_00008**.
    *   **Precision:** We use **Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP)** to accommodate the higher resolutions (512/384) within the GPU memory budget **Cite solution_lesson_node_00019**.

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference Weights:** We generate predictions using the **EMA weights**, not the final training weights, to ensure maximum generalization.
2.  **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):** We generate predictions for the **Original** image and a **Horizontal Flip**. We explicitly **exclude Vertical Flips and Transpose** to respect the natural gravity priors of the leaf images **Cite solution_lesson_node_00012**.
3.  **Ensemble:** The final submission is the unweighted average of the probability vectors from all 10 trained models (2 architectures $\times$ 5 folds).
