# Optimized Strategy: U-Net with ConvNeXt-Small, Statistical Alignment, and TTA

### Model
I propose upgrading the model architecture to a **U-Net with a ConvNeXt-Small backbone**.
*   **Structural Innovation (Backbone Scaling):** Previous experiments confirmed that modern, isotropic backbones (ConvNeXt-Tiny) significantly outperform traditional ones (EfficientNet) due to their larger effective receptive fields, which are essential for capturing long, linear contrail structures (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00014`). Furthermore, it was established that the encoder's representation power is the primary driver of performance, outweighing decoder complexity (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00018`). Therefore, I will scale from ConvNeXt-Tiny to **ConvNeXt-Small**. This increases the depth of the critical third stage (from 9 to 27 blocks) and the channel width, providing the necessary capacity to resolve fine-grained spatial details that the Tiny variant misses.
*   **Decoder:** A standard symmetric U-Net decoder will be employed, utilizing skip connections from the ConvNeXt encoder stages.
*   **Input Adaptation:** The first projection layer of the backbone will be structurally modified to accept **6 input channels**, enabling the direct ingestion of the physics-informed tensor (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00001`).

### Data
I will strictly implement the feature engineering and normalization pipeline that yielded the current state-of-the-art results:
*   **Physics-Informed Input:** The input will be a **6-channel tensor** consisting of the "Ash" false-color composite (Bands 11, 13, 14, 15) and the pixel-wise temporal difference ($Ash_t - Ash_{t-1}$). This explicitly encodes the physical properties and temporal dynamics of the contrails (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00001`).
*   **Statistical Alignment (Crucial):** Unlike early iterations, I will apply **ImageNet-style normalization** (subtracting mean, dividing by standard deviation) to the 6-channel input. Previous lessons demonstrated that matching the input statistics to the pretrained backbone's internal distribution is critical for maximizing transfer learning performance (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00020`).
*   **Augmentation:** To prevent the higher-capacity ConvNeXt-Small backbone from overfitting, I will apply strong geometric augmentations (ShiftScaleRotate, Horizontal/Vertical Flips) (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00002`).

### Training
The training protocol is designed to support the heavier backbone without falling into the optimization traps identified in previous failures:
*   **Optimization Budget:** I will train for **40 epochs** on the **full dataset**. I will explicitly manage the batch size (using Gradient Accumulation if necessary) to ensuring the total number of gradient updates is not reduced, avoiding the under-optimization observed when scaling up models previously (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00011`, `solution_lesson_node_00012`).
*   **Loss Function:** A **Hybrid Dice Loss + Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) Loss** will be used to prevent the model from optimizing for the background class (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00010`).
*   **Precision:** I will use **Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP)** to reduce memory footprint and accelerate training, which is essential for training the larger "Small" backbone within the time limit (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00020`).
*   **Stabilization:**
    *   **Scheduler:** Cosine Annealing with `T_max=40` (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00009`).
    *   **Online Top-K Checkpointing:** I will maintain a priority queue of the **Top-5** checkpoints to manage disk usage (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00006`).
    *   **Convergence-Aware Averaging:** The final model will be an average of the Top-5 checkpoints, strictly restricted to the latter half of training (e.g., Epoch > 20) to ensure stability (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00007`).

### Evaluation
*   **Test Time Augmentation (TTA):** The inference pipeline will include **Test Time Augmentation**. The model will predict on the original image and geometric transformations (flips), and the results will be averaged. This provides a reliable, zero-training performance boost by marginalizing over prediction variance (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00016`).
