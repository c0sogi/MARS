Based on the comprehensive analysis of previous experiments and the specific lessons learned, I propose the **Full-Resolution Dilated U-Net with Group Normalization**.

This solution represents a structural evolution that synthesizes the strengths of the sequential dilated backbone (Lesson 00015) with the feature aggregation capabilities of U-Net architectures, while explicitly eliminating the downsampling operations that caused previous U-Net attempts to fail (Lesson 00008). It addresses the integration of local texture and global context via iterative refinement rather than the unstable parallel branches (Lesson 00015) or unstructured hypercolumns (Idea 7).

### Model: Full-Resolution Dilated U-Net (FRDU-Net)

The architecture is a U-shaped fully convolutional network that operates entirely at the original spatial resolution. It replaces the traditional downsampling/upsampling path with a **Dilated Encoder** and an **Iterative Aggregation Decoder**.

1.  **Learnable 2.5D Projection (Input Stage):**
    The network accepts the full 3D volume `(Batch, 65, H, W)` and initializes with a learnable `1x1` convolution to compress the depth dimension into a compact feature space (e.g., 32 channels). This retains the highly effective "soft" slice selection mechanism **Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**.

2.  **Dilated Encoder (Sequential Backbone):**
    Instead of pooling, the encoder increases the receptive field using **Sequential Dilated Residual Blocks**.
    *   **Structure:** A series of blocks with hierarchically increasing dilation rates (e.g., $r=1, 2, 4, 8, 16$).
    *   **Feature Preservation:** Crucially, the output of *each* dilation stage is preserved (saved) for the decoder. This respects the lesson that sequential hierarchy is superior for feature extraction **Cite solution_lesson_node_00015** while maintaining full resolution to capture pixel-thin ink **Cite solution_lesson_node_00008**.
    *   **Constraint:** We limit the depth to ~5-6 stages to prevent the overfitting observed in deeper models **Cite solution_lesson_node_00027**.

3.  **Iterative Aggregation Decoder:**
    Instead of a massive final concatenation (Hypercolumn), the network employs a structured "backward" path.
    *   **Mechanism:** Starting from the deepest feature map (e.g., $r=16$), the decoder iteratively fuses it with the stored feature map from the previous stage (e.g., $r=8$).
    *   **Fusion Block:** `Output_k = Conv(Concat(Feature_k, Output_{k+1}))`.
    *   **Rationale:** This allows the model to selectively refine global shape information (high dilation) with local texture details (low dilation) step-by-step, providing a more structured and non-linear combination than simple dense connectivity.

4.  **Group Normalization:**
    All normalization layers are **Group Normalization (GN)**. This ensures training stability and allows us to maintain the memory-intensive "Full Resolution" feature maps without being destabilized by the small batch sizes that might be required **Cite solution_lesson_node_00025**.

### Data: Global Consistency and Deterministic Validation

1.  **Preprocessing:**
    Input volumes are Z-score normalized using **Global Statistics** (mean/std) calculated from the training set. We explicitly reject instance normalization to preserve the physical density signal of the ink **Cite solution_lesson_node_00028**.

2.  **Patch Size & Augmentation:**
    *   **Large Context:** Training will strictly use **large patches (256x256)**. We will not sacrifice spatial context for throughput **Cite solution_lesson_node_00019**.
    *   **Geometric Augmentation:** Random horizontal/vertical flips and discrete 90-degree rotations will be applied to force rotational invariance **Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**.

3.  **Deterministic Validation:**
    Validation will be performed on a **fixed, deterministic grid** of tiles covering the validation fragment. This eliminates metric volatility and ensures that model selection is based on genuine generalization **Cite solution_lesson_node_00010**.

### Training: Balanced Optimization and Regularized Sampling

1.  **Loss Function:**
    The model will be trained using a **Balanced Loss** (`BCE + Dice`). We avoid skewing the loss weights (e.g., Tversky with high alpha) during training, as this degrades the quality of the learned probability landscape **Cite solution_lesson_node_00011**.

2.  **Sampling Density (Regularization):**
    We will explicitly **limit the number of patches sampled per epoch** (e.g., to 12,000). Previous experiments showed that increasing sampling density on a single-fragment dataset leads to intra-domain overfitting (memorizing the specific fiber structure of the training fragment). Limiting the "views" per epoch acts as a necessary regularizer **Cite solution_lesson_node_00034**.

3.  **Optimizer:**
    Use AdamW with weight decay to further prevent overfitting.

### Evaluation: Precision-Oriented Inference

1.  **Tiled Inference with TTA:**
    Predictions will be generated using overlapping tiles. **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA)** (averaging predictions across 4 rotations and 2 flips) will be applied to suppress false positives caused by specific fiber orientations **Cite solution_lesson_node_00033**.

2.  **Dynamic Threshold Tuning:**
    The optimal binarization threshold will be explicitly searched for on the validation set to maximize the F0.5 score. This effectively adapts the balanced model output to the competition's precision-weighted metric without biasing the training process **Cite solution_lesson_node_00033**.
