# Proposed Strategy: ConvNeXt-Tiny with High-Resolution Training

## Model
We propose a structural evolution from the MobileNet and EfficientNet architectures to the **ConvNeXt-Tiny** backbone. 
- **Architecture**: Unlike the Inverted Residual blocks of MobileNet (Idea 1) or Fused-MBConv blocks of EfficientNetV2 (Idea 2), ConvNeXt modernizes the standard Convolutional Network with design choices inspired by Vision Transformers (e.g., large **7x7 kernels**, Layer Normalization, and GELU activations). This "structural innovation" allows the model to capture both the global shape and the fine-grained local textures (like leaf venation) crucial for plant identification, while maintaining a parameter count (~28M) comparable to EfficientNetV2-Small.
- **Classification Head**: The model will project the 768-dimensional feature embedding to the 15,501 class logits using a single linear layer. Notably, the smaller embedding dimension of ConvNeXt (768 vs. 1280 in EfficientNetV2-S) significantly reduces the parameter count of the classification head (approx. 12M vs. 20M parameters), allocating more capacity to the feature extractor itself.

## Data
We will upgrade the data pipeline to prioritize resolution and regularization, addressing the fine-grained nature of the task.
- **Resolution**: We will increase the input resolution to **384x384** pixels. Previous ideas used 224x224 or 260x260, but distinguishing between 15,501 plant species often requires resolving minute details that are lost at lower resolutions.
- **Augmentation**: We will rigorously apply **Strong Data Augmentation** (specifically `RandomResizedCrop`, `ShiftScaleRotate`, and `ColorJitter`). **Cite solution_lesson_node_00004**. This strategy was proven to prevent overfitting and double the F1 score in previous experiments.
- **Regularization**: We will implement **Mixup** and **CutMix** augmentations. Given the increased capacity of the ConvNeXt backbone and the high number of classes, these techniques are essential to smooth the decision boundaries and improve generalization on tail classes.

## Training
The training recipe synthesizes the successful elements of previous lessons while adapting to the new architecture.
- **Loss Function**: We will use **Cross-Entropy Loss** with **Label Smoothing (0.1)**. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00004**. As established in **Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**, standard cross-entropy is sufficient for this high-cardinality distribution, and label smoothing helps manage the confidence of predictions.
- **Optimizer**: We will utilize **AdamW** combined with the **OneCycleLR** scheduler. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**. This combination ensures rapid convergence, which is critical given the larger image size.
- **Duration**: We will extend the training duration to **15-20 epochs**. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00004**. The lessons demonstrated that short runs (1 epoch) are insufficient for high-cardinality tasks, and performance scales significantly with longer training.
- **Precision**: We will use **Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP)** to manage the memory footprint of the 384x384 inputs and speed up the 7x7 convolutions.

## Evaluation
- **Inference**: Predictions will be generated at **384x384** resolution.
- **Test Time Augmentation (TTA)**: To further maximize the Macro F1 score, we will employ a simple TTA strategy by averaging the predictions of the original image and its **horizontal flip**. This reduces variance for hard-to-classify examples without incurring the cost of multi-crop evaluation.
