Based on the comprehensive analysis of the previous experiments and lessons learned, I propose the following optimized strategy. This solution addresses the critical **"High Dimension, Low Sample Size" (HDLSS)** instability identified in Lesson 00033 by structurally altering the data topology during training, while preserving the high-performance feature extraction and fusion components validated in Lessons 00021 and 00039.

# Proposed Solution: View-Expanded Manifold Stabilization for Linear Discriminant Analysis

## Model
The architecture retains the successful **Dual-Stream Early Fusion** design but fundamentally changes how the linear classifier interacts with the data manifold.
1.  **Feature Extraction**:
    *   **Global Geometry Stream**: **DINOv2 (ViT-Large)** to capture self-supervised geometric priors (Cite solution_lesson_node_00021).
    *   **Local Texture Stream**: **ConvNeXt Large** to capture high-frequency margin details (Cite solution_lesson_node_00019).
2.  **Early Fusion**: The embeddings from both backbones are concatenated with the **Quantile-Transformed** tabular features (Cite solution_lesson_node_00039).
3.  **Dimensionality Reduction**: **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)** is applied to the fused vector, retaining **99% of the variance** to preserve discriminative tail signals (Cite solution_lesson_node_00019).
4.  **Classifier**: A single **Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA)** model with **Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage**. We explicitly reject complex ensembles (bagging/boosting) in favor of this stable, low-variance classifier (Cite solution_lesson_node_00042, solution_lesson_node_00028).

## Data
The core innovation lies in the **asymmetric processing** of training and test data to stabilize covariance estimation:
1.  **Training Data (View Expansion)**: Instead of averaging the 4 rotated views (0°, 90°, 180°, 270°) into a single vector (as done in previous ideas), we will treat them as **distinct training samples**.
    *   **Visual**: All 4 views are kept separate.
    *   **Tabular**: The corresponding tabular feature vector is replicated 4 times.
    *   **Impact**: This artificially quadruples the sample size ($N \approx 2800$ instead of $700$), providing the LDA classifier with a dense "cloud" of points per class. This directly stabilizes the covariance matrix estimation and prevents the "brittle boundary" overfitting observed in single-split/centroid-based training (Cite solution_lesson_node_00033).
2.  **Test Data (Centroid Inference)**: For evaluation, we strictly enforce rotation invariance. We generate the 4 views for each test image, extract features, and compute the **element-wise average** to produce a single robust **centroid** vector (Cite solution_lesson_node_00017).

## Training
1.  **Preprocessing**:
    *   Resize images to 518x518 (DINOv2) and 384x384 (ConvNeXt).
    *   Generate the **Expanded Training Set** (4x size) and the **Centroid Validation Set** (1x size).
2.  **Pipeline Fitting**:
    *   **Step 1**: Fit the **Quantile Transformer** and **PCA** on the **Expanded Training Set**. This ensures the projection subspace captures the full variance of the leaf orientation manifold.
    *   **Step 2**: Train the **LDA model** on the **PCA-projected Expanded Training Set**. By learning from the expanded "clouds," the LDA decision boundary becomes robust to orientation-induced variance and is less likely to overfit to specific artifacts of a single view or average.
3.  **Validation**: Use **Stratified K-Fold Cross-Validation** (K=10). In each fold, train on the expanded samples of the training indices and validate on the centroid samples of the validation indices. This ensures the metric reflects the true inference performance.

## Evaluation
1.  **Inference**: For each sample in the test set:
    *   Generate 4 rotated views and extract raw embeddings from DINOv2 and ConvNeXt.
    *   Compute the **element-wise average** of these embeddings to form the sample centroid.
    *   Concatenate with the transformed tabular features.
2.  **Prediction**:
    *   Project the centroid vector using the PCA model fitted on the expanded training data.
    *   Predict probabilities using the LDA model fitted on the expanded training data.
    *   This strategy combines the **statistical stability** of training on a large, noisy dataset with the **precision** of inferring on a noise-reduced signal.
3.  **Formatting**: Apply probability clipping ($[10^{-15}, 1-10^{-15}]$) and format the submission CSV.
