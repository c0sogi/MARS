Based on the lessons learned from the previous experiments, I propose a **Dual-Stream Hybrid Architecture** that fuses the strengths of raw waveform modeling (temporal precision) and spectrogram analysis (spectral dynamics) while explicitly correcting the spatial data corruption observed in earlier iterations.

# Model
The proposed solution is a multi-modal neural network consisting of two parallel processing streams that converge at a final fusion layer:

1.  **Stream A: Raw Waveform Encoder (Temporal Stream)**
    *   **Input**: Raw EEG signals (downsampled to 50Hz) with shape `(Time, 19)`.
    *   **Architecture**: This stream retains the successful **1D-CNN + Bi-directional GRU** architecture from the "Current Best" solution. The 1D-CNN extracts local morphological features, while the GRU captures long-range temporal dependencies.
    *   **Rationale**: This component is preserved because it was empirically shown to outperform the flawed image-based approach by maintaining the integrity of the 19 individual channels Cite {solution_lesson_node_00006}.

2.  **Stream B: Multi-Channel Spectrogram Encoder (Spectral Stream)**
    *   **Input**: Log-Mel Spectrograms with shape `(Frequency, Time, 19)`.
    *   **Architecture**: A **2D Convolutional Neural Network (EfficientNet-B0)**. Crucially, instead of stacking spectrograms vertically (which caused destructive resizing), the 19 EEG channels are stacked along the **depth/channel axis** of the input tensor. The first convolutional layer of the EfficientNet is modified to accept 19 input channels instead of 3.
    *   **Rationale**: This stream addresses the lesson that Time-Frequency representations are necessary to capture spectral dynamics Cite {solution_lesson_node_00001}. By using depth-wise stacking, we ensure that the model processes the full resolution of each channel's spectrogram without the interpolation artifacts that occurred when stacking them vertically Cite {solution_lesson_node_00006}.

3.  **Fusion Head**:
    *   The global average pooled outputs from Stream A and Stream B are concatenated.
    *   A final Dense classification block projects the fused features to the 6 class probabilities.

# Data
*   **Source**: Use `train_eegs` to extract the 50-second annotated windows.
*   **Preprocessing Stream A (Raw)**:
    *   Downsample the 50s EEG signal from 200Hz to 50Hz (2500 time steps).
    *   Apply channel-wise Z-score normalization.
    *   Replace NaNs with 0.
*   **Preprocessing Stream B (Spectrogram)**:
    *   Compute Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) on the *original* 200Hz signal (before downsampling) to capture high-frequency content.
    *   Convert to Log-Mel Spectrograms (e.g., 64 Mel bins).
    *   Stack the 19 channels to form a `(64, Time, 19)` tensor.
    *   Apply Z-score normalization.

# Training
*   **Loss Function**: **Kullback-Leibler (KL) Divergence** to align with the soft probability targets.
*   **Regularization**:
    *   **SpecAugment**: Apply Frequency and Time masking specifically to the input of Stream B (Spectrograms) to prevent noise memorization Cite {solution_lesson_node_00001}.
    *   **Dropout**: Apply Dropout before the final classification layer.
*   **Optimizer**: AdamW with a Cosine Annealing scheduler.

# Evaluation
*   **Inference**: Iterate through `test.csv`.
*   **Pipeline**: Load the 50-second test EEGs. Generate both the raw tensor (Stream A input) and the multi-channel spectrogram tensor (Stream B input) using the training preprocessing parameters.
*   **Prediction**: Pass both tensors through the Hybrid model to obtain the final probability distribution.
