Based on the accumulated lessons, particularly the failure of broadband multi-resolution approaches (Idea 10) due to noise and the success of band-limited spectral features (Idea 8), I propose the **Physiologically-Aligned Multi-Resolution Network with Temporal Attention**.

This solution introduces a **Structural Innovation** by replacing the standard "one-size-fits-all" spectrogram generation with a **Physiologically-Aligned Feature Pyramid**. Instead of generating redundant spectrograms, we explicitly decompose the EEG signal into biologically distinct frequency bands (Delta/Theta vs. Alpha/Beta) and process them with time-window resolutions matched to their physical properties (e.g., long windows for slow waves, short windows for fast spikes). Furthermore, it replaces the standard Global Average Pooling with **Temporal Attention Pooling** to resolve the label offset ambiguity.

# Model

The architecture is a dual-stream Convolutional Neural Network designed to optimize the Time-Frequency resolution trade-off while preserving spatial and optimization stability.

1.  **Stream A: Physiologically-Aligned Morphological Encoder (50s Window)**
    *   **Input:** A unified tensor containing **3 distinct Mel-Spectrograms** for each of the 19 EEG channels, stacked depth-wise. Crucially, these are **strictly band-limited** to <25Hz to maximize Signal-to-Noise Ratio **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00022}**.
        *   **View 1 (Slow/Rhythmic):** Frequency `0.5 - 8 Hz` (Delta/Theta). Generated with a **Long STFT Window** (e.g., 2.0s). This maximizes frequency resolution to distinguish Generalized Rhythmic Delta Activity (GRDA) from noise.
        *   **View 2 (Fast/Transient):** Frequency `8 - 25 Hz` (Alpha/Beta). Generated with a **Short STFT Window** (e.g., 0.1s). This maximizes temporal resolution to capture sharp spikes and seizure onsets.
        *   **View 3 (Broadband Context):** Frequency `0.5 - 25 Hz`. Generated with a **Medium STFT Window** (e.g., 1.0s) for general morphology.
    *   **Tensor Shape:** `(Frequency bins, Time steps, Depth = 19 channels × 3 views = 57)`.
    *   **Adapter:**
        *   **Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) Block:** Operates on the 57 input channels to dynamically weight the importance of specific bands and electrodes (e.g., prioritizing the "Fast" view during a spike event) **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00020}**.
        *   **Learnable Projection:** A `1x1 Convolution` projects the 57 channels to 3. This maintains a **Unified Input Tensor**, allowing the model to learn global synchrony across electrodes and bands immediately **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00021}**.
    *   **Backbone:** **EfficientNet-B2** (Pretrained on ImageNet). Using a pretrained 2D backbone ensures robust feature extraction and stable convergence **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00018}**.
    *   **Pooling Innovation (Temporal Attention):** Instead of Global Average Pooling (which destroys temporal localization), we apply **Temporal Attention Pooling**.
        *   The feature map $(C, F, T)$ is averaged across the frequency axis to $(C, T)$.
        *   A lightweight attention head computes a weight $\alpha_t$ for each time step.
        *   The final vector is the weighted sum $\sum \alpha_t x_t$. This allows the model to focus specifically on the labeled central 10 seconds or other salient events within the 50s clip.

2.  **Stream B: Long-Term Context Encoder (10m Window)**
    *   **Input:** The provided 10-minute Spectrograms (4 regions stacked depth-wise) to preserve spatial integrity **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00006}**.
    *   **Backbone:** **EfficientNet-B0** (Pretrained on ImageNet).
    *   **Role:** Provides orthogonal long-term context (e.g., background slowing) **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00015}**.

3.  **Fusion Head: Gated Context Mechanism**
    *   The context vector from Stream B modulates the detail vector from Stream A via element-wise multiplication with a sigmoid gate ($Detail \otimes \sigma(Context)$). This enforces the hierarchical dependency where long-term context interprets short-term morphology **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00020}**.

# Data

1.  **Stream A Preprocessing (Physiologically-Aligned Generation):**
    *   Load the 50-second raw EEG (`train_eegs`).
    *   **Multi-View STFT:** Compute three separate Log-Mel Spectrograms for each of the 19 channels using the specific frequency bands (0.5-8Hz, 8-25Hz, 0.5-25Hz) and window sizes defined above.
    *   **Resizing:** Resize the spatial dimensions of all three views to match (e.g., 128 Mel Bins × 500 Time Steps).
    *   **Stacking:** Stack all outputs along the depth axis to create the 57-channel tensor.
    *   **Normalization:** Normalize to ImageNet statistics.

2.  **Stream B Preprocessing:**
    *   Load 10-minute spectrograms (`train_spectrograms`), Log-transform, resize to `(256, 256)`, and stack the 4 anatomical regions along the depth axis.

3.  **Augmentation:**
    *   **SpecAugment:** Apply Frequency and Time masking to the inputs of both streams to prevent noise memorization **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00001}**.
    *   **MixUp:** Apply MixUp regularization to training batches.

# Training

*   **Loss Function:** **Kullback-Leibler (KL) Divergence**.
*   **Optimizer:** **AdamW** with a **Cosine Annealing** scheduler.
*   **Strategy:** Use a **single, consistent learning rate** (e.g., `3e-4`) for the entire network. Since both backbones (EfficientNet-B2 and B0) are **Pretrained**, we avoid the optimization instability associated with mixing scratch-trained and pretrained models **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00016}**.
*   **Epochs:** Train for ~10-12 epochs with Early Stopping based on Validation KL.

# Evaluation

1.  **Inference Pipeline:** Iterate through `test.csv`.
2.  **Process:**
    *   Load 50s EEG $\to$ Generate 3 Physiologically-Aligned Spectrograms $\to$ Stack $\to$ Stream A (with Temporal Attention).
    *   Load 10m Spectrogram $\to$ Stream B.
    *   Apply Gated Fusion.
3.  **Prediction:** The final Softmax output provides the probability distribution for the 6 classes.
