Based on the dataset characteristics and the constraints provided, here is a proposal for a highly efficient baseline approach that is distinct from the previously suggested architectures.

### Model: Bi-directional GRU with Attention Pooling
This architecture utilizes Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), a fundamental class of models for time-series data that is distinct from the Convolutional (Models 2, 3, 4), Tree-based (Model 1), and Graph-based (Model 5) approaches previously listed.

*   **Input Layer**: Accepts the raw EEG signal with shape `(Time Steps, Channels)`.
*   **Dimensionality Reduction**: A 1x1 Convolutional layer (or Time-Distributed Dense layer) projects the 19 EEG channels down to a smaller dimension (e.g., 12) to reduce noise and computational overhead before temporal processing.
*   **Temporal Processing**: A **Bi-directional Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU)** with 2 stacked layers. GRUs are selected over LSTMs for their simpler architecture and faster training time while maintaining comparable performance for capturing temporal dependencies.
*   **Attention Pooling**: Instead of simple global average pooling, a lightweight Attention mechanism computes a weighted sum of the GRU outputs across the time dimension. This allows the model to dynamically focus on the specific time segments where the harmful activity (e.g., a seizure) occurs, which is crucial since the labels focus on the central 10 seconds of the 50-second clip.
*   **Output Head**: A final Dense layer maps the pooled representation to the 6 class probabilities, normalized via a Softmax activation.

### Data
*   **Selection**: Use the `train_eegs` data. The `train_spectrograms` are ignored to maintain simplicity and focus on fine-grained temporal dynamics.
*   **Slicing**: For the training set, use the `eeg_label_offset_seconds` metadata to extract the exact 50-second window (10,000 samples at 200 Hz) corresponding to the annotated subsample from the consolidated parquet files.
*   **Preprocessing**:
    1.  **Downsampling**: Resample the signal from 200 Hz to 50 Hz. This reduces the sequence length from 10,000 to 2,500 steps, significantly speeding up RNN training without losing the frequency bands relevant for these seizure types (mostly < 25 Hz).
    2.  **Cleaning**: Replace `NaN` values with 0.0 (assuming centered data).
    3.  **Normalization**: Apply standard scaling (Z-score normalization) independently to each channel to ensure zero mean and unit variance.

### Training
*   **Loss Function**: **Kullback-Leibler (KL) Divergence**. This is the competition metric and is appropriate for the soft probability targets provided in the dataset.
*   **Optimizer**: **AdamW** with a default learning rate (e.g., 1e-3) and weight decay to prevent overfitting.
*   **Strategy**: Use a `ReduceLROnPlateau` scheduler to lower the learning rate if validation loss stagnates. Train for a fixed number of epochs (e.g., 10-15) with Early Stopping based on validation KL Divergence.

### Evaluation
*   **Inference**: Iterate through the `test.csv` metadata.
*   **Loading**: Load the corresponding `test_eegs` parquet file. Since test files are exactly 50 seconds long, no slicing is required.
*   **Pipeline**: Apply the same downsampling (200Hz -> 50Hz), NaN handling, and normalization steps used in training.
*   **Prediction**: Pass the processed tensor through the trained GRU model to obtain the probability distribution for the 6 classes. Ensure the output probabilities sum to one.
