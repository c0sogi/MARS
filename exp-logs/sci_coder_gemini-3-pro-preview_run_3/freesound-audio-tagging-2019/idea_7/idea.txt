Based on the analysis of the previous attempts and the "Lessons" derived from them, I propose the following optimized strategy. This solution structurally evolves the architecture by transitioning to a **ConvNeXt** backbone to capture long-range dependencies without the instability of Transformers, while exploiting the proven **Dual-Stream Pooling** and **Single-Channel** strategies.

### Model
I propose a **ConvNeXt-Nano Audio Tagger** equipped with a **Dual-Stream Pooling** head and **Multi-Sample Dropout**.

*   **Backbone (Structural Innovation):** I will replace the EfficientNet architecture with **ConvNeXt-Nano**.
    *   **Rationale:** Previous attempts showed that EfficientNet (3x3 kernels) provides stable convergence but limited receptive fields, while Transformers (Idea 3) failed due to optimization instability on this mid-sized dataset (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010). ConvNeXt bridges this gap by incorporating "Transformer-like" architectural priors—specifically **large 7x7 kernels** and Layer Normalization—into a pure CNN. This allows the model to capture longer-range temporal dependencies and rhythm (crucial for distinguishing sound events) while maintaining the training stability and data efficiency of a CNN.
    *   **Input Adaptation:** The first convolutional layer will be modified to accept **Single-Channel Input** by summing the pretrained RGB weights. This strictly avoids the performance degradation caused by engineering synthetic 3-channel (Mel/Delta/Delta) inputs (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015).

*   **Pooling Head (Strategic Retention):** I will implement the **Dual-Stream Pooling** mechanism to aggregate the feature map $X \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times F \times T}$:
    1.  **Stream A (Context):** **Non-Linear Attention Pooling**. The feature map is flattened to a sequence ($F \times T$) and processed by a scoring network (`Linear -> Tanh -> Linear -> Softmax`). This preserves the full spectral resolution required to disentangle polyphonic sounds (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016) and uses non-linearity to effectively distinguish signal from noise (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014).
    2.  **Stream B (Salience):** **Global Max Pooling**. This computes the maximum activation across time and frequency, ensuring that sparse, high-intensity events (like gunshots) are not diluted by the averaging effect of attention (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
    *   **Fusion:** The outputs of both streams are concatenated to form the final embedding (Cite solution_lesson_node_00019).

*   **Classification Head:** I will incorporate **Multi-Sample Dropout**. Instead of a single dropout layer, the embedding will pass through 5 parallel dropout masks followed by the same linear classifier, with the logits averaged during training. This technique accelerates convergence and improves generalization, directly addressing the need to maximize performance within limited training iterations (Cite solution_lesson_node_00013).

### Data
*   **Preprocessing:** Audio will be converted to **Log-Mel Spectrograms** (128 Mel bins).
*   **Normalization:** I will apply **Per-Instance Normalization** to the spectrograms (normalizing each sample to zero mean and unit variance) to handle varying recording gains and accelerate convergence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00019).
*   **Sampling Rate:** I will strictly use a **32 kHz sampling rate**. This reduces input dimensionality to maximize iteration velocity while retaining sufficient frequency content (Cite solution_lesson_node_00013).
*   **Duration Strategy:** All inputs will be fixed to **30 seconds**. Shorter clips will be padded with silence, and longer clips will be truncated. This ensures the model views the full context, preventing the label noise introduced by naive cropping of weakly labeled data (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
*   **Augmentation:** I will apply **Mixup** ($\alpha=0.4$) and **SpecAugment** (Time/Frequency Masking). Mixup is critical for regularizing the single-channel input and preventing overfitting (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** **Binary Cross-Entropy with Logits Loss (BCEWithLogitsLoss)**.
*   **Optimizer & Scheduler:** **AdamW** optimizer paired with a **OneCycleLR** scheduler to facilitate super-convergence.
*   **Metric-Driven Early Stopping:** The training loop will monitor **Validation LWLRAP** (Label-Weighted Label-Ranking Average Precision) to trigger early stopping. I will strictly decouple this from Validation Loss, as the loss often rises due to calibration shifts even while ranking performance improves (Cite solution_lesson_node_00011).
*   **State Preservation:** When saving the best model checkpoint, I will explicitly use `copy.deepcopy()` to ensure the weights corresponding to the peak metric are preserved, preventing the degradation observed in previous runs (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).

### Evaluation
*   **Inference:** Predictions will be generated using the full 30-second spectrograms of the test files. No windowing or test-time cropping will be applied, ensuring the inference pipeline matches the training signal integrity.
