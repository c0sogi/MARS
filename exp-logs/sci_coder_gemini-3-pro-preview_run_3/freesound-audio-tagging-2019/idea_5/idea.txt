### Model
I propose a **Dual-Stream Hybrid Pooling Network** built upon an **EfficientNet-B0** backbone. This architecture is designed to simultaneously capture sparse, high-intensity events (e.g., gunshots) and complex, polyphonic soundscapes (e.g., street scenes) without the instability of Transformers or the data inefficiency of heavy backbones.

*   **Backbone:** I will use **EfficientNet-B0** initialized with ImageNet weights. This choice prioritizes iteration velocity and convergence on the mid-sized dataset over theoretical capacity, avoiding the underfitting observed with EfficientNet-B2 (Cite solution_lesson_node_00013).
*   **Input Adaptation:** I will modify the first convolutional layer to accept **Single-Channel Input** by summing the pretrained RGB weights. I will strictly avoid the 3-channel "Mel-Delta-Delta" engineering used in Idea 4, as empirical evidence showed that forcing derived features into RGB channels degrades transfer learning performance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015).
*   **Dual-Stream Pooling Head (Structural Innovation):** I will implement a parallel pooling block that processes the feature map $X \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times F \times T}$ through two distinct streams before concatenation:
    1.  **Stream A: Non-Linear Attention Pooling:** This stream flattens the spatial-spectral dimensions ($F \times T$) into a sequence and applies a learnable attention mechanism. Crucially, I will use a **Non-Linear Scoring Network** (`Linear -> Tanh -> Linear -> Softmax`) rather than a simple linear projection. This restores the expressiveness required to distinguish relevant signals from noise (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014) while preserving the full spectral resolution to handle polyphony (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016).
    2.  **Stream B: Global Max Pooling:** This stream computes the maximum activation across time and frequency. This explicitly preserves the magnitude of sparse, transient events that might be "washed out" by the weighted averaging of the attention mechanism (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
    *   **Fusion:** The outputs of Stream A and Stream B will be concatenated to form a rich embedding vector that encodes both the *presence* of peak events and the *distribution* of continuous sounds.

### Data
*   **Preprocessing:** Audio will be converted to **Log-Mel Spectrograms** (128 Mel bins).
*   **Sampling Rate:** I will strictly use a **32 kHz sampling rate**. This reduces input dimensionality to maximize training speed (Cite solution_lesson_node_00013) while retaining sufficient frequency content for environmental sounds.
*   **Duration Strategy:** All inputs will be fixed to **30 seconds**. Shorter clips will be padded with silence, and longer clips will be truncated. This ensures the model views the full context, preventing the massive label noise introduced by random cropping of weakly labeled data (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
*   **Augmentation:** I will apply **SpecAugment** (Time/Frequency Masking) and **Mixup** (alpha=0.4). Mixup is critical for regularizing the single-channel input and preventing the overfitting observed when it was omitted in Idea 4 (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** The model will be trained using **Binary Cross-Entropy with Logits Loss (BCEWithLogitsLoss)**.
*   **Optimizer:** I will use **AdamW** with a **OneCycleLR** scheduler to ensure efficient super-convergence.
*   **Metric-Driven Early Stopping:** The training loop will monitor **Validation LWLRAP** to trigger early stopping. I will strictly decouple this from Validation Loss, as the loss often rises due to calibration shifts even while ranking performance improves (Cite solution_lesson_node_00011).
*   **State Preservation:** I will explicitly use `copy.deepcopy()` when saving the best model checkpoint to ensure the weights corresponding to the peak metric are preserved, preventing the degradation observed in previous runs (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).

### Evaluation
*   **Inference:** Predictions will be generated using the full 30-second spectrograms of the test files. No windowing or test-time cropping will be applied, ensuring the inference pipeline matches the training signal integrity.
