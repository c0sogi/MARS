Based on the analysis of the task and the failure of the scalar aggregation approach, I propose a **Spatiotemporal Graph Neural Network (GNN) with Global Eigen-Context**. This approach evolves beyond the independent processing of Idea 1 (PointNet) and the manual feature engineering of Idea 2/3 by explicitly modeling the relationships between pulses while retaining the critical geometric insights derived from the lessons.

# Proposed Solution: Spatiotemporal Graph Neural Network with Global Eigen-Context

### Model
The architecture is a **Graph Neural Network (GNN)** designed to capture the local causal structure of the neutrino event, augmented with a global geometric shortcut.
1.  **Graph Construction:** The event is treated as a graph where nodes are pulses. For each pulse, edges are drawn to its $k$ nearest neighbors in $(x, y, z)$ space.
2.  **Vectorial Edge Features:** To address the loss of directional information identified in the previous attempts, edge features are explicitly calculated as the relative differences in space and time $(\Delta x, \Delta y, \Delta z, \Delta t)$ between connected pulses. This ensures the network processes **local vectors** rather than just scalar positions, preserving the trajectory information required for direction prediction (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
3.  **Message Passing:** A series of graph convolution layers (e.g., EdgeConv or GAT) aggregate features along these edges, allowing the model to "flow" information along the track of the particle.
4.  **Global Eigen-Context Injection:** While the GNN learns local structure, we explicitly retain the successful concept of **Spatiotemporal Eigen-Features** (SVD of the covariance matrix). The global eigenvalues and eigenvectors are pre-computed and concatenated with the GNN's global pooled output before the final regression head. This creates a "geometric skip connection," guaranteeing the model has access to the gross track axis and ensuring it does not suffer from the scalar aggregation blindness observed previously (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
5.  **Output Head:** An MLP regresses the 3D unit vector components $(n_x, n_y, n_z)$.

### Data
1.  **Geometry Merging:** Merge `sensor_geometry.csv` with the pulse data to obtain $(x, y, z, t, q)$.
2.  **Graph Preprocessing:**
    *   **Sampling:** To fit GPU memory and ensure batching, sample a fixed number of pulses (e.g., $N=128$ or $N=256$) per event, prioritizing those with high charge or those within a specific time window of the trigger.
    *   **Normalization:** Standardize coordinates and time. Log-transform charge.
    *   **Eigen-Feature Extraction:** For every event, compute the charge-weighted covariance matrix of pulse positions and extract the primary eigenvector and eigenvalues to serve as the "Global Eigen-Context" (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
3.  **Target Generation:** Convert `azimuth` and `zenith` to Cartesian unit vectors $(n_x, n_y, n_z)$.

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** **Cosine Similarity Loss**. Unlike MSE on vector components, this directly optimizes the angular alignment between the predicted vector and the ground truth, which is the exact objective of the competition.
*   **Optimizer:** Use **AdamW** with a cosine annealing learning rate schedule.
*   **Strategy:** Train on the GPU. Since GNNs are computationally more intensive than MLPs, monitor validation loss closely. Use the "Global Eigen-Context" features to stabilize early training, as they provide a rough directional guess immediately.

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** For each test event, sample pulses, construct the k-NN graph, and compute the global eigen-features.
2.  **Prediction:** Forward pass through the GNN to obtain the predicted vector $\vec{v}_{pred}$.
3.  **Post-Processing:** Normalize $\vec{v}_{pred}$ to unit length and convert to spherical coordinates (`azimuth`, `zenith`) for submission.
