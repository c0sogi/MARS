# Idea 6: Swin Transformer-based Cascade R-CNN

## Model
I propose a **Multi-Task Cascade R-CNN with a Swin Transformer Backbone**. This architecture represents a structural evolution from the Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) based approaches of previous ideas (ResNet in Idea 1, ResNeXt in Idea 5) to a **Hierarchical Vision Transformer** paradigm.

-   **Backbone (Structural Innovation)**: I will replace the CNN backbone with a **Swin Transformer (Base)**. Unlike standard CNNs that process images with fixed local receptive fields, the Swin Transformer utilizes shifted windows to compute self-attention. This allows the model to dynamically model long-range dependencies and global context—which is critical for identifying diffuse "Typical" vs. "Atypical" viral patterns—while maintaining the hierarchical structure necessary to detect small opacities.
-   **Neck**: A **Feature Pyramid Network (FPN)** will be attached to the hierarchical stages of the Swin Transformer to construct a multi-scale feature map, ensuring robust detection of opacities ranging from small focal consolidations to large ground-glass opacities.
-   **Detection Head**: I will retain the **Cascade R-CNN** head from Idea 5. This module consists of a sequence of detectors trained with increasing IoU thresholds (e.g., 0.5, 0.6, 0.7), which progressively refines bounding box localization and is superior to standard Faster R-CNN or YOLO heads for optimizing the mAP metric.
-   **Study Classification Head**: A dedicated classification branch will be attached to the deepest layer of the FPN. This branch will use Global Average Pooling followed by a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) to predict the four study-level categories, leveraging the global context captured by the Transformer backbone.

## Data
-   **Preprocessing (Strategic Retention)**: I will strictly implement **Letterbox Resizing** for all input images. Instead of warping images to a square, I will resize the longest dimension to the target size (e.g., 800 pixels) and pad the shorter dimension with zeros. This preserves the original aspect ratio of the radiographs, preventing the morphological distortion of lung opacities that was identified as a critical failure mode **Cite solution_lesson_node_00010**.
-   **Augmentation**: I will employ a strong augmentation pipeline including **RandomRotate90**, **ShiftScaleRotate**, and **Cutout**. I will explicitly exclude "Mosaic" augmentation (used in Idea 2/4) because the study-level classification relies on the global anatomical structure of the thorax, which Mosaic disrupts.

## Training
-   **Loss Function**: A multi-task objective will be minimized:
    -   **Cascade Detection Loss**: The sum of Cross-Entropy (classification) and Smooth L1 (regression) losses from all three cascade stages.
    -   **Study Classification Loss**: A **Focal Loss** will be applied to the study-level head to address the class imbalance between the frequent "Typical" appearance and the rarer "Atypical" and "Indeterminate" classes.
-   **Optimizer**: I will use **AdamW** instead of SGD. Transformers require AdamW to effectively handle the adaptive learning rates needed for the attention mechanism parameters.
-   **Schedule**: A linear warmup followed by a Cosine Annealing decay will be used to stabilize the training of the Transformer backbone.

## Evaluation
-   **Inference**: For each test image, the model will perform a single forward pass to generate bounding boxes and study-level class probabilities.
-   **Prediction Logic**:
    1.  **Study Level**: The label is determined by the class with the highest probability from the Study Classification Head.
    2.  **Image Level**:
        -   If the predicted study label is "Negative for Pneumonia", the image prediction is forced to `none 1 0 0 1 1`.
        -   Otherwise, the bounding boxes from the final stage of the Cascade R-CNN are processed via Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) and formatted as the "opacity" prediction string.
