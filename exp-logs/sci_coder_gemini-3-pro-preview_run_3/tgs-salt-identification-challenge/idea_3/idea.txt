Based on the empirical findings from the provided lessons, I propose the following optimized strategy. This solution evolves the architecture to address the persistent "empty mask" failure mode identified in the failure analysis while retaining the proven inference techniques.

### Model
**Architecture:** U-Net with ResNeXt-50 (32x4d) Encoder and scSE Attention.
*   **Backbone Evolution:** Building on the finding that increasing encoder capacity (ResNet18 to ResNet34) significantly improved convergence and metrics (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002), I will upgrade the encoder to **ResNeXt-50 (32x4d)**. This architecture uses grouped convolutions (cardinality) to capture more complex seismic textures than standard ResNets without a proportional increase in parameters.
*   **Decoder Strategy:** I will utilize a **U-Net** decoder. While Lesson 4 suggested that U-Net alone yielded negligible gains over LinkNet for this resolution (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004), the concatenative skip connections of U-Net are necessary to preserve the full feature depth required for the attention mechanism proposed below.
*   **Structural Innovation (Attention):** To directly address the strong negative correlation between performance and salt coverage (the "empty mask" problem) identified in the failure analysis (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001), I will integrate **Concurrent Spatial and Channel Squeeze & Excitation (scSE)** modules into the decoder blocks. These attention gates will explicitly recalibrate feature maps, helping the model suppress background noise in images containing no salt—a crucial capability that pure convolutional architectures struggled with in previous iterations.

### Data
**Preprocessing:**
*   **Depth Fusion:** I will continue to fuse the scalar depth variable (`z`) as a spatial channel in the input tensor, as this provides essential context for the subsurface structures.
*   **Padding:** Input images will be padded from 101x101 to **128x128** using reflection padding to ensure compatibility with the ResNeXt encoder's downsampling operations.
*   **Augmentation:** I will apply horizontal flips and random brightness/contrast adjustments during training.

### Training
**Loss Function & Strategy:**
*   **Lovász-Hinge Loss:** Previous iterations relied on BCE + Dice loss. To better optimize the competition metric (mAP at IoU thresholds), I will implement the **Lovász-Hinge Loss**. This loss is a differentiable surrogate for the Jaccard index and is specifically designed to handle class imbalance and the discrete nature of the IoU metric, making it more effective for optimizing the "all-or-nothing" scoring of empty masks.
*   **Procedure:** Training will occur in two stages:
    1.  **Warm-up:** Train with **BCE + Dice Loss** to establish a stable convergence.
    2.  **Fine-tuning:** Switch to **Lovász-Hinge Loss** for the final epochs to directly maximize the IoU score.

### Evaluation
**Inference Pipeline:**
*   **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):** I will strictly adhere to the finding that averaging predictions from original and horizontally flipped images improves robustness and metrics (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
*   **Threshold Optimization:** I will not use a hardcoded 0.5 threshold. Instead, I will perform a post-inference sweep on the validation set to identify the optimal probability threshold that maximizes the mAP, effectively filtering out low-confidence predictions in empty images (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
