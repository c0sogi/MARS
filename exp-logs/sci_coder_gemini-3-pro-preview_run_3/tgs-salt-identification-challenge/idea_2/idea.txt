Based on the empirical insights from the provided lessons, I propose the following optimized strategy. This approach focuses on increasing model capacity to capture complex seismic textures, integrating attention mechanisms to resolve the "empty mask" ambiguity, and aligning the training objective directly with the competition metric.

### Model
**Architecture:** U-Net with ResNeXt-50 (32x4d) Encoder and scSE Attention.
*   **Backbone Evolution:** Building on the finding that the deeper ResNet34 encoder significantly outperformed ResNet18 (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002), I will upgrade the encoder to **ResNeXt-50 (32x4d)**. This architecture introduces "cardinality" (split-transform-merge strategy), providing a more robust feature extraction capability for subtle seismic textures without the massive parameter cost of simply deepening the network.
*   **Decoder Strategy:** I will utilize a **U-Net** decoder structure. Although Lesson 4 indicated diminishing returns when comparing U-Net to LinkNet on this low-resolution data (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004), the concatenative skip connections of U-Net are essential for the proposed structural innovation below, as they preserve the full channel depth required for effective attention gating.
*   **Structural Innovation (Attention):** To address the high error rate in sparse/empty masks (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001), I will integrate **Concurrent Spatial and Channel Squeeze & Excitation (scSE)** modules into the decoder blocks. Unlike standard convolutions, scSE blocks explicitly recalibrate feature maps by emphasizing informative spatial locations (Spatial SE) and relevant feature channels (Channel SE). This attention mechanism acts as a learnable filter, helping the model to suppress background noise in "empty" images and focus sharply on small salt bodies.

### Data
**Preprocessing & Augmentation:**
*   **Depth Fusion:** I will retain the successful strategy of fusing the scalar depth variable (`z`) as a fourth spatial channel (or third, replacing one channel) in the input tensor.
*   **Resolution:** The input images will be padded from 101x101 to **128x128** using reflection padding to satisfy the divisibility requirements of the deeper ResNeXt encoder.
*   **Augmentation:** Standard geometric augmentations (horizontal flip) and intensity shifts will be applied.

### Training
**Optimization Strategy:**
*   **Loss Function:** The previous solutions relied on BCE + Dice Loss. To better handle the "all-or-nothing" nature of the IoU metric on empty masks (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001), I will implement the **Lovász-Hinge Loss**. This loss function serves as a differentiable surrogate for the Jaccard (IoU) index and is specifically designed to optimize the competition metric directly, rather than approximating it.
*   **Procedure:** Training will proceed in two stages:
    1.  **Warm-up:** Train with BCE + Dice Loss for initial convergence.
    2.  **Fine-tuning:** Switch to Lovász-Hinge Loss to directly maximize the IoU score and refine boundary decisions.

### Evaluation
**Inference Pipeline:**
*   **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):** I will explicitly retain the TTA strategy (averaging predictions of original and horizontally flipped images), as it was proven to boost robustness and metric performance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
*   **Threshold Optimization:** I will continue to treat the binarization threshold as a hyperparameter. A post-inference sweep will be performed on the validation set to identify the optimal probability cutoff (likely > 0.5) that maximizes the mAP, effectively filtering out low-confidence false positives in empty images (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
