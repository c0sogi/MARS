# Raw-Waveform Mel-Spectrogram Pipeline with EfficientNet and SpecAugment

### Model
The solution will transition from the ResNet-18 architecture to **EfficientNet-B0** (pretrained on ImageNet). EfficientNet is selected for its superior parameter efficiency and feature extraction capabilities, which are critical for maximizing generalization on this extremely small dataset (206 training samples).
- **Architecture:** The model will use the `timm` implementation of EfficientNet-B0. The first convolutional layer will be modified to accept 1-channel input (mono spectrograms) instead of the standard 3-channel RGB.
- **Pooling & Output:** The network will use global average pooling followed by a single fully connected layer with 19 output neurons (one per species) and Sigmoid activation.

### Data
Instead of using the provided pre-computed BMP spectrograms, the solution will process the **raw WAV files** (`essential_data/src_wavs`). This structural change allows for the generation of high-resolution **Log-Mel Spectrograms** on-the-fly, enabling the application of audio-specific augmentations that are impossible or less effective in the image domain.

**Preprocessing & Augmentation Pipeline:**
1.  **Spectrogram Generation:** Convert 10-second raw audio (16kHz) into Log-Mel Spectrograms (e.g., 128 Mel bins, FFT window ~32ms). This ensures we capture the temporal context effectively (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
2.  **Waveform Augmentation (Gain & Noise):** Before spectrogram conversion, apply random amplitude scaling (Gain) and add Gaussian noise to the waveform. This explicitly replicates the benefits of "Photometric Augmentations" (Brightness/Contrast) observed in previous experiments by physically simulating variations in recording volume and Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
3.  **SpecAugment:** Apply **Frequency Masking** and **Time Masking** to the spectrograms. This replaces generic image occlusions with domain-aware regularization, forcing the model to recognize birds even when specific frequencies or time segments are obscured.
4.  **Time Shifting:** Apply random cyclic rolling along the time axis (Horizontal Translation) to simulate temporal variability (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
5.  **Mixup:** Apply Mixup regularization (linear interpolation of spectrograms and labels) to smooth decision boundaries and prevent overfitting on the sparse data (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
6.  **Constraint:** Strictly avoid Horizontal Flipping, as this corresponds to time reversal and distorts the semantic meaning of bird calls (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).

### Training
The training process will focus on robustness through ensembling and advanced scheduling.
- **Cross-Validation:** Implement **Stratified 5-Fold Cross-Validation**. Given the small dataset size, a single validation split is prone to high variance. We will train 5 separate models (one for each fold) and ensemble them.
- **Loss Function:** Use **BCEWithLogitsLoss** (Binary Cross Entropy) suitable for multi-label classification.
- **Optimizer & Scheduler:** Use the **AdamW** optimizer combined with a **Cosine Annealing Warm Restarts** scheduler to efficiently navigate the loss landscape (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
- **Regularization:** The combination of Mixup, SpecAugment, and the lightweight EfficientNet backbone serves as the primary defense against overfitting.

### Evaluation
- **Ensemble Inference:** For the test set, generate predictions using all 5 trained models.
- **Aggregation:** Average the predicted probabilities across the 5 models for each recording and species. This averaging acts as a stabilizer, smoothing out individual model biases.
- **Submission:** Flatten the averaged probability matrix into the required `Id,Probability` format (where `Id = rec_id * 100 + species_id`).
