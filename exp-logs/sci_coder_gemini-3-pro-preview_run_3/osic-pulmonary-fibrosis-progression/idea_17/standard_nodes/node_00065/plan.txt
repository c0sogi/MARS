Based on the failure analysis of the previous solution, the model exhibits a significant correlation between prediction error and the `Base_FVC` (0.277) and `Time_Rel` (0.273) features. This indicates that the architecture is struggling to capture the dominant linear autoregressive trend ($FVC_{t} \approx FVC_{base} + \text{slope} \times t$), likely due to the non-linear processing of these features in the `clinical_mlp`.

To address this, I will implement the **Dual-Stream Point-Wise Residual (DSPRNet)** architecture described in **Lesson 00052**. This involves splitting the network into two streams:
1.  **Linear Stream**: A direct linear mapping of the dominant features (`Base_FVC` and `Time`) to the latent space. This preserves the strong linear signal required for the baseline and temporal decay (Cite **Lesson 00052**). I will over-parameterize this stream by projecting it to the embedding dimension (128) before fusion, as recommended in **Lesson 00060**, to avoid optimization bottlenecks.
2.  **Deep Stream**: A non-linear path that fuses image embeddings and tabular features to learn complex residuals (e.g., fibrosis patterns correcting the linear trend).

The outputs of these streams will be summed in the latent space before passing to the final head. This ensures that both the linear trend and the deep residuals contribute to both the mean prediction and the uncertainty estimate, satisfying **Lesson 00055**.

I will also increase the training epochs from 20 to 25 to ensure the new architecture has sufficient time to converge, as Lesson 00052 noted convergence around epoch 14 but 25 provides a safety margin.
