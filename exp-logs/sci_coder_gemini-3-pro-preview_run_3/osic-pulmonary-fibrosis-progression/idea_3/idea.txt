Based on the lessons learned from the previous experiments, I propose a new strategy called the **Content-Adaptive 2.5D Multi-View Network**. This approach aims to resolve the critical failure of the previous multi-slice attempt (Idea 2) by replacing geometric slicing with a robust, content-aware selection mechanism, while integrating the stability enhancements identified in the single-slice baseline (Idea 1).

### 1. Model
The architecture is a hybrid 2.5D CNN-MLP designed to capture volumetric lung heterogeneity without the computational cost of full 3D convolutions.

*   **Image Branch (Content-Adaptive 2.5D):**
    *   **Input:** The model processes **three distinct slices** per patient to capture the Apical (upper), Middle, and Basal (lower) lung regions.
    *   **Backbone:** A shared, pre-trained **EfficientNet-B0** extracts features from each slice. The backbone remains **frozen** to ensure rapid convergence and prevent overfitting on this small dataset.
    *   **Projection & Fusion:** The three feature vectors (1280-dim each) are concatenated (3840-dim). Crucially, this high-dimensional vector is immediately passed through a **Linear Projection Layer** (3840 $\to$ 128) before fusion. This prevents the image features from overwhelming the tabular signal **Cite solution_lesson_node_00008**.
*   **Tabular Branch:**
    *   A simple MLP processes the clinical metadata (`Age`, `Sex`, `SmokingStatus`, `Weeks`, `Baseline FVC`).
    *   **Design:** This branch uses simple `Linear -> ReLU` blocks. **Batch Normalization is explicitly excluded** to avoid the generalization instability observed in small-batch regimes **Cite solution_lesson_node_00010**.
*   **Prediction Head:**
    *   The projected image features and tabular features are concatenated and passed to a final regression head.
    *   **No Dropout:** Dropout is removed from the final layer to preserve the strong linear relationship between `Baseline FVC` and the target **Cite solution_lesson_node_00007**.
    *   **Output:** The model predicts two values: standardized FVC ($\mu$) and standardized confidence ($\sigma$).

### 2. Data
*   **Content-Adaptive Slice Selection (The Core Innovation):**
    *   Instead of selecting slices based on fixed file indices (which caused the failure in Idea 2 due to lack of registration **Cite solution_lesson_node_00012**), we employ a **Lung-Extent Heuristic**:
        1.  **Anchor (Middle):** Identify the slice with the maximum lung pixel count (pixels between -1000 and -400 HU).
        2.  **Upper/Lower Bounds:** Scan outwards from the anchor to find the first slices where the lung pixel count drops to 50% of the maximum. These define the "Apical" and "Basal" views.
    *   This ensures the model consistently sees the center and the functional extremities of the lungs, regardless of patient positioning or scan length.
*   **Target Scaling:**
    *   The target variable `FVC` is **Z-score standardized** (mean 0, std 1) during training to resolve the magnitude gap **Cite solution_lesson_node_00001**.
    *   Clinical inputs (`Age`, `Weeks`, `Baseline FVC`) are also standardized.

### 3. Training
*   **Loss Function:** Modified Laplace Log Likelihood.
*   **Uncertainty Stabilization:**
    *   To prevent the "NLL Singularity" (validation loss explosion) observed in Idea 3 **Cite solution_lesson_node_00013**, we enforce a numerical floor on $\sigma$ within the model: $\sigma = \text{softplus}(\text{raw\_output}) + 1\text{e-}3$.
    *   We **do not** enforce the metric's specific 70ml clip in the architecture, allowing the gradients to flow naturally for small uncertainty values **Cite solution_lesson_node_00014**.
*   **Schedule:**
    *   The training duration is extended to **35 epochs**. This is necessary to allow the projection head to fully converge and for the uncertainty estimation (which lags behind the mean prediction) to calibrate **Cite solution_lesson_node_00009**, **Cite solution_lesson_node_00011**.

### 4. Evaluation
*   **Inference:**
    *   Predict standardized $\mu$ and $\sigma$.
*   **Inverse Transformation:**
    *   Unscale $\mu$: $\mu_{final} = \mu_{pred} \times \sigma_{target} + \mu_{target}$.
    *   Unscale $\sigma$: $\sigma_{final} = \sigma_{pred} \times \sigma_{target}$ (Scale only, no mean addition) **Cite solution_lesson_node_00006**.
*   **Post-Processing:**
    *   Apply the metric-required clipping: $\sigma_{final} = \max(\sigma_{final}, 70)$.
