To optimize the solution, I will implement a **Residual Baseline Connection** and a **Learning Rate Scheduler**.

**1. Residual Baseline Connection (Architecture Modification)**
The failure analysis of the previous solution revealed a correlation between prediction error and `Baseline_FVC` (0.156) and `Weeks` (0.316). This indicates the model is struggling to effectively utilize the baseline measurement to predict the progression.
I will modify the `MultiViewNet` to explicitly model the *residual* (change in FVC) rather than the absolute FVC.
- **Current:** $\mu_{pred} = \text{Network}(Image, Tabular)$
- **New:** $\mu_{pred} = \text{Network}(Image, Tabular) + \text{Baseline\_FVC}_{standardized}$
By adding the standardized baseline FVC (available in the tabular input) directly to the output, the network is forced to learn the *deviation* from the baseline (i.e., the disease progression over `Weeks`), which is a much simpler function than mapping CT features to absolute lung volume. This acts as a strong inductive bias.

**2. Cosine Annealing Scheduler (Optimization)**
Cite {solution_lesson_node_00009}: "Ensure sufficient gradient updates... A training schedule that is too short often results in the image branch underfitting."
Cite {solution_lesson_node_00011}: "Uncertainty calibration converges slower than point prediction."
I will extend the training epochs from 35 to 50 and introduce a `CosineAnnealingLR` scheduler. This allows for aggressive learning in early epochs and fine-grained weight refinement in later epochs, helping the model settle into a sharper minimum for both the regression target and the uncertainty parameter (`sigma`).

**3. Configuration Updates**
- Increase `epochs` to 50.
- Keep `patience` at 10.
