Based on the comprehensive analysis of previous attempts and the specific lessons learned, I propose a **Calibrated Noisy Student Self-Training Pipeline**.

Previous attempts at semi-supervised learning failed due to specific violations of established best practices: `idea_12` failed because the Teacher model was trained with a suboptimal batch size (Cite solution_lesson_node_00022), and `idea_13` failed because the Student model was subjected to excessive Mixup regularization ($\alpha=1.0$) which destroyed signal structure (Cite solution_lesson_node_00030).

This strategy structurally evolves the solution by wrapping the **Optimized Supervised Baseline** (`idea_6`) into a rigorous self-training loop. It leverages the unlabeled test data to improve generalization while strictly adhering to the regularization and signal processing constraints identified as critical in the lessons.

# Model

**Backbone: EfficientNetV2-Medium**
I will utilize the **EfficientNetV2-Medium** architecture for both the Teacher and the Student models.
*   **Rationale:** This architecture has consistently outperformed Transformer-based models (like ConvNeXt) for this spectrogram task (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016). I will retain the "Medium" variant rather than scaling up to "Large" to ensure we can maintain the critical Batch Size of 64 within the GPU memory constraints (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).

**Head: Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling**
Both models will employ **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling** to aggregate feature maps.
*   **Rationale:** GeM pooling allows the model to dynamically focus on the high-activation time-frequency regions (the transient whale call) while suppressing the dominant background noise, a mechanism proven superior to Global Average Pooling for weakly supervised detection (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).

# Data

**Input Representation: High-Fidelity Log-Mel Spectrograms**
I will generate Log-Mel Spectrograms using the optimal parameters identified in previous lessons:
*   **Resolution:** **384 Mel bands** to match the Compound Scaling capacity of the V2-Medium backbone (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010), avoiding the information bottleneck of lower resolutions (Cite solution_lesson_node_00017).
*   **Temporal Dynamics:** A large FFT window (N_FFT=1024) for frequency definition, paired with a **short hop length (approx. 10ms)** to capture rapid temporal transients. This single high-fidelity representation renders multi-stream architectures redundant (Cite solution_lesson_node_00019).
*   **Normalization:** **Instance-level Min-Max Normalization** will be used to preserve spectral contrast, avoiding the signal degradation caused by frequency-wise standardization (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015).

# Training

**Strategy: Calibrated Noisy Student Pipeline**
The training will proceed in three distinct phases:

1.  **Phase 1: Optimized Teacher Training**
    *   Train the EfficientNetV2-Medium on the labeled training set.
    *   **Critical Constraint:** Maintain a **Batch Size of 64** to ensure robust Batch Normalization statistics (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
    *   **Regularization:** Apply **Stochastic Depth (DropPath)** and **Classifier Dropout** to prevent overfitting (Cite solution_lesson_node_00024).
    *   **Augmentation:** Use **Mixup with $\alpha=0.4$**. This low-alpha setting preserves the harmonic structure of the signal, unlike the uniform distribution ($\alpha=1.0$) which caused regression in previous attempts (Cite solution_lesson_node_00030).

2.  **Phase 2: Soft Pseudo-Labeling**
    *   Use the converged Teacher (reloaded from the best checkpoint) to generate **soft probability predictions** for the entire unlabeled `test2` dataset. Soft labels are preserved to convey uncertainty information.

3.  **Phase 3: Student Training**
    *   Train a fresh EfficientNetV2-Medium (the Student) on the combined dataset (Labeled Train + Pseudo-Labeled Test).
    *   **Noise Injection:** The Student will be trained with the exact same **Mixup ($\alpha=0.4$)** and **SpecAugment** settings as the Teacher. This ensures the Student learns a smoother decision boundary without the signal destruction caused by aggressive uniform mixing (Cite solution_lesson_node_00030).
    *   **Loss Function:** Use **Binary Cross-Entropy**. For labeled data, apply **Inverse Class Frequency Weighting** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001). For pseudo-labeled data, calculate the loss against the soft targets. Mixup losses will be calculated by mixing the **weighted scalar losses** to preserve gradients (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).

**Optimization**
*   **Optimizer:** **AdamW** with a **Cosine Annealing Learning Rate Scheduler** for stable convergence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).

# Evaluation

**Inference Strategy**
*   **Checkpoint Reloading:** During both Teacher and Student training phases, the pipeline will monitor Validation AUC and save the best model state. Before generating predictions (for pseudo-labeling or final submission), the system will **explicitly reload these best-saved weights**, discarding the potentially degraded state from the final training epoch (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008).
