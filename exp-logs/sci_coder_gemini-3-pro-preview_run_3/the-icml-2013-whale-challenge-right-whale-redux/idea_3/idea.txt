# Idea: EfficientNet-B0 with Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling

Building on the success of the SE-ResNet and high-resolution spectrograms, I propose a structural evolution to an **EfficientNet-B0** backbone enhanced with **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling**. This strategy leverages the superior efficiency of Inverted Residual blocks while addressing the specific "event detection" nature of the task through a specialized pooling mechanism.

## Model
*   **Backbone:** Replace the custom SE-ResNet with **EfficientNet-B0**. This architecture represents a structural shift from standard Residual blocks to **MBConv (Mobile Inverted Bottleneck Convolution)** blocks. MBConv blocks utilize depthwise separable convolutions and inverted residuals, which provide a more parameter-efficient way to model complex features. Crucially, this architecture natively integrates the **Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE)** modules that were proven effective in previous lessons (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
*   **Head (Structural Innovation):** Replace the standard Global Average Pooling (GAP) layer with **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling**. Whale calls are transient events that occupy only a small fraction of the 2-second clip. GAP tends to dilute the strong signal of the call by averaging it with the dominant background noise. GeM pooling introduces a learnable parameter $p$ (where $p \to \infty$ approximates Max Pooling and $p=1$ approximates Average Pooling), allowing the model to dynamically focus on the high-activation time-frequency regions where the call is present.

## Data
*   **Input Representation:** Continue to use **High-Resolution Log-Mel Spectrograms** (128 Mel bands, hop length < 20ms). Previous experiments demonstrated that high time-frequency resolution is essential for resolving the morphology of right whale calls (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
*   **Normalization:** Apply instance-level normalization to scale inputs for the CNN.

## Training
*   **Loss Function:** Utilize **Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) with Inverse Class Frequency Weighting**. This remains the most effective method for handling the dataset's ~9:1 imbalance without the complexity of resampling (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
*   **Regularization & Augmentation:**
    *   **Mixup with Mixed Losses:** Apply Mixup augmentation. Crucially, calculate the loss by mixing the **weighted scalar losses** of the input pair rather than mixing the labels or weights. This ensures the minority class gradients are not diluted by the majority class weights (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).
    *   **SpecAugment:** Continue using time and frequency masking to prevent overfitting to noise artifacts (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
    *   **Stochastic Depth:** Introduce Stochastic Depth (DropPath) regularization, which randomly drops entire residual blocks during training. This is a standard regularizer for EfficientNet architectures that complements SpecAugment.
*   **Optimization:** Use the AdamW optimizer paired with a **Cosine Annealing Learning Rate Scheduler** to allow for aggressive initial learning followed by stable convergence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).

## Evaluation
*   **Inference:** Preprocess test clips into Log-Mel Spectrograms and perform a forward pass.
*   **Checkpointing:** Explicitly **reload the best model weights** (determined by Validation AUC) saved during training before generating the final submission. This prevents the performance degradation often observed in the final epochs of training (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008).
