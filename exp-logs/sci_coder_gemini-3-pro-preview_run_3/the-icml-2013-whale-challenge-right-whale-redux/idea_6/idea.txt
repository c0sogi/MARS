Based on the progression of empirical findings and the lessons learned, I propose the following optimized strategy. This approach structurally evolves the architecture by adopting **EfficientNetV2**, which integrates the "Wide" layer principles (Lesson 7) into a modern backbone optimized for high-resolution inputs (Lesson 10), and pairs it with specialized pooling for weakly supervised detection (Lesson 9).

# Model

**Backbone: EfficientNetV2-Medium (EfficientNetV2-M)**
I propose replacing the previous EfficientNet-B0/B2 and ConvNeXt backbones with **EfficientNetV2-M**. This represents a structural innovation over the V1 architecture used in Lesson 9/10 through the introduction of **Fused-MBConv** blocks in the early stages.
*   **Rationale:** Standard Depthwise Separable Convolutions (used in V1) can be too sparse to capture the rich, low-level spectral textures of whale calls. Fused-MBConv blocks replace the depthwise bottleneck with full dense 3x3 convolutions. This effectively implements the "Scale Width" lesson (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007) by increasing the connectivity of spatial filters in the initial layers, while the "Medium" variant provides the deep capacity needed for complex pattern recognition.
*   **Initialization:** The model will be initialized with ImageNet-21k pretrained weights to leverage robust texture feature extractors.

**Head: Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling**
I will retain **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling** to aggregate the feature maps.
*   **Rationale:** As established, whale calls are transient events localized in time and frequency. GeM pooling allows the model to dynamically learn to focus on high-activation regions (the call) while suppressing the dominant background noise, a critical mechanism for this weakly supervised task (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).

# Data

**Input Representation: Ultra-High-Resolution Log-Mel Spectrograms**
*   **Resolution Scaling:** Following the principle of **Compound Scaling** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010), I will increase the vertical resolution to **384 Mel bands**. The EfficientNetV2-M backbone is designed to process higher resolution inputs than smaller variants. Providing a coarse input (e.g., 128 Mels) to such a deep model would create an information bottleneck; 384 Mels ensures the input fidelity matches the model's capacity.
*   **Temporal Fidelity:** I will use a short hop length (~10ms) to preserve the rapid temporal dynamics of the whale chirps (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
*   **Normalization:** Inputs will be standardized using instance-level Min-Max normalization (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).

# Training

**Loss Function: Binary Cross-Entropy with Inverse Class Frequency Weighting**
*   I will use **BCE Loss weighted by the inverse class frequency**. This remains the most robust method for handling the ~9:1 class imbalance without the computational overhead of resampling (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).

**Regularization & Augmentation**
*   **Mixup with Mixed Losses:** I will apply Mixup augmentation. Crucially, I will compute the loss by mixing the **weighted scalar losses** of the input pair rather than mixing the labels or weights. This ensures that the gradients from the minority class are not diluted by the majority class weights (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).
*   **SpecAugment:** I will apply random time and frequency masking to the spectrograms to force the model to learn robust partial features (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
*   **Stochastic Depth:** Given the depth of the V2-M model, I will apply Stochastic Depth (DropPath) regularization to randomly drop residual paths during training, preventing overfitting.

**Optimization**
*   **Optimizer:** Use **AdamW** combined with a **Cosine Annealing Learning Rate Scheduler**. This schedule allows for aggressive initial learning followed by stable convergence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).

# Evaluation

**Inference Strategy**
*   **Checkpoint Reloading:** The training pipeline will monitor Validation AUC and save the model state with the highest score. Before generating the final submission, the system will **explicitly reload these best-saved weights**, discarding the state from the final training epoch. This prevents the use of a potentially degraded model caused by overfitting in the late stages of training (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008).
