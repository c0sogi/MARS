# Proposed Strategy: Domain-Adapted Hybrid Ensemble with Heterogeneous Backbones

## Model
The proposed solution advances the "Hybrid Transformer-Statistical Ensemble" (Idea 2) by introducing **Domain-Adaptive Pre-training (DAPT)** and increasing neural diversity without the complexity of stacking.

1.  **Statistical Branch (The Lexical Expert):**
    *   Retain the **TF-IDF pipeline** utilizing both **Word N-grams (1-2)** and **Character N-grams (3-5)**. This explicitly captures the morphological and punctuation signatures proven to be critical for this task **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00002}**.
    *   The classifier will be a weighted ensemble of **Logistic Regression (Discriminative)** and **Multinomial Naive Bayes (Generative)**. Crucially, the internal weights between LR and NB will be mathematically optimized on the validation set rather than hardcoded, ensuring the ensemble respects the dataset's preference for generative signals **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00014}**.

2.  **Neural Branch (The Context Experts):**
    *   Deploy two distinct Transformer architectures to ensure error decorrelation:
        *   **DeBERTa-v3-base:** For its disentangled attention mechanism.
        *   **RoBERTa-base:** For its robust absolute positional encodings and distinct tokenizer (BPE).
    *   **Structural Innovation (DAPT):** Before fine-tuning for classification, both backbones will undergo **Masked Language Modeling (MLM)** training on the combined training and test text. This adapts the modern pre-trained weights to the specific 19th-century literary style and vocabulary of Poe, Lovecraft, and Shelley, addressing the domain shift.
    *   **Head Design:** Both models will utilize the **`[CLS]` token** for classification. We explicitly reject Mean Pooling to prevent the neural representations from mimicking the "bag-of-words" behavior of the statistical branch, thereby maximizing ensemble orthogonality **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00010}**.

3.  **Global Ensemble:**
    *   The final predictions will be a linear weighted average of the three branches: $P_{final} = w_1 P_{stat} + w_2 P_{deberta} + w_3 P_{roberta}$.

## Data
1.  **Preprocessing:**
    *   **MLM Corpus:** Concatenate the raw text from `train.csv` and `test.csv` (ignoring labels) to create a domain-specific corpus for pre-training.
    *   **Statistical:** Apply standard lowercasing.
    *   **Neural:** Use respective tokenizers for DeBERTa and RoBERTa.
2.  **Vectorization (Statistical):**
    *   Fit a `TfidfVectorizer` with `sublinear_tf` scaling on the training data.
    *   Generate a sparse feature matrix combining word and character n-grams **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00002}**.

## Training
1.  **Phase 1: Domain Adaptation (MLM):**
    *   Train both `deberta-v3-base` and `roberta-base` on the MLM corpus for a small number of epochs (e.g., 3-5) using the **Masked Language Modeling** objective. This primes the encoders for the specific linguistic patterns of the authors.
2.  **Phase 2: Supervised Fine-Tuning:**
    *   Fine-tune the adapted encoders on the labeled training set using **Categorical Cross-Entropy Loss**.
    *   Use **AdamW** with a linear learning rate scheduler and warmup.
    *   Train the Statistical models (LR and NB) on the TF-IDF features.
3.  **Phase 3: Weight Optimization:**
    *   Generate predictions for the validation set from all components: $P_{LR}$, $P_{NB}$, $P_{DeBERTa}$, $P_{RoBERTa}$.
    *   **Step A (Statistical Blend):** Optimize the mixing weight $\alpha$ for the statistical branch ($P_{stat} = \alpha P_{LR} + (1-\alpha) P_{NB}$) to minimize Log Loss **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00014}**.
    *   **Step B (Global Blend):** Optimize the scalar weights $w_1, w_2, w_3$ for the global ensemble on the validation set. This direct optimization is preferred over training a meta-learner (Stacking) to avoid overfitting and training constraints **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00011}**.

## Evaluation
1.  **Inference:**
    *   Pass the test data through the optimized Statistical pipeline and the two fine-tuned Neural models.
2.  **Aggregation:**
    *   Combine the probability distributions using the learned weights.
    *   Apply the numerical stability clipping ($max(min(p, 1-10^{-15}), 10^{-15})$) before submission.
