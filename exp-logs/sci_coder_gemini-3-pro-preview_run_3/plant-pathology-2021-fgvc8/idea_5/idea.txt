Here is a proposal for an optimized strategy that evolves the architecture to handle higher fidelity features while correcting the stabilization mechanisms that caused the previous high-resolution attempt to fail.

### Model
We will upgrade the backbone from ConvNeXt-Tiny to **ConvNeXt-Small**.
*   **Structural Innovation (Backbone)**: We scale up to the **ConvNeXt-Small** variant. **Cite Lesson 14** demonstrated that the "Transformer-style" ConvNeXt architecture outperforms ResNet, and the "Small" variant provides the necessary depth and width to capture the complex feature hierarchies of disease patterns that the "Tiny" version might miss.
*   **Structural Innovation (Head)**: We will replace the standard Global Average Pooling (GAP) layer with **Generalized Mean Pooling (GeM)**. Standard GAP averages out feature activations across the entire spatial map, which can dilute the signal of small, localized disease spots (e.g., early Rust or Frog Eye Leaf Spot). GeM is a learnable pooling operation that can focus on high-activation regions (salient features) while suppressing background noise, making it structurally superior for fine-grained classification tasks involving local textures.

### Data
We will implement a **High-Resolution Input Strategy** with specific augmentation constraints.
*   **Resolution**: Input images will be resized to **384x384**. **Cite Lesson 9** highlighted the importance of local feature resolution; increasing the pixel count provides the GeM pooling layer with higher fidelity spatial maps to operate on.
*   **Augmentation Strategy**:
    *   **RandomResizedCrop**: We will strictly maintain the aggressive minimum scale of **0.5**. **Cite Lesson 9** and **Cite Lesson 4** proved that forcing the model to classify based on partial views is the most effective regularizer for this dataset.
    *   **Orientation**: We will apply both **HorizontalFlip** and **VerticalFlip**. **Cite Lesson 11** confirmed that leaf images are rotationally invariant (no fixed "up"), and removing vertical flips degrades performance.
    *   **Color Jitter**: We will apply brightness and contrast perturbations with a strength of **0.2**. **Cite Lesson 12** established this as the maximum safe intensity that provides lighting invariance (**Cite Lesson 1**) without destroying color-dependent disease signatures.

### Training
We will implement a **Corrected Model EMA Protocol** to stabilize the training of the larger model.
*   **Stabilization (EMA)**: We will utilize **Model Exponential Moving Average (EMA)** but with a corrected decay rate of **0.99** (or 0.995). **Cite Lesson 21** identified that the standard decay of 0.9999 is too slow for short training schedules (<25 epochs), causing the inference model to lag behind the learned weights. A faster decay ensures the EMA model converges within the allotted time while still smoothing out the optimization noise of the larger ConvNeXt-Small backbone.
*   **Loss Function**: We will use **BCEWithLogitsLoss**. We avoid the complex Asymmetric Loss/MixUp combination which failed due to short training horizons in **Cite Lesson 6**.
*   **Schedule**: We will train for **20 epochs** using **AdamW** and a **Cosine Annealing** scheduler. The increased model capacity and resolution require slightly more time to converge than the baseline, but the corrected EMA will ensure efficient utilization of this budget.

### Evaluation
*   **Test Time Augmentation (TTA)**: We will perform inference on the original image, a horizontally flipped version, and a vertically flipped version. The logits will be averaged before thresholding. This leverages the rotational invariance confirmed in **Cite Lesson 11** to reduce prediction variance.
*   **Thresholding**: A fixed threshold of **0.5** will be applied to the averaged probabilities.
