Based on the empirical evidence gathered from the previous lessons, here is a proposal for an optimized strategy.

### Model
We will evolve the architecture from ConvNeXt-Tiny to **ConvNeXt-Small**.
*   **Structural Evolution**: While **Cite Lesson 14** proved the superiority of the "Transformer-style" ConvNeXt-Tiny over ResNet18, the "Small" variant offers a deeper and wider capacity to capture complex feature hierarchies. To manage this increased capacity and prevent the "excess capacity" overfitting issues noted in **Cite Lesson 1**, we will strictly employ **Stochastic Depth (DropPath)** regularization within the blocks.
*   **Head Design**: The classification head will utilize a **Layer Normalization** step before the final fully connected layer, a standard practice in ConvNeXt designs to stabilize the feature distribution before classification.

### Data
We will implement a **High-Resolution Input Strategy** to address the fine-grained nature of the disease spots.
*   **Resolution**: We will increase the input resolution from 224x224 to **384x384**. Apple disease symptoms (spots, rust) are often small, local textures. **Cite Lesson 9** demonstrated that forcing the model to look at local details (via aggressive cropping) improves performance; increasing the native resolution complements this by providing higher fidelity pixels for those local features.
*   **Augmentation Strategy**:
    *   **RandomResizedCrop**: We will maintain the aggressive minimum scale of **0.5** (**Cite Lesson 9**) to force learning from partial views.
    *   **Orientation**: We will strictly include **VerticalFlip** alongside HorizontalFlip, as **Cite Lesson 11** confirmed that leaf images lack a canonical vertical orientation.
    *   **Color Jitter**: We will apply mild brightness and contrast perturbations (strength **0.2**). **Cite Lesson 12** established this as the "sweet spot" that provides invariance to lighting (**Cite Lesson 1**) without destroying color-dependent disease signatures.

### Training
We will introduce **Model Exponential Moving Average (EMA)** as a structural stabilizer.
*   **Technique (EMA)**: Instead of using the raw weights from the final epoch, we will maintain a moving average of the model weights throughout training. This effectively creates an ensemble of the model at different time steps, smoothing out the optimization trajectory and improving generalization, which is crucial when training larger models like ConvNeXt-Small on moderate-sized datasets.
*   **Loss Function**: We will use **BCEWithLogitsLoss**. We will avoid the Asymmetric Loss (ASL) + MixUp combination that failed in **Cite Lesson 6** due to insufficient training horizons.
*   **Schedule**: We will extend the training duration to **25 epochs**. **Cite Lesson 6** and **Cite Lesson 9** highlighted that increased regularization (DropPath) and task difficulty (High Resolution) require longer convergence times.
*   **Precision**: Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP) will be used to handle the increased memory load of 384x384 resolution on the A100 GPU.

### Evaluation
*   **Test Time Augmentation (TTA)**: We will perform inference on the original image, a horizontally flipped version, and a vertically flipped version. The logits will be averaged. This leverages the rotational invariance confirmed in **Cite Lesson 11** to reduce prediction variance.
*   **Thresholding**: A fixed threshold of **0.5** will be applied to the averaged probabilities.
