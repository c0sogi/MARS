Based on the analysis of the previous experiments and the lessons learned, I propose the following optimized strategy. This solution introduces the **EfficientNet-V2** architecture to the pipeline, specifically designed to optimize the trade-off between training throughput and parameter efficiency, addressing the bottlenecks encountered with ConvNeXt and EfficientNet-B4.

# Model
I propose a **U-Net++** architecture utilizing an **EfficientNet-V2-M** backbone.

*   **Backbone (EfficientNet-V2-M):** Previous experiments highlighted a conflict between the need for high-resolution context **Cite solution_lesson_node_00016** and the requirement for high training throughput **Cite solution_lesson_node_00019**. EfficientNet-V2 introduces **Fused-MBConv** blocks in the early stages, which replace the slow depthwise-separable convolutions of V1 (and ConvNeXt) with standard dense convolutions. This structural change significantly accelerates training speed on GPUs, allowing us to scale the input resolution to **768x768** without incurring the latency penalty that caused the ConvNeXt-Base experiment to fail **Cite solution_lesson_node_00020**.
*   **Decoder (U-Net++ with Deep Supervision):** We retain the nested decoder structure with **Deep Supervision**. We compute the loss as a weighted sum of outputs from four semantic levels (`[1.0, 0.5, 0.25, 0.125]`). This ensures robust feature learning at all scales and accelerates convergence, a critical factor given the 24-hour time limit **Cite solution_lesson_node_00013**.

# Data
*   **Hierarchical ROI Sampling:** We will strictly adhere to the strategy of using anatomical structure annotations (Cortex/Medulla) to filter the training set. We will only extract tiles that intersect with the valid tissue mask, ignoring background areas to maximize computational efficiency **Cite solution_lesson_node_00003**.
*   **Optimized Resolution (768x768):** Leveraging the efficiency of the V2 backbone, we will increase the input resolution to **768x768**. This provides significantly better spatial context and boundary definition than the 640x640 used in the baseline **Cite solution_lesson_node_00014**, while remaining computationally lighter than the failed 1024x1024 attempt **Cite solution_lesson_node_00020**.
*   **Raw RGB Input:** We explicitly reject the use of fixed stain deconvolution layers. As discovered, fixed mathematical priors conflict with geometric and color augmentations **Cite solution_lesson_node_00022**. We will feed raw **RGB** images to the network, allowing the initial Fused-MBConv layers to learn optimal color separation filters dynamically.

# Training
*   **GPU-Accelerated Augmentation:** To handle the "different tissue preparation pipelines" without slowing down training, we will employ **GPU-based** augmentations (RandomRotate90, Flip, Transpose, and heavy ColorJitter/HSV) rather than CPU-bound normalization techniques like Macenko, which were shown to destroy training throughput **Cite solution_lesson_node_00019**.
*   **Loss Function:** We will use the **Deep Supervision Loss** (Weighted BCE + Dice) applied to the nested outputs.
*   **Optimization:** We will use the **AdamW** optimizer with a **Cosine Annealing** schedule (`T_max=15 epochs`). The efficiency of the V2-M backbone should allow us to complete the full 15 epochs on the entire dataset, ensuring full convergence.

# Evaluation
*   **ROI-Constrained Inference:** We generate the tissue mask from anatomical polygons and perform inference only on valid tissue tiles **Cite solution_lesson_node_00003**.
*   **Gaussian-Weighted Stitching:** Predictions from the 768x768 tiles will be stitched using a Gaussian weight map to seamlessly blend overlapping regions, prioritizing the center of predictions where the model is most confident **Cite solution_lesson_node_00013**.
