Based on the comprehensive analysis of the previous experiments and the lessons learned, I propose the following optimized solution. This strategy introduces **Progressive Resizing** to resolve the conflict between the need for high-resolution context and the requirement for sufficient training iterations. It also adopts a **ConvNeXt-Tiny** backbone to leverage large-kernel context modeling without the computational penalty of larger variants.

# Model
I propose a **U-Net++** architecture with a **ConvNeXt-Tiny** backbone.

*   **Backbone (ConvNeXt-Tiny):** We replace the EfficientNet-B4 backbone with **ConvNeXt-Tiny**. While EfficientNet performed well, its small $3 \times 3$ kernels limit the effective receptive field. As suggested by the desire for better context in previous attempts, ConvNeXt employs large $7 \times 7$ kernels that mimic the long-range dependency modeling of Transformers. This allows the model to better distinguish FTUs from surrounding tissue structures (e.g., distinguishing cortex from medulla). Crucially, the "Tiny" variant (~28M parameters) remains computationally feasible, avoiding the severe underfitting observed with the heavier ConvNeXt-Base **Cite solution_lesson_node_00020**.
*   **Decoder (U-Net++ with Deep Supervision):** We retain the nested decoder structure. We continue to use **Deep Supervision**, computing the loss as a weighted sum of outputs from four semantic levels (`[1.0, 0.5, 0.25, 0.125]`). This ensures robust feature learning at all scales and accelerates convergence, which is critical given the time constraints **Cite solution_lesson_node_00013**.

# Data
*   **Hierarchical ROI Sampling:** We strictly maintain the strategy of using anatomical structure annotations (Cortex/Medulla) to filter the dataset. We will only extract tiles that intersect with the valid tissue mask, ignoring the vast background areas to maximize computational efficiency **Cite solution_lesson_node_00003**.
*   **Raw RGB with Learnable Filters:** We explicitly reject the use of fixed stain deconvolution layers. As discovered, fixed mathematical priors conflict with geometric and color augmentations **Cite solution_lesson_node_00022**. Instead, we feed raw **RGB** images to the network, allowing the initial layers of the ConvNeXt backbone to learn the optimal color separation filters dynamically.
*   **GPU-Accelerated Augmentation:** To handle the "different tissue preparation pipelines" without slowing down training, we employ GPU-based augmentations (RandomRotate90, Flip, Transpose, and ColorJitter) rather than CPU-bound normalization techniques like Macenko, which were shown to destroy training throughput **Cite solution_lesson_node_00019**.

# Training
I introduce a **Progressive Resizing** training strategy to synthesize the benefits of high resolution **Cite solution_lesson_node_00014** with the need for high training throughput **Cite solution_lesson_node_00020**.

*   **Phase 1 (Coarse Learning):** For the first 60% of the training epochs, we train on tiles resized to **512 x 512**. This lower resolution allows for a larger batch size and faster iteration speed, enabling the model to see the entire dataset multiple times and rapidly learn high-level semantic features and tissue textures.
*   **Phase 2 (Fine-Tuning):** For the remaining 40% of the epochs, we increase the input resolution to **768 x 768** (or 1024 x 1024 if memory permits). We fine-tune the model on these larger tiles to resolve fine-grained boundaries and leverage the full spatial context of the ConvNeXt backbone. This approach ensures we do not sacrifice convergence speed for resolution.
*   **Optimization:** We use the **AdamW** optimizer. The learning rate will follow a Cosine Annealing schedule that resets or adjusts when the resolution transition occurs.

# Evaluation
*   **ROI-Constrained Inference:** We generate the tissue mask from anatomical polygons and perform inference only on valid tissue tiles, skipping background regions entirely **Cite solution_lesson_node_00003**.
*   **Gaussian-Weighted Stitching:** Predictions from the final resolution tiles (e.g., 768px) will be stitched using a Gaussian weight map to seamlessly blend overlapping regions, prioritizing the center of predictions where the model is most confident **Cite solution_lesson_node_00013**.
