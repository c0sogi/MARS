# Proposed Strategy: Latent Spatial-Contextualized BiGRU

This strategy introduces a **Latent Spatial Injection** mechanism. This structural innovation resolves the conflict identified in previous experiments: it allows the model to leverage explicit 3D structural dependencies (pairing) without disrupting the extraction of local sequential motifs (k-mers) by the convolutional stem. This architecture is coupled with a high-capacity backbone and a stabilized training regimen that strictly adheres to the protocols proven effective in the lessons.

### Model

The architecture is a **Bidirectional GRU with Post-Convolutional Spatial Injection**.

1.  **Input Representation:**
    *   The input is a sequence-preserved tensor of shape $(N, 107, 14)$.
    *   **Feature Channels:** We strictly use **One-Hot Encoding** for the nucleotide sequence (4), secondary structure (3), and predicted loop type (7). We do **not** use learned embeddings, as they increase parameters without benefit in this low-data regime. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00009**.
    *   We maintain the sequence dimension to preserve locality and translation invariance. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**.

2.  **Convolutional Stem (Local Feature Extraction):**
    *   The raw one-hot input is processed by a **1D Convolutional Layer** (kernel size 3, filters 256, activation GELU).
    *   **Purpose:** This projects the sparse inputs into a dense embedding space and aggregates immediate local context (k-mers). Crucially, this happens *before* any structural mixing, ensuring the convolution learns clean, local sequential patterns without interference from non-local signals. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00027** and **Cite solution_lesson_node_00037**.

3.  **Latent Spatial Injection (Structural Innovation):**
    *   **Mechanism:** After the convolutional projection, we inject the structural context. For every position $i$, we use the provided secondary structure to identify its paired index $j$.
    *   **Operation:** We gather the latent feature vector $h_j$ produced by the Conv Stem. If position $i$ is unpaired, we use a zero vector. We then concatenate the local vector $h_i$ and the paired vector $h_j$ and pass them through a Linear projection (restoring the dimension to 256).
    *   **Benefit:** This "linearizes" the graph structure by providing the backbone with direct access to spatially neighboring features **Cite solution_lesson_node_00033**. By performing this in the latent space, we avoid the feature corruption observed when concatenating raw inputs. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00037**.

4.  **Backbone (High-Capacity BiGRU):**
    *   The spatially-enriched sequence is fed into a **Bidirectional GRU** (3 layers, hidden dimension 384).
    *   We prioritize the BiGRU over Transformers for its superior inductive bias on short sequences. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00017**.
    *   We maximize backbone capacity (width and depth) rather than adding complex hierarchical encoders (like Inception), as capacity has proven more valuable than architectural complexity. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00026** and **Cite solution_lesson_node_00040**.

5.  **Output Head:**
    *   A final Linear layer projects the hidden states to the 5 target values.

### Data

1.  **Full-Spectrum Training:**
    *   We will train on the **entire training dataset**, including samples with low signal-to-noise ratios (`SN_filter == 0`). We explicitly reject filtering strategies, as training on the full noisy distribution is required to generalize to the noisy public test set and avoid distribution shift. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00015** and **Cite solution_lesson_node_00013**.

2.  **Robust Pipeline:**
    *   We will implement **hash-based caching** for the preprocessed data (e.g., `train_data_{hash(config)}.npy`). This ensures that the new Latent Spatial Injection logic is correctly executed and versioned, preventing stale data loading. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00011**.

### Training

1.  **Objective (Unweighted Loss):**
    *   We will optimize the standard **MCRMSE** on all 5 target columns. We will **not** use inverse-variance weighting, as down-weighting noisy samples degrades performance on the unweighted competition metric. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00034**.

2.  **Optimizer & Stability:**
    *   **Optimizer:** **AdamW** with a **Cosine Annealing Learning Rate Scheduler** to ensure convergence to a deep minimum. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00031**.
    *   **Gradient Clipping:** We will explicitly apply **Gradient Clipping** (max norm 1.0) to the training loop. This is mandatory to stabilize the high-capacity RNN and prevent the exploding gradients that likely hindered previous high-capacity attempts. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00046**.
    *   **Regularization:** Dropout will be applied after the Convolutional Stem and between GRU layers.

### Evaluation

1.  **Global Metric Aggregation:**
    *   We will implement a **Global Evaluation Loop** that concatenates predictions and targets for the entire validation set *before* calculating the MCRMSE. We will **not** average batch-wise RMSEs, as this produces optimistically biased results. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00045**.

2.  **Targeted Validation:**
    *   While the model predicts all 5 targets, we will calculate the validation metric specifically on the **3 scored columns** (`reactivity`, `deg_Mg_pH10`, `deg_Mg_50C`) to ensure alignment with the competition leaderboard. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00017**.

3.  **Inference:**
    *   The test set sequences will be processed through the Conv Stem, Latent Spatial Injection layer, and BiGRU backbone.
    *   Predictions will be generated for all 107 positions, then sliced to the first 68 positions (`seq_scored`) for submission.
