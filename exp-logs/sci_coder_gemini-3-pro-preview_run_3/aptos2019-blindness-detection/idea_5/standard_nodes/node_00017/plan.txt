My optimization strategy focuses on enhancing the model's ability to detect fine-grained retinal lesions (microaneurysms, hemorrhages) which are critical for distinguishing between DR severity levels.

1.  **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling**: I will replace the standard Global Average Pooling in the ConvNeXt architecture with GeM Pooling. While Average Pooling treats all spatial locations equally, GeM Pooling (with a learnable parameter $p$) can focus on high-activation regions, effectively maximizing the signal-to-noise ratio for small pathologies. This aligns with the insight from **Lesson 7** regarding the importance of fine-grained feature detection.
2.  **Cosine Annealing Scheduler**: I will replace the `ReduceLROnPlateau` scheduler with `CosineAnnealingLR`. For a fixed training budget, Cosine Annealing typically provides a more optimal convergence trajectory by smoothly decaying the learning rate, allowing the model to settle into wider, more robust minima.
3.  **Extended Training Duration**: I will increase the training epochs from 8 to 10 to accommodate the full cycle of the Cosine schedule and allow the learnable GeM parameter to converge.

I will preserve the ConvNeXt Base architecture and Layer Normalization strategy, as **Lesson 12** demonstrated their necessity for stability when training with small physical batch sizes on high-resolution images.
