To improve upon the baseline while addressing the specific weaknesses identified in the lessons, I propose a **High-Resolution EfficientNet Regression** strategy. This approach evolves the backbone to handle higher fidelity inputs and refines the preprocessing pipeline to preserve geometric integrity.

### Model
I propose replacing the ResNet18 backbone with **EfficientNet-B3**.
*   **Architecture Upgrade**: The empirical analysis in **Cite Lesson 00001** revealed a correlation between prediction errors and original image dimensions, suggesting that the ResNet18 model (limited to 224x224 inputs) suffers from information loss when downscaling high-resolution fundus images. EfficientNet-B3 is structurally designed to operate effectively at higher resolutions (typically 300x300) and possesses a deeper, more parameter-efficient architecture capable of capturing the fine-grained retinal lesions (e.g., microaneurysms) lost by the lighter ResNet18.
*   **Regression Head**: Retaining the successful formulation from **Cite Lesson 00001**, the classification head will be replaced with a **Global Average Pooling** layer followed by a **Dropout** layer and a single **Linear** output neuron. This preserves the regression-based modeling of the ordinal severity scale.

### Data
The data pipeline will be restructured to maximize information retention and stability:
*   **Resolution**: Input image size will be increased to **300x300** pixels. This directly addresses the bottleneck identified in **Cite Lesson 00001** by providing the model with ~80% more pixels per image compared to the 224x224 baseline.
*   **Aspect-Aware Preprocessing**: Instead of the simple resizing used previously (which distorts the eye's circular geometry), images will be **padded to square** (using black padding) before resizing. This preserves the natural aspect ratio of the retina, ensuring that structural features remain consistent across different camera types.
*   **Augmentation Strategy**: We will strictly adhere to the findings of **Cite Lesson 00002** by avoiding aggressive geometric augmentations (like rotation or vertical flips) that caused underfitting. The pipeline will utilize:
    *   **Random Horizontal Flip**: A medically safe and proven augmentation.
    *   **Random Brightness/Contrast**: To address the "underexposed or overexposed" variations noted in the dataset description, without altering the geometric structure the model struggles to learn under tight constraints.

### Training
*   **Loss Function**: The model will be trained using **Mean Squared Error (MSE)**. As established in **Cite Lesson 00001**, this loss function aligns the training objective with the Quadratic Weighted Kappa metric by penalizing the magnitude of prediction errors on the ordinal scale.
*   **Optimization**: We will use the **Adam** optimizer. To accommodate the larger EfficientNet-B3 backbone within the compute budget, **Mixed Precision (FP16)** training will be enabled.
*   **Regularization**: A Dropout rate (e.g., 0.3) will be applied before the final linear layer to prevent overfitting, balancing the increased capacity of the B3 backbone.

### Evaluation
The inference process remains deterministic and metric-aligned:
*   **Prediction**: The model will output a continuous scalar severity score.
*   **Post-Processing**: These scores will be **clipped** to the range [0, 4] and **rounded** to the nearest integer. This discrete value is then used to calculate the Quadratic Weighted Kappa, leveraging the regression formulation's strength as proven in **Cite Lesson 00001**.
