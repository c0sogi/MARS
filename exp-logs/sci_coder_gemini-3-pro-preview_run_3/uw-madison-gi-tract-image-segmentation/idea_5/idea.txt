Based on the comprehensive analysis of previous attempts and the specific lessons learned, I propose the following optimized strategy. This approach structurally evolves the architecture to a **U-Net++** (validated in Lesson 00020) while fundamentally redesigning the preprocessing pipeline to preserve anatomical geometry (addressing Lesson 00022) and aligning the training objective with the distance-based metric (addressing Lesson 00010).

### Model
**Architecture: 2.5D U-Net++ with Deep Supervision**
I propose replacing the standard U-Net with a **U-Net++ (Nested U-Net)** architecture using an **EfficientNet-B4** backbone.
*   **Nested Skip Connections**: Unlike the standard U-Net used in earlier ideas, U-Net++ employs dense, nested skip pathways. This bridges the semantic gap between encoder and decoder, which is critical for segmenting organs that vary drastically in scale (e.g., small bowel vs. stomach), leading to significantly faster convergence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00020).
*   **Deep Supervision**: We will enable Deep Supervision, computing loss at multiple decoder levels. This acts as a strong regularizer and accelerates gradient flow, allowing the heavier B4 backbone to train efficiently (Cite solution_lesson_node_00020).
*   **2.5D Input**: The model will accept 3-channel inputs consisting of slices $t-1, t, t+1$ to leverage volumetric context (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).

### Data
**Preprocessing: Geometry-Preserving Pipeline**
To address the geometric errors and metric divergence caused by warping, I will implement an **Aspect-Ratio Preserving** preprocessing pipeline.
*   **Pad-to-Square Resizing**: Instead of warping images to a fixed resolution (which distorts physical shapes and degrades Hausdorff scores), we will resize the longest dimension of the image to the target size (e.g., 320) and **pad** the shorter dimension with zeros to achieve a square input. This preserves the physical aspect ratio of the anatomy (Cite solution_lesson_node_00022).
*   **Robust Normalization**: We will strictly apply **Per-Slice Robust Normalization** (clipping to 1st/99th percentiles then Min-Max scaling). We reject volume-global scaling to avoid artifact suppression (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004) and simple min-max to avoid outlier sensitivity (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
*   **Full Dataset**: We will train on the **100% dataset**, avoiding the instability and overfitting observed when subsampling data for complex models (Cite solution_lesson_node_00013).

### Training
**Protocol: Metric-Aligned Objective and Stateful Optimization**
*   **Loss Function**: To resolve the anti-correlation between pixel-wise loss and the Hausdorff metric (Cite solution_lesson_node_00022), we will use a composite loss: **BCE + Tversky + Boundary Loss**.
    *   **Tversky Loss** ($\alpha=0.3, \beta=0.7$) penalizes False Positives to aid the Hausdorff score.
    *   **Boundary Loss** explicitly minimizes the distance between predicted and ground-truth contours, aligning the optimization objective with the competition's distance-based metric (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).
*   **Stateful AMP**: We will use **Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP)** to maximize throughput, allowing for a larger batch size. Crucially, the `GradScaler` will be instantiated **outside** the epoch loop to ensure gradient scaling states persist, preventing the training instability observed previously (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
*   **Batch Size**: We will maximize the batch size to the hardware limit to stabilize Batch Normalization statistics (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).

### Evaluation
**Validation & Inference: Volumetric Integrity**
*   **Volumetric Checkpointing**: We will ignore 2D Slice Dice for model selection. Instead, at the end of each epoch, we will run a full validation loop that reconstructs 3D volumes and calculates the **exact competition metric** (0.4 Dice + 0.6 Hausdorff). We will save the checkpoint with the best **3D score** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010, solution_lesson_node_00012).
*   **3D Post-Processing**: During inference, we will reverse the padding (crop to original aspect ratio) and apply **3D Connected Component Analysis (CCA)**. We will retain only the largest connected volume per class to eliminate scattered false positives that disproportionately penalize the Hausdorff distance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).
