# Idea: Residual Dense Network (RDN) with High-Density Patching

### Model
I propose evolving the architecture from a standard chain of convolutional layers (DnCNN) to a **Residual Dense Network (RDN)**. This structural innovation introduces **Dense Connectivity** and **Local Residual Learning** to maximize feature reuse and information flow, addressing the limitations of plain deep networks.
*   **Backbone Architecture**: The network is constructed from a series of **Residual Dense Blocks (RDBs)**.
    *   **Dense Connectivity**: Within each RDB, the input of every convolutional layer is the concatenation of the outputs of *all* preceding layers in that block. This ensures that the network preserves all local information (both low-level noise details and high-level text structure) without it vanishing through depth.
    *   **Resolution Preservation**: Strictly adhering to **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00009}**, the network contains **no pooling or downsampling layers**. Every convolution operates at the original spatial resolution to ensure fine text strokes are never lost.
    *   **Dense Convolutions**: All convolutions within the blocks are standard $3 \times 3$ filters with stride 1 and dilation 1. This adheres to **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00011}**, prioritizing dense local pixel correlations over the spatially sparse receptive fields of dilated convolutions.
*   **Global Feature Fusion**: The outputs of all RDBs are concatenated and fused via a $1 \times 1$ convolution before the final reconstruction layer. This allows the model to adaptively combine features from different depths.
*   **Global Residual Learning**: Retaining the critical success of the previous best solution **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00012}**, the network is architected to predict the **noise residual** rather than the clean image. The final output is `Input - Predicted_Noise`.

### Data
1.  **Preprocessing**: Normalize image pixel intensities to $[0, 1]$.
2.  **High-Density Patch Extraction**:
    *   To support the increased capacity of the RDN without overfitting (addressing the failure in **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00013}**), we must significantly increase the density of the training data.
    *   Extract patches of size **$50 \times 50$**.
    *   **Crucial Change**: Use a **small stride** (e.g., 10 or 15 pixels) rather than a large stride. This creates highly overlapping patches, effectively multiplying the number of training examples available from the small 92-image dataset. This ensures the "wider" dense connections have sufficient data to learn robust features.
3.  **Augmentation**: Apply random horizontal/vertical flips and 90-degree rotations to the patches to enforce rotation invariance.

### Training
*   **Loss Function**: Minimize the **Mean Squared Error (MSE)** between the **predicted noise residual** and the **ground truth noise** (Input - Target).
*   **Optimizer**: Use **Adam** with a learning rate scheduler (e.g., Cosine Annealing or ReduceLROnPlateau) to stabilize the training of the dense connections.
*   **Strategy**: Train on the dense set of overlapping patches. The dense connectivity of the RDBs acts as a regularizer, allowing gradients to flow more easily to earlier layers, facilitating the training of this deeper/more complex structure.

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference**:
    *   Feed the **full-resolution** noisy test images into the trained RDN.
    *   Since the model is fully convolutional and resolution-preserving, it handles the full image in a single forward pass (or large tiles if memory limited).
2.  **Reconstruction**:
    *   Calculate: $\text{Clean Prediction} = \text{Noisy Input} - \text{Predicted Noise}$.
3.  **Post-processing**:
    *   Clip pixel values to $[0, 1]$.
    *   Flatten the output into the `image_row_col` format for submission.
