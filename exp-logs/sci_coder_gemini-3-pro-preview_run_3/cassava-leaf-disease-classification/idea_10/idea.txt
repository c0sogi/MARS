Based on the progression of experiments and the lessons learned, I propose the following optimized strategy. This solution introduces a **Tri-Modal Heterogeneous Ensemble**, a structural evolution that synthesizes the strengths of Supervised Attention, Self-Supervised Attention, and Modern Convolutional architectures. This approach explicitly addresses the "Ensemble Dilution" problem (Lesson 00033) by integrating a CNN that matches the performance of Transformers, thereby restoring the architectural diversity attempted in earlier experiments (Lesson 00025) without the associated performance penalty.

# Model
I propose an **Independent Heterogeneous Ensemble** comprising three distinct, high-performance backbones. This design maximizes diversity across two critical axes: **Architecture** (Global Attention vs. Local Convolution) and **Pre-training Objective** (Supervised vs. Masked Image Modeling).

1.  **Model A: ViT-Base (patch16_384)**
    *   **Role:** Supervised Attention Expert.
    *   **Rationale:** Retained as the reliable baseline. Its global self-attention mechanism excels at capturing distributed disease patterns (Cite Lesson 00015).
2.  **Model B: BEiT-Base (patch16_384)**
    *   **Role:** Masked Image Modeling (MIM) Expert.
    *   **Rationale:** Retained for its unique feature representations derived from reconstructing masked image patches. This provides error diversity compared to standard supervised learning (Cite Lesson 00038).
3.  **Model C: ConvNeXt-Base (384)**
    *   **Role:** Modern Convolutional Expert.
    *   **Structural Innovation:** We introduce **ConvNeXt**, a modern CNN that adopts Transformer-like design choices (e.g., large kernels, LayerNorm) while retaining the inductive bias of sliding windows. This addresses the "Local Detail" gap identified in Lesson 00025. Crucially, unlike the EfficientNet-B4 used previously, ConvNeXt-Base offers performance parity with ViT-Base, preventing the "Ensemble Dilution" observed when averaging strong and weak learners (Cite Lesson 00033).
    *   **Configuration:** We will select a variant pre-trained at **384x384** (e.g., `convnext_base_384_in22ft1k`) to strictly adhere to the resolution alignment requirement (Cite Lesson 00022).

# Data
*   **Preprocessing:** All images will be resized to **384x384 pixels**. This unifies the input resolution for all three backbones, ensuring optimal feature extraction without interpolation artifacts (Cite Lesson 00022).
*   **Augmentation Strategy:**
    *   **Geometric (Comprehensive):** We will apply `HorizontalFlip`, `VerticalFlip`, **`Transpose`**, and **`ShiftScaleRotate`**. This full suite of augmentations is critical to compensate for the lack of spatial priors in the Transformer models (Cite Lesson 00032) and ensures invariance to the Dihedral group D4 (Cite Lesson 00027).
    *   **Mixing:** We will strictly apply **MixUp and CutMix** regularization to all models. This is essential to prevent overfitting and enforce linear decision boundaries (Cite Lesson 00012).
    *   **Determinism:** Augmentation decisions will be seeded deterministically within the data loader (Cite Lesson 00014).

# Training
We will execute three independent training runs using a unified, precision-optimized protocol. Independent training avoids the optimization conflicts inherent in joint-fusion architectures (Cite Lesson 00025).

*   **Regularization:** We will apply **Stochastic Depth** (drop path rate ~0.1-0.2) to all models. This structural regularization is critical for fine-tuning high-capacity models on mid-sized datasets, preventing memorization of training noise (Cite Lesson 00040).
*   **Schedule:** We will train for a **fixed duration** (e.g., 10-12 epochs) using a Cosine Annealing scheduler. We will explicitly **disable Early Stopping** to ensure the models benefit from the final convergence phase of the learning rate schedule (Cite Lesson 00037).
*   **Precision:** **Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP)** will be enabled to maximize throughput, allowing the addition of the third model without exceeding the 24-hour runtime limit (Cite Lesson 00038).
*   **Loss Function:** **Cross Entropy Loss with Label Smoothing (0.1)** (Cite Lesson 00007).

# Evaluation
*   **Inference:** We will apply **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA)** for all three models, averaging predictions across the original image and its flipped/transposed versions (Cite Lesson 00010).
*   **Ensembling:** The final prediction will be generated by **averaging the Softmax probabilities** of Model A, Model B, and Model C. Because ConvNeXt-Base is performance-competitive with the Transformers, this unweighted averaging will robustly combine the diverse inductive biases (CNN vs. ViT) and pre-training signals (Supervised vs. MIM) without diluting the ensemble's accuracy (Cite Lesson 00033).
