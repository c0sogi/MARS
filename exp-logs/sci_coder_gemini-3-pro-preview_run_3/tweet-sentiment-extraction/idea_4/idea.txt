Based on the comprehensive analysis of the provided lessons and previous attempts, I propose the following optimized strategy. This solution shifts the focus from adding architectural complexity (which proved detrimental in Lesson {solution_lesson_node_00018}) to **scaling the backbone capacity** while rigorously enforcing the data and inference protocols that have been empirically proven to work.

### Model: DeBERTa-v3-Large with Simplified Head
**Structural Innovation (Exploration):**
I will upgrade the backbone from `DeBERTa-v3-base` to **`microsoft/deberta-v3-large`**. Previous experiments confirmed that DeBERTa's disentangled attention mechanism is superior for boundary detection Cite {solution_lesson_node_00014}, but attempts to improve it with complex heads (Weighted Layer Pooling) failed Cite {solution_lesson_node_00018}. By scaling to the "Large" architecture, we increase the model's capacity to resolve complex syntactic dependencies and sentiment contexts within the deep encoder layers themselves, rather than relying on noisy aggregation at the output.

**Head Design (Exploitation):**
I will strictly retain the **Simple Linear Head** acting on the final hidden layer. As learned, aggregating lower layers dilutes the spatial precision required for span extraction Cite {solution_lesson_node_00018}. The model will output two logits per token: `start_logit` and `end_logit`.

### Data
**Preprocessing:**
1.  **Raw Input Fidelity:** I will feed the **raw, unmodified text** into the tokenizer. I will explicitly avoid whitespace normalization or stripping, as this destroys structural cues and degrades performance Cite {solution_lesson_node_00020}.
2.  **Neutral Exclusion:** I will filter out all training samples with "neutral" sentiment. The model will focus exclusively on the non-trivial "positive" and "negative" cases Cite {solution_lesson_node_00006}.
3.  **Alignment Filtering:** I will remove training samples where the character-level `selected_text` cannot be cleanly mapped to token boundaries by the DeBERTa tokenizer, preventing the model from training on "impossible" targets Cite {solution_lesson_node_00016}.

**Target Generation:**
I will use a **Mask-Based Overlap** strategy. Instead of searching for exact start/end offsets, I will generate a binary mask of the character span and label any token with non-zero overlap as part of the target. This provides robust supervision for noisy annotations Cite {solution_lesson_node_00012}.

### Training
**Objective Function:**
The model will be trained using **Cross-Entropy Loss with Label Smoothing (0.1)**. This prevents overconfidence in subjective boundaries and improves generalization compared to standard Cross-Entropy or the failed Soft Jaccard loss Cite {solution_lesson_node_00010}.

**Constraints:**
To prevent overfitting on this small, noisy dataset:
-   **Epochs:** Strictly limited to **3 epochs**.
-   **Batch Size:** **32** (or equivalent via gradient accumulation if memory is constrained, though A100 should suffice).
-   **Sequence Length:** Capped at **96 tokens**.
These constraints match the data density and prevent memorization Cite {solution_lesson_node_00007}.

### Evaluation
**Inference Pipeline:**
1.  **Deterministic Rule:** If the sentiment is "neutral", the pipeline will bypass the model and return the entire `text` string. This heuristic is near-perfect for this class Cite {solution_lesson_node_00006}.
2.  **Summation Decoding:** For "positive" and "negative" predictions, I will select the span $(i, j)$ (where $i \le j$) that maximizes the **sum of probabilities** ($P_{start}(i) + P_{end}(j)$). This "soft voting" approach is more robust to boundary ambiguity than the joint probability product ($P_{start} \times P_{end}$) Cite {solution_lesson_node_00019}.
3.  **Ensembling:** I will implement a **5-Fold Cross-Validation Ensemble**. The final logits will be the average of 5 models trained on stratified folds. This stabilizes predictions without the complexity of architectural ensembles Cite {solution_lesson_node_00018}.
