To improve the model's performance, I will implement a targeted feature engineering strategy that enriches the kinematic representation of both player-player and player-ground interactions.

**Strategy Outline:**
1.  **Enhance Stream A (Player-Player) with Facing Alignment:** I will introduce `cos_p1_facing_p2` and `cos_p2_facing_p1` features. These calculate the cosine similarity between a player's orientation vector and the relative position vector to the other player. This explicitly models whether players are looking at or moving towards each other, which is critical for distinguishing intentional contact (tackles) from incidental proximity (Cite Lesson 00006: Explicit Interaction Dynamics).
2.  **Enhance Stream B (Player-Ground) with Centripetal Acceleration:** I will introduce a `centripetal_accel` feature, calculated as `speed * angular_velocity_of_direction`. This proxy for centripetal force captures high-intensity cutting maneuvers and sharp turns, which are physically unstable states often preceding ground contact (Cite Lesson 00027: Enrich Kinematic Models with Higher-Order Derivatives).
3.  **Configuration Update:** I will update `library/config.py` to include these new features in the respective feature lists (`STREAM_A_BASE_FEATURES` and `STREAM_B_BASE_FEATURES`), ensuring they are automatically picked up by the pipeline and triggering a cache invalidation (Cite Lesson 00008: Deterministic Input Structure).

This approach focuses on providing the XGBoost model with explicit physics-based signals that are difficult for the model to infer from raw coordinate data alone.
