# Optimized Strategy: Multi-Scale Feature Aggregation with Auxiliary Background Suppression

## Model
I propose a **Multi-Scale Feature Aggregation** architecture designed to explicitly capture the fine-grained spatial details of thin catheters that are often lost in the final downsampled layers of standard CNNs.

*   **Backbone:** I will retain the **EfficientNetV2-S** backbone, as it has proven effective in feature extraction. However, instead of utilizing only the final feature map (Stride 32), I will extract intermediate feature maps from **Stride 8, Stride 16, and Stride 32**.
*   **Feature Aggregation (Structural Innovation):** Standard Global Average Pooling (GAP) smoothes out high-frequency details (like thin lines) over the spatial dimensions. To address this, I will implement **Generalized Mean Pooling (GeM)** on each of the three extracted feature scales independently. GeM is a learnable pooling operation that can focus on salient features more effectively than standard average or max pooling. The pooled vectors from all three scales will be concatenated to form a rich, multi-scale representation vector before being passed to the final classification head.
*   **Auxiliary Head:** I will maintain the auxiliary segmentation head to force the encoder to learn spatial localization. This head will accept the fused multi-scale features and output a binary mask.

## Data
*   **Preprocessing (Geometry):** I will strictly apply **Aspect-Ratio Preserving Resizing (Letterboxing)** to a resolution of **640x640**. This ensures that the physical morphology of the catheters is not distorted by anisotropic scaling, which was shown to degrade performance on "wide" images **Cite solution_lesson_node_00001**.
*   **Preprocessing (Signal):** I will apply **CLAHE (Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization)** to the input images. This enhances the local contrast of the thin tubes against the thoracic background, working in synergy with the higher resolution to provide a stronger signal to the model **Cite solution_lesson_node_00004**.

## Training
*   **Loss Function:** The model will be trained using a weighted sum of **Binary Cross Entropy (BCE)** for the classification task and **Pixel-wise BCE** for the auxiliary segmentation task. I will explicitly avoid Dice Loss for the auxiliary head, as previous experiments demonstrated that pixel-wise BCE provides a superior background suppression signal that benefits the primary classification metric more effectively **Cite solution_lesson_node_00018**.
*   **Optimization:** I will use the AdamW optimizer with Cosine Annealing.
*   **Batch Size Management:** I will ensure the effective batch size remains **at least 32**. If the memory overhead of the multi-scale architecture requires a smaller physical batch size, I will utilize **Gradient Accumulation** to maintain the stability of the optimization trajectory, preventing the degradation associated with small-batch updates **Cite solution_lesson_node_00003**. I will also ensure Batch Normalization statistics are handled correctly (e.g., via SyncBN or by keeping the physical batch size above the critical threshold of 16) **Cite solution_lesson_node_00016**.

## Evaluation
*   **Inference:** During inference, the auxiliary segmentation head will be discarded. The input images will be preprocessed with the same letterboxing and CLAHE techniques. The model will output predictions based on the concatenated multi-scale feature vector, ensuring that both fine details (from Stride 8) and global context (from Stride 32) contribute to the final probability.
