To optimize the solution, I will implement the strategy of using **Fixed Feature Extraction** with lighter backbones instead of fine-tuning large models.

**Rationale:**
1.  **Revert Fine-Tuning (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014):** The previous execution showed that fine-tuning large backbones (`swin_large` and `efficientnetv2_l`) led to overfitting (Train RMSE ~8.6 vs Val RMSE ~18.88) and degradation compared to the baseline. I will skip the fine-tuning stage entirely and use the backbones with their original ImageNet weights.
2.  **Use Lighter Backbones (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014):** I will switch from the "Large" variants to `swin_base_patch4_window7_224` and `tf_efficientnetv2_m`. These models provide a better balance of capacity and generalization for this dataset size and match the configuration of the "Current Best Solution" described in the lessons.
3.  **Leverage Stacking on Deep Embeddings (Cite solution_lesson_node_00012):** The existing stacking ensemble (SVR+PCA, LightGBM, Ridge) is already well-suited for high-dimensional fixed embeddings. By feeding it robust, non-overfitted features from the frozen backbones, we expect to recover the performance (RMSE ~18.09).

1.  **`library/config.py`**: Update backbone constants to the Base/Medium variants.
2.  **`runfile.py`**: Remove the fine-tuning step. Instantiate the `AdaptiveBackbone` directly, set it to evaluation mode, and proceed immediately to feature extraction and stacking.
