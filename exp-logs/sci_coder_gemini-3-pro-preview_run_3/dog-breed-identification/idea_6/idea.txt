# Proposed Solution: Heterogeneous Stratified Ensemble with SWA and Temperature Calibration

## Model
We will implement a **Heterogeneous Ensemble** composed of two distinct architectures to maximize feature diversity and reduce prediction variance.
1.  **Primary Backbone: ConvNeXt-Small.** We retain this architecture as it has proven to be the optimal balance of capacity and generalization for this specific dataset, significantly outperforming smaller variants (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009) while avoiding the overfitting issues of larger ones (Cite solution_lesson_node_00013).
2.  **Secondary Backbone: Swin Transformer (Tiny or Small).** We introduce a Vision Transformer architecture to complement the ConvNeXt CNN. While ConvNeXt excels at local texture extraction (fur patterns), Swin Transformers utilize self-attention to capture global shape and context. Ensembling models with such distinct inductive biases is a structural innovation that typically yields higher gains than ensembling identical architectures.
3.  **Heads:** Both backbones will feed into simple linear classification heads initialized randomly.

## Data
We will enforce the **Geometric Diversity** preprocessing pipeline that was identified as critical for performance.
*   **Augmentation:** The pipeline will strictly follow the sequence: **`RandomResizedCrop`** $\rightarrow$ **`RandomHorizontalFlip`** $\rightarrow$ **`RandAugment`**.
    *   We retain `RandomResizedCrop` to prevent the overfitting observed with static resizing (Cite solution_lesson_node_00012).
    *   We apply `RandomHorizontalFlip` *before* `RandAugment` to ensure the model learns spatial symmetries (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014).
*   **Resolution:** Inputs will be fixed at **224x224**. We explicitly reject scaling to higher resolutions (e.g., 384x384) as previous experiments showed this necessitates reducing batch size, which destabilizes training and degrades performance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016).
*   **Targets:** We will use **Hard Labels** (0 or 1) and strictly avoid Label Smoothing. This ensures the training objective remains perfectly aligned with the Log Loss evaluation metric (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).

## Training
We will employ a **5-Fold Stratified Cross-Validation** strategy with **Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA)**.
*   **Phases:** We will continue the **Two-Phase Transfer Learning** strategy (Freeze Backbone $\rightarrow$ Fine-tune) to stabilize the random head before updating features (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
*   **Schedule:** We will use a **Cosine Annealing** scheduler with `T_max=30` epochs to allow for deep convergence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007, Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).
*   **SWA (Structural Innovation):** In the final 25% of the training epochs, we will activate Stochastic Weight Averaging. SWA averages the weights of the model at different points in the optimization trajectory, converging to a flatter minimum in the loss landscape. This improves model calibration and generalization on unseen data without the computational cost of TTA during training.
*   **Loss:** Standard **Cross-Entropy Loss** will be used for both optimization and validation metric tracking (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).

## Evaluation
*   **Ensemble Inference:** We will generate predictions using all trained models (across folds and architectures).
*   **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):** For each model, we will average predictions from the original image and a horizontally flipped version.
*   **Temperature Scaling (Structural Innovation):** Before aggregating the ensemble, we will perform post-hoc calibration. Using the Out-Of-Fold (OOF) validation predictions, we will optimize a scalar **Temperature ($T$)** parameter for each model such that the Cross-Entropy of $Softmax(logits / T)$ is minimized. This explicitly aligns the predicted probabilities with the empirical accuracy, directly optimizing the final Multi-Class Log Loss score.
