# Proposed Solution: Homogeneous Stratified Ensemble with Manual Weight Averaging

## Model
We will implement a **Homogeneous Ensemble** consisting of 5 independent instances of the **ConvNeXt-Small** architecture.
*   **Architecture Choice:** We strictly adhere to **ConvNeXt-Small** as the backbone. It has been empirically identified as the optimal capacity "sweet spot" for this dataset, significantly outperforming smaller variants (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009) while avoiding the overfitting and optimization instability observed with larger "Base" and "Large" variants (Cite solution_lesson_node_00013, Cite solution_lesson_node_00016).
*   **Ensemble Strategy:** We reject the heterogeneous approach (mixing different architectures) which previously diluted performance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00022). Instead, we employ a homogeneous design where all models share the optimal architecture but are trained on different data splits (Bagging), maximizing the stability of the predictions.

## Data
We will enforce the rigid preprocessing pipeline identified as critical for this specific data regime.
*   **Preprocessing Pipeline:** The training data will undergo **`RandomResizedCrop`** $\rightarrow$ **`RandomHorizontalFlip`** $\rightarrow$ **`RandAugment`**.
    *   We explicitly retain `RandomResizedCrop` to prevent the overfitting caused by static resizing (Cite solution_lesson_node_00012).
    *   We ensure `RandomHorizontalFlip` is applied *before* `RandAugment` to guarantee the model learns essential spatial symmetries (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014).
*   **Resolution:** Inputs will be fixed at **224x224**. We explicitly reject scaling to higher resolutions (e.g., 384x384) as previous experiments proved this necessitates reducing batch size, which destabilizes training (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016).
*   **Targets:** We will use **Hard Labels** (0 or 1) and strictly avoid Label Smoothing. This ensures the training objective maximizes the Log Loss metric without artificial penalties (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).

## Training
We will employ a **5-Fold Stratified Cross-Validation** strategy enhanced by **Manual Weight Averaging**.
*   **Procedure:** We will train 5 separate models, each on a distinct 80% fold of the training data.
*   **Phases:** We will use the **Two-Phase Transfer Learning** strategy: freeze the backbone for 1 epoch to align the head, then fine-tune the entire network (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
*   **Optimization:** We will use a **Conservative Learning Rate** of `1e-5` for the fine-tuning phase, rejecting aggressive rates or complex layer-wise decay schemes that disrupt pre-trained features (Cite solution_lesson_node_00022). The schedule will use **Cosine Annealing** over 30 epochs (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
*   **Structural Innovation (Manual Weight Averaging):** To capture the generalization benefits of Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA) without the implementation risks that caused previous failures (Cite solution_lesson_node_00021), we will implement **Manual Weight Averaging**. Instead of using a wrapper like `AveragedModel`, we will save checkpoints for the final 5 epochs of training. After training concludes, we will mathematically average the state dictionaries of these 5 checkpoints to create the final model for that fold. This "Model Soup" approach flattens the loss landscape and improves calibration on unseen data.
*   **Loss:** We will use standard **Cross-Entropy Loss** for both optimization and validation to ensure metric coupling (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).

## Evaluation
*   **Inference:** We will generate predictions using the **Manually Averaged** models from all 5 folds.
*   **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):** For each model, we will predict probabilities for the original image and a **horizontally flipped** version. These two views will be averaged to reduce prediction variance.
*   **Aggregation:** The final submission will be the arithmetic mean of the probabilities from all 5 folds. This combination of Bagging (across folds), TTA (across views), and Weight Averaging (across epochs) will minimize the Log Loss.
