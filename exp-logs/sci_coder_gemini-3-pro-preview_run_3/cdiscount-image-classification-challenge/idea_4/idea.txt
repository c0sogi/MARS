# Baseline Proposal: Full-Scale ResNet-50 with Dual-Statistic Pooling

### Model
I propose a **Dual-Statistic Aggregation Network** that enhances the feature representation capability while maintaining the computational efficiency of decoupled learning.
*   **Backbone:** Upgrade the feature extractor to **ResNet-50** (pre-trained on ImageNet). This provides a deeper, more expressive feature space (2048 dimensions) compared to the ResNet-18 used previously, essential for distinguishing 5,270 fine-grained categories. The backbone will remain **frozen**.
*   **Aggregation (Structural Innovation):** Instead of the simple Mean Pooling (Idea 2) or the complex Attention mechanism (Idea 3), I will implement **Dual-Statistic Pooling**. For every product, the model will compute both the **element-wise Mean** and the **element-wise Maximum** of the image feature vectors. These two vectors will be concatenated to form a single 4096-dimensional product embedding.
    *   *Rationale:* Mean pooling reduces noise but can "wash out" strong, distinct features (like a specific logo on one side of a box). Max pooling preserves the strongest activations regardless of which image they appear in. Combining them captures both the holistic appearance and the most salient details.
*   **Classifier:** A Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) with Batch Normalization and Dropout will process the 4096-dimensional input to predict the 5270 categories.

### Data
*   **Full-Dataset Decoupled Caching (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003):** I will apply the decoupled feature extraction strategy to the **entire dataset** (7 million products), rather than a small subset.
    *   **Process:** Iterate through `train.bson` and `test.bson` using the frozen ResNet-50 on the GPU. Extract features for every image.
    *   **Storage:** Calculate the Mean and Max vectors for each product on-the-fly and store the resulting aggregated embeddings in RAM.
    *   **Feasibility:** Storing 7 million product embeddings (4096 floats each) requires approximately 115 GB of RAM. This fits comfortably within the provided 220 GB, allowing us to train on the full dataset to eliminate the overfitting observed in previous small-scale experiments.

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** Use standard **Cross-Entropy Loss**. I will explicitly **avoid** using inverse-frequency class weights. As established in previous experiments, weighting for class imbalance optimizes for macro-recall, which harms the target metric of global categorization accuracy (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).
*   **Optimization:** Train the MLP using the **AdamW** optimizer on the in-memory dataset.
*   **Regularization:** Given the increased dimensionality (4096 inputs) and the use of the full dataset, I will use Batch Normalization and moderate Dropout (p=0.25) to maintain generalization without underfitting.

### Evaluation
*   **Inference:**
    1.  Load the pre-computed, aggregated test set embeddings from RAM.
    2.  Pass them through the trained MLP to generate logits.
    3.  Select the category with the highest score.
*   This approach ensures that inference remains a fast, matrix-multiplication-only task, completing in seconds.
