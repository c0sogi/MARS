# Optimized Strategy: Stabilized Dual-Graph Network with Continuous Filter Convolutions

This strategy synthesizes the structural benefits of Line Graphs (to capture angular geometry) with the expressive power of Continuous Filter Convolutions (proven in Lesson 00007), while strictly adhering to numerical stability guidelines (Lesson 00008) and handling target heterogeneity (Lesson 00003).

### Model
1.  **Backbone: Dual-Graph Architecture (Atom + Line Graph)**
    *   The model will operate on two hierarchical graphs to explicitly model 3-body interactions (angles) which are critical for Karplus-like coupling effects.
        *   **Atom Graph:** Nodes are atoms; edges are bonds (distances).
        *   **Line Graph:** Nodes are the *bonds* of the Atom Graph; edges connect adjacent bonds (representing angles).
    *   **Stable Feature Lifting:**
        *   **Radial Basis:** In the Atom Graph, scalar Euclidean distances will be expanded into high-dimensional vectors using **Gaussian Radial Basis Functions (RBF)**. This allows the model to learn non-linear distance filters **Cite solution_lesson_node_00007**.
        *   **Angular Basis:** In the Line Graph, we will compute the cosine of the bond angles ($\cos \theta_{ijk}$). To avoid the catastrophic instability of recursive Spherical Bessel Functions, we will expand these cosine values using **Gaussian RBFs** defined over the interval $[-1, 1]$ **Cite solution_lesson_node_00008**. This provides a numerically stable, high-dimensional representation of angular geometry.

2.  **Interaction Mechanism: Continuous Filter Convolutions**
    *   We will replace standard graph convolutions with **Continuous Filter Convolutions** in both graphs.
    *   **Filter Generation:** For every interaction, a "Filter Generator" MLP will take the lifted geometric features (Radial RBFs for atoms, Angular RBFs for bonds) and output a dense weight matrix. This dynamically modulates the information flow based on precise geometric configurations **Cite solution_lesson_node_00007**.
    *   **Flow:** Information will propagate from the Line Graph (updating bond representations based on angles) to the Atom Graph (updating atom representations based on bonds), enabling the flow of angular information into the atomic latent space.

3.  **Readout Architecture**
    *   **Type-Specific Prediction Heads:** Recognizing the distinct physical mechanisms of different couplings, the model will use **8 separate MLP heads**. The final representation of the interacting atom pair will be routed to the specific head corresponding to its coupling type (e.g., `1JHC`, `3JHH`).
    *   **Auxiliary Physics Heads:** To regularize the model and enforce chemical realism, auxiliary heads will be attached to the atom representations to predict **Magnetic Shielding Tensors** and **Mulliken Charges**.

### Data
1.  **Preprocessing**:
    *   Construct **Atom Graphs** (cutoff 5.0 Å) and corresponding **Line Graphs** (connecting edges that share an atom).
    *   Precompute Gaussian RBF expansions for distances and angular cosines.
2.  **Target Standardization**:
    *   We will strictly apply **Per-Group Target Standardization**.
    *   Compute the mean ($\mu_{type}$) and standard deviation ($\sigma_{type}$) for the `scalar_coupling_constant` independently for each of the 8 coupling types.
    *   Transform the training targets: $z = \frac{y - \mu_{type}}{\sigma_{type}}$. This ensures that high-magnitude types (like `1JHC`) do not dominate the gradients **Cite solution_lesson_node_00003**.

### Training
1.  **Loss Function**:
    *   Use a **Multi-Task Loss**: $L_{total} = L_{coupling} + \lambda_{phys} (L_{shielding} + L_{charge})$.
    *   $L_{coupling}$ is the Mean Absolute Error (L1) on the standardized targets.
2.  **Optimizer**:
    *   **AdamW** with a **Cosine Annealing Warm Restarts** scheduler to efficiently converge within the 24-hour limit.

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference**:
    *   Pass test structures through the Dual-Graph Network.
    *   Route the resulting embeddings to the appropriate **Type-Specific Head** to generate the standardized prediction $z_{pred}$.
2.  **Inverse Transformation**:
    *   Convert the predictions back to the original physical scale using the pre-computed training statistics: $y_{pred} = z_{pred} \cdot \sigma_{type} + \mu_{type}$ **Cite solution_lesson_node_00003**.
3.  **Submission**:
    *   Combine the IDs with the restored scalar coupling constants into the final CSV.
