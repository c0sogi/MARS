Based on the accumulated lessons, I propose a **Multi-Resolution Heterogeneous Ensemble with Asynchronous Training Schedules**.

This strategy structurally evolves the "Current Best" (Idea 8) by addressing the specific weaknesses identified in the "Third Pillar" (Transformer) and the "Scaling" experiments. Instead of forcing all models to a single resolution or training duration, we will decouple these parameters to match the specific inductive biases and convergence profiles of each architecture.

### Model
**Architecture:** Implement a **Heterogeneous Ensemble** consisting of three distinct backbones, selected to maximize error decorrelation (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016):
1.  **ResNet-50:** The "Standard CNN" anchor. Provides robust, texture-based feature extraction (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
2.  **ConvNeXt-Small:** The "Modern CNN". Captures semantic context via large kernels. We retain this high-performing model but scale its input fidelity.
3.  **MaxViT-Tiny:** The "Multi-Axis Transformer". **Structural Innovation.** This replaces Swin-Tiny. MaxViT employs Block (Local) and Grid (Global) attention, offering a stronger global context mechanism than Swin's local windows. We explicitly correct previous failures (Idea 10, 14) by stripping away complex heads (MSD) and distillation, relying instead on a tailored training schedule to ensure convergence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00034).

**Initialization:** Use the highest quality pre-trained weights available via the `timm` library (e.g., `resnet50.a1_in1k`, `convnext_small.fb_in1k`, `maxvit_tiny_tf_224.in1k`) to strictly adhere to the lesson that modern recipes outperform legacy weights (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).

### Data
**Preprocessing (Multi-Resolution Decoupling):** Implement three distinct input pipelines. This introduces **Resolution Diversity** into the ensemble, allowing models to capture features at different scales (Cite solution_lesson_node_00028, solution_lesson_node_00005):
1.  **Pipeline A (ResNet-50):** Resize to **256x256**. Balances detail with the standard receptive field of ResNet.
2.  **Pipeline B (ConvNeXt-Small):** Resize to **288x288**. **Innovation:** We push the resolution higher for this capable modern CNN to exploit fine-grained details, avoiding the diminishing returns of 320px+ seen in Idea 9/31.
3.  **Pipeline C (MaxViT-Tiny):** Resize to **224x224**. Matches the native grid structure of the Transformer to avoid interpolation artifacts (Cite solution_lesson_node_00028).

**Augmentation:** Apply **Context-Preserving Augmentation** across all pipelines.
*   Use `RandomResizedCrop` with a scale of `(0.8, 1.0)` to ensure the subject is never cropped out (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).
*   Apply `ColorJitter` with intensity $\ge 0.2$ to handle lighting variance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00024).
*   Strictly use **Bicubic Interpolation** for all resizing (Cite solution_lesson_node_00024).

### Training
**Strategy:** Train each model independently with **Asynchronous Schedules**.
*   **Rationale:** Previous attempts failed because they treated all architectures identically. Transformers (MaxViT) typically require more optimization steps to align their complex attention maps than CNNs.
*   **Optimizer:** **AdamW** with **Cosine Annealing** scheduler (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
*   **Loss Function:** **BCEWithLogitsLoss** *without* Label Smoothing (Cite solution_lesson_node_00012).
*   **Schedules:**
    *   **ResNet-50 & ConvNeXt-Small:** Train for **8 epochs**. This is sufficient for CNN convergence on this dataset (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
    *   **MaxViT-Tiny:** Train for **12-15 epochs**. This extended horizon compensates for the slower convergence of the Multi-Axis attention mechanism, preventing the underfitting observed in previous Transformer attempts (Cite solution_lesson_node_00034).

### Evaluation
**Inference & Aggregation:**
1.  **Test Time Augmentation (TTA):** Generate probabilities using both the original and a horizontally flipped version for each model. Average these pairs to stabilize predictions (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
2.  **Ensemble Averaging:** Calculate the final probability by taking the **arithmetic mean** of the outputs from the three models. This combines the benefits of architectural diversity (CNN/ModernCNN/Transformer), resolution diversity (256/288/224), and optimized convergence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016).
