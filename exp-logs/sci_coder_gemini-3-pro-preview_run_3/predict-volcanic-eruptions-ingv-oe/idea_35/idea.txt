Based on the rigorous analysis of the "Lessons" and the empirical progression of "Previous Ideas," I propose the **Single-Stream Hybrid-Granularity Decomposition Strategy**.

This approach structurally evolves the "Current Best" Idea 15 by synthesizing its successful components (Single High-Capacity Model, Dense Quantiles, Flattened Windows) with critical corrective innovations derived from subsequent failures. Specifically, it integrates **Wavelet Texture Analysis** (to correct the simplicity of residuals, Cite solution_lesson_node_00060) while strictly enforcing **Large-Window Smoothing** (to prevent frequency leakage, Cite solution_lesson_node_00070) and **High-Resolution Spectral Extraction** (to prevent aliasing, Cite solution_lesson_node_00082). It explicitly reverts to **Dense Quantiles on Derivatives** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00059), rejecting the "Moment-based" approach that failed in Idea 34 (Cite solution_lesson_node_00106).

# Model
The predictive core will be a **Single, Highly-Optimized LightGBM Regressor**.

*   **Architecture:** A single LightGBM model with high capacity (e.g., `num_leaves=128`, `learning_rate` < 0.05).
*   **Rationale:** Lesson 00106 demonstrated that in data-scarce regimes (low sample-to-feature ratio), training on the full dataset significantly outperforms Cross-Validation Ensembling (Bagging) because the loss of training data in CV folds harms manifold learning more than variance reduction helps. The single-model approach of Idea 15 remains the benchmark to beat (Cite solution_lesson_node_00106).
*   **Objective Function:** The model will be trained using **L2 Loss (Mean Squared Error)**.
    *   **Rationale:** Lesson 00060 proved that optimizing L2 Loss yields better convergence and lower final MAE than L1 Loss due to superior gradient dynamics (informative gradients vs. constant sign gradients).

# Data
The data processing pipeline implements a **Hybrid-Granularity Orthogonal Decomposition** to extract physically distinct signal components using the most effective transform for each domain.

1.  **Preprocessing:**
    *   **Imputation:** Fill missing values with the segment-wise column mean to preserve DC offsets (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
    *   **Decomposition:** For each sensor, generate three distinct signal views:
        *   **View A (Trend):** Apply a **Savitzky-Golay filter** with a **large window size (e.g., 51)** and a **Polynomial Order of 2 (Quadratic)**.
            *   *Correction:* Strictly use **Window 51** to isolate baseline drift (Cite solution_lesson_node_00070) and **Order 2** to prevent fitting local noise artifacts (Cite solution_lesson_node_00094).
        *   **View B (Texture):** Apply a **Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT)** (e.g., `db4`) to the **Residuals** ($Raw - Trend$) and extract Detail Coefficients.
            *   *Structural Innovation:* This replaces simple residual statistics with multi-resolution texture analysis, capturing signal roughness more effectively (Cite solution_lesson_node_00060).
        *   **View C (Raw):** Retain the original **Raw** signal.

2.  **Feature Engineering (The Hybrid-Granularity Paradigm):**
    *   **From View A (Trend & Kinematics - Dense Granularity):**
        *   Compute **First (Velocity)** and **Second (Acceleration)** derivatives of the Trend.
        *   Extract a **Dense Grid of Quantiles** (e.g., 1%, 5%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 95%, 99%) for **Trend**, **Velocity**, and **Acceleration**.
        *   *Rationale:* Reverting to Idea 15's strategy. Lesson 00059 and Lesson 00106 confirm that dense quantiles on smoothed derivatives outperform moments (kurtosis), which tend to correlate with error.
    *   **From View B (Wavelet Texture):** Extract **Energy**, **Entropy**, and **Quantiles** from the DWT Detail Coefficients (Cite solution_lesson_node_00049).
    *   **From View C (Absolute Intensity):** Extract **Global Minimum**, **Global Maximum**, and **Peak-to-Peak Range**.
        *   *Rationale:* Strictly preserves the absolute intensity of the event, adhering to the lesson that "outliers are the signal" (Cite solution_lesson_node_00031).
    *   **From View C (High-Resolution Spectral Structure):** Compute **PSD Band Power** using **Welch's Method** with **Closed Intervals** (Low: 0.1-3Hz, Mid: 3-10Hz, High: 10-45Hz).
        *   *Constraint:* Explicitly set the window size (`nperseg`) to **1024** to ensure at least 10-20 frequency bins fall within the critical 0.1-3Hz Low band (Cite solution_lesson_node_00082).
    *   **From View C (Flattened Temporal Profiling):**
        *   Divide the signal into $N$ non-overlapping windows. Compute **RMS** and **Mean** for each.
        *   **Flatten** these into explicit features (e.g., `w1_rms`, `w2_rms`) to preserve the "arrow of time" (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).

3.  **Feature Organization:**
    *   **Spatial Concatenation:** Concatenate features for all 10 sensors side-by-side. Do **not** aggregate across sensors (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).

# Training
*   **Strategy:** Train on the largest possible data split (e.g., 90% Train / 10% Validation) to maximize the sample-to-feature ratio (Cite solution_lesson_node_00106).
*   **Optimization:** Use **Early Stopping** based on the Validation MAE (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
*   **Hyperparameters:** Set `num_leaves=128` to leverage the high capacity of the single model (Cite solution_lesson_node_00075).
