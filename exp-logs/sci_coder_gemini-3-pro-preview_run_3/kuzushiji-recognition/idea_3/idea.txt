Based on the provided lessons and the analysis of previous attempts, I propose a **High-Resolution Tiled Two-Stage Pipeline**. This strategy addresses the primary bottleneck identified in **Lesson 00011** (resolution loss due to downscaling) and leverages the structural necessity of a two-stage approach established in **Lesson 00001**.

### Model
I propose a structural upgrade to the detector's backbone and a shift in the inference paradigm.

1.  **Stage 1: High-Resolution Detector (HRNet-W32)**
    *   **Architecture:** Replace the ResNet-FPN backbone with **HRNet-W32 (High-Resolution Network)**. Unlike ResNet, which downsamples the feature map significantly (losing spatial fidelity for small characters), HRNet maintains high-resolution representations throughout the network by connecting multi-resolution subnetworks in parallel. This is critical for dense text detection where characters can be as small as 10-20 pixels.
    *   **Heads:** Retain the **Heatmap**, **Offset**, and **Size** heads. The improved spatial precision of HRNet will directly improve the Heatmap quality, addressing the stagnation seen in **Lesson 00014**.
    *   **Rationale:** This structural change prioritizes spatial detail preservation over abstract semantic feature extraction, directly countering the "downscaling bottleneck" Cite solution_lesson_node_00011.

2.  **Stage 2: Robust Classifier (ResNet-34)**
    *   **Architecture:** Retain the **ResNet-34** classifier. As noted in **Lesson 00012**, this model has sufficient capacity to converge on the 3,848 classes (~90% accuracy).
    *   **Input:** $64 \times 64$ crops extracted based on Stage 1 predictions.

### Data
The data pipeline will be fundamentally altered to support **Native-Resolution Processing**, moving away from the "Global Resize" paradigm.

1.  **Detector Data (Patch-Based):**
    *   **Training Input:** Instead of resizing the full page (approx. 2000x3000) to $1024 \times 1024$, generate training samples by extracting random **$1024 \times 1024$ crops** from the *original resolution* images. This ensures the detector learns features at the native scale of the document.
    *   **Augmentation:**
        *   **Scale:** Apply aggressive random scaling ($\pm 30\%$) to these patches to handle variable character sizes. Cite solution_lesson_node_00011.
        *   **Rotation:** Strictly limit rotation to $\pm 5^\circ$ to respect the vertical text structure. Cite solution_lesson_node_00007.
        *   **Mosaic:** Optionally combine 4 smaller crops into one training image to simulate complex layouts and improve batch efficiency.

2.  **Classifier Data:**
    *   Extract crops using Ground Truth boxes from the training set. Resize to $64 \times 64$. Use a **Class-Balanced Sampler** to ensure rare characters are adequately represented. Cite solution_lesson_node_00001.

### Training
1.  **Detector Training:**
    *   Train for **20+ epochs**. As observed in **Lesson 00012**, regression heads (Size/Offset) require longer to converge than classification heads.
    *   Monitor **Heatmap Loss** specifically, not just total loss, to ensure the model is improving its recall of character centers. Cite solution_lesson_node_00014.

2.  **Classifier Training:**
    *   Train independently using Cross Entropy Loss.

### Evaluation
The inference strategy will be adapted to match the patch-based training:

1.  **Tiled Inference:**
    *   Split each test image into overlapping tiles (e.g., $1024 \times 1024$ tiles with 25% overlap).
    *   Run the **HRNet Detector** on each tile. This allows the model to detect characters at full resolution, avoiding the artifacts introduced by global downscaling.
2.  **Global NMS:**
    *   Translate tile-relative coordinates back to the global image coordinate system.
    *   Apply **Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS)** to merge duplicate detections from overlapping tile regions.
3.  **Crop & Classify:**
    *   Use the refined global bounding boxes to crop characters from the original high-res image.
    *   Pass crops to the Stage 2 Classifier to obtain final labels.
4.  **Submission:**
    *   Format the output as `image_id, {label X Y} ...` using the center points from Stage 1 and labels from Stage 2.
