### Model
I propose a **Hybrid Ensemble with Multi-Level Feature Aggregation**. This strategy introduces a structural innovation in the classification head design to explicitly address the challenge of detecting "small, localized, and sparse" disease lesions (like rust spots) which can be lost in the final stages of deep networks.

1.  **Backbone 1: EfficientNet-B4 with Multi-Level Pooling:**
    *   Standard CNN classifiers typically utilize Global Average Pooling (GAP) only on the final feature map (lowest spatial resolution). For small disease lesions, this aggressive downsampling can dilute discriminative signals.
    *   **Innovation:** We will implement a **Multi-Level Pooling Head**. We will extract feature maps from the last three reduction stages of the EfficientNet-B4 (e.g., reduction factors 16x, and 32x). We will apply Global Average Pooling to each stage independently, concatenate the resulting vectors, and feed this fused, multi-scale representation into the final fully connected layer. This ensures that fine-grained texture details from earlier layers are directly available for classification.
2.  **Backbone 2: Swin Transformer (Tiny):**
    *   To provide architectural diversity, we will employ a **Swin Transformer**. Unlike the ConvNeXt (used in previous ideas) or EfficientNet, Swin utilizes **Shifted Window Attention**. This mechanism computes self-attention within local windows, making it inherently effective at modeling the relationships between local features (lesions) while maintaining computational efficiency.
3.  **Ensemble Structure:** The final solution will be a "Bagging" ensemble derived from a **5-Fold Stratified Cross-Validation** setup, resulting in 10 total models (5 EfficientNet variants + 5 Swin variants).

### Data
1.  **Preprocessing:**
    *   **EfficientNet-B4 Pipeline:** Images will be resized to **380x380** to maximize the resolution of small features.
    *   **Swin Transformer Pipeline:** Images will be resized to **224x224** to align with the standard window configurations of the architecture.
2.  **Augmentation:**
    *   We will employ a **Strong Geometric Augmentation** strategy, including `ShiftScaleRotate` (with wide scaling limits), `RandomFlip`, and `Transpose`. We will strictly maintain high intensity to force the learning of invariant features **Cite solution_lesson_node_00003**.
    *   Crucially, we will **strictly exclude** any spatial occlusion augmentations (such as Cutout, CoarseDropout, or GridMask). The removal of image patches risks deleting the only visual evidence of small disease lesions, creating noisy labels **Cite solution_lesson_node_00004**.

### Training
1.  **Loss Function:** We will use **Cross-Entropy Loss with Inverse Frequency Class Weights**. This aligns the optimization objective with the macro-averaged evaluation metric by penalizing misclassifications of the minority "multiple_diseases" class (approx. 5% of data) more heavily than majority classes **Cite solution_lesson_node_00001**.
2.  **Optimization:** We will use the **AdamW** optimizer with a Cosine Annealing learning rate scheduler for both architectures.
3.  **Execution:** We will train each of the 5 folds independently. For each fold, we will save the model checkpoint that achieves the highest validation ROC AUC.

### Evaluation
1.  **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):** During inference, we will generate predictions for the original test image, a horizontally flipped version, and a vertically flipped version. These predictions will be averaged for each model instance to reduce variance and marginalize out framing artifacts **Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**.
2.  **Final Prediction:** The submission will be generated by averaging the probability vectors from all 10 trained models (2 architectures $\times$ 5 folds). This extensive bagging approach minimizes the risk of overfitting to the small training set.
