Based on the lessons learned from the previous experiments, I propose the **Feature Pyramid Ensemble with Deep Supervision**. This strategy structurally evolves the previous best approach (Idea 11) by replacing the simple concatenation of multi-level features with a **Feature Pyramid Network (FPN)**. This explicitly addresses the "small, localized" nature of the disease lesions by enriching high-resolution texture features with global semantic context before pooling.

### Model
I propose a **Heterogeneous Ensemble of Fused-Pyramid Experts**. This design retains the diverse backbones from Idea 11 but fundamentally alters the feature aggregation mechanism to maximize the detection of small lesions.

1.  **Backbones:**
    *   **EfficientNetV2-M (Texture Expert):** We utilize the EfficientNetV2-M backbone for its Fused-MBConv blocks, which excel at extracting high-frequency textural details (e.g., rust edges).
    *   **MaxViT-Small (Global Expert):** We utilize the MaxViT-Small backbone for its Multi-Axis Attention (Block + Grid), which captures long-range dependencies across the leaf surface.
    *   **Stability:** We wrap both models in **Model Exponential Moving Average (EMA)** to smooth the optimization trajectory and handle the variance inherent in these high-capacity architectures on small data **Cite solution_lesson_node_00016**.

2.  **Structural Innovation: Feature Pyramid Aggregation:**
    *   **Problem:** Previous ideas independently pooled features from different stages (e.g., stride 8, 16, 32) and concatenated them. This isolates the high-resolution features (which are semantically weak) from the low-resolution features (which are semantically strong but lack spatial detail).
    *   **Solution:** We implement a **Feature Pyramid Network (FPN)** head.
        *   We extract feature maps from the last three stages of the backbone.
        *   We create a **Top-Down Pathway**: The deepest, semantically strongest feature map is upsampled and added element-wise to the preceding stage's map (via lateral 1x1 convolutions).
        *   **Effect:** This propagates strong semantic class information (e.g., "this is a disease") down to the high-resolution layers that contain the fine spatial details (e.g., "here is the rust spot"), significantly improving the representation of small, localized lesions **Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**.

3.  **Head Design:**
    *   **Pooling:** Instead of standard pooling, we apply **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling** ($p=3$) to *each* of the fused FPN levels. This retains the sensitivity to high-activation anomalies (lesions) **Cite solution_lesson_node_00018**.
    *   **Deep Supervision:** We attach auxiliary classification heads to the outputs of the intermediate FPN levels. This forces the model to learn discriminative features at all scales, not just the final output.

### Data
1.  **Preprocessing:**
    *   **EfficientNetV2 Pipeline:** Resize images to **512x512** to maximize the retention of minute feature details.
    *   **MaxViT Pipeline:** Resize images to **384x384** to balance resolution with the computational cost of the attention mechanism.
2.  **Augmentation:**
    *   We employ a **Strong Geometric Augmentation** strategy (`ShiftScaleRotate`, `RandomFlip`) with high intensity to act as the primary regularizer **Cite solution_lesson_node_00003**.
    *   **Strict Exclusions:**
        *   **No Spatial Occlusion:** We strictly exclude Cutout/CoarseDropout. Removing patches risks deleting the sparse disease lesions that define the class **Cite solution_lesson_node_00004**.
        *   **No Photometric Augmentation:** We strictly exclude Brightness/Contrast shifts to preserve the specific color signals (e.g., rust red vs. scab brown) critical for diagnosis **Cite solution_lesson_node_00014**.

### Training
1.  **Loss Function:** We use **Deeply Supervised Cross-Entropy Loss**.
    *   $L_{total} = L_{final} + 0.5 \cdot (L_{fpn\_level\_1} + L_{fpn\_level\_2})$.
    *   We apply **Inverse Frequency Class Weights** to all loss components to prioritize the minority "multiple_diseases" class **Cite solution_lesson_node_00001**.
2.  **Optimization:**
    *   **Optimizer:** AdamW with **Cosine Annealing** scheduler.
    *   **Schedule:** We train for **25 epochs** with a **Relaxed Patience of 10 epochs**. This extended patience is crucial to allow the EMA weights to converge and prevents premature termination during the high-variance phase of the Cosine schedule **Cite solution_lesson_node_00008**.
    *   **Precision:** We use **Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP)** to enable the training of these larger backbones at higher resolutions (512/384) on the A100 GPU **Cite solution_lesson_node_00019**.
3.  **Cross-Validation:** The strategy is implemented using **5-Fold Stratified Cross-Validation**, resulting in 10 trained models (2 architectures $\times$ 5 folds).

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference Weights:** We generate predictions using the **EMA weights**, which provide a more robust estimate of the model parameters than the final training step.
2.  **Domain-Aware Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):**
    *   We generate predictions for the **Original** image and a **Horizontal Flip**.
    *   **Constraint:** We explicitly **exclude Vertical Flips and Transpose**. These transformations violate the natural gravity priors of leaf images (stems typically point up/down), creating "unnatural" inputs that degrade inference accuracy **Cite solution_lesson_node_00012**.
3.  **Ensemble:** The final submission is the unweighted average of the probability vectors from all 10 models.
