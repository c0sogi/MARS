I will optimize the solution by removing the heterogeneous stacking ensemble and focusing resources on the strongest architecture (`convnext_tiny`). The previous solution suffered from under-convergence (only 3 epochs) and dilution of performance by a weaker ensemble member (`efficientnetv2_s`).

My strategy involves:
1.  **Simplification**: Restricting the model backbone to `convnext_tiny` only. This removes the need for meta-learner training and stacking (Cite {lesson_lesson_node_00015}).
2.  **Convergence**: Increasing the training duration to 20 epochs per fold. The previous run showed that 3 epochs were insufficient, while prior lessons indicated that 20 epochs allow `convnext_tiny` to reach its full potential (Cite {lesson_lesson_node_00014}).
3.  **Robust Evaluation**: Replacing the stacking logic with simple averaging of the 5-fold models (K-Fold Ensembling). This leverages the entire training dataset and reduces variance (Cite {lesson_lesson_node_00013}).

The training will run for 5 folds * 20 epochs, which fits comfortably within the 3.5-hour remaining window (estimated ~110 minutes).
