Based on the progression of experiments and the specific lessons learned, I propose a **Converged Heterogeneous Stacking Ensemble**. This strategy addresses the limitations of the previous "Naive Ensembling" attempt (Idea 2) and the "Homogeneous Ensemble" (Idea 3) by combining architecturally diverse models, ensuring they are fully converged, and utilizing a learned aggregation strategy to maximize performance.

# Model
I propose a **Heterogeneous Ensemble** comprising two distinct architectures to capture complementary feature representations:
1.  **ConvNeXt-Tiny:** Retained as the primary backbone. Its modern, Transformer-inspired design (large 7x7 kernels, LayerNorm) has been empirically proven to yield the highest single-model performance on this dataset (Cite solution_lesson_node_00013).
2.  **DenseNet-121:** Introduced as the secondary backbone. Unlike the residual summation of ResNets or the stage-wise design of ConvNeXt, DenseNet uses dense concatenation connectivity. This architecture is historically effective in medical imaging for preserving gradients and maximizing feature reuse, providing a distinct inductive bias from ConvNeXt.

To address the "Naive Ensembling" failure where disparate performers degraded accuracy (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015), I will implement a **Stacking Meta-Learner**. Instead of simple arithmetic averaging, a **Logistic Regression** model will be trained on the Out-Of-Fold (OOF) predictions of the base models to learn the optimal weighting for the final ensemble.

# Data
- **Preprocessing (Cropping):** We will strictly maintain the **Center Crop of 64x64 pixels** for all inputs. This maximizes the Signal-to-Noise Ratio by focusing on the labeled 32x32px ROI while retaining the necessary 16px context buffer (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
- **Augmentation:** We will employ the proven geometric augmentation suite: **RandomRotate90**, HorizontalFlip, and VerticalFlip. This ensures the models learn the rotational invariance inherent in histopathology images (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).

# Training
- **Cross-Validation:** We will implement **5-Fold Cross-Validation** for *both* architectures (10 models total). This ensures we utilize 100% of the training data and generate robust OOF predictions for the stacking meta-learner (Cite solution_lesson_node_00013).
- **Convergence Strategy:** We will extend training to **30 epochs** for all folds. Previous analysis showed that loss was still decreasing at epoch 20, and extending the schedule allows the Cosine Annealing scheduler to reach deeper minima (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014). This directly mitigates the under-training failure observed in previous ensemble attempts (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008).
- **Optimization:** We will maximize the **Batch Size** (saturating the A100 VRAM) to ensure stable Batch Normalization statistics and gradient updates (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).
- **Checkpointing:** We will utilize **TTA-Integrated Checkpointing**. Model weights will be saved based on the validation AUC calculated using Test Time Augmentation, ensuring the saved state is explicitly optimized for the inference strategy (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).

# Evaluation
- **Base Inference:** We will apply **Test Time Augmentation (TTA)** with 4 views (Original, Horizontal Flip, Vertical Flip, Combined Flip) for every test image to generate robust base predictions (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).
- **Stacked Aggregation:** The OOF predictions from the training phase will be used to train the Logistic Regression meta-learner. This meta-learner will then process the TTA-averaged test set predictions from all 10 models (2 architectures × 5 folds) to produce the final submission probability, effectively weighing the models based on their discriminative reliability.
