Based on the comprehensive analysis of the accumulated lessons, I propose the **Anatomically ROI-Focused ConvNeXt MIL Network**.

This solution represents a structural evolution designed to resolve the critical "Resolution vs. Batch Size" trade-off (Cite solution_lesson_node_00072). It synthesizes the **LayerNorm-Native Architecture** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00076), **Multi-Task Aggregation** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00045), and **Signal Preservation** principles (Cite solution_lesson_node_00042) into a cohesive pipeline.

### 1. Model
**Architecture:** 2.5D ConvNeXt-Tiny with ROI-Focused Input and Multi-Task MIL
*   **Backbone:** **ConvNeXt-Tiny** (pretrained on ImageNet).
    *   *Strategic Retention:* We utilize ConvNeXt because it natively employs **Layer Normalization**. This eliminates the "Micro-Batch Instability" observed with ResNet, allowing us to train a stable model with a Batch Size of 8 (Cite solution_lesson_node_00022). We strictly avoid replacing normalization layers manually to preserve pretrained weights (Cite solution_lesson_node_00048).
    *   *Spatial Pooling:* Apply **Global Average Pooling (GAP)** to the backbone features. We retain GAP over Max Pooling for spatial aggregation, as GMP tends to overfit to high-frequency noise in medical scans (Cite solution_lesson_node_00079).
*   **Input (Structural Innovation):** **ROI-Cropped 2.5D Stacks**.
    *   Instead of resizing the full $512 \times 512$ CT slice to $224 \times 224$ (which blurs fine fractures, Cite solution_lesson_node_00042) or increasing input resolution to $256 \times 256$ (which forces unstable micro-batches, Cite solution_lesson_node_00072), we apply a **Center Crop** of $224 \times 224$ pixels from the original image.
    *   *Justification:* The cervical spine is anatomically centered in standard CT reconstructions. By cropping the central region, we effectively "zoom in" on the vertebrae, increasing the pixel density of the target structure by ~2.3x compared to resizing, *without* increasing the input tensor size. This preserves the high-frequency fracture signals while maintaining the memory efficiency required for a stable Batch Size of 8.
*   **Context Module:** **Non-Linear 1D Convolution**.
    *   Structure: `Conv1d(k=3, padding=1) -> LayerNorm -> GELU`.
    *   *Strategic Retention:* We retain the 1D Convolution to leverage Z-axis spatial continuity (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014). We enforce Non-Linearity and Normalization for stability (Cite solution_lesson_node_00027) but explicitly reject Residual connections to prevent overfitting (Cite solution_lesson_node_00026).
*   **Heads:** **Multi-Task Instance Classifiers**.
    *   The model projects the contextualized features to **8 distinct logits** per slice: [C1...C7, Patient_Overall].
    *   *Strategic Retention:* We use **Multi-Task Learning** (dedicated patient head) rather than deriving the patient prediction via `max()`. This stabilizes training by allowing independent gradient flow for the global label (Cite solution_lesson_node_00045). We strictly adhere to **Instance-Level Aggregation** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00024).
*   **Aggregation:** **Global Max Pooling** applied per channel across the slice sequence.
    *   *Strategic Retention:* Max Pooling is superior to Attention or Average Pooling for detecting sparse anomalies like fractures in the sequence dimension (Cite solution_lesson_node_00019, Cite solution_lesson_node_00047).

### 2. Data
*   **Ingestion Pipeline:**
    *   **Sorting:** Sort slices strictly by `ImagePositionPatient` z-coordinate to ensure physical continuity (Cite solution_lesson_node_00031).
    *   **Calibration:** Apply `RescaleSlope` and `RescaleIntercept` to convert raw pixels to Hounsfield Units (HU) (Cite solution_lesson_node_00031).
    *   **Windowing:** Apply **Standard Bone Window** (Level: 400, Width: 1800). Do not use High-Density windows (Cite solution_lesson_node_00029).
    *   **Cropping (New):** Apply a fixed **Center Crop** of $224 \times 224$ to the windowed data *before* stacking.
*   **Caching:** Preprocess and cache the cropped 2.5D stacks as **`uint8`** to maximize I/O throughput (Cite solution_lesson_node_00021).
*   **Sampling:** Uniformly sample **64 slices** per exam (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).
*   **Augmentation:**
    *   **Volumetric-Consistent:** Use a deterministic replay mechanism to apply the *exact same* geometric transform to every slice in the bag (Cite solution_lesson_node_00013).
    *   **Single-Step Affine:** Fuse rotation, scaling, and shifting into a single affine operation (`ShiftScaleRotate`) to minimize resampling blur (Cite solution_lesson_node_00042).
    *   **Non-Destructive:** Explicitly avoid `Cutout` or `Dropout` on inputs (Cite solution_lesson_node_00037).
    *   **Necessity:** Augmentation is structurally required to prevent memorization (Cite solution_lesson_node_00011).

### 3. Training
*   **Loss Function:** **Implicitly Weighted Multi-Task Loss**.
    *   $L = \text{mean}(\text{BCE}_{C1..C7}) + \text{BCE}_{\text{Patient}}$.
    *   *Strategic Retention:* This formulation naturally creates the **1:7 weighting ratio** required by the metric without explicit scalar multipliers that distort gradients (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008).
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with **Batch Size 8**.
    *   *Strategic Retention:* Small batch size acts as a necessary regularizer for this small dataset (Cite solution_lesson_node_00032). The LayerNorm backbone ensures stability.
*   **Scheduler:** **Decoupled Cosine Annealing**. Set `T_max` to **1.5x the number of epochs**.
    *   *Strategic Retention:* Prevents premature learning rate decay (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
*   **Regularization:** Do **not** use Dropout in the classification heads (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).

### 4. Evaluation
*   **Inference:**
    *   Load test scans using the sorted/calibrated pipeline.
    *   Apply the **Center Crop** ($224 \times 224$) to the calibrated volume.
    *   Process through ConvNeXt-Tiny -> Context -> Multi-Task Heads.
    *   Aggregate via Global Max Pooling to obtain 8 probabilities.
    *   Submit the 7 vertebral probabilities and the distinct `patient_overall` probability directly.
    *   *Metric Scaling:* Ensure validation metrics are normalized by the sum of weights (not the number of heads) to avoid artificial deflation (Cite solution_lesson_node_00077).
