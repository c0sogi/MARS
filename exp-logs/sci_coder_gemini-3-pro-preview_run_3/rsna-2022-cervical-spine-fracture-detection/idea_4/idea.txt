Based on the comprehensive analysis of the provided lessons and previous attempts, I propose the **Anatomically-Guided Attention-MIL Network**.

This solution represents a structural evolution from the "Instance-Space Aggregation" (Classify-then-Max-Pool) used in Idea 3 to a **"Feature-Space Aggregation"** paradigm. It synthesizes the anatomical awareness of Idea 3 with the noise-robustness suggested in Lesson 00001, addressing the brittleness of Max Pooling identified in Lesson 00010.

### 1. Model
**Architecture:** ResNet18 with Gated Attention Multiple Instance Learning
*   **Backbone:** **ResNet-18** (2.5D).
*   **Input:** 2.5D Stacks (3 channels: slices $z-1, z, z+1$).
*   **Feature Extraction:** The backbone processes each of the 64 sampled slices independently to produce a feature vector $F$ (after Global Average Pooling).
*   **Positional Injection:** Concatenate the normalized relative depth $d_z = z/N_{total}$ to the feature vector $F$.
    *   **Purpose:** This retains the critical spatial context established in **Idea 3**, allowing the subsequent aggregation layer to distinguish between "fracture texture at C1 level" vs "fracture texture at C7 level" **(Cite Lesson 00018)**.
*   **Structural Innovation (Aggregation):** Replace Global Max Pooling with a **Gated Attention Mechanism** (Ilse et al., 2018).
    *   The model learns a scalar attention weight $a_k$ for each slice $k$ via a trainable neural network (using `tanh` and `sigmoid` gates).
    *   The study-level representation $Z$ is computed as the weighted sum of slice features: $Z = \sum_{k=1}^{64} a_k F_k$.
    *   **Purpose:** This addresses the "False Positive Risk" of Max Pooling **(Cite Lesson 00010)**. Instead of blindly selecting the single noisiest slice, the attention mechanism learns to assign high weights to fracture signals and low weights to noise/artifacts, providing a robust "soft" aggregation **(Cite Lesson 00001)**.
*   **Classifier Head:** A multi-label classifier maps the aggregated vector $Z$ to 7 logits (C1–C7).
*   **Patient Prediction:** The `patient_overall` logit is dynamically derived as `max(C1...C7)`.

### 2. Data
*   **Preprocessing:**
    *   **Windowing:** Bone window (Center: 300, Width: 2000).
    *   **Resizing:** 256x256 pixels.
    *   **Sampling:** Uniformly sample **64 slices** per exam. Do not increase to 96 to avoid saturating the attention mechanism with background noise **(Cite Lesson 00010)**.
*   **Augmentation:**
    *   Apply **Volumetric-Consistent Augmentation**. Use a deterministic replay mechanism to apply the exact same geometric transformation to every slice in the bag.
    *   **Purpose:** This preserves the 3D anatomical continuity required for the model to learn valid spatial relationships, preventing the degradation caused by independent slice augmentation **(Cite Lesson 00013)**.

### 3. Training
*   **Loss Function:** **Hierarchical Compound Loss**.
    *   Sum of `BCEWithLogits` for the 7 vertebral sub-labels and `BCEWithLogits` for the derived `patient_overall` prediction.
    *   **Purpose:** This constrains the model to ensure that high confidence on any vertebra logically necessitates high confidence for the patient, enforcing calibration **(Cite Lesson 00005)**.
*   **Weighting:** **Implicit Weighting**.
    *   Calculate the loss as `mean(vertebrae_losses) + patient_loss`.
    *   **Purpose:** This naturally enforces the competition's effective 1:7 weighting ratio without manual scalar tuning **(Cite Lesson 00008)**.
*   **Optimizer Strategy:**
    *   Optimizer: AdamW.
    *   Scheduler: Cosine Annealing with `T_max` set to **1.5x the number of epochs**.
    *   **Purpose:** This prevents premature learning rate decay, allowing the attention mechanism sufficient time to converge on discriminative features before the learning rate vanishes **(Cite Lesson 00007)**.

### 4. Evaluation
*   **Inference:**
    *   Process test scans (64 slices) through the backbone + positional encoding.
    *   Compute attention weights and aggregate features into a single study vector.
    *   Predict C1-C7 probabilities from the study vector.
    *   Calculate `patient_overall` as `max(C1...C7)`.
*   **Submission:** Format predictions to match the sample submission, ensuring logical consistency between rows.
