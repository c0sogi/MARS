Based on the accumulated lessons, I propose a **Pseudo-Labeled DeBERTa-v3-Large Ensemble**.

This strategy introduces a **Semi-Supervised Learning** paradigm (specifically, self-training via pseudo-labeling) as the structural innovation. This directly addresses the primary constraint identified throughout the lessons: **Data Scarcity** (~3,000 training samples). By leveraging the high-confidence predictions on the test set (which is nearly equal in size to the training set), we can synthetically expand the training volume without the computational overhead of Domain-Adaptive Pre-training (DAPT), which was found to be inferior to simply using a stronger backbone **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00022}**.

# Model
1.  **Backbone: DeBERTa-v3-Large**
    We will utilize **DeBERTa-v3-Large** as the core architecture. While previous comparisons showed marginal gains over RoBERTa for explicit sentiment **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00020}**, it remains the strongest available backbone and outperformed domain-adapted alternatives **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00022}**.
2.  **Aggregation: Mean Pooling**
    We will employ **Mean Pooling** of the final hidden states. We explicitly reject complex heads like Attention Pooling, which were shown to overfit and degrade performance on this small dataset **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00013}**, preferring the robustness of parameter-free aggregation **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00007}**.
3.  **Regularization & Efficiency**
    *   **Dropout:** Set to **0.2** to combat the overfitting observed with large models on small data **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00008}**.
    *   **Layer Freezing:** We will **freeze the embeddings and the bottom 6 encoder layers**. This provides necessary regularization and improves training efficiency, a technique proven effective in previous iterations **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00010}**.

# Data
1.  **Preprocessing:** Standard unicode decoding will be applied.
2.  **Tokenization:** We will use the DeBERTa-v3 tokenizer with a **Maximum Sequence Length of 160** to preserve context in longer comments **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00008}**.
3.  **Dataset Construction:**
    *   **Initial Training Set:** We will **explicitly concatenate** the Train and Validation sets into a single corpus **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00021}**. We reject Cross-Validation splits as they fragment the already small dataset, degrading model quality **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00018}**.
    *   **Augmented Training Set:** In the second stage of training, this dataset will be augmented with **Pseudo-Labeled Test Data** (high-confidence predictions from the Teacher model).

# Training
The training procedure follows a **Teacher-Student** workflow:

1.  **Stage 1: Teacher Ensemble (Seed Averaging)**
    *   Train **3 independent DeBERTa-v3-Large models** on the Initial Training Set using different random seeds. This mitigates hardware stochasticity **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00012}**.
    *   **Stability:** Use **Gradient Accumulation** to maintain an effective batch size of 16 or 32, ensuring stable convergence despite the physical batch size limits **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00015}**.
    *   **Scheduler:** Linear decay with warmup, decoupling the steps from the epoch count to prevent schedule compression **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00011}**.

2.  **Stage 2: Pseudo-Label Generation**
    *   Generate predictions on the unlabeled Test set using the average of the 3 Teacher models.
    *   Select samples with high confidence (e.g., probability > 0.95 or < 0.05).
    *   Assign hard labels (0 or 1) to these samples and merge them with the Initial Training Set.

3.  **Stage 3: Student Ensemble (Seed Averaging)**
    *   Train **3 new DeBERTa-v3-Large models** (Student) on the **Augmented Training Set**.
    *   Use the same stability settings (Gradient Accumulation, Layer Freezing) as Stage 1.

# Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** The test set will be processed by the 3 Student models from Stage 3.
2.  **Prediction:** The final submission will be the **arithmetic mean** of the probability outputs from these 3 Student models. This combines the benefits of a stronger backbone, expanded training data via pseudo-labeling, and variance reduction via ensembling.
