Based on the accumulated lessons, particularly the trade-off between architectural complexity and training stability on small datasets, I propose a **Cross-Validated DeBERTa-v3-Large Ensemble with Gradient Accumulation**.

This strategy structurally evolves the solution by shifting from a single-model training paradigm to a **K-Fold Cross-Validation workflow**. This directly addresses the high variance and "hardware stochasticity" observed in previous experiments **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00012}**, ensuring that the final predictions are robust to noise. Furthermore, it enables the use of the superior DeBERTa-v3 backbone by correcting the stability parameters (Batch Size and Regularization) that caused it to fail in previous iterations **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00015}**.

# Model
1.  **Backbone: DeBERTa-v3-Large**
    We will utilize **DeBERTa-v3-Large** to capture the strongest possible semantic signals. We explicitly reject the "Attention Pooling" head used in previous failed attempts **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00013}** and revert to **Mean Pooling** of the final hidden states. This aligns with the finding that parameter-free pooling generalizes better on small datasets **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00007}**.

2.  **Regularization Configuration**
    To prevent the overfitting observed with this large backbone:
    *   **Dropout:** We will set the dropout rate to **0.2**, strictly avoiding the default 0.1 which led to instability **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00008, solution_lesson_node_00015}**.
    *   **Layer Freezing:** We will **freeze the embeddings and the bottom 6 encoder layers**. This reduces the trainable parameter space, acting as strong regularization while improving computational efficiency **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00010}**.

# Data
1.  **Preprocessing:** Standard unicode decoding will be applied.
2.  **Tokenization:** We will use the DeBERTa-v3 tokenizer with a **Maximum Sequence Length of 160**. This preserves the context at the end of longer comments, which was found to be critical **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00008}**.
3.  **Structural Split: Stratified K-Fold**
    Instead of a single Train/Val split, we will divide the training data into **5 Stratified Folds**. This ensures that every data point is used for training in at least 4 models, maximizing the utility of the small dataset (~3k samples).

# Training
1.  **Gradient Accumulation for Stability**
    A critical failure in previous DeBERTa attempts was the forced reduction of Batch Size to 8 **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00015}**. To solve this, we will decouple the physical batch size from the optimization dynamics. We will use a physical batch size that fits comfortably in memory (e.g., 8 or 16) but apply **Gradient Accumulation** to achieve a stable **Effective Batch Size of 32**. This ensures accurate gradient estimation.
2.  **Loop:** We will train **5 independent models**, one for each fold.
3.  **Optimization:** AdamW optimizer with a linear scheduler and warmup. We will use **Mixed Precision (FP16)** to accelerate training and reduce memory usage.

# Evaluation
1.  **Ensemble Inference:** The test set will be processed by all 5 trained models.
2.  **Aggregation:** The final submission will be the **arithmetic mean** of the probability outputs from the 5 folds. This averaging acts as a powerful variance reduction technique, smoothing out the noise inherent in small-data fine-tuning **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00012}**.
