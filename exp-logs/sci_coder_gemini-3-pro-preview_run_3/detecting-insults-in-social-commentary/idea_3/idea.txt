Based on the lessons learned, particularly the dominance of large transformer models over statistical baselines and the sensitivity of these models to hyperparameter settings on small datasets, I propose the following optimized strategy.

# Model

The architecture represents a structural evolution from the RoBERTa-Large baseline to a more advanced Transformer backbone with a specialized aggregation head and robust regularization.

1.  **Backbone: DeBERTa-v3-Large**
    We will replace the RoBERTa-Large backbone with **DeBERTa-v3-Large**. While RoBERTa-Large proved effective **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00007}**, DeBERTa-v3 introduces disentangled attention and a mask decoder, which generally yield superior performance on Natural Language Understanding tasks. This addresses the need for the strongest possible semantic representation.

2.  **Aggregation: Attention Pooling**
    Instead of the Mean Pooling strategy which treats all tokens equally **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00007}**, we will implement **Attention Pooling** (also known as Weighted Layer Pooling). This mechanism learns a weight for each token's embedding, allowing the model to dynamically emphasize specific "insulting" keywords or phrases while down-weighting neutral context. This is particularly effective for detecting toxicity where a single phrase can change the label of an entire paragraph.

3.  **Regularization: Multi-Sample Dropout**
    To combat the high variance and sensitivity to dropout rates observed in previous experiments **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00008, solution_lesson_node_00012}**, we will implement **Multi-Sample Dropout** in the classification head. Instead of a single dropout layer, the model will pass the pooled embeddings through distinct dropout masks (e.g., 5 parallel instances) and average the resulting logits. This technique acts as a low-cost internal ensemble, stabilizing the training process and improving generalization on small datasets.

# Data

1.  **Preprocessing:** We will continue to decode unicode-escaped characters to ensure text integrity.
2.  **Tokenization:** We will use the DeBERTa-v3 tokenizer. Crucially, we will maintain the **maximum sequence length of 160 tokens**. Previous lessons demonstrated that the standard length of 128 truncates valuable context in longer, rant-style comments, leading to performance degradation **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00008}**.

# Training

1.  **Layer-wise Learning Rate Decay (LLRD)**
    We will evolve the "partial freezing" strategy **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00010}** into **Layer-wise Learning Rate Decay**. Instead of hard-freezing the bottom layers, we will assign progressively lower learning rates to earlier layers of the network (e.g., decaying by a factor of 0.9 per layer). This preserves the general linguistic knowledge in the bottom layers (similar to freezing) while allowing for subtle adaptations, offering a more refined balance between efficiency and plasticity.
2.  **Scheduler:** We will use a linear scheduler with warmup, strictly adhering to a **4-epoch** duration. We will ensure the scheduler steps are calculated based on this duration to prevent the compressed decay curve that previously caused premature convergence **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00011}**.
3.  **Loss Function:** Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) with Logits, computed on the averaged output from the Multi-Sample Dropout head.

# Evaluation

1.  **Inference:** The test data will be processed through the trained DeBERTa-v3-Large pipeline.
2.  **Prediction:** The model will output probabilities (0-1), which will be saved directly to the submission file. No external ensembling with statistical models will be performed, as previous experiments showed this yields zero marginal gain **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00006}**.
