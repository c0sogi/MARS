Based on the lessons learned, specifically the effectiveness of residual learning (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007), the superiority of Gradient Boosting for sensor data (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010), and the critical need to handle heavy-tailed outliers before smoothing (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014), I propose the following **Physics-Featurized Residual Boosting with Kinematic Gating** strategy.

### 1. Model
The core architecture will be a **LightGBM Regressor** (Gradient Boosting Decision Trees).
*   **Backbone:** Two independent LightGBM models will be trained to predict the **East** and **North** position errors, respectively.
*   **Target:** The model will predict the **ENU (East-North-Up) Residuals** in meters.
    *   $\Delta E = East_{GT} - East_{WLS}$
    *   $\Delta N = North_{GT} - North_{WLS}$
    *   This leverages the robust physical prior of the WLS baseline while focusing the model's capacity on correcting non-linear biases (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).

### 2. Data
The preprocessing pipeline is designed to capture physical signal properties and kinematic inconsistencies without using raw temporal windowing, thereby avoiding trajectory memorization (Cite solution_lesson_node_00011).

*   **Coordinate Transformation:** Convert all Ground Truth and WLS Baseline coordinates from Geodetic (Lat/Lon) to a local **ENU** Cartesian frame centered at the first WLS point of each drive.
*   **Feature Engineering:**
    *   **Signal Quality Aggregates:** Mean, Max, and Standard Deviation of `Cn0DbHz`; Count of visible satellites; Mean `SvElevationDegrees`.
    *   **Baseline Kinematics (Innovation):** Calculate the **Speed** and **Acceleration** implied by the WLS baseline trajectory ($v = \Delta P_{WLS} / \Delta t$). High implied speeds or accelerations in the baseline often indicate multipath jumps.
    *   **Sensor Consistency (Innovation):** A "Discrepancy" feature comparing the WLS-derived acceleration with the IMU-derived acceleration magnitude. A high discrepancy (e.g., WLS jumps 20m but IMU is static) acts as a strong signal for the model to predict a large correction.
    *   **Context:** `phone_name` (Label Encoded) to account for hardware-specific antenna gain biases.

### 3. Training
*   **Loss Function:** **Mean Absolute Error (MAE)**. This L1 loss is essential to minimize the influence of the massive outliers inherent in urban GNSS data (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
*   **Validation:** **GroupKFold** cross-validation grouped by `drive_id`. This strictly enforces testing on unseen drives, ensuring the model learns general signal error patterns rather than memorizing specific routes (Cite solution_lesson_node_00011).

### 4. Evaluation
The inference pipeline introduces a **Robust Kinematic Smoother** to address the "smearing" issue caused by heavy-tailed predictions (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014).

1.  **Prediction:** Generate predicted ENU residuals ($\hat{\Delta E}, \hat{\Delta N}$) for the test set using the trained LightGBM models.
2.  **Base Correction:** Apply predictions to the WLS baseline: $\hat{P}_{corr} = P_{WLS} + \hat{\Delta P}$.
3.  **Innovation-Gated Kalman Smoothing (Innovation):** Apply a Kalman Smoother to the sequence of $\hat{P}_{corr}$ with a strict **Outlier Rejection Gate**:
    *   **State:** Position and Velocity $[x, y, v_x, v_y]$.
    *   **Motion Model:** Constant Velocity.
    *   **Gating Logic:** At each step, calculate the **Innovation** (distance between the Kalman predicted position and the ML-corrected observation $\hat{P}_{corr}$).
    *   **Threshold:** If the Innovation exceeds a physical feasibility threshold (e.g., equivalent to $> 10 m/s^2$ acceleration error), **reject the observation** (treat as missing data) and rely solely on the motion model update.
    *   This prevents the massive outliers (which the regression model may occasionally miss or under-correct) from dragging the smoothed trajectory off-course, solving the degradation observed in previous smoothing attempts (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014).
