Based on the lessons learned, particularly the failure of temporal windowing in disjoint domains (Cite solution_lesson_node_00011) and the superiority of Gradient Boosting for sensor residuals (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010), I propose the following strategy. This approach shifts the regression target to a physically consistent coordinate system and delegates temporal smoothing to a dedicated state estimation layer.

### **Strategy: ENU-Frame Residual Boosting with Kinematic Smoothing**

#### **1. Model**
The core architecture consists of two independent **LightGBM Regressors**.
*   **Target Transformation:** Instead of predicting residuals in Geodetic coordinates (Latitude/Longitude degrees), which suffer from latitude-dependent scaling, the model will predict residuals in the **East-North-Up (ENU)** local tangent plane (in meters). This makes the target variable isotropic and directly aligned with the evaluation metric (Haversine distance).
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Model 1 predicts the **East** error component ($\Delta E$).
    *   Model 2 predicts the **North** error component ($\Delta N$).
*   **Backbone:** Gradient Boosting Decision Trees (GBDT) are selected for their ability to handle the non-linear, multimodal noise distributions of GNSS data without the overfitting risks of sequence models (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).

#### **2. Data**
The preprocessing pipeline is designed to be **Point-Wise** and **Physics-Aware**, strictly avoiding temporal windowing of features to prevent trajectory memorization (Cite solution_lesson_node_00011).

*   **Coordinate Conversion:**
    1.  Convert the Ground Truth (GT) and Baseline (WLS) positions from Geodetic (Lat/Lon) to **ECEF** (Earth-Centered, Earth-Fixed) coordinates.
    2.  Compute the ECEF residual vector: $\vec{r}_{ecef} = \vec{P}_{GT} - \vec{P}_{WLS}$.
    3.  Rotate $\vec{r}_{ecef}$ into the local **ENU** frame centered at $\vec{P}_{WLS}$ to obtain targets $\Delta E$ and $\Delta N$.
*   **Feature Engineering:**
    *   **Signal Quality:** Aggregated statistics (Mean, Max, Std) of `Cn0DbHz` (Signal Strength).
    *   **Frequency Splitting:** Create separate aggregate features for **L1** and **L5** signals. L5 signals are more robust to multipath; explicitly exposing their presence/quality allows the model to weight epochs with modern signals higher.
    *   **Satellite Geometry:** Count of visible satellites and mean `SvElevationDegrees`.
    *   **IMU Dynamics:** Mean and Standard Deviation of the total accelerometer magnitude ($\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}$). This serves as a proxy for device movement state, helping the model distinguish between static drift and kinematic lag (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).

#### **3. Training**
*   **Loss Function:** **Mean Absolute Error (MAE)**. This minimizes the L1 distance in meters (East/North), ensuring robustness against the extreme outliers common in urban canyon GNSS data (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
*   **Validation Strategy:** **GroupKFold** grouped by `drive_id`. This ensures the model is trained and validated on disjoint sets of drives, enforcing generalization to unseen environments (Cite solution_lesson_node_00011).

#### **4. Evaluation**
The inference pipeline applies the learned corrections and then enforces physical continuity:
1.  **Prediction:** Generate $\hat{\Delta E}$ and $\hat{\Delta N}$ for test data using the trained LightGBM models.
2.  **Reconstruction:**
    *   Convert the predicted ENU offsets back to Geodetic offsets ($\Delta Lat, \Delta Lon$) using the local earth radius.
    *   Apply corrections to the WLS baseline: $\hat{P}_{corr} = P_{WLS} + \Delta P$.
3.  **Kinematic Smoothing:** Apply a **Kalman Smoother** (forward-backward pass) to the sequence of $\hat{P}_{corr}$.
    *   **Observation:** The ML-corrected positions.
    *   **State:** Position and Velocity.
    *   **Transition:** Constant Velocity model.
    *   This step reintroduces temporal consistency and smooths high-frequency jitter, effectively replacing the failed feature windowing approach with a robust state estimator (Cite solution_lesson_node_00011).
