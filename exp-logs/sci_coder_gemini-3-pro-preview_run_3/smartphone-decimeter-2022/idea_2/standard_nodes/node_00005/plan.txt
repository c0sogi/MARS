 The issue is that the dataset generation process allows rows with missing baseline Weighted Least Squares (WLS) positions (`lat_wls`, `lon_wls`) to propagate into the training and validation sets. When the baseline position is `NaN` (which happens when the GNSS receiver fails to compute a fix), the calculated regression targets (`lat_error`, `lon_error`) also become `NaN`. While LightGBM might handle `NaN` features, it cannot handle `NaN` targets during training (though here it seems to have trained, likely ignoring them or producing garbage), and critically, the validation metric calculation fails because `haversine_distance` produces `NaN`s, causing `np.percentile` to return `nan`.

To fix this, I will modify `library/features.py` to drop rows from the merged dataframe where the baseline WLS positions are missing. This ensures that we only include samples where a valid baseline exists, preventing `NaN` targets and ensuring valid coordinate reconstruction for evaluation. This applies **Debug Lesson 2: Sanitize Regression Targets, Not Just Input Features**.
