Based on the lessons learned, specifically the superiority of Gradient Boosting over RNNs for sensor residuals (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010) and the effectiveness of residual learning with heuristic baselines (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007), I propose the following optimized strategy. This strategy evolves the point-wise regression of "Idea 2" into a **Context-Aware Windowed Regression** pipeline augmented with **Kinematic Smoothing**.

### **Strategy: Windowed Gradient Boosting with Kalman Smoothing**

#### **1. Model**
The core architecture remains **LightGBM (Gradient Boosting Decision Trees)**, chosen for its proven ability to map noisy, instantaneous sensor statistics to error magnitudes without the overfitting risks associated with sequence models like BiLSTMs (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).

However, to address the limitation of treating timestamps independently, the model structure will be upgraded to process **Temporal Windows**. Instead of a single time-step input $x_t$, the model will receive a flattened feature vector representing a short time window centered on the target: $[x_{t-k}, \dots, x_t, \dots, x_{t+k}]$. This allows the GBDT to detect short-term signal trends (e.g., rapid signal degradation entering an urban canyon) that characterize specific error patterns, effectively capturing temporal context through feature engineering rather than recurrent state.

#### **2. Data**
The preprocessing pipeline will focus on constructing a rich, temporally aware tabular dataset:
*   **Base Feature Aggregation:** For every second (`UnixTimeMillis`), compute the successful aggregated statistics identified in previous iterations:
    *   **GNSS:** Mean, Standard Deviation, Min, and Max of `Cn0DbHz` (Signal Strength); Count of visible satellites; Mean `SvElevationDegrees`.
    *   **IMU:** Mean and Standard Deviation of the total accelerometer magnitude ($\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}$). High variance here correlates with dynamic movement errors (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
*   **Windowed Feature Engineering:** Create a sliding window dataset. For a chosen window size (e.g., $k=2$, total width 5 seconds), concatenate the aggregated features of neighboring timestamps into the current row. This explicitly provides the model with "past" and "future" context (available during post-processing).
*   **Target Calculation:** Compute the additive residual targets:
    $$ \Delta Lat_t = Lat_{GT, t} - Lat_{WLS, t} $$
    $$ \Delta Lon_t = Lon_{GT, t} - Lon_{WLS, t} $$
    This normalizes the target distribution, allowing the model to focus solely on correcting non-linear biases in the physical WLS baseline (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).

#### **3. Training**
*   **Loss Function:** **Mean Absolute Error (MAE)** (L1 Loss). This is crucial to maintain robustness against the extreme outliers common in GNSS multipath events, preventing the model from skewing towards rare, massive errors (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
*   **Strategy:** Train two independent LightGBM regressors (one for Latitude correction, one for Longitude correction).
*   **Validation:** Use **GroupKFold** cross-validation grouped by `drive_id`. This ensures that the model is evaluated on unseen drives, preventing leakage of trajectory-specific characteristics.

#### **4. Evaluation**
The inference process introduces a kinematic smoothing stage to enforce physical consistency, which independent regression points lack:
1.  **Feature Construction:** Generate the windowed feature set for the test data.
2.  **Residual Prediction:** Use the trained LightGBM models to predict $\hat{\Delta Lat}$ and $\hat{\Delta Lon}$.
3.  **Baseline Correction:** Apply predictions to the WLS baseline: $\hat{P}_{raw} = P_{WLS} + \hat{\Delta P}$.
4.  **Kinematic Smoothing (Kalman Filter):** Apply a **Kalman Smoother** (forward and backward pass) to the sequence of corrected positions $\hat{P}_{raw}$.
    *   **State:** Position and Velocity $[lat, lon, v_{lat}, v_{lon}]$.
    *   **Observation:** The ML-corrected positions $\hat{P}_{raw}$.
    *   **Model:** Constant Velocity motion model.
    *   This step filters out high-frequency jitter remaining after the ML correction and enforces a smooth, physically plausible trajectory.
