Based on the comprehensive analysis of previous attempts and the accumulated lessons, I propose a **Multi-Scale Distributional Geometric Fingerprinting** strategy.

This approach synthesizes the most successful feature engineering paradigms identified in the lessons—**Radial Distribution Functions (RDF)** for global structure and **Percentile Aggregation** for local structure—into a unified pipeline. It addresses the "feature dilution" of sparse histograms (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00016}) and the "information loss" of mean/variance statistics (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00054}) by rigorously characterizing the *shape* of geometric distributions at three distinct scales: individual chemical bonds (Interaction), atomic coordination shells (Site), and the overall crystal lattice (Global).

# Model
-   **Architecture**: **XGBoost Regressor** (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00003}). Tree ensembles have consistently proven to be the most robust predictor for this dataset when provided with dense, high-signal descriptors.
-   **Input Features**: A concatenated feature vector representing the material at three scales:
    1.  **Interaction Fingerprints**: Chemically resolved percentiles of bond lengths and bond angles.
    2.  **Site Fingerprints**: Chemically resolved percentiles of atom-centric coordination and distortion metrics.
    3.  **Global Fingerprints**: Radial Distribution Functions (RDF) and macroscopic physical properties.
    4.  **Tabular Metadata**: Atomic composition and Spacegroup.

# Data
1.  **Geometric Feature Extraction (Raw Geometry)**:
    -   Load crystal structures from raw `.xyz` files using the `ase` library to ensure fidelity (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00018}).
    -   **Global Scale**:
        -   Calculate **Unit Cell Volume** and **Mass Density** directly from the atomic objects (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00018}).
        -   Compute **Element-Resolved RDFs** (e.g., Al-O, Ga-O) using a moderate cutoff (e.g., 6.0 Å) and normalize by **atom count** (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00033}).
    -   **Interaction Scale (Bond-Centric)**:
        -   Identify all chemical bonds (e.g., Al-O, Ga-O) and bond angles (e.g., O-Al-O, Al-O-Al) using a neighbor list.
        -   Instead of mean/variance, collect the full population of values for each type and compute **Percentiles** (Min, 10%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 90%, Max). This captures the specific range of bond stretching and polyhedral tilting (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00055}).
    -   **Site Scale (Atom-Centric)**:
        -   For every metal atom, calculate continuous local metrics: **Effective Coordination Number (ECoN)** and **Bond Angle Variance** (a proxy for polyhedral distortion).
        -   Group these values by element type (Al, Ga, In) and compute **Percentiles**. This explicitly resolves the population of distinct crystallographic sites (e.g., distinguishing between distorted surface sites and bulk sites) which simple averages obscure (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00054}).

2.  **Preprocessing**:
    -   **Target Transformation**: Apply the natural logarithm transformation $z = \log(1 + y)$ to the target variables (`formation_energy_ev_natom` and `bandgap_energy_ev`) (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00002}).
    -   **Feature Cleaning**: Drop constant columns to prevent feature dilution (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00016}).

# Training
-   **Algorithm**: Train separate XGBoost regressors for formation energy and bandgap energy.
-   **Optimization Strategy**:
    -   **Shrinkage**: Use a **low learning rate** (e.g., $\eta \approx 0.01$) and a **high number of estimators** (e.g., 3,000) to maximize generalization (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00004}).
    -   **Stochastic Subsampling**: Set row (`subsample`) and column (`colsample_bytree`) subsampling to $\approx 0.6 - 0.7$. This forces the model to utilize the diverse multi-scale information rather than overfitting to specific dominant features (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00004}).
-   **Loss Function**: Mean Squared Error (MSE) on the log-transformed targets.

# Evaluation
-   **Inference**:
    1.  Process the test set `.xyz` files to generate the Multi-Scale Distributional Fingerprints.
    2.  Generate predictions in the log-transformed space using the trained XGBoost models.
    3.  Apply the inverse transformation $\hat{y} = \exp(z) - 1$ to obtain the final energy predictions.
