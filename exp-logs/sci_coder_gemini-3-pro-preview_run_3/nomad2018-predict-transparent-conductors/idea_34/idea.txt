Based on the comprehensive analysis of previous attempts and the accumulated lessons, I propose a **Sublattice-Resolved Multi-Scale Distributional Fingerprinting** strategy.

This approach synthesizes the most successful feature engineering paradigms identified in the lessons—**Bond Valence Sums (BVS)** for chemical stability (Cite solution_lesson_node_00060), **Effective Coordination Number (ECoN)** for steric packing (Cite solution_lesson_node_00057), and **Percentile Aggregation** for distributional robustness (Cite solution_lesson_node_00054).

The core structural innovation is the explicit inclusion of **Cation-Cation (Metal-Metal) Correlations** alongside the standard Metal-Oxygen interactions. While previous ideas focused heavily on the immediate coordination environment (M-O bonds), the bandgap of these semiconductor alloys is critically dependent on the orbital overlap between metal cations (the conduction band minimum). By explicitly modeling the distribution of Metal-Metal distances (via RDFs) and Metal-Oxygen-Metal angles (via Percentiles), this strategy captures the long-range connectivity and cation ordering that drive electronic properties, while using BVS and ECoN to constrain thermodynamic stability (formation energy).

# Model
-   **Architecture**: **XGBoost Regressor** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003). Tree ensembles remain the most robust architecture for this dataset when provided with dense, high-signal descriptors.
-   **Input Features**: A concatenated feature vector representing the material from three physical scales, resolved by sublattice (Cation vs. Anion):
    1.  **Atomic Scale (Site Stability)**: Chemically resolved percentiles of **Bond Valence Sums (BVS)** and **Effective Coordination Numbers (ECoN)**.
    2.  **Interaction Scale (Bonding & Connectivity)**:
        *   **Radial**: Element-resolved Radial Distribution Functions (RDF) for **Metal-Oxygen** (primary bond) and **Metal-Metal** (cation correlation) pairs.
        *   **Angular**: Percentiles of **Oxygen-Metal-Oxygen** (polyhedral shape) and **Metal-Oxygen-Metal** (framework tilt) bond angles.
    3.  **Macroscopic Scale**: Unit Cell Volume, Mass Density, and Tabular Metadata (Cite solution_lesson_node_00058).

# Data
1.  **Geometric Feature Extraction (Raw Geometry)**:
    -   Load crystal structures from raw `.xyz` files using the `ase` library to ensure fidelity (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).
    -   **Macroscopic**: Calculate **Unit Cell Volume** and **Mass Density** directly from the atomic objects.
    -   **Atomic Scale (Site Fingerprints)**:
        -   For every atom, calculate:
            1.  **Bond Valence Sum (BVS)**: Using standard $R_0$ parameters (Al: 1.651, Ga: 1.708, In: 1.907). This captures local chemical saturation (Cite solution_lesson_node_00060).
            2.  **Effective Coordination Number (ECoN)**: A continuous measure of packing density (Cite solution_lesson_node_00057).
        -   Group these values by element type (Al, Ga, In, O) and compute **Percentiles** (Min, 25%, 50%, 75%, Max). This captures the distribution of distinct crystallographic sites (Cite solution_lesson_node_00054).
    -   **Interaction Scale (Network Fingerprints)**:
        -   **Radial (RDF)**: Compute element-resolved RDFs. Crucially, include **Metal-Metal pairs** (e.g., Al-Ga, In-In) in addition to Metal-Oxygen pairs to capture cation clustering/ordering. Normalize by **atom count** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00033).
        -   **Angular (Topology)**:
            -   **Intra-polyhedral (O-M-O)**: Angles at the Metal center. Captures polyhedral distortion.
            -   **Inter-polyhedral (M-O-M)**: Angles at the Oxygen center. Captures framework connectivity and band-width controlling overlaps (Cite solution_lesson_node_00059).
            -   Compute **Percentiles** of these angular distributions to avoid the sparsity of histograms (Cite solution_lesson_node_00034).

2.  **Preprocessing**:
    -   **Target Transformation**: Apply the natural logarithm transformation $z = \log(1 + y)$ to the target variables (`formation_energy_ev_natom` and `bandgap_energy_ev`) (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
    -   **Feature Cleaning**: Drop constant columns. Use NaN for missing element statistics (e.g., in binary oxides) to allow XGBoost to handle them natively without feature dilution (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016).

# Training
-   **Algorithm**: Train separate XGBoost regressors for formation energy and bandgap energy.
-   **Optimization Strategy**:
    -   **Shrinkage**: Use a **low learning rate** (e.g., $\eta \approx 0.01$) and a **high number of estimators** (e.g., 3,000) to maximize generalization (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).
    -   **Stochastic Subsampling**: Set row (`subsample`) and column (`colsample_bytree`) subsampling to $\approx 0.6 - 0.7$. This forces the model to utilize the diverse structural views (Site vs. Network vs. Global) rather than overfitting to specific features (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).
-   **Loss Function**: Mean Squared Error (MSE) on the log-transformed targets.

# Evaluation
-   **Inference**:
    1.  Process the test set `.xyz` files to generate the Sublattice-Resolved Multi-Scale features.
    2.  Generate predictions in the log-transformed space using the trained XGBoost models.
    3.  Apply the inverse transformation $\hat{y} = \exp(z) - 1$ to obtain the final energy predictions.
