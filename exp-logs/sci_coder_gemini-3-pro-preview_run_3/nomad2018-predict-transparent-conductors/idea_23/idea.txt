Based on the comprehensive analysis of previous attempts and the accumulated lessons, I propose an **Interaction-Centric Distributional Fingerprinting** strategy.

This approach evolves the successful **Distributional Local Environment (DLE)** strategy (Idea 21) by shifting the aggregation focus from *atom-level* metrics (like coordination number) to *interaction-level* geometric primitives (bond lengths and bond angles). While Idea 21 successfully used percentiles to capture the distribution of atomic environments, it compressed the rich angular information into a single "Variance" scalar per atom. Conversely, explicit Angular Distribution Functions (ADF) failed due to histogram sparsity (Cite Lesson 34). This new strategy resolves this trade-off by applying **Percentile Aggregation** directly to the populations of bond lengths and bond angles. This creates a dense, high-resolution map of the lattice distortion field (e.g., capturing the exact range of bond stretching and polyhedral tilting) without the sparsity of bins or the information loss of summary statistics.

# Model
-   **Architecture**: **XGBoost Regressor** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003). Tree ensembles remain the most effective architecture for this dataset when provided with dense, high-signal descriptors.
-   **Input Features**: A concatenated feature vector consisting of:
    1.  **Explicit Physical Descriptors**: Unit Cell Volume and Mass Density.
    2.  **Radial Fingerprints (RDF)**: Element-resolved pairwise distance histograms (capturing global/medium-range structure).
    3.  **Interaction Distributional Fingerprints (The Innovation)**: Chemically resolved percentiles of specific bond lengths and bond angles.
    4.  **Tabular Metadata**: Atomic composition and Spacegroup.

# Data
1.  **Geometric Feature Extraction (Raw Geometry)**:
    -   Load crystal structures from raw `.xyz` files using the `ase` library to ensure fidelity (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).
    -   **Physical Descriptors**: Calculate **Unit Cell Volume** and **Mass Density** directly from the atomic objects (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).
    -   **Radial Features (RDF)**: Compute element-resolved Radial Distribution Functions (e.g., Al-O, Ga-O, In-O) using a moderate cutoff (e.g., 6.0 Å). Normalize histograms by **atom count** to decouple topology from density (Cite solution_lesson_node_00033).
    -   **Interaction-Centric Fingerprints**:
        -   **Neighbor Analysis**: Construct a neighbor list with a bonded cutoff (e.g., 3.0 Å).
        -   **Bond Length Distributions**: For each cation-anion pair type (Al-O, Ga-O, In-O), collect *all* individual bond lengths in the crystal into a single population. Compute the **Percentiles** (Min, 10%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 90%, Max) of this distribution. This explicitly encodes the range of radial strain.
        -   **Bond Angle Distributions**:
            1.  **Intra-polyhedral (O-Metal-O)**: Collect all angles centered on Metal atoms. Group by Metal type (Al, Ga, In) and compute percentiles. This captures polyhedral distortion.
            2.  **Inter-polyhedral (Metal-Oxygen-Metal)**: Collect all bridging angles centered on Oxygen. Compute percentiles. This captures the tilting/connectivity of the framework.
        -   **Advantage**: This method creates dense features that characterize the exact shape of the geometric distributions (e.g., identifying a bimodal bond length distribution typical of mixed sites) without the "feature dilution" of sparse histograms (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016) or the smoothing of mean/variance aggregation (Cite solution_lesson_node_00054).

2.  **Preprocessing**:
    -   **Target Transformation**: Apply the natural logarithm transformation $z = \log(1 + y)$ to the target variables (`formation_energy_ev_natom` and `bandgap_energy_ev`) (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
    -   **Feature Cleaning**: Drop constant columns to prevent noise.

# Training
-   **Algorithm**: Train separate XGBoost regressors for formation energy and bandgap energy.
-   **Optimization Strategy**:
    -   **Shrinkage**: Use a **low learning rate** (e.g., $\eta \approx 0.01$) and a **high number of estimators** (e.g., 3,000) to maximize generalization (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).
    -   **Stochastic Subsampling**: Set row (`subsample`) and column (`colsample_bytree`) subsampling to $\approx 0.6 - 0.7$. This forces the model to utilize the diverse structural views (Global RDF vs. Local Interaction Percentiles) rather than overfitting to specific features (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).
-   **Loss Function**: Mean Squared Error (MSE) on the log-transformed targets.

# Evaluation
-   **Inference**:
    1.  Process the test set `.xyz` files to generate Physical Descriptors, RDFs, and Interaction Distributional Fingerprints.
    2.  Generate predictions in the log-transformed space using the trained XGBoost models.
    3.  Apply the inverse transformation $\hat{y} = \exp(z) - 1$ to obtain the final energy predictions.
