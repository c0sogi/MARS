Based on the progression of insights from the provided lessons, I propose the following optimized strategy. This approach evolves the architecture from a pure Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) to a **Convolutional Recurrent Neural Network (CRNN)** to explicitly model the temporal dynamics of sound events, while retaining the successful feature extraction and aggregation mechanisms identified in previous attempts.

# Model
**Architecture: EfficientNet-B2 based CRNN with Attention Pooling**
- **Input Adaptation:** Prepend a **learnable Batch Normalization layer** to the network. This ensures that the statistics of the Log-Mel spectrograms are dynamically adapted to match the distribution expected by the pretrained backbone, preventing the training instability observed when transferring ImageNet weights to audio data (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
- **Backbone:** Utilize **EfficientNet-B2** initialized with **ImageNet pretrained weights** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004). This backbone serves as the primary feature extractor, converting the input spectrogram into a sequence of high-level feature maps.
- **Temporal Modeling (Structural Innovation):** Insert a **Bi-directional Gated Recurrent Unit (BiGRU)** layer after the CNN backbone.
    - *Rationale:* While the previous CNN-only approach (Idea 2) treats the audio largely as a static image, sound is inherently temporal. A "revving" engine or "footsteps" are defined by their evolution over time. The CNN extracts spectral features, and the BiGRU processes these features as a time sequence, allowing the model to capture temporal dependencies and context that a pure CNN might miss.
- **Aggregation Head:** Apply **Attention Pooling** on the output of the BiGRU.
    - *Rationale:* This mechanism acts as a soft Multiple Instance Learning (MIL) layer. It allows the model to assign high weights to the specific time steps (frames) where the sound event occurs and suppress silence or background noise. This is superior to Global Max Pooling for weak supervision (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005) and enables the model to effectively learn from the "Noisy" dataset by down-weighting irrelevant segments (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).

# Data
**Preprocessing:**
- **Spectrograms:** Generate Log-Mel Spectrograms with **128 Mel bins** to capture sufficient frequency detail (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
- **Training Input:** Perform **random 5-second crops** on the audio data during training. This acts as implicit data augmentation and increases the signal-to-noise ratio within the training batches (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).

**Augmentation:**
- **Mixup:** Apply Mixup augmentation (interpolating inputs and targets) to smooth the decision boundary and accelerate convergence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).
- **SpecAugment:** Apply time and frequency masking to the spectrograms. This prevents the model from overfitting to specific frequency bands or time artifacts, forcing it to learn robust features (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).

# Training
- **Strategy:** Train on the **combined dataset** (Curated + Noisy). The Attention Pooling mechanism combined with the robust CRNN architecture will allow the model to filter out the noise inherent in the "Noisy" labels.
- **Loss Function:** Use `BCEWithLogitsLoss` to handle the multi-label classification task.
- **Optimizer:** Use **AdamW** with a Cosine Annealing learning rate scheduler to ensure efficient convergence and prevent overfitting.

# Evaluation
- **Inference Process:** Perform inference on the **full-length audio clips** (approx. 13s) rather than crops.
    - *Rationale:* Since the CRNN (CNN + BiGRU + Attention) is designed to handle variable sequence lengths, processing the full clip ensures that no sound events are missed due to cropping (addressing the issue identified in Cite solution_lesson_node_00001). The Attention Pooling head will automatically aggregate the predictions across the entire duration of the file.
