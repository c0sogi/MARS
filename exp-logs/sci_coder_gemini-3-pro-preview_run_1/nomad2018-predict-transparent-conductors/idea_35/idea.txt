Based on the lessons learned from the previous experiments, I propose the **Multi-Neighbor Packing-Aware Deep Sets (MNPA-DS)** strategy.

This strategy structurally evolves the successful "Augmented Point-Cloud Deep Sets" (Idea 4). It refines the local feature engineering by expanding the "Nearest Neighbor" concept to a **Multi-Order Neighborhood** ($d_1, d_2, d_3$) to capture local structural granularity (e.g., distinguishing dimers from clusters) while adhering to the principle of parsimony (Lesson 21) and sharpness (Lesson 77). Simultaneously, it enhances the global stream with a physics-based **Atomic Packing Factor**, providing a robust inductive bias for structural stability without the noise of atomic-level physical constants (Lesson 23) or the dilution of simple averages (Lesson 73). The architecture strictly enforces the "Wide, Regularized" paradigm (Wide MLPs, Batch Norm, Dropout) established as critical in Lessons 17, 24, and 36.

### Model
The architecture consists of two parallel processing streams that merge via Late Fusion:

1.  **Atomic Stream (Multi-Neighbor Point Processor):**
    *   **Input Features:** For each atom, construct a dense feature vector (10 dimensions):
        *   **Atomic Identity:** One-hot encoding (Al, Ga, In, O) (4 dims).
        *   **Spatial Context:** Centered Cartesian coordinates $(x, y, z)$ relative to the unit cell centroid. This retains critical node-level spatial variance (**Cite Lesson 10**).
        *   **Multi-Order Nearest Neighbor Distances:** The scalar distances to the **1st, 2nd, and 3rd** nearest neighbors, calculated using Periodic Boundary Conditions (PBC).
            *   *Rationale:* While $d_1$ (used in Idea 4) captures the immediate bond constraint (**Cite Lesson 15**), $d_2$ and $d_3$ provide context on the local geometry (e.g., linear vs. tetrahedral coordination) without the high dimensionality of full fingerprint vectors (**Cite Lesson 21**) or the smoothing of density functions (**Cite Lesson 77**).
    *   **Encoder:** A **Wide MLP** (e.g., 512 hidden units) to project features into a high-dimensional latent space via **Immediate Expansion** (**Cite Lesson 6**).
    *   **Regularization:** **Batch Normalization** (**Cite Lesson 36**) and **Dropout** (e.g., 0.1) (**Cite Lesson 24**) are applied after every ReLU activation to handle heterogeneous input scales and prevent overfitting.
    *   **Output:** A **Linear** projection (no activation) to the final embedding space (**Cite Lesson 18**).
    *   **Aggregation:** **Dual Pooling** (concatenation of Global Mean and Global Max) to capture both average properties and salient features (**Cite Lesson 5**). Sum pooling is excluded (**Cite Lesson 7**).

2.  **Global Stream (Packing-Enhanced Context):**
    *   **Input:** Macroscopic features: Lattice vector lengths, lattice angles, unit cell volume, atomic density, **explicit stoichiometry** (**Cite Lesson 13**), and the **Total Number of Atoms** (**Cite Lesson 35**).
    *   **Physics Injection (New):** **Atomic Packing Factor (APF)**. Calculated as the ratio of the total theoretical volume of atoms (summing spherical volumes based on covalent radii and stoichiometry) to the unit cell volume. This provides a high-level physical descriptor of structural efficiency and strain potential, serving as a strong global prior.
    *   **Encoder:** A **High-Capacity MLP** (e.g., 256 units) with Batch Normalization and Dropout to model non-linear boundary condition effects (**Cite Lesson 9**).

3.  **Fusion Head:**
    *   **Late Fusion:** Concatenate the aggregated atomic embedding and the global embedding (**Cite Lesson 19**).
    *   **Regressor:** A final MLP predicts `formation_energy_ev_natom` and `bandgap_energy_ev`.

### Data
1.  **Geometry Preprocessing:**
    *   Parse `geometry.xyz` files.
    *   **Centering:** Subtract the unit cell centroid from all atomic coordinates.
    *   **PBC Neighbor Search:** Compute the pairwise distance matrix using lattice vectors to respect Periodic Boundary Conditions.
    *   **Feature Extraction:** For each atom, sort the neighbor distances and extract the first three non-zero values ($d_1, d_2, d_3$).
2.  **Tabular Preprocessing:**
    *   Extract lattice parameters, stoichiometry, and **Total Atoms**.
    *   Calculate Unit Cell Volume and Atomic Density.
    *   **Compute APF:** Define a dictionary of covalent radii (Al: 1.21, Ga: 1.22, In: 1.42, O: 0.66 Å). Calculate $V_{atoms} = \sum N_{elem} \cdot \frac{4}{3}\pi r_{elem}^3$. Compute $APF = V_{atoms} / V_{cell}$.
    *   **Scaling:** Apply Standard Scaling (z-score) to all continuous features (atomic coords, neighbor distances, global features).
3.  **Target Transformation:**
    *   Apply `log(1+y)` to both target variables to align the training objective (MSE) with the evaluation metric (RMSLE).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** Mean Squared Error (MSE) on the log-transformed targets.
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with weight decay ($1e-4$) to regularize the wide layers.
*   **Scheduler:** `ReduceLROnPlateau` monitoring validation loss.
*   **Strategy:** Train for **200 epochs** with early stopping (patience ~20 epochs). The wide, regularized architecture allows for stable convergence over a longer schedule (**Cite Lesson 17**).

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** Process the test set using the identical pipeline (Centering, PBC-Neighbor calculation, APF calculation, Scaling).
2.  **Prediction:** Pass the processed data through the trained model.
3.  **Inverse Transformation:** Apply `exp(x) - 1` to the model outputs.
4.  **Submission:** Format the results as `id,formation_energy_ev_natom,bandgap_energy_ev`.
