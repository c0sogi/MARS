Based on the comprehensive analysis of previous experiments and the extracted lessons, I propose the **Ratio-Enhanced Multi-Scale Wide Deep Sets (REMS-WDS)** strategy.

This strategy represents a structural evolution of the current best solution, **Multi-Scale Context Wide Deep Sets** (Idea 41). It refines the local feature engineering by introducing a **dimensionless geometric descriptor** (Packing Ratio) to capture local structural distortion without the redundancy of raw distance statistics (Lesson 82). It also enhances the global stream with **Lattice Aspect Ratios** to explicitly model macroscopic anisotropy. Crucially, it retains the **Multi-Scale Chemical Context** (Lesson 92) and strictly enforces the **Sparse Batching** (Lesson 89) and **Wide/Regularized Architecture** (Lessons 17, 24, 36) that were decisive for previous successes.

### Model
The architecture consists of two parallel processing streams that merge via Late Fusion:

1.  **Atomic Stream (Ratio-Enhanced Point Processor):**
    *   **Input Features:** For each atom, construct a dense feature vector (17 dimensions):
        *   **Atomic Identity:** One-hot encoding (Al, Ga, In, O) (4 dims).
        *   **Spatial Context:** Centered Cartesian coordinates $(x, y, z)$ relative to the unit cell centroid. This retains critical node-level spatial variance (**Cite Lesson 10**).
        *   **Nearest Neighbor Distance ($d_{min}$):** The scalar distance to the single closest neighbor (PBC-corrected), providing a sharp proxy for steric constraints (**Cite Lesson 15**).
        *   **Local Packing Ratio (New):** The ratio $R = d_{min} / d_{mean\_12}$, where $d_{mean\_12}$ is the mean distance to the $K=12$ nearest neighbors. This dimensionless scalar serves as a robust proxy for **local structural distortion**. A ratio near 1.0 indicates a highly symmetric/isotropic cage (e.g., perfect close packing), while a lower ratio indicates a distorted environment with specific short bonds (e.g., Jahn-Teller distortion). This adds orthogonal geometric information to the chemical contexts without the redundancy of raw mean distances (**Cite Lesson 21, Lesson 82**).
        *   **Multi-Scale Chemical Contexts:** Two 4-dimensional vectors representing the **Inverse-Distance Weighted Composition** of the **$K=6$** (Short-Range) and **$K=24$** (Medium-Range) nearest neighbors. These capture the chemical identity of the coordination shell and the broader crystal field, respectively (**Cite Lesson 92**).
    *   **Encoder:** A **Wide MLP** (e.g., 3 layers of 512 hidden units) to project features into a high-dimensional latent space via **Immediate Expansion** (**Cite Lesson 6**).
    *   **Regularization:** **Batch Normalization** (**Cite Lesson 36**) and **Dropout** (e.g., 0.1) (**Cite Lesson 24**) are applied after every ReLU activation. This is essential to handle the heterogeneous scales of the inputs and prevent overfitting.
    *   **Output:** A **Linear** projection (no activation) to the final embedding space (**Cite Lesson 18**).
    *   **Aggregation:** **Dual Pooling** (concatenation of Global Mean and Global Max) to capture both average properties and salient features (**Cite Lesson 5**). Sum pooling is excluded (**Cite Lesson 7**).

2.  **Global Stream (Anisotropy-Aware Context):**
    *   **Input:**
        *   **Standard:** Lattice vector lengths, lattice angles, unit cell volume, atomic density, **explicit stoichiometry** (**Cite Lesson 13**), and **Total Number of Atoms** (**Cite Lesson 35**).
        *   **Lattice Aspect Ratios (New):** The ratios of the lattice vector lengths ($a/b, b/c, c/a$). These explicitly encode the **macroscopic anisotropy** of the crystal unit cell, which correlates with bandgap directionality, providing a signal distinct from raw volume or density.
    *   **Encoder:** A **High-Capacity MLP** (e.g., 256 units) with Batch Normalization and Dropout to model non-linear boundary condition effects (**Cite Lesson 9**).

3.  **Fusion Head:**
    *   **Late Fusion:** Concatenate the aggregated atomic embedding and the global embedding (**Cite Lesson 19**).
    *   **Regressor:** A final MLP predicts `formation_energy_ev_natom` and `bandgap_energy_ev`.

### Data
1.  **Geometry Preprocessing:**
    *   Parse `geometry.xyz` files.
    *   **Centering:** Subtract the unit cell centroid from all atomic coordinates.
    *   **PBC Neighbor Search:** Compute the pairwise distance matrix using lattice vectors to respect Periodic Boundary Conditions.
    *   **Feature Extraction:**
        *   Identify neighbors for $K=1, 6, 12, 24$.
        *   Compute $d_{min}$ ($K=1$) and $d_{mean\_12}$ ($K=12$). Calculate **Packing Ratio**.
        *   Compute **Weighted Context Vectors** for $K=6$ and $K=24$: Sum the one-hot vectors of neighbors weighted by $1/d$, then normalize.
2.  **Tabular Preprocessing:**
    *   Extract lattice parameters, stoichiometry, and **Total Atoms**.
    *   Calculate Unit Cell Volume, Atomic Density, and **Lattice Aspect Ratios**.
    *   **Scaling:** Apply Standard Scaling (z-score) to all continuous features.
3.  **Batching Strategy:**
    *   Use **Sparse (Flattened) Batching**: Concatenate all atoms from a batch into a single tensor `[Total_Batch_Atoms, Features]` with an index vector. This prevents padding zeros from corrupting Batch Normalization statistics (**Cite Lesson 89**).
4.  **Target Transformation:**
    *   Apply `log(1+y)` to both target variables to align the training objective (MSE) with the evaluation metric (RMSLE).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** Mean Squared Error (MSE) on the log-transformed targets.
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with weight decay ($1e-4$) to regularize the wide layers.
*   **Scheduler:** `ReduceLROnPlateau` monitoring validation loss.
*   **Strategy:** Train for **200 epochs** with early stopping (patience ~20 epochs). The wide, regularized architecture allows for stable convergence over a longer schedule (**Cite Lesson 17**).

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** Process the test set using the identical pipeline (Centering, PBC-MultiScale/Ratio calculation, Scaling).
2.  **Prediction:** Pass the processed data through the trained model using sparse batching.
3.  **Inverse Transformation:** Apply `exp(x) - 1` to the model outputs to recover the original energy scale.
4.  **Submission:** Format the results as `id,formation_energy_ev_natom,bandgap_energy_ev`.
