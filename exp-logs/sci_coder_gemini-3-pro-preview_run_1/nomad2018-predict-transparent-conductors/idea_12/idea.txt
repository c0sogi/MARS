Based on the lessons learned from the previous experiments, I propose the **Potential-Augmented Wide Deep Sets (PA-WDS)** strategy.

This strategy evolves the current best approach (**Augmented Point-Cloud Deep Sets**, Idea 4) by enriching the atomic feature space with a physics-inspired scalar proxy for local interaction potential, while strictly enforcing the regularization and architectural constraints that prevented overfitting in previous high-capacity attempts (Idea 6, Idea 10). It addresses **Lesson 21** (Parsimony) by avoiding high-dimensional geometric fingerprints and **Lesson 24** (Regularization) by mandating Dropout in wide layers.

### Model
The architecture consists of two parallel processing streams that merge via a Late Fusion mechanism:

1.  **Atomic Stream (Wide Point Processor):**
    *   **Input Features:** For each atom, construct a compact feature vector containing:
        *   **Atomic Identity:** One-hot encoding (Al, Ga, In, O).
        *   **Spatial Context:** Centered Cartesian coordinates $(x, y, z)$ relative to the unit cell centroid (**Cite Lesson 10**).
        *   **Nearest Neighbor Distance:** The scalar distance to the closest atom, capturing immediate steric constraints (**Cite Lesson 15**).
        *   **Local Potential Proxy (New):** A scalar value $P_i = \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i} \frac{1}{d_{ij}}$, representing the sum of inverse distances to the $K$ nearest neighbors (e.g., $K=12$). This physically-grounded feature approximates the local electrostatic/Van der Waals potential field experienced by the atom, providing a more robust measure of local density than simple distances, without the dimensionality explosion of sorted neighbor vectors (**Cite Lesson 21**).
    *   **Backbone:** A **Wide Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP)** (e.g., width 512) to project these features into a high-dimensional latent space (**Cite Lesson 17**). Crucially, **Dropout** layers are inserted after activations to prevent the overfitting observed when capacity was increased in previous iterations (**Cite Lesson 24**).
    *   **Output:** A **Linear** projection (no activation) to the final embedding space to preserve vector magnitude information (**Cite Lesson 18**).
    *   **Aggregation:** **Dual Pooling** (concatenation of Global Mean and Global Max) to capture both average properties and salient structural anomalies (**Cite Lesson 5**). Sum pooling is explicitly excluded (**Cite Lesson 7**).

2.  **Global Stream (Thermodynamic Context):**
    *   **Input:** Macroscopic features: Lattice vector lengths, lattice angles, unit cell volume, atomic density, and **explicit stoichiometry** (composition percentages) (**Cite Lesson 13**).
    *   **Backbone:** A **High-Capacity MLP** (e.g., 3 layers, 256 units) to strictly model the non-linear influence of boundary conditions on energy (**Cite Lesson 9**).

3.  **Fusion Head:**
    *   **Late Fusion:** Concatenate the aggregated atomic embedding and the global embedding (**Cite Lesson 19**).
    *   **Regressor:** A final MLP predicts `formation_energy_ev_natom` and `bandgap_energy_ev`.

### Data
1.  **Geometry Preprocessing:**
    *   Parse `geometry.xyz` files.
    *   **Centering:** Subtract the unit cell centroid from all atomic coordinates.
    *   **PBC Calculations:** Compute the pairwise distance matrix using lattice vectors to respect Periodic Boundary Conditions.
    *   **Feature Extraction:** For each atom, extract the minimum non-zero distance (NN Dist) and compute the sum of inverse distances to the 12 nearest neighbors (Local Potential Proxy).
2.  **Tabular Preprocessing:**
    *   Extract lattice parameters and stoichiometry.
    *   Calculate **Unit Cell Volume** and **Atomic Density**.
    *   **Scaling:** Apply Standard Scaling (z-score) to all continuous features (atomic coords, distances, potential proxy, global features) to ensure balanced gradients.
3.  **Target Transformation:**
    *   Apply `log(1+y)` to both target variables to align the training objective (MSE) with the evaluation metric (RMSLE).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** Mean Squared Error (MSE) on the log-transformed targets.
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with weight decay ($1e-4$) to regularize the wide layers.
*   **Scheduler:** `ReduceLROnPlateau` monitoring validation loss.
*   **Strategy:** Train for **200 epochs** with early stopping (patience ~20 epochs). The combination of wide layers and Dropout allows for stable, extended training without the rapid overfitting seen in unregularized models.

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** Process the test set using the identical pipeline (Centering, PBC-Potential calculation, Scaling).
2.  **Prediction:** Pass the processed data through the trained model.
3.  **Inverse Transformation:** Apply `exp(x) - 1` to the model outputs to recover the original energy scale.
4.  **Submission:** Format the results as `id,formation_energy_ev_natom,bandgap_energy_ev`.
