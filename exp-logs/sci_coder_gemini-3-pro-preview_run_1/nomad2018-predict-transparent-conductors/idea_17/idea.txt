Based on the lessons learned from the previous experiments, I propose the **Potential-Calibrated Wide Deep Sets (PC-WDS)** strategy.

This strategy represents a structural evolution of the successful "Augmented Point-Cloud Deep Sets" (Idea 4). It integrates the physics-inspired **Local Potential Proxy** (attempted in Idea 12 and 15) to better capture local packing density, but crucially, it corrects the specific implementation flaws—missing **Batch Normalization** (Lesson 36) and missing **System Cardinality** (Lesson 35)—that caused those previous iterations to fail. It strictly adheres to the architectural safeguards (Wide MLPs, Dropout, Late Fusion, Cartesian Coordinates) that have proven essential for generalization on this dataset.

### Model
The architecture consists of two parallel processing streams that merge via Late Fusion:

1.  **Atomic Stream (Wide Point Processor):**
    *   **Input Features:** For each atom, construct a compact, non-redundant feature vector (9 dimensions):
        *   **Atomic Identity:** One-hot encoding (Al, Ga, In, O) (4 dims).
        *   **Spatial Context:** Centered Cartesian coordinates $(x, y, z)$ relative to the unit cell centroid. This retains critical node-level spatial variance without the ambiguity of unconditioned fractional coordinates (**Cite Lesson 10, Lesson 39**).
        *   **Nearest Neighbor Distance:** The scalar distance to the closest neighbor (PBC-corrected), capturing immediate steric constraints (**Cite Lesson 15**).
        *   **Local Potential Proxy:** The scalar sum of inverse distances to all other atoms in the unit cell ($P_i = \sum_{j \neq i} \frac{1}{d_{ij}}$), calculated using Periodic Boundary Conditions. This provides a robust, permutation-invariant measure of the local atomic density and electrostatic-like environment, enriching the representation without the dimensionality bloat of sorted neighbor vectors (**Cite Lesson 21**).
    *   **Encoder:** A **Wide MLP** (e.g., 512 hidden units) to project features into a high-dimensional latent space (**Cite Lesson 6, Lesson 17**).
    *   **Regularization:** **Batch Normalization** (**Cite Lesson 36**) and **Dropout** (**Cite Lesson 24**) are applied after every ReLU activation. This is critical to handle the heterogeneous scales of the input features (one-hot vs. potential) and prevent overfitting in the wide layers.
    *   **Output:** A **Linear** projection (no activation) to the final embedding space (**Cite Lesson 18**).
    *   **Aggregation:** **Dual Pooling** (concatenation of Global Mean and Global Max) to capture both average properties and salient features (**Cite Lesson 5**). Sum pooling is explicitly excluded to maintain robustness for intensive targets (**Cite Lesson 7**).

2.  **Global Stream (Thermodynamic Context):**
    *   **Input:** Macroscopic features: Lattice vector lengths, lattice angles, unit cell volume, atomic density, **explicit stoichiometry** (**Cite Lesson 13**), and the **Total Number of Atoms** (**Cite Lesson 35**).
    *   **Encoder:** A **High-Capacity MLP** (e.g., 3 layers, 256 units) with Batch Normalization and Dropout to model non-linear boundary condition effects (**Cite Lesson 9**).

3.  **Fusion Head:**
    *   **Late Fusion:** Concatenate the aggregated atomic embedding and the global embedding (**Cite Lesson 19**).
    *   **Regressor:** A final MLP predicts `formation_energy_ev_natom` and `bandgap_energy_ev`.

### Data
1.  **Geometry Preprocessing:**
    *   Parse `geometry.xyz` files.
    *   **Centering:** Subtract the unit cell centroid from all atomic coordinates.
    *   **PBC Calculations:** Compute the pairwise distance matrix using lattice vectors to strictly respect Periodic Boundary Conditions.
    *   **Feature Extraction:** For each atom, extract the minimum non-zero distance (NN Dist) and compute the sum of inverse distances (Potential Proxy).
2.  **Tabular Preprocessing:**
    *   Extract lattice parameters, stoichiometry, and **Total Atoms**.
    *   Calculate Unit Cell Volume and Atomic Density.
    *   **Scaling:** Apply Standard Scaling (z-score) to all continuous features (atomic coords, distances, potentials, global features) to ensure balanced gradients.
3.  **Target Transformation:**
    *   Apply `log(1+y)` to both target variables to align the training objective (MSE) with the evaluation metric (RMSLE).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** Mean Squared Error (MSE) on the log-transformed targets.
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with weight decay ($1e-4$) to regularize the wide layers.
*   **Scheduler:** `ReduceLROnPlateau` monitoring validation loss.
*   **Strategy:** Train for **200 epochs** with early stopping (patience ~20 epochs). The wide, regularized architecture allows for stable convergence over a longer schedule (**Cite Lesson 17**).

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** Process the test set using the identical pipeline (Centering, PBC-Potential calculation, Scaling).
2.  **Prediction:** Pass the processed data through the trained model.
3.  **Inverse Transformation:** Apply `exp(x) - 1` to the model outputs to recover the original energy scale.
4.  **Submission:** Format the results as `id,formation_energy_ev_natom,bandgap_energy_ev`.
