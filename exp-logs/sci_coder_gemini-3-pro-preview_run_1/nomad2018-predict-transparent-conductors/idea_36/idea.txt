Based on the comprehensive analysis of previous experiments and the extracted lessons, I propose the **Parsimonious Geometric Wide Deep Sets (PG-WDS)** strategy.

This strategy structurally evolves the successful "Augmented Point-Cloud Deep Sets" (Idea 4) by refining the local feature engineering to capture both **bonding constraints** and **local packing density** without incurring the penalties of sparsity, discontinuity, or overfitting observed in other attempts. It introduces a **Dual-Scalar Geometric Fingerprint** ($d_{min}$ and $d_{mean}$) to the atomic stream. Crucially, it rigorously enforces the **Wide MLP**, **Batch Normalization**, **Dropout**, and **System Cardinality** safeguards that have been identified as essential for stability and generalization in Lessons 17, 24, 35, and 36.

### Model
The architecture consists of two parallel processing streams that merge via Late Fusion:

1.  **Atomic Stream (Wide Geometric Point Processor):**
    *   **Input Features:** For each atom, construct a compact, dense feature vector (9 dimensions):
        *   **Atomic Identity:** One-hot encoding (Al, Ga, In, O) (4 dims).
        *   **Spatial Context:** Centered Cartesian coordinates $(x, y, z)$ relative to the unit cell centroid. This retains critical node-level spatial variance which is lost with fractional coordinates (**Cite Lesson 10, Lesson 39**).
        *   **Nearest Neighbor Distance ($d_{min}$):** The scalar distance to the single closest neighbor (PBC-corrected). This serves as a sharp, high-signal proxy for immediate bond constraints and steric repulsion (**Cite Lesson 15, Lesson 77**).
        *   **Local Packing Density ($d_{mean}$):** The arithmetic mean of the distances to the $K=12$ nearest neighbors (PBC-corrected). This provides a robust, continuous scalar proxy for the local crowding and coordination environment. It complements the sharp $d_{min}$ with a smoother environmental descriptor, adhering to the principle of parsimonious features (**Cite Lesson 21**) while avoiding the noise of higher-order statistics like standard deviation (**Cite Lesson 33**).
    *   **Encoder:** A **Wide MLP** (e.g., expanding immediately to 512 units) to project these low-dimensional features into a high-dimensional latent space (**Cite Lesson 6**).
    *   **Regularization:** **Batch Normalization** (**Cite Lesson 36**) and **Dropout** (e.g., 0.1) (**Cite Lesson 24**) are applied after every ReLU activation. This is critical to normalize the heterogeneous scales of the inputs (one-hot vs. coords vs. distances) and prevent the overfitting observed in high-capacity models.
    *   **Output:** A **Linear** projection (no activation) to the final embedding space to preserve the full vector magnitude for pooling (**Cite Lesson 18**).
    *   **Aggregation:** **Dual Pooling** (concatenation of Global Mean and Global Max) to capture both average properties and salient structural features (**Cite Lesson 5**). Sum pooling is explicitly excluded to maintain robustness for intensive targets (**Cite Lesson 7**).

2.  **Global Stream (Thermodynamic Context):**
    *   **Input:** Macroscopic features: Lattice vector lengths, lattice angles, unit cell volume, atomic density, **explicit stoichiometry** (**Cite Lesson 13**), and crucially, the **Total Number of Atoms** (**Cite Lesson 35**).
    *   **Encoder:** A **High-Capacity MLP** (e.g., 256 units) with Batch Normalization and Dropout to model non-linear boundary condition effects (**Cite Lesson 9**).

3.  **Fusion Head:**
    *   **Late Fusion:** Concatenate the aggregated atomic embedding and the global embedding (**Cite Lesson 19**).
    *   **Regressor:** A final MLP predicts `formation_energy_ev_natom` and `bandgap_energy_ev`.

### Data
1.  **Geometry Preprocessing:**
    *   Parse `geometry.xyz` files.
    *   **Centering:** Subtract the unit cell centroid from all atomic coordinates.
    *   **PBC Neighbor Search:** Compute the pairwise distance matrix using lattice vectors to respect Periodic Boundary Conditions.
    *   **Feature Extraction:** For each atom, extract the distances to the 12 nearest neighbors. Use the first (minimum) as $d_{min}$ and the average of all 12 as $d_{mean}$.
2.  **Tabular Preprocessing:**
    *   Extract lattice parameters, stoichiometry, and **Total Atoms**.
    *   Calculate Unit Cell Volume and Atomic Density.
    *   **Scaling:** Apply Standard Scaling (z-score) to all continuous features (atomic coords, geometric scalars, global features) to ensure balanced gradients.
3.  **Target Transformation:**
    *   Apply `log(1+y)` to both target variables to align the training objective (MSE) with the evaluation metric (RMSLE).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** Mean Squared Error (MSE) on the log-transformed targets.
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with weight decay ($1e-4$) to regularize the wide layers.
*   **Scheduler:** `ReduceLROnPlateau` monitoring validation loss.
*   **Strategy:** Train for **200 epochs** with early stopping (patience ~20 epochs). The wide, regularized architecture allows for stable convergence over a longer schedule (**Cite Lesson 17**).

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** Process the test set using the identical pipeline (Centering, PBC-Geometric calculation, Scaling).
2.  **Prediction:** Pass the processed data through the trained model.
3.  **Inverse Transformation:** Apply `exp(x) - 1` to the model outputs to recover the original energy scale.
4.  **Submission:** Format the results as `id,formation_energy_ev_natom,bandgap_energy_ev`.
