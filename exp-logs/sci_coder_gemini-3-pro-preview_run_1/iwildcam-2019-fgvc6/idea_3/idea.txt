Based on the analysis of the previous attempts and the specific lessons learned, I propose a solution that fundamentally shifts the strategy from "sampling-based imbalance handling" to "regularization-based imbalance handling" on the full dataset.

# Model
I will utilize the **ConvNeXt-Tiny** architecture. This model was identified in **Idea 2** as a strong candidate, but its potential was capped by data subsampling. ConvNeXt offers a modern design (hierarchical structure, large kernels) that bridges the gap between standard CNNs and Transformers, making it highly effective for feature extraction in complex environments.
*   **Weights:** Initialized with ImageNet-1k pre-trained weights.
*   **Head:** A single linear classification head outputting 23 logits.

# Data
The data pipeline will undergo a structural overhaul to resolve the conflict between **Lesson 00002** (oversampling causes memorization) and **Lesson 00008** (subsampling hurts performance).
1.  **Full Dataset Utilization:** I will train on the complete 196k image training set. **Cite Lesson 00008** established that subsampling to fit a model budget is detrimental; the A100 GPU is sufficiently powerful to process the full dataset with ConvNeXt-Tiny within the 24-hour limit.
2.  **No Sampler:** I will explicitly remove the `WeightedRandomSampler`. **Cite Lesson 00002** demonstrated that mechanically repeating rare images (which are grouped by location) forces the model to memorize static backgrounds rather than animal features.
3.  **Mixup and CutMix:** To address the imbalance without sampling, I will implement **Mixup** and **CutMix** augmentations. These techniques generate synthetic training examples by convexly combining pairs of images and their labels. This provides two critical benefits:
    *   It effectively "oversamples" the feature space of rare classes by mixing them with the abundant "empty" class (e.g., an image that is 50% "deer" and 50% "empty"), forcing the model to distinguish animal features from the background without seeing the exact same rare image repeatedly (**Cite Lesson 00002**).
    *   It maintains the presence of the "empty" class (background), preventing the loss of precision identified when the background is aggressively undersampled (**Cite Lesson 00014**).

# Training
The training process will be optimized to handle the long-tail distribution mathematically rather than mechanically.
1.  **Loss Function:** I will use **Class-Balanced Focal Loss**, but with a corrected implementation. The class weights (calculated by inverse frequency) will be applied as an **external multiplier** to the computed loss, ensuring the gradient for the majority class is not zeroed out by the probability computation itself (**Cite Lesson 00004**).
2.  **Optimizer:** AdamW with a standard weight decay (e.g., 0.05) and a Cosine Annealing learning rate scheduler.
3.  **EMA (Exponential Moving Average):** I will maintain an EMA of the model weights during training. This stabilizes the training trajectory, especially when using high-variance augmentations like Mixup, and generally leads to better generalization on the test set.

# Evaluation
For the test set, images will be resized to 224x224 and normalized. No test-time augmentation (TTA) will be used initially to ensure inference speed, but the model used for prediction will be the **EMA weights** saved at the end of training, which typically provide a more robust decision boundary than the final raw weights.
