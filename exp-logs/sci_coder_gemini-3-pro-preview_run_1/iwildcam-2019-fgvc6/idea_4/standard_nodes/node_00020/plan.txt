Based on the failure analysis of the previous solution, which showed a negative correlation between error and class weight (indicating the model performed *better* on rare classes than common ones), I will optimize the solution by dampening the class weights. The previous inverse-frequency weighting was likely too aggressive, causing the model to hallucinate rare animals in the dominant "empty" background images (False Positives), thereby hurting the precision of animal classes.

My strategy is:
1.  **Dampen Class Weights**: Modify `library/loss.py` to use the square root of the inverse frequency ($w_c = \sqrt{N/N_c}$) instead of the raw inverse frequency. This reduces the relative weight of rare classes from ~3800x to ~60x, balancing the trade-off between recalling rare species and maintaining precision on the majority background class.
2.  **Increase Training Epochs**: Modify `runfile.py` to increase epochs from 5 to 10. Since we are reducing the "force" of the gradients from rare classes, the optimization landscape is less steep/distorted, and the model may benefit from a longer training schedule to converge effectively. The A100 GPU allows this within the time limit.
3.  **Fresh Experiment**: Update `library/config.py` to use a new working directory (`idea_5`) to ensure training starts from scratch.
