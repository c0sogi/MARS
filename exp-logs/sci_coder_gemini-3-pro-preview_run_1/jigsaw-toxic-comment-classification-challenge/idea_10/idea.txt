# Solution: Domain-Adapted DeBERTa-v3 with Multi-Layer Fusion and Adversarial Robustness

## Model
I propose a robust architecture designed to capture both the high-level semantics and the low-level lexical triggers of toxicity, addressing the specific failure modes identified in the lessons.

1.  **Backbone (Strategic Retention):** We will utilize **DeBERTa-v3-base**. This model outperforms RNNs due to pre-trained semantic knowledge (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007) and offers superior context understanding via disentangled attention. We explicitly select the "Base" variant to ensure we can train on the **full dataset** using Cross-Validation within the 24-hour limit, avoiding the performance pitfalls of subsampling required by "Large" models (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014).
2.  **Multi-Layer Fusion (Structural Innovation):** Instead of using only the final hidden state, we will implement a **learnable weighted sum of the last 4 encoder layers**. Lesson 00009 highlighted that lower layers contain critical lexical information (slang, profanity) that must be adapted. By aggregating these layers, we provide the classification head with direct access to these raw signals alongside the high-level semantic context.
3.  **Hybrid Pooling Head (Strategic Retention):** We will apply the proven hybrid pooling strategy to the fused layer representation:
    *   **Global Max Pooling:** To extract the strongest local toxic signal (e.g., a specific slur) regardless of context (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
    *   **Linear Attention Pooling:** To compute a context-aware weighted average, prioritizing relevant tokens while filtering noise (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002). We will strictly use **Linear Attention** ($w^T h_t$) to ensure efficient convergence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
    *   These two vectors will be concatenated.
4.  **Multi-Sample Dropout (Strategic Retention):** The concatenated vector will pass through 5 parallel dropout masks. This smooths the loss landscape and improves generalization, provided the model is trained for sufficient epochs (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016).

## Data
1.  **Tokenization (Strategic Retention):** We will use the **DeBERTa-v3 Tokenizer** (SentencePiece). This preserves punctuation and special characters (e.g., "!!", "f**k") as distinct tokens, which are high-signal features for toxicity often lost in standard cleaning (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).
2.  **Unsupervised Corpus Construction:** We will concatenate the text from both `train.csv` and `test.csv` to create a large domain-specific corpus for the pre-training stage.
3.  **Supervised Dataset:** We will use the **Full Training Dataset** for the classification stage (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014).

## Training
I propose a **Two-Stage Training Pipeline** (Structural Innovation) to maximize domain adaptation and robustness.

**Stage 1: Domain-Adaptive Pre-training (DAPT)**
*   **Objective:** We will fine-tune the DeBERTa-v3 backbone on the combined (train + test) corpus using the **Masked Language Modeling (MLM)** objective for 3 epochs.
*   **Rationale:** Lesson 00009 highlighted that lower layers fail to adapt to toxic vocabulary (slang, profanity) when learning rates are dampened or when training is purely supervised. DAPT explicitly adapts the tokenizer embeddings and lower-layer features to the specific distribution of Wikipedia comments *before* the classification task begins, resolving the lexical gap without the risks of Layer-wise Learning Rate Decay (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).

**Stage 2: Robust Supervised Fine-Tuning (5-Fold CV)**
*   **Strategy:** We will fine-tune the domain-adapted backbone using **5-Fold Cross-Validation**.
*   **Adversarial Weight Perturbation (AWP) (Strategic Retention):** We will implement AWP starting from the second epoch. By perturbing weights to maximize loss during training, AWP forces the model to find flatter, more robust minima, squeezing out the final performance gains in this high-AUC regime (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).
*   **Optimization:** We will use the **AdamW** optimizer with a **Uniform Learning Rate**. We will explicitly **reject Layer-wise Learning Rate Decay (LLRD)**, allowing the aggregated lower layers to update at the full rate to capture toxic vocabulary (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
*   **Scheduling:** We will use a **OneCycleLR** scheduler with a duration of **5 epochs per fold**. This extended duration is critical to allow the complex architecture (Layer Fusion + Multi-Sample Dropout) to converge effectively, avoiding the underfitting observed with compressed schedules (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006, solution_lesson_node_00016).

## Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** We will generate predictions for the test set using each of the 5 trained fold models.
2.  **Ensembling:** The final prediction for each comment will be the arithmetic mean of the probabilities output by the 5 models. This ensemble approach reduces variance and maximizes the Mean Column-wise ROC AUC.
3.  **Submission:** The averaged probabilities will be formatted into the required CSV structure.
