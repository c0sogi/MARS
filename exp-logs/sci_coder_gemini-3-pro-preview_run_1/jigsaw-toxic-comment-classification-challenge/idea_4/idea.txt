# Context-Aware DeBERTa-v3 with Hybrid Pooling and Multi-Sample Dropout

## Model
I propose a structural evolution that integrates the superior representation capabilities of **DeBERTa-v3-base** with the highly effective feature aggregation strategies identified in previous experiments.

1.  **Backbone (Structural Innovation):** We will utilize **DeBERTa-v3-base** as the feature extractor. While Lesson 00007 demonstrated that Transformers (RoBERTa) significantly outperform RNNs due to pre-trained semantic knowledge, DeBERTa-v3 improves upon RoBERTa by using disentangled attention (separating content and position embeddings) and a Replaced Token Detection (RTD) objective. This makes it exceptionally capable of handling the subtle, context-dependent toxicity found in Wikipedia comments.
2.  **Hybrid Aggregation Head (Strategic Retention):** We will retain the custom pooling strategy that consistently outperformed standard methods. The model will extract the sequence of hidden states and apply two parallel pooling operations:
    *   **Global Max Pooling:** To capture the strongest local signal (e.g., a specific slur) regardless of its position or surrounding context (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
    *   **Linear Attention Pooling:** To compute a weighted average of the sequence, prioritizing relevant tokens while filtering out noise (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002). We will strictly use a simple **Linear Attention** projection ($w^T h_t$) rather than complex additive attention, as the latter adds computational overhead without performance gains (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
    *   These two vectors will be concatenated to form the final representation.
3.  **Multi-Sample Dropout (Structural Innovation):** To further regularize the model and smooth the loss landscape, we will implement **Multi-Sample Dropout**. The concatenated representation will be passed through multiple (e.g., 5) parallel dropout layers with different masks. The outputs will share the same final dense classification layer, and the resulting logits will be averaged during both training and inference.

## Data
1.  **Tokenization:** We will use the **DeBERTa-v3 Tokenizer** (SentencePiece-based). This tokenizer treats the input as a raw stream and preserves punctuation and special characters as distinct tokens. This is critical for detecting obfuscated toxicity and emotional intensity (e.g., "!!", "f**k"), which are valuable signals often lost by standard alphanumeric cleaning (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).
2.  **Preprocessing:** Input comments will be tokenized and padded/truncated to a fixed length (e.g., 300 tokens).

## Training
1.  **Optimization Strategy:** We will use the **AdamW** optimizer. Crucially, we will apply a **Uniform Learning Rate** across all layers. We will explicitly **reject Layer-wise Learning Rate Decay (LLRD)**. Experiments have shown that dampening the learning rate of lower layers hinders the model's ability to adapt its embeddings to the specific toxic vocabulary (slang, profanity) required for this task (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
2.  **Scheduling:** We will employ a **OneCycleLR** scheduler. We will set the training duration to a sufficient number of epochs (e.g., 4 to 5) to ensure the learning rate decay is gradual. We will avoid compressing the schedule into too few epochs, as this forces premature convergence and degrades generalization (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
3.  **Loss Function:** Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) with Logits will be used. The loss will be calculated on the averaged logits from the Multi-Sample Dropout branches.

## Evaluation
The evaluation pipeline will mirror the training setup. Test comments will be processed with the DeBERTa-v3 tokenizer. For each sample, the model will output the probability of toxicity for all six classes (averaged across the Multi-Sample Dropout heads). These probabilities will be formatted into the submission CSV with the specified columns.
