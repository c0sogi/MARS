# Context-Aware DeBERTa-v3 with Multi-Sample Dropout

## Model
I propose evolving the architecture by replacing the RoBERTa backbone with **DeBERTa-v3-base** and enhancing the classification head with **Multi-Sample Dropout**.

1.  **Backbone (Structural Innovation):** We will transition from RoBERTa to **DeBERTa-v3-base** (Decoding-enhanced BERT with disentangled attention). While RoBERTa proved superior to RNNs (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007), DeBERTa introduces a disentangled attention mechanism where content and position are encoded separately. This allows the model to better distinguish between the semantic meaning of a word (e.g., a slur) and its relative position, providing a more nuanced representation of toxicity in context.
2.  **Hybrid Aggregation (Strategic Retention):** We will retain the highly effective hybrid pooling strategy. The model will compute two distinct vectors from the transformer's hidden states:
    *   **Global Max Pooling:** To capture the strongest toxic signal (e.g., a specific keyword) regardless of the surrounding text (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
    *   **Linear Attention Pooling:** To compute a context-aware weighted sum of tokens, prioritizing relevant phrases while filtering noise (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002). We will strictly use **Linear Attention** (a single projection) rather than complex additive attention, as the latter has been shown to slow convergence without performance gains in this domain (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
    *   These two vectors will be concatenated.
3.  **Multi-Sample Dropout (Structural Innovation):** Instead of a single dropout layer before the final classifier, we will implement **Multi-Sample Dropout**. The concatenated embedding will be passed through multiple parallel dropout masks (e.g., 5 distinct masks). The output of each masked stream will be fed into the final dense layer (shared weights), and the resulting logits will be averaged. This technique effectively acts as an ensemble within a single model, smoothing the loss landscape and improving generalization.

## Data
1.  **Tokenization:** We will use the **DeBERTa-v3 Tokenizer** (SentencePiece-based). This tokenizer treats the input as a raw stream, effectively preserving punctuation and special characters as distinct features, which is critical for detecting obfuscated toxicity and emotional intensity (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).
2.  **Formatting:** Inputs will be padded/truncated to a fixed sequence length (e.g., 300 tokens) to balance context capture with computational efficiency.

## Training
1.  **Optimization Strategy:** We will employ the **AdamW** optimizer with a **Uniform Learning Rate** across all layers. We will explicitly **avoid Layer-wise Learning Rate Decay (LLRD)**. Previous experiments demonstrated that dampening the learning rate of lower layers hinders the model's ability to adapt its embeddings to the specific toxic vocabulary of this dataset (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
2.  **Scheduling:** We will use a **OneCycleLR** scheduler. Crucially, we will set the training duration to a sufficient number of epochs (e.g., 4) to ensure the decay phase is gradual. Compressing the schedule into too few epochs has been shown to degrade performance by forcing premature convergence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
3.  **Loss Function:** Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) with Logits will be used for the multi-label objective.

## Evaluation
The evaluation pipeline remains consistent. The test data will be tokenized using the DeBERTa-v3 tokenizer, and the model will output the average probability across the Multi-Sample Dropout head for each of the six toxicity classes. These probabilities will be formatted into the required submission CSV.
