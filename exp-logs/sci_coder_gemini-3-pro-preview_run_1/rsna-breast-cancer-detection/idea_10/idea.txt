Based on the progression of empirical findings—particularly the persistent "Age Shortcut" bias (Cite solution_lesson_node_00012, solution_lesson_node_00014) and the need to capture fine-grained lesions like microcalcifications that global pooling might obscure—I propose a **Pyramid Symmetry-Difference Siamese Network**.

This architecture evolves the previous "Spatial Difference" concept (Idea 9) by extending the symmetry check to **multiple scales** of the feature pyramid. This ensures that asymmetry is detected not just at the semantic level (masses) but also at the textural level (microcalcifications), while mathematically neutralizing the demographic bias at every stage of abstraction.

# Model
**Architecture: Pyramid Symmetry-Difference Siamese EfficientNet-B2**

*   **Backbone:** **EfficientNet-B2** in a Siamese configuration (shared weights).
*   **Input Strategy (Early Fusion):**
    *   **Tensor Structure:** $3 \times 768 \times 768$, comprising the **Image**, a spatially broadcasted **Age** map, and a spatially broadcasted **Implant** map (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
    *   **Demographic Cancellation:** Since Age and Implant maps are identical for the target and contralateral views of the same patient, they will be mathematically zeroed out in the difference operations described below, strictly preventing the "Age Shortcut."
*   **Multi-Scale Difference Module (The Structural Innovation):**
    *   Instead of computing the difference only at the final layer, extract feature maps from **three distinct stages** of the backbone: **P3** (fine-grained texture), **P4** (mid-level patterns), and **P5** (global semantic structure).
    *   For each stage $i \in \{3, 4, 5\}$, compute the **Signed Feature Difference**: $D_i = F_{target, i} - F_{contra, i}$.
    *   *Rationale:* This forces the network to look for asymmetry in *texture* (P3) as well as *shape* (P5). This is critical because microcalcifications are local textural anomalies that may be lost in the final global pooling, and they are less correlated with age than global density.
*   **Fusion & Classification Head:**
    *   Apply **Global Average Pooling (GAP)** to both the Target features ($F_{target, i}$) and the Difference features ($D_i$) at all three stages.
    *   **Concatenate** all resulting vectors: $V_{final} = \text{Concat}(\text{GAP}(F_{target, 3}), \text{GAP}(D_3), ..., \text{GAP}(F_{target, 5}), \text{GAP}(D_5))$.
    *   Pass $V_{final}$ through a single fully connected layer to predict the cancer probability.

# Data
**Preprocessing & Augmentation**

*   **Paired Data Loading:**
    *   Load the **Target** image and its matching **Contralateral** image (same patient, same view).
    *   **Fail Loudly:** Raise an exception immediately if a file is missing or corrupt. Do not use silent fallbacks (e.g., black images), as this leads to model collapse (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016).
    *   **Missing Counterparts:** Only if a patient physically lacks a contralateral breast, substitute a zero-tensor.
*   **Normalization:**
    *   Apply **Standard Scaling** (Zero Mean, Unit Variance) to the Age metadata before spatial broadcasting. This ensures the scalar signal does not dominate the gradients (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).
*   **Augmentation:**
    *   **Synchronized Geometric Augmentation:** Apply identical random spatial transformations (Flip, Rotate, Shift) to *both* the target and contralateral images to maintain pixel-wise correspondence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
    *   **Disable Photometric Augmentation:** Do not use brightness or contrast shifts. Absolute pixel intensity represents physical tissue density; altering it destroys the diagnostic signal (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008).

# Training
**Procedure**

*   **Objective:** **Single-Task Learning (STL)**.
    *   Train exclusively on the binary cancer target. Do not use auxiliary heads for Breast Density.
    *   *Rationale:* Auxiliary tasks on global features (like density) are "easier" than lesion detection and exacerbate the age bias, leading to negative transfer (Cite solution_lesson_node_00021).
*   **Loss Function:** `BCEWithLogitsLoss` with `pos_weight` set to **47.0** (approximating the inverse class frequency).
    *   Aggressive positive weighting is required to maintain Probabilistic Recall in this highly imbalanced regime (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
*   **Gradient Strategy:** **Disable Gradient Clipping**.
    *   Clipping gradients neutralizes the effect of the high `pos_weight`, preventing the optimizer from making the large updates necessary to correct errors on the minority class (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW (LR 1e-4) with a Cosine Annealing scheduler.

# Evaluation
**Inference Pipeline**

*   **Pair Construction:** Iterate through `test.csv`. For each prediction row, identify and load the contralateral view.
*   **Prediction:** Pass the pair through the Pyramid Siamese network.
*   **Aggregation:** Group predictions by `prediction_id` (breast level). Compute the **Max** probability across all available views (CC and MLO) for that breast to generate the final submission score.
