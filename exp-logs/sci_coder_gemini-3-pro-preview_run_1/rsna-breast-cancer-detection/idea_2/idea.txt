Based on the empirical lessons provided, I propose a **Multi-Task Metadata-Embedded Network** with increased input resolution. This approach evolves the successful "Early Fusion" architecture by introducing an auxiliary learning objective (Breast Density) to enforce robust feature extraction, while scaling up the backbone and resolution to capture fine-grained microcalcifications.

### Model
**Architecture:** Multi-Task Metadata-Embedded EfficientNet-B2
*   **Backbone:** Upgrade from EfficientNet-B0 to **EfficientNet-B2**. The B2 variant offers a deeper and wider parameter space, necessary for capturing the subtle textural differences in higher-resolution mammograms.
*   **Input Strategy (Early Fusion):** Retain the **Spatial Channel Expansion** technique. The input tensor will be $3 \times H \times W$, consisting of:
    1.  **Image Channel:** The preprocessed mammogram.
    2.  **Age Channel:** A spatially broadcasted map of the patient's age.
    3.  **Implant Channel:** A spatially broadcasted map of the binary implant status.
    *   *Cite Lesson 00002:* This structure allows the convolutional filters to learn context-aware features (e.g., age-conditioned tissue analysis) from the first layer.
*   **Multi-Task Heads:**
    1.  **Primary Head (Cancer):** A dense layer outputting the probability of malignancy.
    2.  **Auxiliary Head (Density):** A separate dense layer predicting the 4-class Breast Density (A, B, C, D). Although density is not required for the submission, training the backbone to predict it forces the network to explicitly encode tissue texture and structural patterns, which are highly correlated with cancer detection difficulty.

### Data
**Preprocessing:**
*   **Image Pipeline:**
    *   Load DICOMs using `rasterio`.
    *   Apply a threshold-based **ROI Crop** to remove the black background and center the breast.
    *   Resize images to **768x768** (up from 512x512). This higher resolution is critical for the B2 backbone to resolve small lesions.
*   **Metadata Normalization:**
    *   **Age:** Apply **Standard Scaling** (subtract training mean, divide by training std) before broadcasting. This ensures the metadata channel has unit variance and contributes gradients comparable to the image channel.
    *   *Cite Lesson 00005:* Standard scaling prevents the metadata signal from being overpowered by image features, unlike simple min-max scaling.
*   **Augmentation:**
    *   Apply **Moderate Photometric Augmentation** (RandomBrightnessContrast with $p=0.2$ and conservative limits).
    *   *Cite Lesson 00007 & 00008:* Moderate augmentation acts as a regularizer to shift reliance toward stable metadata, but aggressive augmentation must be avoided to preserve the density-based diagnostic signal.

### Training
**Procedure:**
*   **Loss Function:** A weighted sum of the primary and auxiliary losses: $L_{total} = L_{cancer} + \lambda L_{density}$.
    *   **Cancer Loss:** `BCEWithLogitsLoss` with a **High Positive Weight** (e.g., `pos_weight=45.0`). This value is chosen to approximate the inverse class frequency (~1:47).
    *   *Cite Lesson 00003:* Aggressive positive weighting is necessary to maintain Probabilistic Recall in this highly imbalanced regime, even if it increases raw false positive rates.
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with a learning rate of 1e-4 and Cosine Annealing schedule.
*   **Gradient Strategy:** **Disable Gradient Clipping**.
    *   *Cite Lesson 00009:* Clipping gradients when using a high `pos_weight` neutralizes the weighting mechanism and destabilizes convergence. We will rely on the learning rate scheduler for stability.

### Evaluation
**Inference Pipeline:**
*   **Per-Image Prediction:** Pass each test image through the trained model (discarding the auxiliary density head output).
*   **Aggregation:** Group predictions by `prediction_id` (which represents a specific breast). Compute the **Max** probability across all images (views) for that ID.
    *   *Rationale:* In screening mammography, if *any* view (CC or MLO) shows signs of cancer, the breast is considered positive. Max pooling aligns with this clinical logic and handles the variable number of images per patient.
