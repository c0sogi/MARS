### Proposed Strategy: High-Capacity Deep Residual U-Net with Stochastic Depth and Curriculum Lovasz-Snapshot Ensembling

Based on the analysis of previous experiments, I propose a strategy that resolves the conflict between the need for high model capacity (to capture texture) and the risk of overfitting on small data. This solution synthesizes the **High-Capacity Backbone** (attempted in Idea 9 but failed due to static loss) with **Stochastic Depth Regularization** and the superior **Curriculum Loss Strategy** (proven in Lesson 00045), integrated into the robust **Snapshot Ensembling** pipeline.

#### 1. Model: High-Capacity Deep Residual U-Net with Stochastic Depth
I will employ a custom **Deep Residual U-Net** that maximizes channel capacity while explicitly managing over-parameterization through structural regularization.

*   **Backbone (High-Capacity Encoder):** A custom Deep Residual Encoder initialized with **64 filters** and expanding to a bottleneck of **1024 filters**. Previous lessons demonstrated that reducing this capacity (e.g., to 512) degrades performance `Cite solution_lesson_node_00034`, as high channel width is essential for capturing the complex textural features of salt `Cite solution_lesson_node_00020`.
*   **Structural Innovation (Stochastic Depth):** To enable this high capacity without incurring the overfitting penalties seen in heavy backbones `Cite solution_lesson_node_00015`, I will implement **Stochastic Depth (DropPath)** within the residual blocks. This technique randomly drops entire residual paths during training (with probability increasing linearly with depth), acting as a powerful regularizer that promotes robust feature learning across the ensemble of sub-paths.
*   **Decoder:**
    *   **Attention:** **Concurrent Spatial and Channel Squeeze & Excitation (scSE)** modules will be placed in the decoder blocks to recalibrate features during upsampling `Cite solution_lesson_node_00007`. I will restrict attention to the decoder to avoid disrupting encoder feature extraction `Cite solution_lesson_node_00017`.
    *   **Upsampling:** I will strictly use **Bilinear Upsampling** followed by a convolution, avoiding Transpose Convolutions to prevent checkerboard artifacts `Cite solution_lesson_node_00026`.
*   **Deep Supervision:** Auxiliary prediction heads will be attached at resolutions $32 \times 32$, $64 \times 64$, and $128 \times 128$ `Cite solution_lesson_node_00021`.
*   **Input Fusion:** Depth information (`z`) will be fused as a dense channel at the input `Cite solution_lesson_node_00002`.

#### 2. Data: Maximum Utilization and Integrity
*   **Preprocessing:** Images will be padded to $128 \times 128$ using **Reflection Padding** to handle the $101 \times 101$ dimensions without introducing edge artifacts `Cite solution_lesson_node_00002`.
*   **Data Partitioning:** I will use a single **90/10 Stratified Split** (Train/Val). I will explicitly avoid K-Fold Cross-Validation to maximize the training signal for the single model, preventing the performance drop observed when fragmenting the small dataset `Cite solution_lesson_node_00019`.
*   **Augmentation:** I will restrict augmentation to **Horizontal Flips** only. I will strictly avoid interpolation-heavy transformations (rotation, scaling) to preserve the edge sharpness required for the high-IoU metric `Cite solution_lesson_node_00024`.

#### 3. Training: Curriculum Lovasz-Cycle Schedule
This pipeline adopts the **Curriculum Loss Strategy** proven superior to static compound losses `Cite solution_lesson_node_00045`, while correcting the implementation errors of previous attempts.

*   **Optimizer & Scheduler:** **AdamW** with **Cosine Annealing Warm Restarts**.
    *   **Schedule:** **3 constant cycles of 50 epochs** (Total: 150 epochs). The constant cycle length allows the model to settle into robust minima before restarting `Cite solution_lesson_node_00022`, `Cite solution_lesson_node_00023`.
*   **Phase 1 (Cycles 1 & 2, Epochs 0-100):**
    *   **Loss:** **BCE + Sample-Wise Dice Loss**. Crucially, the Dice loss will be calculated **per image** and averaged. This ensures that empty masks (39% of data) are correctly penalized, fixing the batch-wise aggregation failure `Cite solution_lesson_node_00025`.
    *   **Goal:** Establish a robust feature extractor and converge to a stable minimum `Cite solution_lesson_node_00018`.
*   **Phase 2 (Cycle 3, Epochs 101-150):**
    *   **Loss:** Switch to **Lovasz-Hinge Loss** (weighted with BCE). This switch occurs *only* after the model has fully converged in Phase 1, directly optimizing the Jaccard index (IoU) for the final fine-tuning `Cite solution_lesson_node_00045`.
*   **Checkpointing:** I will save the "Best Model" for each cycle based strictly on the **Validation mAP**, not the loss `Cite solution_lesson_node_00016`.

#### 4. Evaluation: Snapshot Ensembling
*   **Inference Strategy:** I will implement **Snapshot Ensembling** by averaging the predictions of the **Best Model from Cycle 2** (Robust BCE/Dice Baseline) and the **Best Model from Cycle 3** (Lovasz Refined). This leverages the diversity of the local minima visited by the cyclic schedule to improve generalization without the cost of training multiple independent models `Cite solution_lesson_node_00035`.
*   **TTA:** Test-Time Augmentation (Horizontal Flip) will be applied to each snapshot before averaging `Cite solution_lesson_node_00004`.
*   **Post-Processing:** Predictions will be center-cropped to $101 \times 101$ before thresholding `Cite solution_lesson_node_00002`.
