Based on the comprehensive analysis of the provided lessons and previous experiments, I propose the following optimized strategy. This solution structurally evolves the successful ConvNeXt-Small pipeline by scaling the architecture to **ConvNeXt-Base**, implementing **Layer-wise Learning Rate Decay (LLRD)** to effectively train the deeper network, and integrating **Exponential Moving Average (EMA)** for weight stabilization.

# Model
- **Architecture:** Upgrade the backbone to **ConvNeXt-Base** (pre-trained on ImageNet-21k).
    - **Rationale:** While ConvNeXt-Small was effective **Cite solution_lesson_node_00018**, the "Base" variant offers significantly higher parameter capacity. This is necessary to capture the extremely subtle, fine-grained textural differences between disease classes that smaller models might miss.
- **Head:** Retain the **Multi-Sample Dropout** classification head.
    - **Rationale:** This structure accelerates convergence and improves generalization by averaging logits from multiple dropout masks **Cite solution_lesson_node_00012**.
- **Weight Stabilization:** Implement **Model Exponential Moving Average (EMA)**.
    - **Rationale:** Maintain a shadow copy of the model parameters that updates as a moving average of the training weights. This decouples the final model from the stochastic variance of the final few batches (especially under strong augmentation) and often leads to a wider, more robust minima **Cite solution_lesson_node_00017**.

# Data
- **Preprocessing (Progressive Resizing):**
    - **Phase 1 (Coarse):** Resize images to **384x384** for the initial training phase.
    - **Phase 2 (Fine):** Resize images to **512x512** for the fine-tuning phase. This leverages the finding that high-resolution fine-tuning yields significant accuracy gains **Cite solution_lesson_node_00018**.
- **Augmentation:**
    - **Mixing:** Apply **MixUp** and **CutMix** to synthesize novel training samples and continuous labels, handling the class imbalance implicitly **Cite solution_lesson_node_00011**.
    - **Geometric:** Apply aggressive transformations (Transpose, VerticalFlip, ShiftScaleRotate) to encode rotational invariance **Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**.
    - **Contextual Cropping:** Constrain `RandomResizedCrop` to a scale of `(0.3, 1.0)` to prevent training on ambiguous macro-crops **Cite solution_lesson_node_00015**.
    - **Determinism:** Explicitly seed the `worker_init_fn` in DataLoaders **Cite solution_lesson_node_00007**.

# Training
- **Loss Function:** Use **Soft Target Cross Entropy** to handle the continuous targets generated by MixUp/CutMix **Cite solution_lesson_node_00011**.
- **Optimizer:** AdamW with **Layer-wise Learning Rate Decay (LLRD)**.
    - **Innovation:** Instead of a single learning rate, apply a decay factor (e.g., 0.8) layer-by-layer, such that the early layers (closer to the input) have a much lower learning rate than the head. This preserves the robust, pre-trained low-level features of the deeper ConvNeXt-Base backbone while allowing the high-level semantic features to adapt aggressively.
- **Regularization:** Explicitly set **Stochastic Depth (Drop Path)** rate to `0.0`.
    - **Rationale:** Avoid over-regularizing the model, as the combination of MixUp, CutMix, and Multi-Sample Dropout is already providing strong regularization **Cite solution_lesson_node_00019**.
- **Strategy:**
    1.  **Warmup:** Train for 1 epoch at 384x384 with the backbone frozen **Cite solution_lesson_node_00001**.
    2.  **Coarse Training:** Train for ~12 epochs at 384x384 with LLRD and EMA enabled.
    3.  **High-Res Fine-Tuning:** Train for ~5 epochs at 512x512 with a reduced base learning rate.
- **Batch Size:** Use **Gradient Accumulation** to ensure the effective batch size remains $\ge 32$, preserving Batch Normalization stability **Cite solution_lesson_node_00016**.

# Evaluation
- **Inference:** Perform **Test Time Augmentation (TTA)** by averaging predictions from the original image and its horizontally/vertically flipped versions **Cite solution_lesson_node_00011**.
- **Model Selection:** Use the weights from the **EMA** model for the final inference, as they represent a more stable convergence point than the raw weights.
