# Proposed Solution: DeBERTa-v3 with Semantic Triangulation and Bias-Weighted Loss

This strategy represents a structural evolution of the Multi-Task Learning approach (Idea 4). It upgrades the backbone to a state-of-the-art architecture specifically designed for disentangling signal dependencies and introduces a "Semantic Triangulation" objective to explicitly model the difference between identity mentions and identity attacks.

### Model
**Architecture:**
We replace the DistilBERT backbone with **DeBERTa-v3-base**.
*   **Backbone Innovation:** DeBERTa (Decoding-enhanced BERT with disentangled attention) uses a disentangled attention mechanism that represents words using two vectors: one for content and one for position. This structural change allows the model to better distinguish between the *content* of a word (e.g., "gay") and its *contextual sentiment* (e.g., used neutrally vs. pejoratively), which is the core challenge of the BPSN metric.
*   **Structural Innovation (Semantic Triangulation):** We expand the Multi-Task Learning framework to include three distinct classification heads sharing the DeBERTa encoder:
    1.  **Primary Toxicity Head:** Predicts the main `target`.
    2.  **Auxiliary Identity Head:** Predicts the presence of identity attributes (e.g., `male`, `muslim`). **Cite solution_lesson_node_00004**.
    3.  **Auxiliary "Identity Attack" Head:** A new head that specifically predicts the `identity_attack` subtype provided in the dataset.
    *   *Rationale:* This creates a "triangulation" signal. The model learns that if the Identity Head is active but the Identity Attack Head is inactive, the Primary Toxicity should likely be low. This explicitly resolves the ambiguity where the model conflates identity mentions with toxicity.

### Data
**Preprocessing:**
1.  **Tokenization:** Use the DeBERTa-v3 tokenizer on `comment_text` with dynamic padding to optimize training speed.
2.  **Target Engineering:** Prepare soft targets for the Primary Head (`target`), binary vectors for the Identity Head, and soft targets for the Identity Attack Head (`identity_attack`).
3.  **Bias-Centric Weight Generation:** We strictly retain the weighting strategy from the lessons. We generate a sample weight vector where examples belonging to the "Bias Trap" subgroups (Non-Toxic + Identity Mention, Toxic + Identity Mention) are assigned a weight of 5.0, while others remain at 1.0. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**.

### Training
**Procedure:**
1.  **Objective Function:** The total loss is a weighted sum: $L_{total} = L_{primary} + \lambda_1 L_{identity} + \lambda_2 L_{attack}$.
    *   **Primary Loss:** We use **Weighted Binary Cross-Entropy** on the soft toxicity targets. Crucially, this component is multiplied by the pre-calculated bias-centric sample weights to force the optimizer to focus on the difficult subgroups. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**.
    *   **Auxiliary Losses:** We use standard Binary Cross-Entropy for the Identity and Identity Attack heads to supervise representation learning without applying the bias weights (to preserve the true data distribution for feature learning). **Cite solution_lesson_node_00004**.
2.  **Optimization:** Train on the full dataset (unlike the subset in Idea 4) using the AdamW optimizer with a linear learning rate schedule and warmup. The A100 GPU allows for efficient fine-tuning of the DeBERTa-base model on the full 1.4M rows within the time limit.

### Evaluation
**Inference:**
The test set is processed through the fine-tuned DeBERTa backbone. We extract predictions **only from the Primary Toxicity Head** for the final submission. The auxiliary heads are discarded, having served their purpose of regularizing the encoder to disentangle identity concepts from toxicity concepts during training.
