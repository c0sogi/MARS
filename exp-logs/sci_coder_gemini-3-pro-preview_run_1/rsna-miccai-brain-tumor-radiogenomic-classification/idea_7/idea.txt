# 2.5D Spatial Montage Network (Grid-View)

This solution introduces a **Spatial Montage** input paradigm. Instead of compressing the volume via MIP (which destroys texture, Cite solution_lesson_node_00003), stacking slices in the channel dimension (which destroys RGB priors, Cite solution_lesson_node_00009), or processing them independently in a Siamese network (which fails due to signal dilution, Cite solution_lesson_node_00018), this approach arranges multiple deterministic slices into a single **$2 \times 2$ spatial grid**. This allows the CNN to process volumetric context simultaneously as a single high-resolution image, leveraging its inherent spatial attention mechanisms to identify the tumor in the relevant quadrant while ignoring the others.

### Model
*   **Architecture:** We will use **EfficientNet-B0** as the backbone. This architecture is retained for its parameter efficiency and proven stability on this dataset (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
*   **Input Structure:** The network accepts a single tensor of shape $(3, H \times 2, W \times 2)$. Specifically, we construct a **Montage** of 4 distinct axial slices arranged in a $2 \times 2$ grid.
    *   **Channels:** The 3 channels correspond to **FLAIR**, **T1wCE**, and **T2w** modalities, preserving the pretrained RGB feature extractors.
    *   **Spatial Layout:**
        *   Top-Left: Slice at 35% depth.
        *   Top-Right: Slice at 45% depth.
        *   Bottom-Left: Slice at 55% depth.
        *   Bottom-Right: Slice at 65% depth.
*   **Rationale:** This layout provides "peripheral vision" along the Z-axis, covering the central 30% of the brain volume where the tumor is statistically likely to reside (fixing the "Middle Slice Fallacy", Cite solution_lesson_node_00002) without the computational instability of 3D sequence modeling (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008).

### Data
*   **Deterministic Strided Sampling:** We employ a strict geometric heuristic to select slice indices at **35%, 45%, 55%, and 65%** of the sorted file list for each subject. This avoids the label noise introduced by stochastic sampling (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015) and the risk of unsupervised metrics selecting artifacts (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
*   **Anisotropy Handling:** We strictly use the **Axial** plane (native acquisition). We discard Coronal/Sagittal reconstructions to avoid the performance degradation caused by low-resolution interpolation artifacts (Cite solution_lesson_node_00021).
*   **Preprocessing:**
    *   **Montage Construction:** The 4 selected slices are resized to $224 \times 224$ and tiled to form a $448 \times 448$ composite image.
    *   **Precision:** All operations are performed in **float32**. We explicitly avoid casting to `uint8` to preserve the high dynamic range of the MRI sensors (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016).
    *   **Normalization:** We apply **Instance-level Min-Max Scaling** to the $[0, 1]$ range. We strictly avoid ImageNet mean/std normalization as it distorts the grayscale histograms of medical data (Cite solution_lesson_node_00017).

### Training
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with a learning rate of $1e-4$.
*   **Regularization:**
    *   **Weight Decay:** Set to **$1e-2$**. We retain this aggressive regularization to prevent overfitting on the small dataset (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).
    *   **Dropout:** We utilize the default dropout rate of the EfficientNet backbone, avoiding manual overrides that may degrade performance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00012).
*   **Augmentation:** We apply **Elastic Transformations** and **Grid Distortions** to the entire $448 \times 448$ montage. This ensures spatial consistency across the grid while providing the non-rigid deformations necessary for small-data generalization (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).
*   **Strategy:** We employ **5-Fold Cross-Validation** to mitigate the high stochastic variance observed in single training runs (Cite solution_lesson_node_00011).

### Evaluation
*   **Inference:** For each test subject, we deterministically generate the same $2 \times 2$ montage (slices at 35%, 45%, 55%, 65%).
*   **Prediction:** The montage is passed through the trained model. The final prediction is the average probability across the 5 folds. This ensures the model has access to the same volumetric context seen during training, maximizing the probability of detecting off-center tumors.
