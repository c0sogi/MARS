# Stochastic 2.5D Volumetric Ensemble

This solution introduces a **Stochastic Volumetric Sampling** paradigm. Instead of relying on a single static "middle slice" (which risks missing the tumor, Cite solution_lesson_node_00002) or computationally expensive 3D sequence modeling (which fails due to batch size constraints, Cite solution_lesson_node_00008), this approach trains a lightweight 2D classifier to identify tumor signals across a "Central Volume of Interest."

### Model
The core architecture remains a standard **2D EfficientNet-B0** backbone initialized with ImageNet weights.
*   **Input:** A 3-channel composite image $(224 \times 224 \times 3)$ comprising spatially aligned slices from **FLAIR**, **T1wCE**, and **T2w** modalities. This preserves the pre-trained RGB priors and avoids the signal dilution observed when naively stacking many depth slices (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
*   **Backbone:** EfficientNet-B0 is selected for its parameter efficiency, allowing for larger batch sizes which are critical for Batch Normalization stability (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008).
*   **Head:** A simple Global Average Pooling followed by a fully connected layer.
*   **Configuration:** We will retain the default dropout rate of the backbone (typically 0.2) rather than manually forcing a higher rate, as "accidental defaults" have been shown to outperform manual tuning in this specific transfer learning regime (Cite solution_lesson_node_00012).

### Data
The data pipeline shifts from static file selection to **Dynamic Stochastic Sampling**:
1.  **Central Volume Definition:** For each subject, we define a **Central Volume of Interest (VOI)** spanning the middle 30% of the scan (e.g., slice indices from 35% to 65% of the total depth). This relies on the robust geometric prior that the brain is centrally located (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006) while expanding the search space beyond a single slice to account for anatomical variance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
2.  **Training (Stochastic Sampling):** During training, for each epoch and each subject, we **randomly sample one slice index** from the Central VOI. This acts as a powerful form of data augmentation, ensuring the model sees a diverse range of tumor appearances and cross-sections, preventing it from memorizing a specific "middle slice" artifact.
3.  **Augmentation:** We will apply **Elastic Transformations** and **Grid Distortions** in addition to standard affine flips. Non-rigid augmentations are critical for preventing overfitting in small medical datasets (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).

### Training
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with a learning rate of $1e-4$.
*   **Regularization:** We will apply aggressive **Weight Decay ($1e-2$)**. Previous experiments demonstrated that standard weight decay ($1e-4$) is insufficient for this dataset size, while stronger decay significantly improves generalization (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).
*   **Batch Size:** Maintain a high batch size (e.g., 32 or 64) to ensure stable gradient convergence, which is only possible because we are using a 2D architecture (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008).
*   **Validation:** To mitigate the high stochastic variance observed in small-data training (Cite solution_lesson_node_00011), we will employ **5-Fold Cross-Validation** (or a robust hold-out with multiple seed averaging) rather than relying on a single training run.

### Evaluation
*   **Slice Ensemble Inference:** For each test subject, we will deterministically extract **5 equidistant slices** from the Central VOI (e.g., at 35%, 42.5%, 50%, 57.5%, and 65% depth).
*   **Aggregation:** Each of the 5 slices will be passed through the model independently. The final `MGMT_value` probability will be the **mean** of these 5 predictions. This ensemble approach mitigates the risk of selecting a single non-informative slice (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002) without the texture-destroying artifacts of Maximum Intensity Projection (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
