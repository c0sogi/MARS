Based on the comprehensive analysis of the previous experiments and the derived lessons, I propose the following optimized strategy. This solution introduces a **Pyramid Dual-Axis Attention Network**, a structural evolution that enhances the model's ability to capture both fine-grained texture (honeycombing) and global structural deformation (lung shrinkage) by leveraging multi-scale feature aggregation.

# Model: Pyramid Dual-Axis Attention Network

**Architecture Design**
The model builds upon the successful **Dual-Axis** and **Symmetric Attention** paradigms but addresses the limitation of using only the final convolutional layer, which may compress critical textural information.

1.  **Independent Visual Backbones:**
    *   Two separate **EfficientNet-B0** backbones (initialized with ImageNet weights) process the orthogonal views (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014).
    *   **Branch A (Axial):** Processes Axial Tri-Slab inputs.
    *   **Branch B (Coronal):** Processes Coronal Tri-Slab inputs (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).

2.  **Multi-Scale Pyramid Pooling (Structural Innovation):**
    *   Instead of extracting features only from the final layer (Stride 32), we extract feature maps from **three distinct stages** of the backbone:
        *   **Level 1 (Stride 8):** Captures fine-grained texture details (e.g., reticulation).
        *   **Level 2 (Stride 16):** Captures mid-level patterns.
        *   **Level 3 (Stride 32):** Captures high-level semantic structure and global geometry.
    *   **Global Aggregation:** We apply **Global Average Pooling (GAP)** to each of these feature maps. This respects the lesson to prefer global descriptors over complex spatial maps (Cite solution_lesson_node_00020) while enriching the representation with multi-scale information.
    *   **Projection:** Each of the 6 vectors (3 levels $\times$ 2 views) is projected to a common dimension $D$ (e.g., 256).

3.  **Symmetric Pyramid Attention:**
    *   **Tokenization:** The 6 visual tokens are combined with a projected **Tabular Token** (encoding Age, Sex, Smoking, Percent) to form a sequence of 7 tokens.
    *   **Fusion:** A **Multi-Head Self-Attention** layer (Transformer Encoder) processes this sequence. This allows the model to dynamically weigh the importance of "Axial Texture" vs. "Coronal Shape" vs. "Clinical Metadata" symmetrically (Cite solution_lesson_node_00028).

4.  **Residual Anchor & Parametric Head:**
    *   **Skip Connection:** The output tabular token from the attention layer is concatenated with the **raw tabular embedding** via a skip connection. This ensures the regression head retains direct access to strong clinical priors (Baseline FVC, Percent) (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).
    *   **Prediction:** The head predicts $\alpha$ (Slope), $\sigma_{base}$, and $\sigma_{growth}$ (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).
    *   **Constraint:** We apply **Softplus** activation to the $\sigma$ outputs to strictly enforce positivity via architecture (Cite solution_lesson_node_00021).

# Data: Fixed Overlapping Tri-Slabs

**Preprocessing**
1.  **Dual-View Generation:** For each patient, generate two inputs to capture 3D spatial heterogeneity.
    *   **Axial Tri-Slab (RGB):** Split the CT volume along the Z-axis (Depth) into three slabs with **fixed boundaries** (0-33%, 33-66%, 66-100%) and **15% overlap**. Compute the Maximum Intensity Projection (MIP) of each slab and map to RGB channels (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006). We strictly avoid stochastic jitter to preserve semantic spatial consistency (Cite solution_lesson_node_00035).
    *   **Coronal Tri-Slab (RGB):** Split along the Y-axis (Anterior-Posterior) into three overlapping slabs, compute MIPs, and map to RGB.
2.  **Resolution:** Resize all images to **224x224** (native resolution) to strictly avoid overfitting caused by upscaling artifacts (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).

**Augmentation**
1.  **Spatial Only:** Apply random horizontal flips, shifts, and slight rotations. This is critical for regularizing the small dataset (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
2.  **No Intensity Changes:** Strictly **disable** brightness/contrast augmentations to preserve the physical Hounsfield Unit density signal (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).

# Training: Robust Metric Optimization

**Loss Function**
*   **Metric:** Modified Laplace Log Likelihood.
*   **Robustness:** We explicitly **retain the error clipping** (thresholding absolute error at 1000 ml) within the loss calculation. This acts as a necessary filter for large outliers in the noisy medical data (Cite solution_lesson_node_00013).
*   **No Auxiliary Loss:** We optimize the metric directly to avoid interference with the probabilistic objective (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016).

**Strategy**
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with Cosine Annealing.
*   **Early Stopping:** Use a strict patience (e.g., 6-8 epochs). Training on this small cohort exhausts the generalizable signal rapidly, and we avoid weight averaging (SWA) which was shown to degrade performance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008, solution_lesson_node_00030).

# Evaluation: Parametric Inference

For every `Patient_Week` in the test set:
1.  Generate the Fixed Overlapping Axial and Coronal Tri-Slab images.
2.  Pass inputs and baseline metadata through the **Pyramid Dual-Axis Attention Network**.
3.  Obtain $\alpha, \sigma_{base}, \sigma_{growth}$.
4.  Calculate predictions:
    *   $$ \text{FVC} = \text{Baseline\_FVC} + \alpha \times (\text{Week} - \text{Baseline\_Week}) $$
    *   $$ \text{Confidence} = \sigma_{base} + \sigma_{growth} \times |\text{Week} - \text{Baseline\_Week}| $$
5.  Clip the Confidence at 70 ml as required by the metric.
