Based on the comprehensive analysis of the previous experiments and the derived lessons, I propose the following optimized strategy. This solution introduces a **GeM-Pooled Dual-Axis Transformer**, a structural evolution that refines how visual features are aggregated and how multi-modal context is established.

# Model: GeM-Pooled Dual-Axis Transformer

**Architecture Design**
This architecture addresses the limitation of Global Average Pooling (which dilutes focal fibrosis signals) and rigid concatenation fusion (which lacks context awareness).

1.  **Independent Visual Backbones:**
    *   Two separate **EfficientNet-B0** backbones (initialized with ImageNet weights) process the orthogonal views (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014).
    *   **Branch A (Axial):** Processes the Axial Tri-Slab input.
    *   **Branch B (Coronal):** Processes the Coronal Tri-Slab input.

2.  **Learnable GeM Pooling (Structural Innovation):**
    *   Instead of standard Global Average Pooling (GAP), we implement **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling** at the end of each backbone.
    *   Operation: $f = (\frac{1}{|X|} \sum x^p)^{1/p}$, where $p$ is a learnable parameter (initialized at $p=3$).
    *   *Rationale:* Pulmonary fibrosis manifests as high-density anomalies (scarring). GAP ($p=1$) tends to "wash out" these focal signals against the background of healthy lung, while Max Pooling ($p \to \infty$) is too sensitive to noise. GeM allows the model to learn the optimal sensitivity to high-activation features, providing a superior global descriptor for texture-based pathologies (Cite solution_lesson_node_00020).

3.  **Transformer Fusion with Residual Anchor:**
    *   **Tokenization:** The GeM-pooled visual vectors ($v_{ax}, v_{cor}$) are projected to a dimension $D$. The clinical metadata (Age, Sex, Smoking, Percent) is processed by an MLP to form a tabular token $v_{tab}$.
    *   **Symmetric Attention:** These three tokens form a sequence $[v_{ax}, v_{cor}, v_{tab}]$. We apply a single **Transformer Encoder Layer** (Multi-Head Self-Attention + FeedForward). This allows the visual views to contextualize each other and the clinical data to attend to relevant visual signals symmetrically (Cite solution_lesson_node_00017, solution_lesson_node_00028).
    *   **Residual Anchor:** The output tabular token $v_{tab}'$ is concatenated with the **original raw tabular embedding** $v_{tab}$ via a skip connection. This ensures the regression head has direct access to the strong clinical priors (Baseline FVC, Percent), preventing them from being diluted by the attention mechanism (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).

4.  **Parametric Prediction Head:**
    *   The head predicts three scalars: $\alpha$ (Slope), $\sigma_{base}$ (Base Confidence), and $\sigma_{growth}$ (Uncertainty Growth) (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).
    *   **Constraint:** We apply **Softplus** activation to the $\sigma$ outputs to strictly enforce positivity via architecture (Cite solution_lesson_node_00021).

# Data: Overlapping Orthogonal Tri-Slabs

**Preprocessing**
1.  **Dual-View Generation:** For each patient, generate two inputs to capture 3D spatial heterogeneity (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).
    *   **Axial Tri-Slab (RGB):** Split the CT volume along the Z-axis (Depth) into three slabs with **15% overlap**. Compute the Maximum Intensity Projection (MIP) of each slab and map to RGB channels (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
    *   **Coronal Tri-Slab (RGB):** Split along the Y-axis (Anterior-Posterior) into three overlapping slabs, compute MIPs, and map to RGB.
2.  **Resolution:** Resize all images to **224x224** (native resolution) to strictly avoid overfitting caused by upscaling artifacts (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).

**Augmentation**
1.  **Spatial Only:** Apply random horizontal flips, shifts, and slight rotations. This is critical for regularizing the small dataset (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
2.  **No Intensity Changes:** Strictly **disable** brightness/contrast augmentations to preserve the physical Hounsfield Unit density signal (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).

# Training: Robust Metric Optimization

**Loss Function**
*   **Metric:** Modified Laplace Log Likelihood.
*   **Robustness:** We explicitly **retain the error clipping** (thresholding absolute error at 1000 ml) within the loss calculation. This acts as a necessary filter for outliers in the noisy medical data (Cite solution_lesson_node_00013).
*   **No Auxiliary Loss:** We avoid auxiliary targets to prevent interference with the probabilistic objective (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016).

**Strategy**
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with Cosine Annealing.
*   **Early Stopping:** Use a strict patience (e.g., 5-8 epochs). Training on this small cohort exhausts the generalizable signal rapidly, and we avoid weight averaging (SWA) which was shown to degrade performance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008, solution_lesson_node_00030).

# Evaluation: Parametric Inference

For every `Patient_Week` in the test set:
1.  Generate the Axial and Coronal Tri-Slab images.
2.  Pass inputs and baseline metadata through the **GeM-Pooled Transformer**.
3.  Obtain $\alpha, \sigma_{base}, \sigma_{growth}$.
4.  Calculate predictions (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004):
    *   $$ \text{FVC} = \text{Baseline\_FVC} + \alpha \times (\text{Week} - \text{Baseline\_Week}) $$
    *   $$ \text{Confidence} = \sigma_{base} + \sigma_{growth} \times |\text{Week} - \text{Baseline\_Week}| $$
5.  Clip the Confidence at 70 ml as required by the metric.
