Based on the empirical findings and lessons learned, I propose the following optimized strategy. This solution transitions the modeling paradigm from **Scalar Regression** to **Ordinal Classification** to better align with the discrete, ordered nature of the essay scores and address the "regression to the mean" phenomenon observed in earlier iterations.

# Model
**Architecture**: We will utilize the **DeBERTa-v3-small** transformer as the backbone.
- **Rationale for Backbone**: We retain the Transformer architecture over the DAN to capture the "structural context (syntax, coherence, argumentation flow)" required to distinguish high-quality essays, as identified in **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00001}** and **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00007}**. We specifically select the "Small" variant over "Base" because empirical evidence showed it captures >99% of the signal while avoiding the rapid overfitting and doubled runtime associated with larger models **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00009}**.
- **Structural Innovation (Ordinal Head)**: Instead of the single-neuron linear regression head used in previous ideas, we will implement an **Ordinal Classification Head**. This head will consist of a dense layer with **5 output neurons** (for a 6-point scale, there are 5 thresholds: $>1, >2, >3, >4, >5$).
- **Activation**: Each output neuron will pass through a **Sigmoid** activation function, representing the probability $P(Score > k)$.

# Data
**Preprocessing**:
- **Tokenization**: Use the DeBERTa-v3-specific tokenizer.
- **Sequence Length**: We will maintain the maximum sequence length of **1024 tokens** to ensure the model has access to the full "argumentation flow" without aggressive truncation, addressing the structural blindness of the DAN baseline **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00001}**.
- **Target Transformation**: The scalar scores in the training set will be converted into **ordinal binary vectors**. For example, a score of 3 becomes `[1, 1, 0, 0, 0]` (meaning: greater than 1? Yes. Greater than 2? Yes. Greater than 3? No...).

# Training
**Procedure**:
- **Loss Function**: We will replace Mean Squared Error (MSE) with **Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) Loss** summed over the 5 output logits.
    - **Rationale**: MSE forces the model to minimize the average distance, which contributes to the "regression to the mean" issue where predictions cluster around the majority class (3-4) **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00001}**. Ordinal classification allows the model to learn distinct decision boundaries for each score threshold, effectively distinguishing "outliers" (scores 1 and 6) by independently assessing the probability of crossing each threshold.
- **Optimization**: Use **AdamW** with a linear warmup and cosine decay schedule.
- **Regularization**: To further mitigate the noise and variance identified in **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00010}**, we will employ **Layer-wise Learning Rate Decay (LLRD)**, assigning lower learning rates to the pre-trained backbone and higher rates to the new ordinal head.

# Evaluation
**Inference Strategy**:
- **Prediction**: For each test essay, the model outputs 5 probabilities $p_1, ..., p_5$.
- **Score Calculation**: The final predicted score is calculated as $1 + \sum_{k=1}^{5} p_k$.
    - **Rationale**: This method provides a smooth, continuous score derived from the model's confidence at each threshold, which can then be rounded to the nearest integer. This bridges the gap between the probabilistic nature of the model and the integer constraints of the competition format.
