Based on the comprehensive analysis of the previous experiments and the specific lessons learned, I propose the following optimized strategy. This solution focuses on maximizing model capacity and refining the signal aggregation mechanism while adhering to the loss function that best aligns with the evaluation metric.

# Model
**Architecture**:
- **Backbone**: I propose upgrading the backbone to **DeBERTa-v3-Large**.
    - **Rationale**: Previous experiments demonstrated that model capacity is the primary driver of performance in this semantic scoring task. While the "Base" model outperformed "Small" (**Cite {solution_lesson_node_00009}**), the comparison between "Small + Ordinal" and "Base + MSE" revealed that sophisticated objectives cannot compensate for a lack of representational power (**Cite {solution_lesson_node_00017}**). Given the available A100 GPU and 24-hour runtime, the "Large" variant is computationally feasible and offers the necessary reasoning capacity to capture complex "argumentation flow" and "coherence" that smaller models miss (**Cite {solution_lesson_node_00001}**).
- **Pooling Mechanism**: I will replace the standard Mean Pooling with **Attention Pooling** (also known as Weighted Pooling).
    - **Rationale**: While Contextual Mean Pooling was an improvement over static embeddings (**Cite {solution_lesson_node_00007}**), it still treats all tokens in the sequence with equal importance. Attention Pooling introduces a trainable layer that assigns weights to each token embedding before aggregation. This allows the model to dynamically emphasize critical rhetorical elements (e.g., thesis statements, transitions) and downweight filler text, effectively refining the "structural context" signal identified as a weakness in averaging approaches (**Cite {solution_lesson_node_00001}**).
- **Regression Head**: The weighted sentence embedding will be fed into a linear layer to output a scalar score.

# Data
**Preprocessing**:
- **Tokenization**: Use the **DeBERTa-v3-Large** tokenizer (SentencePiece based).
- **Sequence Length**: Maintain the maximum sequence length of **1024 tokens**.
    - **Rationale**: This length was effective in previous iterations for covering the majority of essay content without aggressive truncation, ensuring the model can view the complete narrative arc required for holistic scoring (**Cite {solution_lesson_node_00001}**).
- **Input Formatting**: No special target transformation (like ordinal vectors) is required; targets remain integer scalars.

# Training
**Procedure**:
- **Loss Function**: Utilize **Mean Squared Error (MSE)**.
    - **Rationale**: Experiments confirmed that MSE aligns mathematically with the Quadratic Weighted Kappa (QWK) metric, whereas Ordinal Classification (BCE) imposes a linear penalty that under-penalizes large errors (**Cite {solution_lesson_node_00012}**). We retain MSE to ensure the optimization objective mirrors the evaluation criteria.
- **Optimization Strategy**: Implement **Layer-wise Learning Rate Decay (LLRD)**.
    - **Rationale**: Larger models like DeBERTa-Large are prone to overfitting and "forgetting" pre-trained knowledge if updated aggressively (**Cite {solution_lesson_node_00009}**). LLRD assigns progressively lower learning rates to the earlier layers of the backbone and higher rates to the head. Although this technique was present in a failed previous idea, the failure was attributed to the insufficient capacity of the "Small" backbone used at the time (**Cite {solution_lesson_node_00017}**). Applied to the "Large" model, LLRD will stabilize training and improve generalization.
- **Optimizer**: AdamW with a linear warmup and cosine decay schedule.

# Evaluation
**Inference Strategy**:
- **Prediction**: The model will output a continuous scalar value for each test essay.
- **Post-Processing**: Predictions will be clipped to the range [1, 6] and rounded to the nearest integer. This simple heuristic effectively bridges the gap between the regression output and the discrete scoring format (**Cite {solution_lesson_node_00001}**).
