Based on the progression of experiments and the specific lessons learned, I propose a **Heterogeneous Cross-Validation Ensemble** strategy. This approach moves beyond the single-architecture paradigm to leverage the diversity of inductive biases between different model families, maximizing generalization on the test set.

# Model
I propose a **Heterogeneous Ensemble** consisting of two distinct backbone architectures trained independently:
1.  **EfficientNetV2-M:** Retained as the primary component due to its proven high performance and efficiency in this domain (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).
2.  **ConvNeXt-Base:** Introduced as the structural innovation. Unlike EfficientNet's MBConv-based design, ConvNeXt modernizes standard ResNet blocks to mimic Vision Transformers (isotropic design, larger kernels), providing a distinct feature extraction perspective.

By combining predictions from these two architecturally diverse models, we aim to reduce the error variance more effectively than a homogeneous ensemble of a single model type. Both models will utilize a simple classification head (Global Average Pooling + Linear Layer) initialized with ImageNet pre-trained weights.

# Data
*   **Resolution:** Input images will be resized to **384x384**. This resolution strikes the optimal balance between capturing fine details (fur, whiskers) and computational efficiency, avoiding the diminishing returns and increased cost observed at higher resolutions (Cite solution_lesson_node_00013).
*   **Augmentation:** We will employ the **Heavy Augmentation** strategy (RandomResizedCrop, ShiftScaleRotate, ColorJitter) to prevent overfitting given the high capacity of the "Medium" and "Base" models (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
*   **Constraints:** We will explicitly **exclude Mixup and CutMix**. As established, the domain overlap between ImageNet and the target is high, and these regularization techniques unnecessarily complicate the learning signal, hindering convergence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).

# Training
*   **Strategy:** We will implement a **5-Fold Cross-Validation** scheme for *both* architectures. This results in a total of 10 trained model instances (5 EfficientNetV2-M + 5 ConvNeXt-Base), ensuring 100% of the training data is utilized for feature learning (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).
*   **Optimization:**
    *   **Optimizer:** AdamW with a standard learning rate (e.g., 1e-4 to 1e-3).
    *   **Scheduler:** Cosine Annealing to ensure smooth convergence to a robust minima.
    *   **Batch Size:** We will strictly maintain a batch size of **16 or higher** (ideally 32) to preserve the stability of Batch Normalization statistics. We will not scale up the model size further (e.g., to "Large" variants) if it forces the batch size below this threshold (Cite solution_lesson_node_00012).
    *   **Duration:** Training will be limited to a short duration (3-5 epochs per fold) with early stopping, leveraging the rapid transfer learning capabilities of pre-trained weights in this high-overlap domain (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
    *   **Precision:** Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP) will be used to maximize throughput and fit the models within the A100 GPU memory.

# Evaluation
*   **Test Time Augmentation (TTA):** During inference, we will generate predictions for both the original test image and a horizontally flipped version, averaging the results to further improve robustness (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).
*   **Ensemble Aggregation:** The final submission probability for each image will be the arithmetic mean of the probabilities output by all 10 models (5 folds × 2 architectures). This "Bagging" approach across different architectures is expected to yield the lowest Log Loss by smoothing out architecture-specific errors.
