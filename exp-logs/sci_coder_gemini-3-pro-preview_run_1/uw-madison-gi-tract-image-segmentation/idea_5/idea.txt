Based on the comprehensive analysis of the previous attempts and the derived lessons, I propose the following optimized strategy. This solution retains the structural potential of **U-Net++** (from Idea 4) but corrects its implementation flaws and introduces a novel input representation to solve the Z-axis consistency problem without incurring the artifacts of volumetric stacking.

# Model
**Architecture:** **2D U-Net++ (Nested U-Net) with Weighted Deep Supervision** utilizing a **ResNet-34** backbone.
*   **Structural Innovation (Input-Level Feature Fusion):** To address the lack of Z-axis consistency identified in **Cite solution_lesson_node_00013** without triggering the "ghosting" artifacts of naive 2.5D stacking (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00010**), I will modify the model to accept a **4-Channel Input**.
    *   Channels 1-3: The 2D MRI slice (replicated to RGB).
    *   Channel 4: A **Relative Slice Depth Map**. This channel will consist of a constant value map representing the normalized position of the slice within the scan (i.e., $z / z_{total}$). This provides the model with explicit global spatial context (e.g., "stomach is usually at the top"), allowing it to learn anatomical priors that stabilize predictions across the Z-axis.
    *   The first convolution layer of the ResNet-34 backbone will be modified to accept 4 input channels.
*   **Backbone (Exploitation):** I will utilize **ResNet-34**. As established in **Cite solution_lesson_node_00012**, ResNet backbones offer superior convergence speed and stability compared to EfficientNet for this dataset, which is critical for tuning the more complex U-Net++ decoder within the time limit.
*   **Corrected Deep Supervision (Exploitation):** I will implement **Weighted Deep Supervision** to fix the failure of Idea 4. As learned in **Cite solution_lesson_node_00015**, I will assign decaying weights to the nested decoder outputs (e.g., 1.0 for the final output $X^{0,4}$, and 0.5, 0.1, 0.1 for auxiliary outputs). This ensures the auxiliary heads facilitate gradient flow without distracting the encoder from learning deep semantic features.

# Data
**Preprocessing:**
*   **Normalization (Exploitation):** I will strictly apply **Robust Percentile Normalization** (clipping to [1st, 99th] percentiles before scaling) instead of Min-Max scaling. This is essential to handle the high dynamic range and outliers in MRI data, as proven in **Cite solution_lesson_node_00011**.
*   **Resolution:** Images will be resized to **320x320** pixels. This balances the spatial detail required for segmentation with the memory overhead of the U-Net++ architecture.
*   **Input Construction:** For each slice, the input tensor will be constructed as `[Slice, Slice, Slice, Depth_Map]`, avoiding the harmful misalignment of neighbor-slice stacking.

# Training
*   **Loss Function:** A combined **BCE + Dice Loss** will be computed as a weighted sum across the four Deep Supervision heads.
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW optimizer with a Cosine Annealing learning rate scheduler.
*   **Strategy:** The model will be trained on the `metadata/train.csv` split and validated on `metadata/val.csv` to ensure generalization to unseen cases.

# Evaluation
*   **3D Reconstruction Pipeline (Exploitation):** Validation will strictly follow the "reconstruct-then-score" methodology. 2D predictions will be stacked into full 3D volumes before calculating metrics, as **Cite solution_lesson_node_00006** demonstrated that averaging slice-level metrics is mathematically incorrect for Hausdorff distance.
*   **Post-Processing (Exploitation):** I will apply **3D Connected Component Analysis (CCA)** to the reconstructed volumes. For each class, only the largest connected 3D object will be retained. This is the primary defense against the "shot noise" that destabilizes the Hausdorff metric, as mandated by **Cite solution_lesson_node_00007**.
