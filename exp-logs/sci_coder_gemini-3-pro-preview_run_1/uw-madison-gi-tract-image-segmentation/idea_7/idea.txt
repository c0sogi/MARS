Based on the comprehensive analysis of the previous experiments and the derived lessons, I propose the following optimized strategy. This solution introduces a **Recurrent U-Net (R-UNet)** architecture. This structural innovation replaces the "naive" channel-stacking approach with a temporal sequence model to capture Z-axis coherence without introducing ghosting artifacts, while strictly adhering to the robust preprocessing and post-processing protocols established in the lessons.

# Model
**Architecture:** **Recurrent U-Net** utilizing a **ResNet-34** backbone with a **Bi-Directional ConvLSTM** bottleneck.
*   **Structural Innovation (Sequence Modeling):** I will transition from a static "2.5D" channel-stacking approach to a **Sequence-based** approach. As identified in **Cite solution_lesson_node_00013**, the primary bottleneck of the current best solution is Z-axis inconsistency. Furthermore, **Cite solution_lesson_node_00010** warned that naive channel stacking on anisotropic data introduces ghosting artifacts. To resolve both, the model will process a **sequence of adjacent slices** (e.g., 5 slices: $z-2, \dots, z+2$).
    *   **Encoder:** A **Time-Distributed ResNet-34** will extract features from each slice in the sequence independently. I select ResNet-34 over EfficientNet because **Cite solution_lesson_node_00012** demonstrated that ResNet offers superior convergence speed and stability, which is critical to offset the computational overhead of the recurrent module.
    *   **Bottleneck:** The sequence of feature maps from the encoder will be passed through a **Bi-Directional ConvLSTM** (Convolutional Long Short-Term Memory) layer. This layer explicitly models the spatial-temporal (Z-axis) dependencies, allowing the segmentation of the central slice to be informed by the anatomical context of its neighbors without the misalignment artifacts of channel stacking.
    *   **Decoder:** A standard U-Net decoder will process the context-enriched feature maps to generate the segmentation mask. I will avoid complex nested decoders (like U-Net++) to maintain training stability (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00025**).

# Data
**Preprocessing:**
*   **Input Format:** The input will be a sequence of **Single-Slice Images** (replicated to 3 channels for the backbone). This avoids the "ghosting" artifacts caused by stacking misaligned anisotropic slices as channels (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00010**).
*   **Normalization (Exploitation):** I will strictly apply **Robust Percentile Normalization** (clipping to [1st, 99th] percentiles before scaling) to handle the high dynamic range and outliers, as mandated by **Cite solution_lesson_node_00011**.
*   **Interpolation (Exploitation):** All image resizing will use **Bilinear Interpolation** to preserve texture gradients, preventing the aliasing artifacts caused by Nearest Neighbor interpolation (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00023**).
*   **Sampling:** Data will be sampled as **consecutive sequences** of slices (e.g., length 5) from the (Case, Day) volumes, rather than random independent slices.

# Training
*   **Loss Function:** A combined **BCE + Dice Loss** will be applied to the output of the central slice (or all slices in the sequence, depending on memory).
*   **Optimizer:** **AdamW** with a Cosine Annealing learning rate scheduler.
*   **Strategy:** The model will be trained on the `metadata/train.csv` split and validated on `metadata/val.csv`. The use of 2.5D sequence modeling avoids the pitfalls of full 3D training (scarcity and batch size limits) identified in **Cite solution_lesson_node_00026**, while providing superior context to 2D models.

# Evaluation
*   **Inference Strategy:** We will perform inference using a sliding window along the Z-axis, feeding sequences of slices into the model to predict the mask for the central slice.
*   **3D Reconstruction (Exploitation):** Validation will strictly follow the "reconstruct-then-score" methodology. Metrics will be calculated on the fully reconstructed 3D volume for each case, as **Cite solution_lesson_node_00006** established that averaging slice-level metrics is mathematically incorrect for Hausdorff distance.
*   **Post-Processing (Exploitation):** We will apply **3D Connected Component Analysis (CCA)** to the reconstructed volumes. For each class, only the largest connected 3D component will be retained. This is the critical defense against "shot noise" outliers that destabilize the Hausdorff metric (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00007**).
