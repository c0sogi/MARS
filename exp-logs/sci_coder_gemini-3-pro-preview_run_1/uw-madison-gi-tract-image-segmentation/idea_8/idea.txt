Based on the comprehensive analysis of the previous experiments and the derived lessons, I propose the following optimized strategy. This solution implements a **High-Fidelity Recurrent U-Net with Deep Supervision**, specifically designed to overcome the resolution bottleneck identified in the most recent experiments while maintaining the Z-axis consistency that proved superior to 2.5D stacking.

# Model
**Architecture:** **Recurrent U-Net (R-UNet) with Weighted Deep Supervision** utilizing a **ResNet-34** backbone.

*   **Structural Innovation (Recurrent Sequence Modeling):** I will maintain the **Bi-Directional ConvLSTM** bottleneck introduced in Idea 7/8. This component processes a sequence of slices (e.g., $T=5$) to capture Z-axis spatial dependencies. This structure is retained because it solves the "ghosting" artifacts of naive 2.5D stacking (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00010**) and the Z-axis inconsistency of independent slice predictions (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00013**).
*   **Backbone (Exploitation):** I will utilize a **Time-Distributed ResNet-34** encoder. Although Idea 8 moved to ResNet-18 for speed, **Cite solution_lesson_node_00038** revealed that the performance plateau was due to preprocessing constraints (image width), not model capacity. Now that I am addressing the resolution bottleneck (see Data section), I will revert to the higher-capacity **ResNet-34** to maximize feature extraction power, as it offers superior convergence properties compared to EfficientNet (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00012**).
*   **Decoder with Weighted Deep Supervision (Synthesis):** I will integrate **Weighted Deep Supervision** into the U-Net decoder. While complex decoders like U-Net++ caused instability (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00025**), Deep Supervision itself is valuable if implemented correctly. I will attach auxiliary segmentation heads to the intermediate decoder blocks and aggregate their losses with **decaying weights** (e.g., 1.0 for final, 0.5 for aux1, 0.25 for aux2), strictly adhering to the correction in **Cite solution_lesson_node_00015**. This ensures robust gradient flow through the recurrent layers without distracting the encoder.

# Data
**Preprocessing:**
*   **Aspect-Ratio Preserving Center Padding (Exploitation):** I will strictly implement **Center Padding** to resize images. As identified in **Cite solution_lesson_node_00035** and **Cite solution_lesson_node_00037**, naive resizing of rectangular images (360x310) to squares distorts anatomy and correlates error with image width. I will scale images to fit the target dimension and pad the remaining space equally on both sides, preserving the physical geometry of the organs.
*   **High-Resolution Input (Correction):** To address the bottleneck identified in **Cite solution_lesson_node_00038** (where error correlated with image width due to downsampling), I will increase the input resolution to **320x320**. This resolution balances the need for fine anatomical detail with the memory constraints of the Recurrent architecture.
*   **Normalization (Exploitation):** I will apply **Robust Percentile Normalization** (clipping to [1st, 99th] percentiles) to handle MRI intensity outliers, as mandated by **Cite solution_lesson_node_00011**.
*   **Interpolation (Exploitation):** All image resizing will use **Bilinear Interpolation** to prevent aliasing artifacts, as required by **Cite solution_lesson_node_00023**.

# Training
*   **Loss Function:** A combined **BCE + Dice Loss** will be computed. The total loss will be a weighted sum of the main output and the auxiliary Deep Supervision outputs, using the decaying weight schedule (1.0, 0.5, 0.25) to prevent the "naive averaging" failure (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00015**).
*   **Optimizer:** **AdamW** with a Cosine Annealing learning rate scheduler.
*   **Strategy:** Training will use the case-aware split (`metadata/train.csv` vs `metadata/val.csv`). The model will be trained on sequences of 5 consecutive slices.

# Evaluation
*   **Inference Strategy:** Sliding window inference will be performed along the Z-axis, feeding sequences of 5 slices to predict the mask for the central slice.
*   **3D Reconstruction (Exploitation):** Validation will strictly follow the "reconstruct-then-score" methodology. Metrics will be calculated on the fully reconstructed 3D volume for each case, as **Cite solution_lesson_node_00006** established that slice-averaged metrics are invalid for Hausdorff distance.
*   **Post-Processing (Exploitation):** I will apply **3D Connected Component Analysis (CCA)** to the reconstructed volumes. For each class, only the largest connected 3D component will be retained. This is the critical defense against "shot noise" outliers that destabilize the Hausdorff metric (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00007**).
