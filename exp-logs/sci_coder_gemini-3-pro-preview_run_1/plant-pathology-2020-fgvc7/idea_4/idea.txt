Based on the lessons learned, I propose a **Heterogeneous K-Fold Ensemble** strategy. This approach structurally evolves the solution by moving from a single-architecture pipeline to a multi-architecture voting system. It combines the proven reliability of the ResNet backbone with the parameter efficiency of a DenseNet backbone, specifically targeting the high variance and small sample size issues identified in previous iterations.

# Model
I will implement a **Heterogeneous Ensemble** consisting of two distinct CNN architectures.
1.  **ResNet34:** I will retain this backbone as it has demonstrated the best balance of capacity and convergence speed for this specific dataset (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
2.  **DenseNet121:** I will introduce this architecture as the structural innovation. Unlike ResNet's summation of residuals, DenseNet uses concatenation of features from all preceding layers. This "feature reuse" mechanism is particularly effective for small datasets (like this one with ~1,300 images) as it maximizes the utility of learned parameters and provides a distinct inductive bias complementary to ResNet.
*   **Heads:** Both backbones will use a standard Global Average Pooling followed by a fully connected layer with 4 outputs.

# Data
*   **Resolution:** All images will be strictly processed at **256x256** pixels during both training and inference. This enforces pipeline consistency to avoid the receptive field mismatches that previously degraded performance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
*   **Splitting:** I will use **Stratified 5-Fold Cross-Validation**. This ensures every image is used for validation and prevents the selection bias observed with single splits (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
*   **Augmentation:**
    *   I will apply the successful `albumentations` pipeline (ShiftScaleRotate, RandomBrightnessContrast) to increase data diversity (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
    *   I will **explicitly exclude Mixup** regularization. Given the limited training budget, avoiding "convergence tax" techniques is necessary to ensure the models fully converge (Cite solution_lesson_node_00011).

# Training
*   **Loss Function:** I will use **Weighted Cross-Entropy Loss** with weights inversely proportional to class frequencies. This is essential to maximize the Macro-Averaged AUC by prioritizing the minority `multiple_diseases` class (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
*   **Optimizer:** The **Adam** optimizer will be used for both architectures.
*   **Scheduler:** I will implement **Cosine Annealing Warm Restarts**. This dynamic learning rate schedule is a critical component for achieving optimal convergence in short training regimes, significantly outperforming constant learning rates (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008).
*   **Procedure:** Both the ResNet34 and DenseNet121 models will be trained independently on the same 5 folds (resulting in 10 total training runs).

# Evaluation
*   **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):** Inference will be performed on the original image and a horizontally flipped version, with predictions averaged to reduce variance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
*   **Ensemble Aggregation:** The final submission will be the average of the probability outputs from all **10 models** (5 ResNet34 folds + 5 DenseNet121 folds). This combines the statistical robustness of K-Fold CV with the representational diversity of the heterogeneous architectures.
