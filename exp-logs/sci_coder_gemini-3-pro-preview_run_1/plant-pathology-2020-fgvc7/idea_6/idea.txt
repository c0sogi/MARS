Based on the comprehensive analysis of previous attempts and the specific lessons learned, I propose the following optimized strategy. This solution introduces a structural evolution via **Internal Ensembling (Multi-Sample Dropout)** and **Learnable Pooling**, while strategically leveraging the extended runtime budget to enable advanced regularization techniques that previously failed due to time constraints.

# Model
I will implement a **ResNet34 with Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling and Multi-Sample Dropout**.
*   **Backbone:** I will retain the **ResNet34** architecture initialized with ImageNet weights. This backbone has consistently proven to be the most robust and efficient for this specific dataset, outperforming heavier architectures like ResNeXt and EfficientNet (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007, solution_lesson_node_00018).
*   **Pooling (Structural Innovation):** Instead of the standard Global Average Pooling (GAP), I will implement **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling**. GeM introduces a learnable parameter $p$ that allows the pooling layer to transition between Average Pooling ($p=1$) and Max Pooling ($p \to \infty$). This allows the model to better focus on dominant features (disease spots) versus background, which is critical for fine-grained classification.
*   **Head (Structural Innovation):** I will replace the single classification head with a **Multi-Sample Dropout** head. This consists of 5 parallel fully connected layers (sharing the same input features) but with different dropout masks. During training, the loss is calculated for each head and summed; during inference, the predictions are averaged. This creates an "internal ensemble" within a single model, providing the variance-reduction benefits of ensembling (Cite solution_lesson_node_00017) without the computational cost of training multiple backbones.

# Data
*   **Preprocessing:** Images will be resized to **256x256** pixels. This resolution is strictly enforced for both training and inference to ensure pipeline consistency (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
*   **Augmentation:** I will employ a strong `albumentations` pipeline including **HorizontalFlip**, **VerticalFlip**, **ShiftScaleRotate**, and **RandomBrightnessContrast**. This preserves the high augmentation intensity required to prevent overfitting on the small dataset (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003, solution_lesson_node_00016).
*   **Regularization (Strategic Evolution):** I will introduce **Mixup** and **CutMix** augmentations. While these techniques previously caused underfitting (Cite solution_lesson_node_00011), they are now viable because I will significantly extend the training duration (see Training section), allowing the model sufficient time to converge on the more complex soft-target loss landscape.

# Training
*   **Splitting:** I will use **Stratified 5-Fold Cross-Validation** to ensure every image is used for validation and to mitigate selection bias (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
*   **Loss Function:** I will use **Cross-Entropy Loss** adapted for soft targets (from Mixup/CutMix). Crucially, I will incorporate **Class Frequency Weighting** into the loss calculation to ensure the model prioritizes the minority `multiple_diseases` class, maximizing the Macro-Averaged AUC metric (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
*   **Optimizer & Scheduler:** I will use the **AdamW** optimizer with a **Cosine Annealing Warm Restarts** scheduler.
*   **Duration (Correction):** I will increase the training budget to **50 epochs** per fold. This directly addresses the "convergence tax" of Mixup (Cite solution_lesson_node_00011) and ensures the scheduler cycle (`T_0=50`) is fully synchronized with the training budget (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015). This is feasible within the 24-hour limit (estimated runtime ~2.5 hours).

# Evaluation
*   **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):** Predictions will be generated on the original image, a horizontally flipped version, and a vertically flipped version. These will be averaged to further reduce variance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
*   **Ensemble Aggregation:** The final submission will be the average of the probability outputs from all **5 folds**. By using the Multi-Sample Dropout head, each fold's model is already an internal ensemble, making the final 5-fold average highly robust.
