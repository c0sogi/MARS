The issue is caused by a mismatch between the model's initialization strategy and the loss function used for verification. The model is initialized to output probabilities matching the natural class frequencies (imbalanced priors), but the verification uses a weighted `CrossEntropyLoss` that heavily penalizes the minority classes. When the model correctly predicts a low probability for a minority class, the weighted loss inflates this value, causing the total loss to exceed the safety threshold derived for a balanced/uniform scenario.

To fix this, I will update `runfile.py` and `library/workflow.py` to use an unweighted `nn.CrossEntropyLoss` specifically during the `verify_initialization` step. This ensures the initial loss is evaluated against the natural distribution the model was initialized for, preventing the `RuntimeError`.
