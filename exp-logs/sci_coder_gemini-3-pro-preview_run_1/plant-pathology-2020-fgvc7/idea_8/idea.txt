Based on the comprehensive analysis of the provided lessons, I propose a solution centered on **Full-Dataset Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA)**. This strategy introduces a structural innovation in the *optimization and model aggregation* phase, moving away from the "Train-Validate-Select" paradigm that fragmented the limited data, towards a "Full-Fit-Average" approach that maximizes signal utilization.

# Model
I will utilize the **ResNet34** architecture, wrapped in a **Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA)** framework.
*   **Backbone:** ResNet34 is selected as the core architecture. It has consistently demonstrated the optimal balance of capacity and convergence speed for this specific dataset, outperforming both shallower (ResNet18) and deeper/complex (ResNeXt, EfficientNet) alternatives (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003, solution_lesson_node_00007, solution_lesson_node_00018).
*   **Head:** The classification head will remain a simple Global Average Pooling layer followed by a single fully connected layer. I will explicitly avoid complex heads like Multi-Sample Dropout or GeM Pooling, which were shown to cause underfitting due to over-regularization on this small dataset (Cite solution_lesson_node_00023).
*   **Structural Innovation (SWA):** Instead of selecting a single "best" model based on validation metrics, I will implement SWA. This technique averages the weights of the model at multiple points along its optimization trajectory (after the initial convergence). This creates a final model centered in the loss basin, providing the generalization benefits of an ensemble without the computational cost of training multiple backbones or the data fragmentation of K-Fold splitting.

# Data
*   **Dataset Usage (Strategic Evolution):** I will train on **100% of the available training data**. Previous experiments showed that splitting the small dataset (~1,300 images) into folds reduced the effective training set size for each model, leading to performance degradation compared to a single model trained on the full set (Cite solution_lesson_node_00025). By utilizing the full dataset, we maximize the discriminative signal available to the SWA model.
*   **Resolution:** Images will be processed strictly at **256x256** pixels. This resolution avoids the batch size instability and overfitting observed with 512x512 fine-tuning (Cite solution_lesson_node_00024) and ensures pipeline consistency between training and inference (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
*   **Augmentation:** I will employ a robust `albumentations` pipeline including **HorizontalFlip**, **ShiftScaleRotate**, and **RandomBrightnessContrast**. This high-intensity augmentation is critical to support the capacity of ResNet34 (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003) and must not be weakened (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016). I will exclude Mixup to avoid the "convergence tax" that previously led to underfitting (Cite solution_lesson_node_00011).

# Training
*   **Loss Function:** I will use **Weighted Cross-Entropy Loss** with weights inversely proportional to class frequencies. This is essential to maximize the Macro-Averaged AUC by prioritizing the minority `multiple_diseases` class (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
*   **Optimizer:** The **Adam** optimizer will be used.
*   **Procedure (SWA Schedule):**
    1.  **Burn-in Phase:** Train for **10 epochs** with a constant learning rate of `1e-4`. This constant rate is chosen to ensure robust convergence to the optimal region without the premature "freezing" caused by aggressive decay schedules (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).
    2.  **SWA Phase:** Continue training for an additional **5 epochs**. At the end of each epoch, the model's weights will be captured.
    3.  **Aggregation:** The captured weights will be averaged to form the final model parameters.
    4.  **BN Update:** A final forward pass over the training data will be performed to update the Batch Normalization statistics using the averaged weights.

# Evaluation
*   **Inference:** Predictions will be generated using the single SWA-averaged model.
*   **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):** For each test image, predictions will be generated on the original and a horizontally flipped version, then averaged. This leverages the model's geometric invariance to reduce variance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
