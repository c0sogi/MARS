Based on the lessons learned from the previous experiments, I propose a **Hierarchical Multi-Task Learning Strategy** powered by a **ConvNeXt** backbone. This approach addresses the high-cardinality fine-grained classification problem by leveraging the taxonomic structure (Family $\rightarrow$ Genus $\rightarrow$ Species) explicitly during training, while upgrading the feature extractor to a modern architecture capable of resolving subtle inter-class differences.

### Model
**Architecture: Hierarchical ConvNeXt-Tiny**
*   **Backbone:** Replace the ResNet50 with a **ConvNeXt-Tiny** architecture initialized with ImageNet-1k weights. This provides a structural innovation over the ResNet family, offering a better accuracy-compute trade-off and higher modeling capacity to handle the 15,501 classes (Cite Lesson 00006).
*   **Multi-Head Design:** Instead of a single classification head, the model will feature three parallel linear projection heads attached to the final feature embedding:
    1.  **Family Head:** Predicts the plant family (272 classes).
    2.  **Genus Head:** Predicts the plant genus (2,564 classes).
    3.  **Species Head:** Predicts the final plant species (15,501 classes).
*   **Rationale:** By forcing the shared backbone to predict coarse-grained labels (Family/Genus) simultaneously with fine-grained ones (Species), we induce a structured embedding space where visually similar species are grouped by their taxonomic lineage. This acts as a strong regularizer and auxiliary supervision signal.

### Data
**Preprocessing & Augmentation**
*   **Resolution:** All images will be resized to **256x256** pixels. Higher resolution is critical for resolving fine-grained details in a high-cardinality setting (Cite Lesson 00010).
*   **Augmentation:** We will employ **conservative augmentation** (RandomResizedCrop with scale 0.8, HorizontalFlip). We will avoid aggressive cropping (scale < 0.8) or heavy distortions, as these have been shown to hinder convergence in short training schedules (Cite Lesson 00005).
*   **Label Generation:** During data loading, we will map each image's `category_id` to its corresponding `family` and `genus` IDs using the training metadata, providing the targets for the auxiliary heads.

### Training
**Strategy: Multi-Task Optimization with Regularization**
*   **Loss Function:** The total loss will be a weighted sum of the three heads:
    $$L_{total} = L_{species} + \lambda_1 L_{genus} + \lambda_2 L_{family}$$
    *   **Species Loss:** We will use **CrossEntropyLoss with Label Smoothing (0.1)**. This prevents the model from becoming overconfident in the 15,501-class space and improves generalization (Cite Lesson 00010). Additionally, we will apply **Class Weighting** (inverse frequency) to this head to address the long-tail imbalance (Cite Lesson 00001).
    *   **Auxiliary Losses:** Standard CrossEntropyLoss will be used for Family and Genus heads to guide the feature learning.
*   **Optimization:**
    *   **Differential Learning Rates:** We will apply a lower learning rate (e.g., $10^{-4}$) to the ConvNeXt backbone and a higher learning rate (e.g., $10^{-3}$) to the three classification heads. This preserves the pre-trained features while allowing the heads to adapt quickly (Cite Lesson 00003).
    *   **Scheduler:** We will use a **Cosine Annealing** scheduler to smoothly decay the learning rate, which has proven effective for convergence (Cite Lesson 00006).
    *   **Loop Structure:** The `scheduler.step()` call will be placed strictly at the end of each epoch (after validation) to ensure robust state management (Cite Lesson 00008).

### Evaluation
**Inference**
*   **Prediction:** For the test set, we will perform a forward pass and extract predictions **only from the Species Head**. The Family and Genus heads are discarded during inference as they serve only as training guides.
*   **Metric:** We will continue to monitor the Macro F1 score on the validation set. We acknowledge that small fluctuations (<2%) may be noise (Cite Lesson 00009), so we will prioritize the trend over multiple epochs.
