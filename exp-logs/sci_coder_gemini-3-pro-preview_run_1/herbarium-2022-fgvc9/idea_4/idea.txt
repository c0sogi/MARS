Based on the progression of experiments and the lessons learned, I propose the following **Hierarchical ConvNeXt-Base Strategy with Stochastic Weight Averaging**. This solution evolves the architecture from ResNet to a modern ConvNeXt backbone to address capacity bottlenecks, maximizes information density via increased resolution, and introduces advanced training dynamics to stabilize the high-variance Macro F1 metric.

### Model
**Architecture: Hierarchical ConvNeXt-Base**
*   **Backbone:** Upgrade the feature extractor to **ConvNeXt-Base** initialized with pre-trained ImageNet-21k weights. This provides a significant increase in modeling capacity over the ResNet50 used in previous successful runs, directly addressing the underfitting observed in high-cardinality settings (Cite Lesson 00006).
*   **Hierarchical Multi-Task Heads:** The model will employ three parallel classification heads attached to the final embedding:
    1.  **Family Head:** Predicts the 272 plant families.
    2.  **Genus Head:** Predicts the 2,564 plant genera.
    3.  **Species Head:** Predicts the 15,501 plant species.
    *   **Rationale:** This structure forces the backbone to learn features that respect the biological taxonomy, grouping visually similar species by their genus and family, which acts as a strong structural regularizer.

### Data
**Preprocessing & Augmentation**
*   **Resolution:** Input images will be resized to **320x320** pixels. Previous experiments showed that increasing resolution from 224 to 256 improved performance (Cite Lesson 00010). Given the available compute budget (2 hours used vs. 24 hours limit), scaling to 320px allows the model to resolve even finer details necessary for distinguishing between 15,501 species.
*   **Augmentation:** We will use **Conservative Augmentation** (RandomResizedCrop with scale 0.8–1.0, HorizontalFlip). We will explicitly avoid aggressive cropping (scale < 0.8), as previous experiments showed this slows convergence and hurts performance in fine-grained tasks with short training schedules (Cite Lesson 00005).

### Training
**Strategy: Multi-Task Optimization with SWA**
*   **Loss Function:** The total loss will be a weighted sum: $L_{total} = L_{species} + \lambda (L_{genus} + L_{family})$.
    *   **Species Loss:** Weighted CrossEntropyLoss with **Label Smoothing (0.1)**. The label smoothing prevents overconfidence in the 15,501-class space (Cite Lesson 00010), while class weights (inverse frequency) address the long-tail imbalance (Cite Lesson 00001).
*   **Optimization:**
    *   **Differential Learning Rates:** Apply a lower learning rate (e.g., $10^{-5}$) to the ConvNeXt backbone and a higher rate (e.g., $10^{-3}$) to the heads. This preserves the high-quality pre-trained features while allowing the new heads to converge (Cite Lesson 00003).
    *   **Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA):** To address the high variance of the Macro F1 metric (Cite Lesson 00009), we will employ SWA in the final epochs. By averaging the weights of the model across multiple points in the optimization trajectory, SWA leads to a wider, more robust optimum that generalizes better to the test set.
    *   **Gradient Accumulation:** Due to the larger model and resolution, we will use gradient accumulation to maintain a healthy effective batch size (e.g., 64 or 128).

### Evaluation
**Inference with Test-Time Augmentation (TTA)**
*   **Method:** We will implement Test-Time Augmentation. For each test image, we will generate predictions for the original and a horizontally flipped version, averaging the logits from the **Species Head** before applying `argmax`. This further mitigates the stochastic noise inherent in single-pass predictions (Cite Lesson 00009).
