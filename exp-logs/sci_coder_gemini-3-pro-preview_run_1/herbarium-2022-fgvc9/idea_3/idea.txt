Based on the progression of experiments and the lessons learned, I propose the following **Hierarchical ConvNeXt-Base Strategy with Test-Time Augmentation**. This solution evolves the architecture from ResNet to a modern ConvNeXt backbone to address capacity bottlenecks, incorporates the taxonomic structure proposed in Idea 2, and rigorously applies the stabilization techniques verified in the Lessons.

### Model
**Architecture: Hierarchical ConvNeXt-Base**
*   **Backbone:** Upgrade the feature extractor to **ConvNeXt-Base** initialized with pre-trained ImageNet weights. This provides a significant increase in modeling capacity over the ResNet50 used in previous successful runs, directly addressing the underfitting observed in high-cardinality settings (Cite Lesson 00006).
*   **Hierarchical Multi-Task Heads:** Instead of a single output layer, the model will employ three parallel classification heads attached to the final embedding:
    1.  **Family Head:** Predicts the 272 plant families.
    2.  **Genus Head:** Predicts the 2,564 plant genera.
    3.  **Species Head:** Predicts the 15,501 plant species.
    *   **Rationale:** This structure forces the backbone to learn features that respect the biological taxonomy, grouping visually similar species by their genus and family, which acts as a strong structural regularizer.

### Data
**Preprocessing & Augmentation**
*   **Resolution:** Input images will be resized to **256x256** pixels. This resolution was proven to improve generalization over 224x224 by resolving finer details necessary for species differentiation (Cite Lesson 00010).
*   **Augmentation:** We will use **Conservative Augmentation** (RandomResizedCrop with scale 0.8–1.0, HorizontalFlip). We will explicitly avoid aggressive cropping (scale < 0.8), as previous experiments showed this slows convergence and hurts performance in fine-grained tasks (Cite Lesson 00005).
*   **Label Mapping:** Training data loaders will map each `category_id` to its corresponding `family` and `genus` labels to supervise the auxiliary heads.

### Training
**Strategy: Multi-Task Optimization with Differential Tuning**
*   **Loss Function:** The total loss will be a weighted sum: $L_{total} = L_{species} + \lambda (L_{genus} + L_{family})$.
    *   **Species Loss:** Weighted CrossEntropyLoss with **Label Smoothing (0.1)**. The label smoothing prevents overconfidence in the 15,501-class space (Cite Lesson 00010), while class weights (inverse frequency) address the long-tail imbalance (Cite Lesson 00001).
    *   **Auxiliary Losses:** Standard CrossEntropyLoss for Family and Genus heads.
*   **Optimization:**
    *   **Differential Learning Rates:** Apply a lower learning rate (e.g., $10^{-5}$) to the ConvNeXt backbone and a higher rate (e.g., $10^{-3}$) to the heads. This preserves the high-quality pre-trained features while allowing the new heads to converge (Cite Lesson 00003).
    *   **Scheduler:** Use **Cosine Annealing** with the step function placed strictly at the **end of the epoch** (after validation) to ensure robust state updates (Cite Lesson 00008).

### Evaluation
**Inference with Test-Time Augmentation (TTA)**
*   **Method:** To combat the high variance inherent in Macro F1 scores for long-tail datasets (Cite Lesson 00009), we will implement Test-Time Augmentation. For each test image, we will generate predictions for the original and a horizontally flipped version, averaging the logits from the **Species Head** before applying `argmax`.
*   **Prediction:** The final submission will be based solely on the species-level predictions, benefiting from the robust feature representations learned via the hierarchical auxiliary tasks.
