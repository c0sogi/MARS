# Model: Cross-Validated DenseNet Family Ensemble
I propose a **Multi-Architecture Ensemble** consisting of **DenseNet121** and **DenseNet169**.
*   **Architectural Choice:** I will retain **DenseNet121** as the core component, as it was the top-performing architecture (AUC 0.9849), proving that its feature concatenation mechanism is structurally superior for capturing the fine-grained texture of tumor cells compared to residual-only (ResNet) or large-kernel (ConvNeXt) architectures. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00016**.
*   **Structural Evolution:** I will introduce **DenseNet169** to the ensemble. This deeper variant increases model capacity and receptive field depth while preserving the inductive bias of dense connectivity that proved successful. This replaces the ResNet50 from Idea 5, focusing the ensemble on the most effective architectural family.
*   **Stem Modification:** For *both* models, I will replace the standard input stem (7x7 stride-2 convolution and max pooling) with a **single 3x3 convolution with stride 1 and padding 1**. This prevents the immediate loss of spatial resolution on the small 48x48 input, allowing the deep layers to process high-fidelity features. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00003**.
*   **Initialization:** Both models will be initialized with **ImageNet pretrained weights** (excluding the modified stem) to leverage learned feature detectors. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00008**.

# Data: Hard Attention and Conservative Augmentation
*   **Preprocessing:** I will apply **Hard Attention via ROI Cropping**, extracting the central **48x48 pixel region** from all images. This aligns the input with the ground truth definition while retaining a minimal context border. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00001**.
*   **Augmentation:** I will apply random horizontal flips, vertical flips, and 90-degree rotations. Crucially, I will restrict color augmentation to **mild brightness and contrast adjustments only**, explicitly **excluding hue and saturation jittering**. This preserves the H&E stain color signatures that are critical for identifying tumor tissue. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00004**.
*   **Dataset Usage:** I will utilize the **full training dataset** for all folds. I will strictly avoid subsampling, ensuring the high-capacity models have sufficient data to learn robust invariants. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00014**.

# Training: 5-Fold Cross-Validation with Volatility-Resistant Schedule
Instead of a single train/validation split, I will implement **5-Fold Cross-Validation**.
*   **Procedure:** The training data will be split into 5 stratified folds. For each architecture (DenseNet121 and DenseNet169), I will train 5 independent models (one for each fold), resulting in a total of 10 trained models. This strategy ensures that the ensemble's predictions are robust to data selection variance and utilizes the entire dataset for training across the folds.
*   **Optimization:** I will use the **AdamW** optimizer with a **Cosine Annealing** learning rate scheduler over 20 epochs per fold.
*   **Stability:** I will maintain a **relaxed Early Stopping patience of 6 epochs**. This ensures that training does not terminate prematurely during the volatile high-learning-rate phases, allowing the models to converge to a stable optimum. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00007**.

# Evaluation: TTA-Ensemble
For the final submission, I will employ a comprehensive inference strategy:
1.  **Test Time Augmentation (TTA):** For each test image, every model in the ensemble (10 total) will generate probability predictions for 4 views: original, horizontal flip, vertical flip, and 90-degree rotation. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**.
2.  **Global Averaging:** The final prediction will be the simple average of these 40 probabilities (2 architectures $\times$ 5 folds $\times$ 4 TTA views). This massive averaging significantly reduces variance and marginalizes out both model-specific and orientation-specific noise.
