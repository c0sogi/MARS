Based on the comprehensive analysis of previous experiments and lessons learned, I propose a **Heterogeneous Ensemble Strategy** that combines the strengths of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Vision Transformers (ViTs). This approach moves beyond the single-architecture paradigm to exploit the diversity of errors between different model families, ensuring robust minimization of Log Loss.

# Model
I propose a **Heterogeneous Ensemble** consisting of two distinct backbone architectures:
1.  **ConvNeXt-Base:** Retained for its proven performance and inductive bias towards local features (texture, shape) `Cite solution_lesson_node_00014`.
2.  **Swin-Base (Swin Transformer):** Introduced as a structural innovation. Unlike CNNs, Swin Transformers utilize self-attention mechanisms to capture long-range dependencies and global context. By combining a CNN and a Transformer, we mitigate the specific biases of each architecture.

Both models will be initialized with **ImageNet-1k** pre-trained weights. We will explicitly avoid ImageNet-22k weights to prevent the feature misalignment and convergence issues observed in previous attempts `Cite solution_lesson_node_00017`. We will also avoid "Large" variants to prevent the overfitting observed with the limited dataset size `Cite solution_lesson_node_00016`. Each backbone will feed into a linear classification head preceded by a **Dropout** layer for regularization `Cite solution_lesson_node_00006`.

# Data
We will enforce a strict, deterministic preprocessing pipeline to preserve geometric integrity, which is critical for fine-grained breed classification.
-   **Preprocessing:** All images will be processed using `Resize(256)` followed by `CenterCrop(224)`. Crucially, we will use the integer argument for resizing (scaling the shorter side to 256) rather than a tuple, ensuring the original aspect ratio is preserved and preventing the geometric distortion that caused previous failures `Cite solution_lesson_node_00018`.
-   **Augmentation:** We will use **only Random Horizontal Flips** during training. We will explicitly avoid aggressive spatial transformations (like `RandomResizedCrop`) that destroy breed-specific aspect ratios `Cite solution_lesson_node_00002`.

# Training
We will implement a **Stratified 5-Fold Cross-Validation** scheme for *each* architecture (totaling 10 trained models). This ensures that every training sample contributes to the validation metric, reducing variance. Within each fold, we will employ the successful **Two-Phase Fine-Tuning** strategy:
1.  **Phase 1 (Head Adaptation):** Freeze the backbone and train only the head to align weights.
2.  **Phase 2 (Fine-Tuning):** Unfreeze the backbone and apply **Discriminative Learning Rates**—a low rate (e.g., `1e-6`) for the backbone and a higher rate (e.g., `1e-4`) for the head—to specialize features without catastrophic forgetting `Cite solution_lesson_node_00006`.

We will use **Cross-Entropy Loss** without Label Smoothing, as smoothing penalizes the high-confidence predictions required to minimize Log Loss `Cite solution_lesson_node_00004`. We will also maintain a **Global Metric Tracker** to save the absolute best checkpoint across phases `Cite solution_lesson_node_00008`.

# Evaluation
Inference will focus on maximizing prediction stability and calibration.
-   **Test Time Augmentation (TTA):** For every test image, we will generate predictions for both the original and a horizontally flipped version `Cite solution_lesson_node_00014`.
-   **Ensemble Aggregation:** We will average the probabilities from all component models (5 ConvNeXt folds + 5 Swin folds) and their TTA views. This "Soft Voting" mechanism leverages the diversity of the heterogeneous architectures to smooth out individual errors and produce well-calibrated probabilities.
