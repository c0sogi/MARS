To address the challenge of labeling artwork with significant attributes, I propose the following optimized strategy. This approach evolves the architecture to a modern, detail-oriented backbone while retaining the robust training and data pipeline optimizations identified in previous lessons.

### Model
I propose replacing the ResNet architecture with a **ConvNeXt-Tiny** backbone. ConvNeXt modernizes standard CNNs with design choices inspired by Vision Transformers (e.g., larger kernel sizes, layer normalization), offering superior feature extraction capabilities for complex visual patterns found in artwork.

**Structural Innovation:**
Instead of the standard Global Average Pooling (GAP), I will implement a **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling** layer at the head of the network.
*   **Rationale:** The dataset contains both "centered objects" and "scenic rooms." GAP treats all spatial locations equally, which dilutes the signal of small but significant attributes (e.g., a specific vase in a room) with background noise. GeM Pooling is a learnable operation that interpolates between Average and Max pooling. It allows the model to dynamically focus on high-activation spatial regions (salient features) while ignoring irrelevant background, making it structurally better suited for multi-label classification in diverse scene types.

### Data
*   **Resolution:** I will increase the input image resolution from 224x224 to **320x320**. Artwork classification relies heavily on fine details (brushstrokes, texture, small artifacts) that are often lost at lower resolutions.
*   **Preprocessing:** I will strictly maintain the optimized data loading pipeline using `num_workers=4` and `pin_memory=True`. This is essential to prevent GPU starvation, especially given the increased I/O load from larger images (Cite Lesson 4).
*   **Augmentation:** I will retain the aggressive augmentation strategy (including `ShiftScaleRotate` and `ColorJitter`) established previously. This is critical to regularize the higher-capacity ConvNeXt model and prevent it from memorizing the training data (Cite Lesson 7).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** I will use `BCEWithLogitsLoss` with a moderate **positive weight (e.g., 10-12)**. This addresses the extreme label sparsity by amplifying the signal for rare positive attributes (Cite Lesson 1) without being so aggressive that it collapses precision (Cite Lesson 3). Additionally, I will introduce **Label Smoothing** (e.g., 0.05) to the loss formulation. Since the task description explicitly states annotations are "noisy" and unverified, smoothing prevents the model from being overconfident in potentially incorrect labels.
*   **Optimization:** I will use the **AdamW** optimizer combined with a **Cosine Annealing** learning rate scheduler. This schedule was proven to facilitate better convergence and generalization for high-capacity models in this domain (Cite Lesson 7).

### Evaluation
*   **Threshold Tuning:** Post-training, I will perform a comprehensive grid search for the optimal decision threshold on the validation set. Crucially, I will search the **full probability range (0.01 to 0.99)** rather than restricting it to a narrow window. This ensures we find the true optimal operating point, which may be significantly shifted away from 0.5 due to the use of positive class weighting (Cite Lesson 6).
