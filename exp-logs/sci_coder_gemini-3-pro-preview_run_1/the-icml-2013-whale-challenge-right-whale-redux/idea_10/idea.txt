Based on the comprehensive history of experiments and lessons learned, I propose the **Time-Preserving Coordinate-Selective-Kernel (CSK) ResNet-18 CRNN**.

This solution represents a structural evolution that synthesizes the "Axis-Awareness" success of **Coordinate Attention** (Lesson 00047) with the "Dynamic Receptive Field" concept of **Selective Kernels** (Idea 9). It addresses the specific physical nature of right whale calls—which contain both short, transient "clicks" and longer, smooth "up-sweeps"—by allowing the network to dynamically select kernel sizes localized in both time and frequency.

### Model

The architecture is a **Convolutional Recurrent Neural Network (CRNN)**.

1.  **Backbone: Time-Preserving CSK-ResNet-18**
    *   **Base Architecture**: We retain the **ResNet-18** depth, as it has proven structurally robust compared to brittle EfficientNet architectures (Cite solution_lesson_node_00031).
    *   **Structural Innovation (CSK Block)**: We replace the standard Residual Blocks with **Coordinate-Selective-Kernel (CSK) Blocks**.
        *   **Multi-Scale Branches**: Like a standard Selective Kernel (SK) unit, the input is split into two branches with different kernel sizes (e.g., $3\times3$ for transient details and $5\times5$ or dilated $3\times3$ for broader spectral sweeps).
        *   **Coordinate Fusion**: In standard SK units, the branches are fused using weights derived from *Global* Average Pooling. This is suboptimal for audio because it forces a single kernel choice for the entire time clip. We replace the Global Pooling with **Coordinate Attention** (pooling separately along Time and Frequency axes).
        *   **Effect**: This generates attention maps that are time-and-frequency aware. The network can now dynamically choose the small kernel for the transient onset of the call and the large kernel for the up-sweep *at the specific time steps where they occur*, rather than making a global decision.
    *   **Time-Preservation**: We strictly maintain **Asymmetric Strides `(2, 1)`** (Frequency, Time) in the final two stages (Layers 3 and 4). This ensures the temporal sequence length remains sufficient (~50-60 steps) for the RNN to detect motion (Cite solution_lesson_node_00012).
    *   **Initialization**: Initialize with **ImageNet pre-trained weights**. Programmatically average the first convolutional layer to adapt to 1-channel input (Cite solution_lesson_node_00020).

2.  **Temporal Modeling: Bi-Directional GRU**
    *   The output feature maps are pooled along the frequency dimension and permuted to a sequence format $(T \times C)$. This sequence is fed into a **Bi-directional GRU**, retaining the component proven most effective for modeling the temporal contour of the call (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).

3.  **Aggregation: Attention Pooling**
    *   We utilize **Attention Pooling** to compute a weighted sum of the GRU outputs, focusing the prediction on the signal segment and suppressing noise.

### Data

1.  **Preprocessing**:
    *   **Input Window**: Generate Log-Mel Spectrograms covering the **full 2.0-second duration** of the clip. Do not truncate to 1.0s, ensuring the signal is never discarded (Cite solution_lesson_node_00023).
    *   **Channel Format**: Use a **1-channel** input representation. Do not repeat the spectrogram to 3 channels (Cite solution_lesson_node_00042).
    *   **Spectrogram Parameters**: Use **128 Mel bins** to provide sufficient frequency resolution for the multi-scale kernels to operate.

2.  **Augmentation**:
    *   **Mixup**: Apply Mixup ($\alpha=0.4$) to training batches to regularize the model and accelerate convergence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00020).
    *   **SpecAugment**: Apply Frequency and Time Masking.
    *   **Constraint**: Strictly limit the **Time Mask width to a maximum of 200ms** (approx. 20 frames). This ensures the augmentation does not occlude the entire short-duration whale call (Cite solution_lesson_node_00011).

### Training

1.  **Loss Function**: Use **Binary Cross-Entropy with Logits Loss**.
    *   **Class Weighting**: Apply a **Positive Class Weight** (e.g., 9.0) to explicitly handle the 1:9 class imbalance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00025).
    *   **Target Handling**: Compute loss against the **mixed soft targets** generated by Mixup.

2.  **Optimizer**: Use **Adam** with a standard learning rate.

3.  **Scheduler**: Implement **ReduceLROnPlateau** to decay the learning rate when Validation AUC stagnates (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).

4.  **Metric Validity**: Monitor **Validation AUC** on unmixed data. Do not rely on training metrics computed against hard labels during Mixup (Cite solution_lesson_node_00022).

### Evaluation

1.  **Inference**: Process test clips into 2.0s Log-Mel Spectrograms (128 bins).
2.  **Prediction**: Pass inputs through the CSK-ResNet-18 model.
3.  **Output**: Apply a Sigmoid activation to the final output of the Attention Pooling layer to generate the probability for the submission file.
