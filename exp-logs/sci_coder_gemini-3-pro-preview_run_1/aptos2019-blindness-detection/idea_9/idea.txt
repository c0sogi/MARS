Based on the comprehensive analysis of the previous experiments and the derived lessons, I propose the following optimized strategy. This solution introduces a **Dual-Stream Pooling** mechanism to resolve the conflict between feature sensitivity and distortion robustness, scales the backbone to fully utilize the A100 hardware, and re-integrates **Model EMA** to stabilize the training dynamics.

# Model
*   **Backbone Architecture:** **ConvNeXt-Base**.
    *   *Structural Evolution:* I propose scaling up from the "Small" variant to **ConvNeXt-Base**.
    *   *Rationale:* Previous experiments established that Layer Normalization-based architectures (ConvNeXt) allow for high-resolution training without the batch-size instability of EfficientNet `Cite solution_lesson_node_00007`. Given the access to an A100 GPU, the "Small" model likely under-utilizes the available compute. Scaling to "Base" increases representational capacity to capture complex retinal features, adhering to the lesson that capacity (within memory limits) drives performance `Cite solution_lesson_node_00004`.
*   **Pooling Mechanism:** **Dual-Stream Pooling (Concatenated GAP + GeM)**.
    *   *Structural Innovation:* Instead of choosing between Global Average Pooling (GAP) or Generalized Mean Pooling (GeM), I propose **concatenating the outputs of both**.
    *   *Rationale:* A critical conflict was identified in the lessons. GeM Pooling is essential for detecting the sparse, high-activation lesions of Diabetic Retinopathy `Cite solution_lesson_node_00028`, but it is highly sensitive to the artifacts introduced by "squashing" resizing, leading to high error rates on large images `Cite solution_lesson_node_00026`. Conversely, GAP is robust to these distortions but tends to wash out small lesions `Cite solution_lesson_node_00026`. By concatenating both feature vectors, we provide the classification head with both the robust global context (GAP) and the sensitive local feature signals (GeM), allowing the linear layer to dynamically weigh them.
*   **Classification Head:** **Ordinal Head with Multi-Sample Dropout**.
    *   *Structure:* [GAP; GeM] $\rightarrow$ LayerNorm $\rightarrow$ Multi-Sample Dropout $\rightarrow$ Linear Layer (4 outputs).
    *   *Rationale:* We retain the Rank-Consistent Ordinal Regression logic `Cite solution_lesson_node_00001`. The Multi-Sample Dropout provides ensemble-like regularization `Cite solution_lesson_node_00028`. A LayerNorm is added before the dropout to normalize the statistics of the concatenated pooling features.

# Data
*   **Resolution:** **512x512 pixels**.
    *   *Rationale:* High resolution is non-negotiable for detecting microaneurysms, as downsampling is a primary bottleneck `Cite solution_lesson_node_00005`.
*   **Preprocessing:** **Simple Resizing ("Squashing")**.
    *   *Method:* Resize source images directly to 512x512, disregarding aspect ratio.
    *   *Rationale:* Despite the geometric distortion, "squashing" maximizes information density compared to padding or cropping `Cite solution_lesson_node_00010`. The proposed Dual-Stream Pooling is specifically designed to mitigate the sensitivity to this distortion.
*   **Augmentation:** **Strict Geometric Invariance**.
    *   *Techniques:* Random Vertical Flips, Random Horizontal Flips, and Random 90-degree Rotations.
    *   *Constraint:* **No Photometric (Hue) Augmentations.**
    *   *Rationale:* We strictly avoid Hue/Saturation shifts to preserve the pathological color markers (hemorrhages vs. exudates) `Cite solution_lesson_node_00024`.

# Training
*   **Training Dynamics:** **Model Exponential Moving Average (EMA)**.
    *   *Method:* Maintain a shadow copy of the model weights updated via moving average.
    *   *Rationale:* While Idea 8 failed, the lesson attributed the failure to the heuristic preprocessing (Graham's Method), not EMA itself `Cite solution_lesson_node_00031`. Re-introducing EMA is critical here to smooth out the high-frequency noise in the GeM features and the gradients from the "squashed" inputs, providing a stable target for convergence.
*   **Loss Function:** **BCEWithLogitsLoss** summed across the 4 ordinal units.
    *   *Rationale:* Ensures numerical stability via the Log-Sum-Exp trick `Cite solution_lesson_node_00018`.
*   **Optimizer:** **AdamW** with **Weight Decay = 0.05**.
    *   *Rationale:* We strictly adhere to the high weight decay requirement for ConvNeXt to maintain feature robustness `Cite solution_lesson_node_00027`.
*   **Scheduler:** **Cosine Annealing Scheduler**.
    *   *Rationale:* Essential for convergence stability in high-capacity models `Cite solution_lesson_node_00012`.

# Evaluation
*   **Inference Strategy:** **4-View Test Time Augmentation (TTA) with EMA Weights**.
    *   *Method:* Generate predictions for [Original, Horizontal Flip, Vertical Flip, Rotate 180] using the **EMA model**. Average the sigmoid probabilities.
    *   *Rationale:* TTA marginalizes geometric variance `Cite solution_lesson_node_00028`, while using EMA weights provides a more robust estimate than the final training snapshot.
*   **Decoding:** Sum the probabilities to obtain a continuous score (0-4) and **round to the nearest integer** `Cite solution_lesson_node_00001`.
