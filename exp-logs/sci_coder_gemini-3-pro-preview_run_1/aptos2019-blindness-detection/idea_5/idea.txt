Based on the analysis of the previous experiments and the specific lessons learned, I propose the following optimized strategy. The core structural innovation is to resolve the "Resolution vs. Batch Size" deadlock by transitioning from a Batch Normalization-based architecture (EfficientNet) to a **Layer Normalization-based architecture (ConvNeXt)**, while rigorously correcting the regularization and stability issues that caused previous attempts with this architecture to fail.

# Model
*   **Backbone Architecture:** **ConvNeXt-Tiny**.
    *   *Structural Innovation:* I propose adopting a modern CNN architecture that utilizes **Layer Normalization** instead of Batch Normalization.
    *   *Rationale:* Previous experiments revealed a critical bottleneck: increasing image resolution to capture fine details forced small batch sizes, which destabilized the Batch Normalization statistics in EfficientNet and degraded performance `Cite solution_lesson_node_00007`. Layer Normalization computes statistics per sample, decoupling training stability from batch size. This allows us to train on high-resolution inputs without the performance penalty associated with "micro-batches".
*   **Classification Head:** **Regularized Rank-Consistent Ordinal Regression Head**.
    *   *Structure:* A Global Average Pooling layer, followed by a **Dropout layer**, and finally a fully connected layer with 4 output units.
    *   *Correction:* Previous attempts to adapt this backbone failed due to overfitting `Cite solution_lesson_node_00017`. I will explicitly re-introduce the **Dropout** layer (typically discarded when removing the original classifier) before the final projection to prevent the high-capacity backbone from memorizing the small training set.
    *   *Logic:* The head predicts ordinal logits (Is severity > 0?, > 1?, etc.) `Cite solution_lesson_node_00001`.

# Data
*   **Resolution:** **512x512 pixels**.
    *   *Rationale:* Correlation analysis confirmed that downsampling to 256x256 was a primary bottleneck, discarding critical high-frequency diagnostic information `Cite solution_lesson_node_00005`. With the switch to a LayerNorm-based backbone, we can now effectively utilize this higher resolution.
*   **Preprocessing:** **Simple Resizing ("Squashing")**.
    *   *Method:* Resize the source image directly to 512x512, disregarding aspect ratio.
    *   *Rationale:* Previous experiments showed that "smart cropping" and "pad-to-square" strategies hurt performance by introducing variable geometric distortions or reducing effective information density `Cite solution_lesson_node_00009` `Cite solution_lesson_node_00010`. Simple resizing ensures consistent distortion and maximizes the pixel area dedicated to retinal tissue.
*   **Augmentation:** **Geometric Invariance Suite**.
    *   *Techniques:* Random Vertical Flips, Random Horizontal Flips, and Random 90-degree Rotations.
    *   *Rationale:* Exploits the rotational invariance of fundus photography to improve generalization `Cite solution_lesson_node_00004`.

# Training
*   **Loss Function:** **BCEWithLogitsLoss** summed across the 4 ordinal output units.
    *   *Correction:* Previous attempts used `Sigmoid` + `BCELoss`, which can be numerically unstable. Using the fused `BCEWithLogitsLoss` utilizes the Log-Sum-Exp trick to prevent gradient saturation and underflow `Cite solution_lesson_node_00018`.
*   **Optimizer:** **AdamW** with weight decay.
*   **Scheduler:** **Cosine Annealing Scheduler**.
    *   *Rationale:* High-resolution, high-capacity models impose a significant optimization burden. A scheduler is strictly required to settle the weights into a robust minima, as seen in the failure of previous unscheduled runs `Cite solution_lesson_node_00012`.
*   **Batch Size:** Maximum that fits in GPU memory (likely 16-24). The use of Layer Normalization makes the specific number less critical for stability `Cite solution_lesson_node_00007`.

# Evaluation
*   **Inference:** Pass test images through the model to generate 4 raw logits, apply Sigmoid to obtain probabilities.
*   **Decoding:** Sum the 4 probabilities to obtain a continuous severity score (0-4) and **round to the nearest integer**. This remains the most robust method for maximizing the Quadratic Weighted Kappa metric `Cite solution_lesson_node_00001`.
