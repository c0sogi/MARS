Based on the comprehensive analysis of the previous experiments and the derived lessons, I propose the following optimized strategy. This solution synthesizes the architectural advantages of modern Vision Transformers/CNNs with the robust preprocessing and optimization techniques identified in the successful baseline.

# Model
*   **Backbone Architecture:** **ConvNeXt-Tiny**.
    *   *Structural Innovation:* I propose shifting from Batch Normalization-based architectures (EfficientNet/MobileNet) to a **Layer Normalization-based architecture**.
    *   *Rationale:* Previous experiments revealed a critical trade-off: increasing resolution to capture fine details forced small batch sizes ("micro-batches"), which destabilized the Batch Normalization statistics in EfficientNet and degraded performance `Cite solution_lesson_node_00007`. ConvNeXt uses Layer Normalization (computing stats per sample), which decouples training stability from batch size. This allows us to train on high-resolution inputs without the performance penalty associated with small batches.
*   **Classification Head:** **Rank-Consistent Ordinal Regression Head**.
    *   *Structure:* A Global Average Pooling layer followed by a fully connected layer with 4 output units and Sigmoid activations.
    *   *Rationale:* This component is retained as it has consistently outperformed standard classification for maximizing the Quadratic Weighted Kappa metric by modeling the cumulative probability of the ordered severity levels `Cite solution_lesson_node_00001`.

# Data
*   **Resolution:** **512x512 pixels**.
    *   *Rationale:* Correlation analysis confirmed that downsampling to 256x256 was a primary bottleneck, discarding critical high-frequency diagnostic information `Cite solution_lesson_node_00005`. With the switch to a LayerNorm-based backbone, we can now effectively utilize this higher resolution.
*   **Preprocessing:** **Simple Resizing ("Squashing")**.
    *   *Method:* Resize the full source image directly to 512x512, disregarding aspect ratio.
    *   *Rationale:* Previous attempts at "smart cropping" (Ben Graham's method) and "pad-to-square" failed because they introduced variable geometric distortions or reduced effective information density `Cite solution_lesson_node_00009` `Cite solution_lesson_node_00010`. Simple resizing ensures that every pixel in the input tensor is used for retinal data and that geometric distortion is consistent across the entire dataset.
*   **Augmentation:** **Full Geometric Invariance Suite**.
    *   *Techniques:* Random Vertical Flips, Random Horizontal Flips, and Random 90-degree Rotations.
    *   *Rationale:* Retained from the best-performing experiments to exploit the rotational invariance of fundus photography `Cite solution_lesson_node_00004`.

# Training
*   **Optimizer & Scheduler:** **AdamW** coupled with a **Cosine Annealing Scheduler**.
    *   *Rationale:* The previous attempt to use ConvNeXt failed partly because it omitted the scheduler. Large, high-resolution models impose a higher optimization burden and require a learning rate scheduler for stability and convergence `Cite solution_lesson_node_00012`.
*   **Loss Function:** **Binary Cross Entropy (BCE)** summed across the 4 ordinal output units.
*   **Batch Size:** Maximize to fit GPU memory (likely 16-32).
    *   *Note:* Unlike EfficientNet, the specific batch size number is less critical for stability due to the use of Layer Normalization `Cite solution_lesson_node_00007`.

# Evaluation
*   **Inference:** Generate 4 sigmoid probabilities for each test image.
*   **Decoding:** Sum the 4 probabilities to obtain a continuous severity score (0-4) and **round to the nearest integer**.
    *   *Rationale:* This remains the most robust method for converting ordinal predictions into discrete classes for the QWK metric `Cite solution_lesson_node_00001`.
