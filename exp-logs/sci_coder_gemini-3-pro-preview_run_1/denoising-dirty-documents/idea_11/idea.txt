Based on the comprehensive analysis of the provided lessons, particularly the trade-off between **Context** (requiring large patches) and **Data Diversity** (requiring small patches), and the recent discovery of **Signal Alignment** benefits, I propose the following optimized strategy.

This solution implements a **Resolution-Aligned Heterogeneous Ensemble** trained on **Inverted Signals**. This approach structurally resolves the "Depth-Resolution Mismatch" (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00026}) by ensuring that every model in the ensemble maintains a consistent bottleneck resolution, while simultaneously leveraging the "Inverted Signal" (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00038}) to eliminate padding artifacts and accelerate convergence.

# Model
**Architecture: Resolution-Aligned Heterogeneous Ensemble**
Instead of forcing a single architecture to compromise between context and diversity, we will deploy a dual-stream ensemble where each stream is architecturally matched to its specific input scale to maintain a constant bottleneck resolution (feature map size).

1.  **Stream A: Context Specialist (4-Level U-Net)**
    *   **Backbone:** A deep **4-Level U-Net** (Encoder: 32 $\to$ 64 $\to$ 128 $\to$ 256 $\to$ 512).
    *   **Input Specification:** Designed for **$320 \times 320$** pixel patches.
    *   **Structural Logic:** With 4 downsampling operations (factor 16), the bottleneck resolution is $320 / 16 = 20 \times 20$. This preserves the critical spatial fidelity required for text reconstruction (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00026}) while capturing the global context needed to distinguish signal from noise (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00034}).
    *   **Upsampling:** Bilinear Upsampling followed by Convolution (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00009}).

2.  **Stream B: Diversity Specialist (3-Level U-Net)**
    *   **Backbone:** A lightweight **3-Level U-Net** (Encoder: 32 $\to$ 64 $\to$ 128 $\to$ 256).
    *   **Input Specification:** Designed for **$160 \times 160$** pixel patches.
    *   **Structural Logic:** With 3 downsampling operations (factor 8), the bottleneck resolution is $160 / 8 = 20 \times 20$. This matches the spatial fidelity of Stream A but utilizes a smaller input window. This smaller window allows for massive combinatorial crop diversity, preventing overfitting and forcing the model to learn robust local texture statistics (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00027}).

# Data
**Preprocessing: Inverted Signal & Differential Patching**
We will implement a signal-aligned data pipeline that serves specific patch sizes to the corresponding model streams.

1.  **Signal Inversion (Structural Innovation):**
    *   All images will be inverted upon loading: $I_{processed} = 1.0 - I_{raw}$.
    *   **Rationale:** This maps the background (white) to 0 and text (black) to 1. This aligns the data with the inductive bias of Zero-Padding and ReLU activations, eliminating "black border" artifacts and significantly accelerating convergence (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00038}).

2.  **Differential Patch Extraction:**
    *   **For Stream A:** Extract random crops of **$320 \times 320$** pixels.
    *   **For Stream B:** Extract random crops of **$160 \times 160$** pixels.
    *   **Augmentation:** Apply random horizontal flips, vertical flips, and 90-degree rotations to all patches (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00003}).

3.  **Deterministic Loading:**
    *   Implement a `worker_init_fn` to seed data loader workers, ensuring full reproducibility of the random cropping pipeline (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00015}).

# Training
**Strategy: Fully Converged Independent Bagging**
We will train a robust ensemble of **10 independent models** (5 instances of Stream A, 5 instances of Stream B) to combine the benefits of global coherence and local texture fidelity.

*   **Full Convergence:** Each model will be trained for **1000 epochs**. We explicitly prioritize training to full convergence over early stopping to minimize bias and resolve high-frequency details (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00016}).
*   **Optimization Velocity:** Use the **Adam** optimizer with a **high learning rate (1e-3)** and **small batch size (16)**. This maximizes the frequency of weight updates, which is critical for traversing the loss landscape on small datasets (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00010}).
*   **Scheduler:** **Cosine Annealing** with `T_max=1000`. This decouples the scheduler from any stopping criteria, ensuring the learning rate decays fully to zero to settle into sharp minima (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00007}).
*   **Loss Function:** Mean Squared Error (MSE) computed on the **inverted** signal.

# Evaluation
**Inference Pipeline**
*   **Inverted Inference:** Test images will be inverted ($1.0 - I$) before processing.
*   **D4 Group Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):** For every test image, we will generate predictions for **8 geometric views** (Original, Rot90, Rot180, Rot270, and their horizontal flips). These will be inversely transformed and averaged. This significantly reduces variance compared to standard 4-view TTA (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00028}).
*   **Heterogeneous Aggregation:** The TTA-averaged outputs from all **10 fully converged models** (5 Context + 5 Diversity) will be averaged together.
    *   **Benefit:** This averages out the specific errors of each approach: Stream A fills in global structure where Stream B might lose context, while Stream B refines local textures where Stream A might over-smooth due to limited training diversity.
*   **Re-Inversion:** The final aggregated prediction is inverted back ($1.0 - I_{pred}$) to the original intensity space for submission.
