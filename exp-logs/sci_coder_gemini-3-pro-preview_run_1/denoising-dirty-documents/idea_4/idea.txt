# Model
**Architecture: Deep Supervision U-Net**
I propose a structural evolution of the baseline U-Net into a **Deep Supervision U-Net**.
*   **Backbone:** We will retain the **Standard U-Net** architecture (starting with 32 filters) rather than the heavier ResUNet. Lesson analysis confirms that on this small dataset (92 images), simpler architectures generalize better and avoid optimization difficulties (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00011}).
*   **Deep Supervision (Structural Innovation):** We will attach auxiliary prediction heads ($1 \times 1$ convolutions) to the output feature maps of the intermediate decoder blocks (e.g., after the first and second upsampling stages). During training, these heads will predict the clean image at lower resolutions. This structural change injects gradients directly into the deeper layers of the network, mitigating the vanishing gradient problem common in small-data regimes and forcing the encoder to learn robust, discriminative features early in the process.
*   **Upsampling Mechanism:** We will strictly employ **Bilinear Upsampling followed by Convolution** for all decoding stages. We will explicitly avoid Transposed Convolutions to prevent the checkerboard artifacts observed in previous attempts (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00009}).

# Data
**Preprocessing & Augmentation**
*   **Maximized Patch Size:** We will train on **large random crops** (e.g., $160 \times 160$ or larger) extracted from the source images. Previous experiments demonstrated that larger patches provide the necessary spatial context to distinguish text structures from noise, significantly reducing error (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00016}).
*   **Aggressive Geometric Augmentation:** We will apply random horizontal flips, vertical flips, and 90-degree rotations to the training patches. This exploits the rotational invariance of the noise patterns to virtually multiply the dataset size (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00003}).
*   **Deterministic Loading:** We will implement a `worker_init_fn` to seed the random number generators of the data loader workers. This ensures that the random cropping and augmentation pipeline is fully reproducible and deterministic across runs (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00015}).

# Training
**Procedure & Optimization**
*   **Loss Function:** We will use a **Multi-Scale Mean Squared Error (MSE)** loss. The total loss will be the weighted sum of the final output MSE and the auxiliary outputs' MSE (interpolated to target size). This aligns the optimization with the competition metric while leveraging the deep supervision structure.
*   **Optimization Velocity:** We will utilize the **Adam** optimizer with a **high learning rate (1e-3)** and a **small batch size (16)**. This combination maximizes the frequency and magnitude of weight updates, ensuring efficient traversal of the loss landscape (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00010}).
*   **Scheduler:** We will employ a **Cosine Annealing** scheduler with a **decoupled horizon**. We will set `T_max` to the full number of epochs (e.g., 1000) to ensure the learning rate decays fully to zero, allowing the model to settle into a sharp minimum (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00007}, Cite {solution_lesson_node_00016}).
*   **Converged Ensemble Strategy:** We will train an ensemble of $N$ (e.g., 5) independent models. Unlike previous attempts that sacrificed training duration for ensemble size, we will train **each model to full convergence** (1000+ epochs). Since the optimal training time is short (~15 minutes), this fits easily within the compute budget. This combines the variance reduction of ensembling with the low bias of fully converged models (Refining Cite {solution_lesson_node_00012}).

# Evaluation
**Inference Pipeline**
*   **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):** For every test image, we will generate predictions for the original and 7 geometric variations (flips and 90/180/270 rotations). These will be inversely transformed and averaged to smooth out prediction noise (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00006}).
*   **Ensemble Aggregation:** The TTA-averaged predictions from all fully converged models in the ensemble will be averaged together to produce the final pixel intensities.
