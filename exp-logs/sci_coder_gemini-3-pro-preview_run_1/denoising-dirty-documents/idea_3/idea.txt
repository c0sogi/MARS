Based on the provided lessons and dataset analysis, I propose the following optimized strategy.

# Model
**Architecture: Attention U-Net**
I propose evolving the baseline architecture from a standard U-Net to an **Attention U-Net**. While the ResUNet failed due to over-parameterization on this small dataset (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00011}), an Attention U-Net improves feature selection without significantly increasing model complexity.
*   **Backbone:** The core structure remains a lightweight Encoder-Decoder network (starting with 32 filters) to prevent overfitting.
*   **Attention Gates (Structural Innovation):** Instead of simple skip connections, we will introduce **Attention Gates** at the junction where encoder features are concatenated with decoder features. These gates utilize the coarser decoder signal to generate a spatial attention map, which filters the high-resolution encoder features. This allows the model to suppress background noise in the skip connections and focus the reconstruction specifically on the text structures, addressing the "high noise" nature of the dataset.
*   **Upsampling:** We will strictly use **Bilinear Upsampling followed by Convolution** rather than Transposed Convolutions to avoid checkerboard artifacts (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00009}).

# Data
**Preprocessing & Augmentation**
*   **Patch-Based Training:** We will continue to train on **fixed-size random crops** (e.g., $128 \times 128$) extracted from the original resolution images. This preserves the high-frequency noise statistics and virtually expands the dataset size (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00001}).
*   **Aggressive Geometric Augmentation:** We will apply random horizontal flips, vertical flips, and 90-degree rotations to the patches. Since text denoising relies on local pixel statistics which are rotationally invariant, this maximizes data diversity (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00003}).
*   **Normalization:** Pixel intensities will be normalized to the $[0, 1]$ range.

# Training
**Procedure & Optimization**
*   **Ensemble Strategy (Structural Innovation):** To combat the stochastic variance inherent in training on such a small dataset (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00005}), we will implement a **Bagging Ensemble**. We will train $N$ (e.g., 5) independent instances of the Attention U-Net with different random seeds. This ensures the final prediction is robust and not dependent on a single lucky initialization.
*   **Optimization Velocity:** We will maintain a **small batch size (16)** and a **high learning rate (1e-3)**. This ensures frequent, significant weight updates, allowing the model to traverse the loss landscape efficiently despite the limited data (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00010}).
*   **Scheduler:** We will use a **Cosine Annealing Learning Rate** scheduler with a **decoupled horizon**. We will set `T_max` (e.g., 1000 epochs) significantly longer than the expected convergence time to maintain a productive learning rate, relying on Early Stopping to terminate training when appropriate (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00007}, Cite {solution_lesson_node_00006}).
*   **Loss Function:** Mean Squared Error (MSE).

# Evaluation
**Inference Pipeline**
*   **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):** For each test image, we will generate predictions for the original, horizontally flipped, vertically flipped, and rotated versions. These will be aligned and averaged to smooth out prediction noise (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00006}).
*   **Ensemble Aggregation:** The TTA-averaged outputs from all $N$ models in the ensemble will be averaged together to produce the final pixel intensities.
*   **Submission:** The final averaged images will be flattened, and pixels will be mapped to their `image_row_col` IDs for submission.
