Based on the comprehensive analysis of the accumulated lessons, I propose a solution that structurally evolves the training paradigm from **Point-Estimate Optimization** to **Bayesian-Approximated Optimization** using **Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA)**. This approach addresses the saturation in generalization performance observed in the "Current Best" solution by explicitly seeking a flatter minimum in the loss landscape, which is critical for robustly handling the high-variance "Unknown" class.

# Model
**Architecture: Dilated EfficientNet-B2 with Attentive Pooling**
We will retain the "Current Best" architecture, as previous attempts to alter the backbone (ConvNeXt, Transformers, CRNNs) or input structure (3-Channel, Multi-Resolution) resulted in degradation or instability.
*   **Backbone:** **EfficientNet-B2** initialized with ImageNet weights (Cite solution_lesson_node_00024).
*   **Input Adaptation:** The first convolutional layer will be adapted to accept **1-channel input** by averaging the pretrained RGB weights. This preserves learned feature detectors better than random initialization (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016) and outperforms explicit temporal derivative inputs (Cite solution_lesson_node_00038).
*   **Resolution Preservation:** We will strictly apply **Dilated Convolutions** (stride=1, dilation=2) in the final backbone stage. This preserves a temporal resolution of ~6-7 frames, preventing the feature map collapse that hinders attention mechanisms (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009, Cite solution_lesson_node_00025).
*   **Head:** We will use **Attentive Pooling** to dynamically weight active speech segments and suppress background noise (Cite solution_lesson_node_00017).
*   **Output:** The model will output probabilities for **31+ Fine-Grained Classes** (all unique command words) rather than the compressed 12-class target set (Cite solution_lesson_node_00028).

# Data
**Preprocessing:**
*   **High-Fidelity Spectrograms:** Generate Log-Mel Spectrograms with **128 Mel bands**, **N_FFT of 1024** (64ms), and a **Hop Length of 160** (10ms). This configuration balances spectral density (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005) with temporal precision (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008).
*   **Energy Preservation:** We will **strictly avoid** Instance Normalization to preserve the absolute energy differences between silence and speech (Cite solution_lesson_node_00039).

**Augmentation:**
*   **Waveform Noise Injection:** Inject background noise into raw waveforms (SNR 10-30dB) to force invariance to environmental conditions (Cite solution_lesson_node_00029).
*   **Dynamic Silence Synthesis:** Randomly crop background noise files to generate diverse 'silence' samples (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
*   **Regularization:** Apply **SpecAugment** (Time/Freq masking) (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003) and **Mixup** with **$\alpha = 1.0$** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016).

**Balancing:**
*   **Variance-Aware Balancing:** Target commands will be upsampled to ~2,000, while the diverse auxiliary classes (the "Unknown" source) will be kept at their natural counts (~9,000 total) to ensure the negative class retains high variance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00026).

# Training
**Strategy: Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA) Pipeline**
I propose a structural innovation in the optimization procedure. Standard training settles into a sharp local minimum, which often leads to brittleness on unseen data (the "Unknown" class). We will implement a multi-stage **SWA Pipeline** to approximate a flatter, more robust minimum.

1.  **Phase 1 (Exploration):** Train the model for **40 epochs** using **AdamW** and a standard **Cosine Annealing** scheduler (learning rate decaying from $1e^{-3}$ to $1e^{-5}$). This allows the model to converge near the optimal solution region (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
2.  **Phase 2 (SWA Collection):** Continue training for an additional **10 epochs** with a **constant, moderately high learning rate** (e.g., $0.05 \times LR_{max}$).
    *   At the end of each epoch in this phase, store a copy of the model weights.
    *   This cyclic exploration allows the optimizer to traverse the periphery of the loss basin.
3.  **Phase 3 (Averaging & Update):**
    *   Compute the **average** of the collected weight snapshots. This averaged model ($W_{SWA}$) sits in the center of the flat minimum, offering superior generalization compared to any single snapshot.
    *   **BatchNorm Update:** Perform a single forward pass over the training data using $W_{SWA}$ to re-calculate the Batch Normalization statistics (mean/variance), as the averaged weights may not align with the tracked statistics of the final epoch.
