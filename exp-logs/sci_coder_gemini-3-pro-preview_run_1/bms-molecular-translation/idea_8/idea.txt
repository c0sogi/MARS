Based on the lessons learned, specifically the critical importance of attribute conditioning and the need for rigorous loss balancing (Lesson 00008), I propose the **Swin-Transformer with Attribute-Augmented Attention** strategy. This approach structurally evolves the previous CNN-based baselines by adopting a Hierarchical Vision Transformer backbone to better capture the non-local graph structure of molecules, while integrating the successful chemical attribute guidance directly into the attention mechanism.

### Model
The architecture is a hybrid Encoder-Decoder system designed to process chemical images as hierarchical visual tokens augmented by explicit chemical constraints.
*   **Encoder**: We replace the CNN backbones (ResNet/EfficientNet) with a **Swin Transformer (Tiny)**. Unlike standard CNNs, Swin Transformer's shifted window attention mechanism effectively models the long-range dependencies between distant atoms in complex molecular graphs while maintaining computational efficiency. The encoder outputs a sequence of flattened visual feature vectors.
*   **Attribute Branch**: A Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) is attached to the global average of the encoder's output. It predicts high-level chemical statistics: the counts of specific atoms (C, H, O, N, S, Halogens) and the total InChI string length. This explicitly forces the encoder to learn semantic summaries of the molecule. Cite {solution_lesson_node_00008}.
*   **Context Fusion**: The predicted attribute vector is projected to the model's embedding dimension to create a set of "Attribute Tokens". These tokens are **concatenated** to the sequence of visual feature vectors produced by the Swin Encoder.
*   **Decoder**: A **Transformer Decoder** (e.g., 4 layers). In the Cross-Attention layers, the decoder attends to the combined sequence of [Visual Tokens + Attribute Tokens]. This allows the model to dynamically query specific image regions for structural details or attend to the attribute tokens to verify the atom budget (e.g., "how many Hydrogens are left?") at any step of the generation.

### Data
*   **Image Preprocessing**: Images are resized to **$224 \times 224$ pixels** (standard for Swin) and normalized using ImageNet mean and standard deviation.
*   **Attribute Engineering**: We parse the ground truth InChI strings to extract the regression targets: atom counts and sequence length. Crucially, these targets are **Z-score normalized** (standardized) using statistics from the training set. This ensures the regression gradients are on the same scale as the classification gradients, preventing the auxiliary task from destabilizing the sequence learning. Cite {solution_lesson_node_00008}.
*   **Text Preprocessing**: InChI strings are tokenized at the character level, with special tokens (`<SOS>`, `<EOS>`, `<PAD>`) added.

### Training
*   **Loss Function**: A balanced Multi-Task Loss: $L_{total} = L_{CrossEntropy} + \lambda \cdot L_{MSE}$. Due to the target normalization, we set $\lambda \approx 1.0$, ensuring both tasks contribute equally to the gradient updates without one dominating the other. Cite {solution_lesson_node_00008}.
*   **Optimizer**: **AdamW** is used to support the Transformer architecture, with a **Cosine Annealing** learning rate scheduler and a linear warmup phase to stabilize the training of attention weights.
*   **Strategy**: The model is trained end-to-end. The attribute branch provides immediate semantic supervision to the encoder, while the decoder learns to generate the sequence via causal masking (parallelized teacher forcing).

### Evaluation
*   **Inference Process**: The test image is passed through the Swin Encoder and Attribute Branch. The predicted attributes are converted to tokens and appended to the visual features.
*   **Decoding**: The Transformer Decoder operates autoregressively using **Greedy Decoding**. It predicts the InChI string character-by-character, conditioned on the hybrid visual-chemical context, until the `<EOS>` token is generated.
