Based on the accumulated lessons, I propose the **1D Atrous Residual U-Net (Atrous ResUNet)**.

This strategy structurally evolves the successful **1D U-Net** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018) by integrating an **Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling (ASPP)** module at the bottleneck. This addresses the limitation of standard U-Nets in capturing long-term dependencies (like atmospheric drift) without resorting to Transformers, which were found to lack the necessary local inductive bias and training stability (Cite solution_lesson_node_00031).

### Model
**Architecture: 1D Atrous Residual U-Net**
The model retains the encoder-decoder structure for efficient sequence-to-sequence prediction but enhances the receptive field and feature integration.
*   **Residual Encoder:** A series of downsampling stages. Each stage consists of **1D Residual Blocks** (two 1D convolutions with a skip connection) followed by Max Pooling. This allows the network to learn deep, hierarchical representations of signal dynamics without gradient degradation.
*   **ASPP Bottleneck (Structural Innovation):** Instead of a simple convolutional bridge, the bottleneck utilizes **Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling**. The feature map from the encoder is fed into multiple parallel 1D Convolutional branches with different **dilation rates** (e.g., $r \in \{1, 6, 12, 18\}$).
    *   *Why:* This allows the model to capture context at multiple scales simultaneously—detecting both immediate multipath spikes (low dilation) and long-term signal drifts (high dilation)—preserving the local inductive bias of CNNs while expanding the global view.
*   **Residual Decoder:** A series of upsampling layers. Features from the Encoder are concatenated via **Skip Connections** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018) to preserve high-frequency local signal details essential for precise timestamp-level corrections.
*   **Output Head:** A final projection layer outputs the 2D residual vector $(\Delta North, \Delta East)$ in meters.

### Data
The preprocessing pipeline strictly adheres to the robust feature engineering principles established in previous lessons:
1.  **Temporal Quantization:** Raw GNSS logs and ground truth are aligned by rounding timestamps to the nearest second (1Hz). This prevents the massive data loss associated with exact timestamp matching (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016).
2.  **Boundary-Aware Aggregation:** Raw satellite features are aggregated per timestamp using dense statistical moments. We explicitly calculate the **Mean, Minimum, and Maximum** for `Cn0DbHz` (Signal Strength) and `SvElevationDegrees` (Cite solution_lesson_node_00020). The "Minimum" statistics are critical for identifying epochs corrupted by weak or low-elevation signals. We strictly avoid distributional histograms (Cite solution_lesson_node_00028) and kinematic features (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).
3.  **Reliability Metadata:** Include `SatCount` and aggregated `RawPseudorangeUncertaintyMeters` to quantify baseline confidence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
4.  **Translation Invariance:** Absolute `Latitude` and `Longitude` are strictly excluded from the input to prevent map-memorization (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
5.  **Target Transformation:** Regression targets are transformed from Degrees (Lat/Lon) to **Meters (East-North)** relative to the WLS baseline to ensure physical consistency (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** **Deep Supervision with Mean Absolute Error (MAE)**. Auxiliary loss heads are attached to the intermediate decoder stages. The total loss is a weighted sum of the final output loss and auxiliary losses. This improves gradient flow to the deep encoder layers and forces the model to learn hierarchical error representations. MAE is used for its robustness to outliers (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with weight decay is used to regularize the residual blocks.
*   **Validation Strategy:** A `GroupShuffleSplit` based on `drive_id` is used to ensure the model is evaluated on entirely unseen trips, preventing data leakage.

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** The preprocessed feature sequence for a full test drive is fed into the model. The model outputs the sequence of Cartesian residuals $(\Delta North, \Delta East)$.
2.  **Reconstruction:** These Cartesian offsets are converted back into angular offsets $(\Delta Lat, \Delta Lon)$ using local WGS84 approximations.
3.  **Correction:** The predicted offsets are added to the baseline WLS coordinates provided in the test set to generate the final submission coordinates.
