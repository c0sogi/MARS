Based on the lessons learned, I propose the **Hybrid 1D TransUNet with Boundary-Aware Signal Aggregation**. This strategy structurally evolves the successful 1D U-Net approach by integrating a Transformer mechanism to capture global trajectory context (addressing the limited receptive field of pure CNNs) and refining the input representation with boundary statistics to better characterize signal health.

### Model
The architecture is a **1D TransUNet (Transformer-U-Net)** designed for sequence-to-sequence residual prediction.
*   **Encoder:** A stack of **1D Residual Convolutional Blocks** that progressively downsamples the time-series input. This efficiently extracts high-frequency local features (e.g., momentary signal drops due to obstacles) (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).
*   **Bottleneck (Structural Innovation):** The deepest layer of the encoder feeds into a **Transformer Encoder** block. Unlike convolutions which process local neighborhoods, the Self-Attention mechanism allows the model to utilize information from the entire drive duration (global context) to calibrate corrections. This is crucial for identifying and correcting slowly varying biases like atmospheric delays or hardware clock drift that persist across the whole trip.
*   **Decoder:** A stack of upsampling layers that restores the temporal resolution. It utilizes **Skip Connections** from the Encoder to reintroduce the high-frequency local details lost during downsampling, ensuring precise temporal localization of the corrections.
*   **Output Head:** A final projection layer outputs the 2D residual vector $(\Delta North, \Delta East)$ in meters for every timestamp in the sequence.

### Data
The preprocessing pipeline synthesizes robust alignment and feature engineering techniques:
1.  **Temporal Quantization:** Raw GNSS logs and ground truth are aligned by rounding timestamps to the nearest second (1Hz). This prevents the data loss associated with exact matching (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016).
2.  **Boundary-Aware Aggregation:** Raw satellite features are aggregated per timestamp. Crucially, we compute **Minimum, Maximum, and Mean** for `Cn0DbHz` and `SvElevationDegrees` (Cite solution_lesson_node_00020). The "Minimum" statistics act as detectors for weak links (e.g., a single low-elevation satellite) that often dominate the positioning error.
3.  **Reliability Metadata:** We include `SatCount` and the aggregated `RawPseudorangeUncertaintyMeters` to explicitly quantify the baseline's uncertainty (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
4.  **Translation Invariance:** Absolute `Latitude` and `Longitude` are strictly excluded from the input to force the model to learn error dynamics rather than memorizing locations (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
5.  **Target Transformation:** Regression targets are transformed from Degrees (Lat/Lon) to **Meters (East-North)** relative to the WLS baseline. This ensures the loss function operates in a physically consistent metric space (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** **Huber Loss**. This function combines the best properties of MAE and MSE. It is quadratic for small errors (providing smooth gradients for fine-tuning) and linear for large errors (providing robustness to the heavy-tailed outliers common in GNSS data) (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW is used with weight decay to prevent overfitting in the Transformer layers.
*   **Validation:** A `GroupShuffleSplit` based on `drive_id` is used to ensure the model is evaluated on entirely unseen trips, preventing data leakage.

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** The preprocessed feature sequence for an entire test drive is fed into the model to predict the sequence of Cartesian residuals $(\Delta North, \Delta East)$.
2.  **Reconstruction:** These Cartesian offsets are converted back into angular offsets $(\Delta Lat, \Delta Lon)$ using local WGS84 approximations.
3.  **Correction:** The predicted offsets are added to the baseline WLS coordinates provided in the test set to generate the final submission coordinates.
