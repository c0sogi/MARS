Based on the accumulated lessons, specifically the success of sequence-to-sequence processing (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018) and the importance of boundary statistics (Cite solution_lesson_node_00020), I propose a **Distribution-Aware 1D ResUNet**.

This strategy evolves the previous "1D U-Net" idea by upgrading the backbone to a **Residual U-Net** to support deeper feature extraction and fundamentally enriching the input representation to capture the *distribution shape* of signal quality, rather than just central tendencies.

### Model
**Architecture: 1D Residual U-Net (ResUNet)**
The model retains the encoder-decoder structure of the U-Net to process entire drive sequences efficiently (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018) but replaces standard convolutional layers with **1D Residual Blocks**.
*   **Residual Encoder:** A series of downsampling stages. Each stage consists of a Residual Block (two 1D convolutions with a skip connection) followed by a Max Pooling layer. This allows the network to learn deeper, more complex representations of the signal error dynamics without suffering from vanishing gradients.
*   **Bottleneck:** A stack of Residual Blocks with **dilated convolutions**. This expands the receptive field to capture long-term dependencies (e.g., atmospheric drifts) across the entire drive sequence.
*   **Residual Decoder:** A series of upsampling stages. Features from the encoder are concatenated via skip connections (preserving high-frequency local signal details) and processed by Residual Blocks to refine the localization.
*   **Output Head:** A final projection layer outputs the 2D residual vector $(\Delta North, \Delta East)$ for every timestamp in the sequence.

### Data
**Preprocessing Pipeline**
1.  **Temporal Quantization:** Align raw GNSS logs with ground truth by rounding all timestamps to the nearest second (1Hz). This prevents data loss caused by minor clock jitter (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016).
2.  **Distribution-Aware Aggregation:** Instead of simple means, aggregate raw satellite features for each timestamp to capture the **signal distribution**:
    *   **Signal Strength (`Cn0DbHz`):** Calculate Mean, Std, **Min, Max** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00020), and **Quantiles** (25th, 50th, 75th). This allows the model to distinguish between a generally weak signal environment and one with specific weak outliers.
    *   **Geometry (`SvElevationDegrees`):** Calculate Mean, **Min** (critical for multipath detection), and Max.
    *   **Metadata:** Include `SatCount` and aggregated `RawPseudorangeUncertaintyMeters` (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
3.  **Translation Invariance:** Explicitly exclude absolute `Latitude` and `Longitude` from the input features to ensure the model learns error correction functions rather than memorizing maps (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
4.  **Target Transformation:** Convert the regression target from Degrees (Lat/Lon) to **Meters (East-North-Up)** relative to the WLS baseline. This ensures physical consistency in the loss function (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** **Mean Absolute Error (MAE)**. This is chosen for its robustness to the heavy-tailed outlier distributions inherent in urban GNSS data (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with weight decay to prevent overfitting.
*   **Validation Strategy:** Use `GroupShuffleSplit` grouped by `drive_id`. This ensures the model is validated on entirely unseen drives, preventing leakage and providing a realistic performance estimate.

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** Feed the preprocessed sequence of aggregated features for a test drive into the trained ResUNet to predict the sequence of Cartesian residuals $(\Delta North, \Delta East)$.
2.  **Reconstruction:** Convert the predicted Cartesian offsets back into angular offsets $(\Delta Lat, \Delta Lon)$ using local WGS84 approximations.
3.  **Correction:** Add these offsets to the provided WLS baseline coordinates to generate the final submission: $Prediction = Baseline_{WLS} + Predicted_{Residual}$.
