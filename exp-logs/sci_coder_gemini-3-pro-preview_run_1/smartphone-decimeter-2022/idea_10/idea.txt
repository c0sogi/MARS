Based on the accumulated lessons, I propose the **Global-Context 1D TransUNet with Boundary-Aware Signal Features**.

This strategy addresses the limitations of the current best model (1D U-Net) by integrating a Transformer at the bottleneck to capture global trajectory biases (e.g., atmospheric drift) which exceed the receptive field of Convolutional Networks, while strictly adhering to the robust feature engineering principles established in previous experiments.

### Model
**Architecture: 1D Hybrid TransUNet**
The model utilizes a U-Net structure for efficient sequence-to-sequence prediction (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018) but replaces the central convolutional bottleneck with a Transformer.
*   **ResNet Encoder:** A series of **1D Residual Blocks** followed by Max Pooling layers. This extracts local, high-frequency features representing immediate signal quality (e.g., passing under a tree).
*   **Transformer Bottleneck (Structural Innovation):** The compressed feature sequence from the encoder is fed into a **Transformer Encoder** with Multi-Head Self-Attention. Unlike CNNs or RNNs, this allows the model to attend to the *entire* drive history simultaneously. It can use high-confidence segments (open sky) to calibrate the bias correction for low-confidence segments (urban canyons) anywhere in the sequence, effectively modeling the slow-varying errors like clock drift.
*   **ResNet Decoder:** Upsampling layers combined with **Skip Connections** from the Encoder. These connections reintroduce the local temporal details lost during downsampling, ensuring precise localization of the corrections.
*   **Output Head:** A final projection layer predicts the 2D residual vector $(\Delta North, \Delta East)$ in meters.

### Data
**Preprocessing Pipeline**
1.  **Temporal Quantization:** Raw GNSS logs and ground truth are aligned by rounding timestamps to the nearest second (1Hz). This prevents data loss caused by exact matching failures due to clock jitter (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016).
2.  **Boundary-Aware Aggregation:** Raw satellite features are aggregated per timestamp using **dense statistical moments**. We explicitly calculate the **Mean, Minimum, and Maximum** for `Cn0DbHz` (Signal Strength) and `SvElevationDegrees` (Cite solution_lesson_node_00020). The "Minimum" statistics are critical for identifying epochs corrupted by weak or low-elevation signals. We strictly avoid distributional histograms, which were shown to degrade performance in sparse data regimes (Cite solution_lesson_node_00028).
3.  **Reliability Metadata:** Include `SatCount` and aggregated `RawPseudorangeUncertaintyMeters` to quantify baseline confidence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
4.  **Translation Invariance:** Absolute `Latitude` and `Longitude` are excluded from the input to prevent map-memorization (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
5.  **Target Transformation:** Regression targets are transformed from Degrees (Lat/Lon) to **Meters (East-North)** relative to the WLS baseline to ensure physical consistency (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** **Mean Absolute Error (MAE)**. This is selected for its robustness to the heavy-tailed outlier distributions inherent in urban GNSS data (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with weight decay is used to prevent overfitting in the Transformer layers.
*   **Validation Strategy:** Use `GroupShuffleSplit` grouped by `drive_id` to ensure the model is evaluated on entirely unseen trips, preventing data leakage.

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** The preprocessed feature sequence for a full test drive is fed into the model. The model outputs the sequence of Cartesian residuals $(\Delta North, \Delta East)$.
2.  **Reconstruction:** These Cartesian offsets are converted back into angular offsets $(\Delta Lat, \Delta Lon)$ using local WGS84 approximations.
3.  **Correction:** The predicted offsets are added to the baseline WLS coordinates provided in the test set to generate the final submission coordinates.
