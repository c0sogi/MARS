Based on the lessons learned, particularly the success of sequence-to-sequence processing with U-Nets (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018) and the importance of boundary statistics in feature engineering (Cite solution_lesson_node_00020), I propose a **Context-Aware 1D TransResUNet**.

This strategy structurally evolves the previous U-Net approach by integrating a **Transformer** at the bottleneck to capture global trajectory dependencies (e.g., correcting drift accumulated over a whole drive) and injecting **Device Context** to account for hardware-specific biases.

### Model
The architecture is a **1D Hybrid Transformer-Residual U-Net**.
*   **Encoder (Local Feature Extraction):** A series of **1D Residual Blocks** (ResNet) that progressively downsample the temporal resolution. This efficiently extracts local high-frequency features (e.g., sudden signal noise due to passing under a bridge) while increasing the receptive field.
*   **Bottleneck (Global Context):** Instead of standard convolutions, the bottleneck utilizes a **Transformer Encoder**.
    *   The compressed feature sequence from the encoder is enriched with learnable **Positional Encodings** and a **Device Embedding** (derived from the `phone_name` metadata).
    *   **Self-Attention** layers then process the entire drive's compressed timeline simultaneously. This allows the model to use high-confidence segments (e.g., open sky sections) to calibrate the bias for low-confidence segments (e.g., urban canyons) anywhere in the sequence, overcoming the limited receptive field of pure CNNs.
*   **Decoder (Localization):** A series of upsampling layers. Features from the Transformer bottleneck are combined with high-resolution features from the Encoder via **Skip Connections** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018) to ensure precise temporal localization of the corrections.
*   **Output Head:** A final projection layer predicts the 2D residual vector $(\Delta North, \Delta East)$ in meters for every timestamp.

### Data
**Preprocessing Pipeline**
1.  **Temporal Quantization:** Align raw GNSS logs with ground truth by rounding timestamps to the nearest second (1Hz). This prevents data loss from exact matching failures (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016).
2.  **Distribution-Aware Aggregation:** Aggregate raw satellite measurements per timestamp to create a fixed-size feature vector.
    *   Compute **Mean, Max, and Min** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00020) for Signal Strength (`Cn0DbHz`) and Satellite Elevation (`SvElevationDegrees`). The "Min" statistics are crucial for identifying epochs corrupted by weak or low-horizon signals.
    *   Include solution metadata: `SatCount` and aggregated `RawPseudorangeUncertaintyMeters` (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
3.  **Translation Invariance:** Explicitly exclude absolute `Latitude` and `Longitude` coordinates from the input to prevent overfitting to training maps (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
4.  **Target Transformation:** Convert the regression target from Degrees (Latitude/Longitude) to **Meters (East-North-Up)** relative to the WLS baseline. This ensures the loss function operates in a physically consistent metric space (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).
5.  **Device Indexing:** Map each unique `phone_name` string to an integer index for the Device Embedding layer.

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** **Mean Absolute Error (MAE)**. This is selected for its robustness to the heavy-tailed outlier distributions inherent in urban GNSS data (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with weight decay to prevent overfitting, particularly in the Transformer layers.
*   **Validation Strategy:** Use `GroupShuffleSplit` grouped by `drive_id`. This ensures the model is validated on entirely unseen trips, preventing leakage.

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** Feed the preprocessed feature sequence of a full test drive into the model. The model outputs the sequence of Cartesian residuals $(\Delta North, \Delta East)$.
2.  **Reconstruction:** Convert the predicted Cartesian offsets back into angular offsets $(\Delta Lat, \Delta Lon)$ using local WGS84 approximations.
3.  **Correction:** Add these predicted offsets to the baseline WLS coordinates provided in the test set to generate the final submission.
