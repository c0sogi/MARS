Based on the comprehensive analysis of the provided lessons and the limitations of previous attempts, I propose the **Fisher-Embedded Bayesian Gaussian Process (FE-BGP)**.

### Model
The solution implements a sequential **"Filter-then-Refine" Pipeline** that fundamentally shifts the role of Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) from a final classifier to a supervised feature extractor, followed by a probabilistic Bayesian classifier.

1.  **Backbone (Supervised Manifold Learning):** A **Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA)** model is deployed strictly as a dimensionality reduction transformer. It projects the 192-dimensional input space into a compact $(C-1)$-dimensional "Fisher Subspace" (98 dimensions) that maximizes the ratio of between-class variance to within-class variance.
    *   *Rationale:* This step effectively compresses the signal and filters out redundant/noisy features identified in early analysis [Cite solution_lesson_node_00001], while preserving the "near-perfect" global linear separability established as the dominant signal [Cite solution_lesson_node_00006].
2.  **Head (Bayesian Calibration):** A **Gaussian Process Classifier (GPC)** is trained on the projected Fisher features.
    *   **Kernel:** A **DotProduct (Linear) Kernel** combined with a **WhiteKernel (Noise)**.
    *   *Rationale:* The Linear Kernel respects the linear correlation structure preserved by the LDA projection, avoiding the distortion caused by non-parametric approaches [Cite solution_lesson_node_00010]. The GPC introduces a Bayesian framework that integrates over parameter uncertainty. Unlike the rigid point-estimates of standard LDA, the GPC with a WhiteKernel explicitly models global noise and aleatoric uncertainty, providing naturally calibrated probabilities. This acts as a "Safety Net" against the overconfidence that leads to infinite log-loss penalties, without the data fragmentation risks of bagging [Cite solution_lesson_node_00011] or hierarchy [Cite solution_lesson_node_00015].

### Data
1.  **Input Source:** The solution will exclusively use the 192 pre-extracted tabular features (`margin`, `shape`, `texture`), ignoring the raw images to prevent overfitting [Cite solution_lesson_node_00001].
2.  **Preprocessing Pipeline:**
    *   **Power Transformation:** Apply the **Yeo-Johnson** transformation to all features. This is strictly retained to enforce the multivariate Gaussian distribution that the LDA backbone relies upon for optimal separation [Cite solution_lesson_node_00006].
    *   **Scaling:** Apply **Standard Scaling** (zero mean, unit variance) to ensure numerical stability for both the LDA projection and the GPC kernel computations. To maximize precision, we disable the internal standardization of the PowerTransformer and rely on the dedicated StandardScaler [Cite solution_lesson_node_00025].
    *   **Exclusion:** Rank-based transformers (like QuantileTransformer) are explicitly rejected to preserve the linear correlation structure [Cite solution_lesson_node_00010].

### Training
1.  **Global Training:** The entire pipeline is fitted on the full training dataset ($N=712$). We explicitly reject data splitting strategies like Bagging [Cite solution_lesson_node_00011] or Taxonomy-based Hierarchy [Cite solution_lesson_node_00015], as maximizing the sample size is critical for robust covariance estimation in small-$N$ regimes.
2.  **Procedure:**
    *   The **LDA** component (with **Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage** [Cite solution_lesson_node_00005]) computes the projection matrix $W$ based on the full data.
    *   The **GPC** component optimizes the kernel hyperparameters (sigma and noise level) by maximizing the Log Marginal Likelihood of the projected data. This "Intrinsic Calibration" avoids the need for a separate validation hold-out, preserving all data for training.

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** Test samples are passed through the transformation pipeline (Yeo-Johnson $\rightarrow$ Scaler $\rightarrow$ LDA Projection).
2.  **Prediction:** The projected samples are classified by the GPC. The probabilistic output (integrals over the latent Gaussian) is used directly.
3.  **Robustness:** By using the GPC's calibrated probabilities, the solution mitigates the risk of the "perfect" LDA model making overconfident predictions on unseen test data (covariate shift), thereby minimizing the multi-class log loss.
