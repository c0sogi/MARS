Based on the progression of experiments and the specific lessons learned, I propose a **Fisher-Embedded Bayesian Gaussian Process**.

This strategy synthesizes the "near-perfect" feature engineering of the Power-Transformed LDA [Cite solution_lesson_node_00006] with a Bayesian inference layer. It addresses the potential brittleness of point-estimate linear models on small datasets by introducing a probabilistic "Safety Net" that does not rely on data fragmentation or unstable bagging.

### Model
The architecture is a sequential **"Filter-then-Refine" Pipeline** that fundamentally changes the role of Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) from a final classifier to a supervised feature extractor.

1.  **Backbone (Supervised Manifold Learning):** A **Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA)** model is deployed as a dimensionality reduction transformer. It projects the 192-dimensional input space into a compact $(C-1)$-dimensional "Fisher Subspace" (98 dimensions) that maximizes the ratio of between-class variance to within-class variance.
    *   *Rationale:* This step effectively compresses the signal and filters out the redundant/noisy features identified in previous iterations [Cite solution_lesson_node_00001], while preserving the global linear separability established as the dominant signal [Cite solution_lesson_node_00006].
2.  **Head (Bayesian Classification):** A **Gaussian Process Classifier (GPC)** is trained on the projected Fisher features. Unlike the rigid linear decision boundaries of standard LDA, the GPC employs a kernel-based approach (using an **Anisotropic RBF Kernel** + **White Noise Kernel**) to model the class probabilities.
    *   *Rationale:* This introduces a Bayesian framework that integrates over parameter uncertainty, providing naturally calibrated probabilities. This is superior to Bagging for stable estimators [Cite solution_lesson_node_00011] and avoids the data sparsity issues of Hierarchical models [Cite solution_lesson_node_00015].

### Data
1.  **Input Source:** The solution will exclusively use the 192 pre-extracted tabular features (`margin`, `shape`, `texture`), ignoring the raw images to prevent overfitting [Cite solution_lesson_node_00001].
2.  **Preprocessing Pipeline:**
    *   **Power Transformation:** Apply the **Yeo-Johnson** transformation to all features. This is strictly retained to enforce the multivariate Gaussian distribution that the LDA backbone relies upon [Cite solution_lesson_node_00006].
    *   **Scaling:** Apply **Standard Scaling** (zero mean, unit variance) to ensure numerical stability for both the LDA projection and the GPC kernel computations.
    *   **Exclusion:** Rank-based transformers (like QuantileTransformer) are explicitly rejected to preserve the linear correlation structure [Cite solution_lesson_node_00010].

### Training
1.  **Global Training:** The entire pipeline is fitted on the full training dataset ($N=712$) without splitting the data into genus-specific subsets, maximizing the statistical power for covariance estimation [Cite solution_lesson_node_00015].
2.  **Procedure:**
    *   The **LDA** component computes the shared covariance matrix and class means to define the projection matrix $W$.
    *   The **GPC** component optimizes the kernel hyperparameters (length-scale and noise level) by maximizing the Log Marginal Likelihood of the projected data.
3.  **Hybrid Ensemble:** To ensure robustness, the final probability output will be a weighted average ($0.5 \times P_{LDA} + 0.5 \times P_{GPC}$), where $P_{LDA}$ is the probability from the standard LDA classifier and $P_{GPC}$ is from the Gaussian Process. This retains the proven baseline of the generative LDA [Cite solution_lesson_node_00005] while adding the discriminative refinement of the GPC.

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** Test samples are passed through the transformation pipeline (Yeo-Johnson $\rightarrow$ Scaler $\rightarrow$ LDA Projection).
2.  **Prediction:** The projected samples are classified by the GPC (and the parallel LDA path).
3.  **Aggregation:** The final probabilities are averaged and submitted. This approach ensures that the linear separation found by LDA is preserved, while the GPC provides better calibration for edge cases or outliers in the test set.
