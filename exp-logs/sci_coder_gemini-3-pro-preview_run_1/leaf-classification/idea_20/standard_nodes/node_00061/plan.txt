2.  **Refine OAS Estimation**: In `library/model.py`, I will switch the `OAS` estimator from `assume_centered=True` to `assume_centered=False`. While the residuals are constructed to be centered, floating-point operations inevitably introduce a negligible non-zero mean (noise). By allowing `OAS` to re-center the data, we remove the squared noise component ($cc^T$) from the covariance matrix. This prevents slight inflation of the covariance, leading to a more precise inverse (precision matrix) and sharper probability estimates, which is crucial for minimizing log loss at the $10^{-9}$ level (Cite Lesson 00055).
3.  **Explicit Precision**: I will explicitly cast the length variables to `float` during prior calculation to ensure consistent `float64` behavior.
