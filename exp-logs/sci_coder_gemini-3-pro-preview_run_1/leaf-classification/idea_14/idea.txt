Based on the progression of lessons learned—specifically the dominance of the Gaussian-assumption Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) [Cite solution_lesson_node_00006] and the sensitivity of the covariance estimator to small sample sizes [Cite solution_lesson_node_00015] and numerical noise [Cite solution_lesson_node_00042]—I propose the **Iterative Gaussian-OAS Discriminant**.

This strategy introduces two structural innovations: **Iterative Gaussianization** (Data) to enforce a stricter Multivariate Normal distribution than simple marginal transformations, and **Oracle Approximating Shrinkage** (Model) to replace the Ledoit-Wolf estimator with one theoretically optimized for the Gaussian data we are engineering.

### Model
The architecture is a **Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA)** classifier, enhanced with a specialized covariance estimation strategy.
1.  **OAS Covariance Estimation (Innovation):** Instead of the standard Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage used in previous best attempts, we will employ the **Oracle Approximating Shrinkage (OAS)** estimator.
    *   *Rationale:* While Ledoit-Wolf is distribution-free, OAS is derived assuming the underlying data is Gaussian. Since our preprocessing pipeline explicitly enforces Gaussianity, OAS provides a lower Mean Squared Error (MSE) estimate of the covariance matrix in small-sample regimes ($N \ll P$ or small $N$) compared to Ledoit-Wolf. This directly addresses the "Small N" instability identified in Lesson 00015.
2.  **Precision Quantization (Retention):** The entire model pipeline will operate in **`float32`** precision.
    *   *Rationale:* As discovered in Lesson 00042, downcasting from `float64` filters out high-frequency numerical noise generated by power transformations, acting as an implicit regularizer for the solver.

### Data
1.  **Input:** The solution will exclusively use the 192 pre-extracted tabular features (`margin`, `shape`, `texture`).
2.  **Deterministic Ordering (Retention):** We will explicitly hardcode the feature list to prevent implicit permutation by the solver [Cite solution_lesson_node_00029].
3.  **Iterative Gaussianization Pipeline (Innovation):** Instead of a single-pass transformation, we implement a multi-stage "Gaussianization" pipeline to approximate a true Multivariate Normal distribution:
    *   **Stage 1 (Marginal):** Apply **Yeo-Johnson Power Transformation** (`standardize=False`) followed by **Standard Scaling**. This Gaussianizes the marginal distributions of the original features [Cite solution_lesson_node_00025].
    *   **Stage 2 (Rotation):** Apply **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)** with **Whitening**, keeping **all components** (no dimensionality reduction).
        *   *Rationale:* This rotates the data to align with its principal axes and decorrelates the features. Crucially, we retain all dimensions to avoid the signal loss observed in Lesson 00037.
    *   **Stage 3 (Refinement):** Apply a second **Yeo-Johnson Power Transformation** (`standardize=False`) followed by **Standard Scaling** on the whitened components.
        *   *Rationale:* Linear rotation (PCA) does not guarantee that the new axes are Gaussian if the original dependencies were non-linear. This second pass Gaussianizes the uncorrelated components, enforcing a stronger approximation of joint Multivariate Normality, which is the core assumption required for the OAS-LDA to achieve optimal separation.

### Training
1.  **Inductive Training (Retention):** The pipeline is fitted strictly on the training data. We explicitly reject Transductive approaches that include the test set in the transformation fit, as this was shown to degrade probabilistic calibration [Cite solution_lesson_node_00041].
2.  **Solver:** Use the **`eigen`** solver for the LDA step to ensure bit-wise deterministic and precise probability estimates [Cite solution_lesson_node_00038].
3.  **Priors:** Use **Empirical Priors** (inferred from class frequencies). We reject Uniform Priors as the stratified nature of the data makes the training distribution a valid predictor of the test distribution [Cite solution_lesson_node_00033].

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** The test data is passed through the fixed Iterative Gaussianization pipeline (YJ $\rightarrow$ Scaler $\rightarrow$ PCA $\rightarrow$ YJ $\rightarrow$ Scaler) and then classified by the OAS-LDA model.
2.  **Output:** The predicted probabilities are formatted directly for submission, leveraging the enhanced calibration from the OAS estimator and the noise-filtering properties of `float32`.
