Based on the progression of experiments and the lessons learned—specifically the "near-perfect" performance of Power-Transformed LDA [Cite solution_lesson_node_00006] and the failure of sample-based bagging [Cite solution_lesson_node_00011]—I propose a **Feature-Bagged Power-LDA Ensemble**.

This strategy introduces **Random Subspace Ensembling (Feature Bagging)** as a structural innovation. While previous iterations proved that *sample* bagging degrades performance by reducing the effective sample size [Cite solution_lesson_node_00011], *feature* bagging improves robustness against specific noisy features (like texture [Cite solution_lesson_node_00001]) without sacrificing the statistical power required for covariance estimation [Cite solution_lesson_node_00015].

### Model
The architecture is a **Homogeneous Ensemble** of **Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA)** estimators, structured to maximize robustness while preserving the strong linear signal identified in previous steps.
*   **Base Estimator:** LDA with **automatic shrinkage** (Ledoit-Wolf lemma). Shrinkage is critical to stabilize the covariance inverse in high-dimensional spaces [Cite solution_lesson_node_00005].
*   **Ensemble Mechanism (Innovation):** Instead of bootstrapping samples, we employ **Random Subspace Sampling**. We will instantiate $B$ (e.g., 20) independent LDA models. Each model will be trained on the **full set of samples** ($N=100\%$) but restricted to a **random subset of features** (e.g., $90\%$).
    *   *Rationale:* This decorrelates the errors of the individual estimators by forcing them to rely on different subsets of the signal (Shape vs. Margin vs. Texture). It specifically mitigates the impact of "noisy" features identified in early analysis [Cite solution_lesson_node_00001] while avoiding the data fragmentation that ruined the Hierarchical model [Cite solution_lesson_node_00015].

### Data
1.  **Input:** The solution will use the 192 extracted tabular features (`margin`, `shape`, `texture`).
2.  **Global Preprocessing:**
    *   **Power Transformation:** Apply the **Yeo-Johnson** transformation to all 192 features. This is strictly retained to enforce the multivariate Gaussian distribution that allows LDA to achieve near-perfect separation [Cite solution_lesson_node_00006].
    *   **Scaling:** Apply **Standard Scaling** (zero mean, unit variance) to ensure numerical stability.
    *   **Note:** We explicitly reject rank-based transformers (like QuantileTransformer) to preserve the linear correlation structure required by LDA [Cite solution_lesson_node_00010].
3.  **Feature Subsampling:** During the training of each ensemble member, a random mask is applied to select a subset of the transformed feature columns.

### Training
1.  **Full-Sample Training:** Every estimator in the ensemble is trained on the **complete training dataset** ($N=712$). This strictly adheres to the lesson that reducing sample size (via bagging or splitting) degrades covariance estimation in small-$N$ regimes [Cite solution_lesson_node_00011, solution_lesson_node_00015].
2.  **Diversity Injection:** Diversity is achieved solely through the random feature masks. For $B=20$ models, each will learn a decision boundary based on a slightly different view of the high-dimensional space.
3.  **Optimization:** No weight optimization is performed. The ensemble relies on the "Wisdom of Crowds" principle where the simple average of stable estimators outperforms complex weighting schemes [Cite solution_lesson_node_00008].

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** For each test sample, the full feature vector is transformed (Yeo-Johnson + Scaling). Then, it is passed to each of the $B$ LDA models (applying the corresponding feature mask for each).
2.  **Aggregation:** The final prediction is the **arithmetic mean** of the probability vectors from all $B$ models.
    *   *Benefit:* This averaging acts as a powerful regularizer for the Log Loss metric. By smoothing the potentially overconfident predictions of a single "perfect" LDA model [Cite solution_lesson_node_00006], it provides better calibration and protection against feature shift (e.g., texture variations) in the test set.
