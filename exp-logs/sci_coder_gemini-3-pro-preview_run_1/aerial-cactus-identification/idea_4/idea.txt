Based on the comprehensive analysis of previous attempts and the extracted lessons, I propose a solution that evolves the architecture from a traditional ResNet to a modern **ConvNeXt-inspired** design. This approach addresses the "dated" components of the baseline ResNet (Idea 1) while avoiding the computational inefficiency of the DenseNet (Idea 2) and Equivariant (Idea 3) approaches.

# Model
I propose a **Custom Shallow ConvNeXt**. This architecture modernizes the CNN backbone by adopting design principles from Vision Transformers (ViT) while retaining the inductive bias of convolutions.

*   **Structural Innovation (Modernized Blocks):** I will replace the standard ResNet "BasicBlocks" (Conv-BN-ReLU) with **ConvNeXt Blocks**. Each block will utilize:
    *   **Depthwise Convolutions:** To efficiently capture spatial features with lower parameter counts than standard convolutions.
    *   **Inverted Bottlenecks:** Expanding the channel dimension (e.g., 4x expansion) inside the block to learn richer feature representations before projecting back.
    *   **Modern Components:** Replacing Batch Normalization with **Layer Normalization** and ReLU with **GELU** activations, which have been shown to stabilize training and improve performance in modern vision tasks.
*   **Strategic Retention (Conservative Downsampling):** Crucially, I will retain the **Conservative Downsampling** strategy. The "stem" will remain a single 3x3 convolution with stride 1 (no pooling), ensuring the 32x32 spatial resolution is preserved entering the first stage. This explicitly avoids the signal destruction caused by standard patchify stems or aggressive pooling **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00001)**.
*   **Efficiency:** By using depthwise convolutions, this model aims to be more computationally efficient than the DenseNet (Idea 2) and Equivariant (Idea 3) models, addressing the "slowness" weakness identified in those iterations.

# Data
*   **Preprocessing:** Images will be normalized using the dataset's channel-wise mean and standard deviation.
*   **Augmentation:**
    *   **Geometric:** The pipeline will utilize **RandomHorizontalFlip** and **RandomVerticalFlip**. This exploits the rotational invariance of the aerial imagery without the computational cost of hard-coded equivariant layers **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00002, solution_lesson_node_00010)**.
    *   **Regularization:** I will apply **Mild Mixup Regularization** (Beta distribution $\alpha=0.2$). This specific intensity is chosen to close the generalization gap and achieve perfect classification without drowning out the signal, as observed when comparing against aggressive Mixup ($\alpha=1.0$) **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00004, solution_lesson_node_00006)**.

# Training
*   **Loss Function:** **Binary Cross-Entropy with Logits** will be used to handle the soft, continuous targets generated by Mixup.
*   **Optimizer:** The **AdamW** optimizer will be employed for its effective weight decay handling **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00002)**.
*   **Scheduler:** A **Cosine Annealing** scheduler will be used to gradually decay the learning rate.
*   **Duration:** The training will run for **30 epochs**. This duration is strictly maintained to accommodate the convergence requirements of Mixup and to ensure the Cosine Annealing schedule follows a sufficiently gradual decay curve, preventing the performance regression seen with shorter schedules **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00003)**.

# Evaluation
*   **Inference Strategy:** To maximize robustness and ensure the "perfect" score is reproducible, I will utilize **4-view Test Time Augmentation (TTA)**. The final prediction will be the average of the probabilities for the original image, a horizontal flip, a vertical flip, and a 180-degree rotation. This leverages geometric symmetries at inference time to marginalize out noise **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00002)**.
