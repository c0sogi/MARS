# Proposed Solution: Cascaded ROI-Sequence Network

### Model
The solution introduces a **Three-Stage Architecture** that evolves the previous Region-of-Interest (ROI) approach by replacing the brittle Max-Pooling aggregation with a learnable sequence model. This structure is designed to capture the 3D nature of fractures while maintaining high 2D resolution.

1.  **Stage 1: Spine Localizer (2D U-Net):**
    A lightweight segmentation network (U-Net) operates on downsampled axial slices to predict the center-of-mass coordinates of the cervical spine. This serves as an attention mechanism to filter out irrelevant anatomy.

2.  **Stage 2: High-Resolution Slice Encoder (2.5D CNN):**
    A robust 2D backbone (e.g., EfficientNet-V2 or ResNet50) acts as a feature extractor. It accepts **high-resolution crops** (e.g., 256x256) extracted from the original image centered on the spine coordinates. The input is a 2.5D stack (3 consecutive slices) to capture immediate Z-axis context. This component outputs a dense feature vector for each slice.

3.  **Stage 3: Sequence Aggregator (Bidirectional LSTM/GRU):**
    This is the primary structural innovation. Instead of independently classifying slices and aggregating via Max Pooling, this stage treats the scan as a sequence. A Bidirectional LSTM (or GRU) processes the sequence of feature vectors generated by Stage 2. It learns the temporal (Z-axis) consistency of fractures—distinguishing between persistent fracture signals (spanning multiple slices) and transient noise artifacts—and outputs the final probabilities for all 8 targets (C1-C7 and `patient_overall`).

### Data
1.  **Preprocessing:**
    *   **Windowing:** Apply a standard "bone window" (Center: 400, Width: 1800) to all DICOM pixel arrays.
    *   **ROI Extraction:** Use the Stage 1 Localizer to generate coordinates for every slice. Extract crops centered on these coordinates from the **original, full-resolution** (512x512) images. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**
    *   **Sequence Construction:** For Stage 3, organize the extracted crops into ordered sequences corresponding to the patient's scan (from top to bottom).

2.  **Labeling:**
    *   **Slice-Level:** Use `train_bounding_boxes.csv` to generate binary targets for Stage 2 training (1 if slice is in bbox, 0 otherwise).
    *   **Patient-Level:** Use `train.csv` targets for Stage 3 training.

### Training
The training procedure is divided into three distinct phases to ensure stability and metric alignment:

1.  **Phase 1: Localizer Training:**
    Train the 2D U-Net on the provided segmentation subset to minimize Dice Loss.

2.  **Phase 2: Encoder Pre-training (Slice Level):**
    Train the Stage 2 CNN on the labeled slice subset. Crucially, use **Unweighted Binary Cross Entropy (BCE)** loss. By ignoring the competition's heavy metric weights at this granular level, the encoder is forced to be conservative and precise, learning to detect only clear fracture features without being biased toward high-recall/low-precision behavior. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**

3.  **Phase 3: Aggregator Training (Patient Level):**
    Freeze the Stage 2 Encoder and use it to pre-compute feature sequences for the entire training set. Train the Stage 3 Sequence Aggregator on these features. In this phase, use the **Weighted Multi-label Logarithmic Loss** defined in the competition metric. Since the RNN aggregates information across the whole volume, it can safely optimize the weighted metric (prioritizing `patient_overall`) without the risk of amplifying single-slice noise inherent to Max Pooling.

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference Pipeline:**
    *   Pass the test scan through the Stage 1 Localizer to get spine coordinates.
    *   Extract high-resolution 2.5D crops for every slice.
    *   Pass crops through the Stage 2 Encoder to generate a sequence of feature vectors.
    *   Pass the feature sequence through the Stage 3 Aggregator to predict the final 8 probabilities for the study.

2.  **Optimization:**
    *   This approach avoids the "low-pass filter" effect of resizing by processing crops **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00002)** and mitigates the "noise amplification" of Max Pooling by using a sequence model trained on the weighted metric **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00005)**.
