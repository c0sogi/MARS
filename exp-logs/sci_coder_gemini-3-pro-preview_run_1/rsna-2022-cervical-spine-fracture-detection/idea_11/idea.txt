Based on the lessons learned and the analysis of previous attempts, I propose the **Dual-Stream Anatomically-Indexed Recurrent Network (DS-AIRN)**. This solution structurally evolves the "Dual-Stream" and "Hierarchical" concepts by explicitly separating local fine-grained detection from global alignment context, and aggregating them via a robust, anatomically-guided sequence model.

# Model

The architecture is a three-stage cascade designed to reconcile the need for high-resolution detail (hairline fractures) with global context (dislocations), while enforcing anatomical consistency.

1.  **Stage 1: Multi-Class Anatomical Localizer (2D U-Net)**
    *   **Purpose:** To provide spatial localization and anatomical indexing.
    *   **Architecture:** A lightweight 2D U-Net trained on the segmentation subset.
    *   **Outputs:** For every slice, it generates:
        1.  **ROI Coordinates:** Center-of-mass of the spinal column for cropping.
        2.  **Binary Bone Mask:** A merged mask of all vertebrae to guide the local encoder.
        3.  **Anatomical Probability Profile:** A probability vector ($P_{C1}, ..., P_{C7}$) indicating the likelihood of each vertebra being present in the slice.

2.  **Stage 2: Dual-Stream Feature Encoder**
    *   **Innovation:** A dual-branch CNN that processes visual information at two distinct scales, directly addressing the need for both detail and context **Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**.
    *   **Branch A (Local Stream):** Accepts **High-Resolution Crops** (e.g., 256x256) extracted from the *original* 512x512 images centered on the spine. It includes the Binary Bone Mask as a 4th channel. This branch preserves the high-frequency spatial frequencies required to detect hairline fractures **Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**.
    *   **Branch B (Global Stream):** Accepts downsampled full-field-of-view slices (e.g., 256x256). This branch captures gross anatomical context, such as spinal alignment and subluxations, which are lost in centered crops.
    *   **Fusion:** The feature vectors from both branches are concatenated to form a comprehensive **Slice Feature Vector**.

3.  **Stage 3: Anatomically-Indexed Recurrent Aggregator (Bi-GRU)**
    *   **Sequence Modeling:** A **Bidirectional GRU** processes the sequence of Slice Feature Vectors for the entire patient. We select a GRU over a Transformer to leverage its inductive bias for sequential data on this small dataset **Cite solution_lesson_node_00009**.
    *   **Soft Anatomical Pooling:** Instead of a single global pool, the model generates 7 distinct **Vertebra Context Vectors** ($V_{C1}, ..., V_{C7}$).
        *   The pooling mechanism computes a weighted average of the GRU hidden states. The weights are derived from a learned attention score **plus** the log-probability of the **Anatomical Probability Profile** from Stage 1.
        *   This "Soft Guidance" ensures that $V_{C1}$ aggregates features specifically from slices physically identified as C1, while allowing the network to refine the exact boundaries based on visual context.
    *   **Hierarchical Prediction Heads:**
        *   **Vertebrae Heads:** 7 independent classifiers predict C1-C7 probabilities using their respective vectors $V_{Ck}$.
        *   **Patient Head:** A separate classifier predicts `patient_overall` using the **concatenation** of all context vectors ($[V_{C1}, ..., V_{C7}]$). This explicitly models the logical dependency of the metric **Cite solution_lesson_node_00008**.

# Data

1.  **Preprocessing:**
    *   **Windowing:** Apply a standard "bone window" (Center: 400, Width: 1800) to all DICOM pixel arrays.
    *   **Stage 1 Inference:** Run the trained U-Net on the entire dataset (train and test) to generate ROI coordinates, Masks, and Anatomical Profiles.
    *   **Dual-Resolution Extraction:**
        *   For the **Local Stream**, extract crops from the **original full-resolution** (512x512) images. **Do not downsample the source image** before cropping **Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**.
        *   For the **Global Stream**, resize the full slice to 256x256.

2.  **Labeling:**
    *   **Stage 2 Targets:** Use `train_bounding_boxes.csv` to generate binary slice-level targets (Fracture vs. Background) for the subset of data where they exist.
    *   **Stage 3 Targets:** Use `train.csv` for patient-level multi-label targets.

# Training

The training strategy is divided into three phases to decouple feature learning from metric optimization, preventing noise amplification.

1.  **Phase 1 (Localizer):** Train the Stage 1 U-Net on the segmentation subset using Dice Loss + Cross Entropy.

2.  **Phase 2 (Encoder):** Train the Stage 2 Dual-Stream CNN on the slice-level bounding box targets.
    *   **Loss Function:** Use **Unweighted Binary Cross Entropy (BCE)**. We explicitly **do not** apply the competition's heavy `patient_overall` weights here. This forces the encoder to be conservative and precise, learning to detect fractures based on visual evidence rather than class bias **Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**.

3.  **Phase 3 (Aggregator):** Freeze the Stage 2 Encoder. Pre-compute feature sequences for all training patients. Train the Stage 3 Bi-GRU on these sequences.
    *   **Loss Function:** Use the **Weighted Multi-label Logarithmic Loss**. Since the RNN aggregates evidence across the volume and uses Anatomical Pooling to route signals, it can safely optimize the weighted metric without the risk of amplifying single-slice noise inherent to Max Pooling **Cite solution_lesson_node_00008**.

# Evaluation

1.  **Inference Pipeline:**
    *   **Step 1:** Pass the test scan through Stage 1 to obtain ROI coordinates, Masks, and Anatomical Profiles.
    *   **Step 2:** Extract High-Res Crops (Local) and Downsampled Slices (Global).
    *   **Step 3:** Pass inputs through the Stage 2 Encoder to generate a sequence of fused features.
    *   **Step 4:** Pass the sequence (concatenated with Anatomical Profiles) to the Stage 3 Bi-GRU.
    *   **Step 5:** The Bi-GRU generates Vertebra Context Vectors via Soft Anatomical Pooling.
    *   **Step 6:** The Hierarchical Heads output the final probabilities for C1-C7 and `patient_overall`.
