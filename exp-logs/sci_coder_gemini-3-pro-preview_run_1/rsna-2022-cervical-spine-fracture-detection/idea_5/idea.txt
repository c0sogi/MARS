Based on the provided lessons and previous ideas, I propose the **Anatomically-Guided Transformer Pipeline**. This solution evolves the "Cascaded ROI" concept by replacing the generic temporal aggregation (RNN) with an attention-based mechanism that explicitly leverages anatomical segmentation to route features, ensuring that the model understands *where* in the spine it is looking, not just *when* in the sequence.

# Model

The architecture is a three-stage pipeline designed to maximize resolution, precision, and anatomical awareness.

1.  **Stage 1: Anatomical Localizer & Segmentor (2D U-Net)**
    A lightweight U-Net operates on downsampled slices. Unlike previous iterations that only predicted a center coordinate, this model is trained to perform **multi-class segmentation** (Background, C1, C2, ..., C7).
    *   **Purpose:** It provides two critical outputs: (1) The center-of-mass for cropping, and (2) An **Anatomical ID** for each slice (e.g., "This slice contains C2"), which serves as a semantic positional tag.

2.  **Stage 2: Mask-Conditioned Focus Encoder (2.5D CNN)**
    A robust 2D CNN (e.g., EfficientNet-V2) extracts features from the image data.
    *   **Input:** A **high-resolution crop** (e.g., 256x256) extracted from the original 512x512 image centered on the spine **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00002)**. The input consists of 4 channels: 3 consecutive CT slices (RGB) and a **Binary Bone Mask** (Alpha) derived from the Stage 1 segmentation.
    *   **Function:** The mask channel forces the encoder to focus strictly on the osseous structure, suppressing soft-tissue noise.

3.  **Stage 3: Anatomically-Embedded Transformer Aggregator**
    This is the primary structural innovation. Instead of a Bi-GRU (which assumes a fixed temporal progression) or Max Pooling (which is brittle), this stage uses a **Transformer Encoder**.
    *   **Anatomical Positional Encoding:** Standard Transformers use relative (1, 2, 3...) positional encodings. Here, we inject the **Anatomical ID** from Stage 1. The feature vector for a slice is combined with a learnable embedding representing its specific vertebra (e.g., Embedding_C1).
    *   **Mechanism:** This allows the Transformer to "attend" to all slices identified as C1 to predict the C1 label, regardless of where they appear in the z-stack or how many slices cover that vertebra. It effectively solves the assignment problem ("Which slices are C1?") and the detection problem ("Is C1 fractured?") simultaneously.

# Data

1.  **Preprocessing:**
    *   **Windowing:** Apply standard "bone window" (Center: 400, Width: 1800).
    *   **High-Resolution Cropping:** Extract fixed-size crops from the **original** 512x512 images based on Stage 1 coordinates. Do not downsample the global image **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00002)**.
    *   **Sequence Construction:** Assemble the extracted feature vectors into a patient-level sequence, paired with their Stage 1 Anatomical IDs.

2.  **Labeling:**
    *   **Slice-Level:** Use `train_bounding_boxes.csv` for Stage 2 targets.
    *   **Patient-Level:** Use `train.csv` for Stage 3 targets.

# Training

The training strategy decouples the feature learning from the metric optimization to prevent noise amplification **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00005)**.

1.  **Phase 1 (Localizer):** Train the U-Net on the segmentation subset using Dice Loss.

2.  **Phase 2 (Encoder):** Train the Stage 2 CNN on the slice-level bounding box targets.
    *   **Loss Function:** Use **Unweighted Binary Cross Entropy (BCE)**. We explicitly **avoid** applying the competition's heavy `patient_overall` weights here. This forces the encoder to be conservative and precise, learning to detect fractures based on visual evidence rather than class bias **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00005)**.

3.  **Phase 3 (Aggregator):** Freeze the Stage 2 Encoder and pre-compute features for all patients. Train the Stage 3 Transformer on these sequences.
    *   **Loss Function:** Use the **Weighted Multi-label Logarithmic Loss**. Since the Transformer aggregates evidence across the entire volume, it can safely optimize the weighted metric (prioritizing `patient_overall`) without the risk of amplifying single-slice noise inherent to Max Pooling **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00008)**.

# Evaluation

1.  **Inference Pipeline:**
    *   Run Stage 1 to get spine coordinates and Anatomical IDs (C1-C7) for every slice.
    *   Extract high-resolution, mask-concatenated crops and pass them through the Stage 2 Encoder.
    *   Feed the sequence of features + Anatomical Embeddings into the Stage 3 Transformer.
    *   The Transformer outputs 8 probabilities (C1-C7, `patient_overall`) for the study.
