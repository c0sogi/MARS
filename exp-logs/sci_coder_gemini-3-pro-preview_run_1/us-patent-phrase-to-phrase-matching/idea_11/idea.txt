### 1. Model: DeBERTa-v3-Large Cross-Encoder with Standardized Head
**Cite Lesson ID: solution_lesson_node_00002, solution_lesson_node_00005, solution_lesson_node_00024**

We will utilize the **`microsoft/deberta-v3-large`** backbone within a **Cross-Encoder** architecture. This model's disentangled attention mechanism provides the necessary capacity to resolve fine-grained semantic differences, significantly outperforming Bi-Encoders (**Cite Lesson 00002**).

*   **Structural Innovation (Head Simplification):** We will revert to the **Standard Classification Head** (instantiated via `AutoModelForSequenceClassification`) and explicitly **remove** the Multi-Sample Dropout (MSD) and Weighted Layer Pooling (WLP) modules proposed in previous ideas.
    *   **Rationale:** As identified in **Lesson 00024**, complex regularization techniques like MSD and WLP inherently slow down convergence. Given the computational constraints and the finding that large models converge rapidly on this dataset, a standard head allows the model to reach optimal performance within a short training horizon (3-4 epochs), whereas heavily regularized heads result in underfitting under the same constraints. We rely on the pre-optimized architecture of the standard head for reliability (**Cite Lesson 00005**).

### 2. Data: Hierarchical Context & Native Pair Encoding
**Cite Lesson ID: solution_lesson_node_00001, solution_lesson_node_00010, solution_lesson_node_00022**

*   **Feature Engineering (Hierarchical Context Expansion):** We will evolve the context injection strategy. Instead of using raw codes or simple descriptions, we will parse `input/description.md` to construct a **Full Hierarchical Context String**. This will concatenate the descriptions of the Section, Class, and Subclass (e.g., "ELECTRICITY; ELECTRIC COMMUNICATION TECHNIQUE; TRANSMISSION"). This maximizes the semantic signal available to the model (**Cite Lesson 00001**).
*   **Structural Innovation (Input Construction):** We will strictly implement **Native Pair Encoding** to resolve tokenizer confusion. We will construct two distinct text segments and pass them as separate arguments (`text` and `text_pair`) to the tokenizer:
    *   **Segment A:** `Hierarchical Context + [SEP] + Anchor Phrase`
    *   **Segment B:** `Target Phrase`
    *   This ensures the tokenizer generates the correct `token_type_ids` (or equivalent signals), allowing the DeBERTa attention mechanism to explicitly distinguish the "Context-Conditioned Anchor" from the "Target" candidate (**Cite Lesson 00022**).
*   **Splitting:** We will employ **Stratified Group K-Fold Cross-Validation** (5 folds). We will group by the `anchor` phrase to strictly prevent leakage and stratify by the discrete `score` to maintain class balance (**Cite Lesson 00010**).

### 3. Training: Stabilized Regression with Layer-wise Decay
**Cite Lesson ID: solution_lesson_node_00007, solution_lesson_node_00011, solution_lesson_node_00018, solution_lesson_node_00019**

*   **Loss Function:** We will use **Mean Squared Error (MSE) Loss**. We explicitly reject Binary Cross Entropy (BCE) because MSE aligns geometrically with the Pearson correlation metric (linear dependence), whereas BCE induces non-linear distortions that degrade performance (**Cite Lesson 00018**).
*   **Structural Innovation (Optimization):** To address the instability and "fold collapse" risks of the Large model (which previously required MSD to fix), we will instead rely entirely on **Layer-wise Learning Rate Decay (LLRD)**. We will assign a high learning rate to the classification head and multiplicatively decay the rate (e.g., 0.9x) for each subsequent lower layer. This stabilizes the optimization by preserving the robust pre-trained features of the backbone while allowing the head to adapt, eliminating the need for slower-converging regularization methods (**Cite Lesson 00019**).
*   **Schedule:** We will set the training duration to **4 epochs**. This strikes the optimal balance between the under-training observed at 2 epochs (**Cite Lesson 00024**) and the instability/diminishing returns observed at 5 epochs (**Cite Lesson 00011**).
*   **Efficiency:** We will use **Dynamic Padding** (collating batches to the length of the longest sequence in the batch rather than a fixed max length) and **Mixed Precision (FP16)** to maximize training speed and memory efficiency (**Cite Lesson 00010**).

### 4. Evaluation: Robust Ensemble Inference
**Cite Lesson ID: solution_lesson_node_00010**

*   **Inference:** We will generate predictions for the test set using all 5 models trained during the Stratified Group K-Fold Cross-Validation.
*   **Ensembling:** The final submission score for each pair will be the **arithmetic mean** of the predictions from the 5 folds. This ensemble approach mitigates the variance of individual models and leverages the full diversity of the training data to maximize the Pearson correlation (**Cite Lesson 00010**).
