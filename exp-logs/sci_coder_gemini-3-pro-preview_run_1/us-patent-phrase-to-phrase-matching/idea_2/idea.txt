### 1. Model: DeBERTa-v3-Large Cross-Encoder
**Cite Lesson ID: solution_lesson_node_00002, solution_lesson_node_00005**

We will upgrade the architecture from a lightweight model to **`microsoft/deberta-v3-large`**.
*   **Architecture:** We will utilize the **Cross-Encoder** architecture, where the `anchor` and `target` are processed jointly. As established in **Lesson 00002**, this resolves the information bottleneck of Bi-Encoders by allowing full token-level self-attention between the phrase pairs, which is critical for detecting subtle semantic differences (e.g., antonyms or hypernyms).
*   **Backbone:** The `deberta-v3-large` model utilizes **disentangled attention** and a mask-prediction objective (RTD), which has been shown to significantly outperform RoBERTa/BERT on semantic similarity and entailment tasks.
*   **Head:** We will instantiate the model using **`AutoModelForSequenceClassification`** with `num_labels=1`. As noted in **Lesson 00005**, this ensures we utilize a standardized, pre-initialized classification head (with appropriate pooling and dropout) rather than a manually implemented regression layer.

### 2. Data: Context Injection & Stratified Group K-Fold
**Cite Lesson ID: solution_lesson_node_00001**

*   **Input Formatting:** We will strictly adhere to the **Contextualized Embedding Injection** strategy from **Lesson 00001**. The input sequence will be constructed by concatenating the context (CPC code), anchor, and target with special tokens:
    `[CLS] Context [SEP] Anchor [SEP] Target [SEP]`
    This forces the model to condition the semantic representation of the `anchor` on the `context` (domain) before comparing it to the `target`.
*   **Splitting Strategy (Structural Innovation):** Instead of a simple Train/Val split, we will implement **Stratified Group K-Fold Cross-Validation** (with $K=4$ or $5$).
    *   **Grouping:** We will group by the `anchor` column. This ensures that all pairs sharing the same `anchor` phrase appear in the same fold (either all in training or all in validation). This prevents "anchor leakage," where the model memorizes the characteristics of a specific anchor seen in training to predict its targets in validation.
    *   **Stratification:** We will stratify by the `score` to ensure the label distribution is consistent across folds.

### 3. Training: Robust Regression with Early Stopping
**Cite Lesson ID: solution_lesson_node_00007**

*   **Loss Function:** We will use **Mean Squared Error (MSE) Loss**, treating the problem as a regression task. The model will predict a continuous float which correlates directly with the Pearson metric.
*   **Optimization:**
    *   **Optimizer:** AdamW with a learning rate of roughly 2e-5.
    *   **Scheduler:** Linear decay with a warmup period (e.g., 10% of steps).
    *   **Precision:** FP16 (Mixed Precision) to fit the Large model into the A100 GPU memory.
*   **Convergence:** We will set a high `max_epochs` (e.g., 10-15) but implement **Early Stopping** based on the validation Pearson correlation. As learned in **Lesson 00007**, we must allow the model to train until the metric plateaus rather than stopping at a fixed epoch count, as under-training significantly hampers performance.

### 4. Evaluation: K-Fold Ensemble
**Cite Lesson ID: solution_lesson_node_00002**

*   **Inference:** We will generate predictions for the test set using **all K models** trained during the Cross-Validation phase.
*   **Ensembling:** The final submission score for each pair will be the **arithmetic mean** of the predictions from the K folds. This ensemble approach reduces the variance of the predictions and typically yields a significant performance boost over any single model, leveraging the full diversity of the training data.
