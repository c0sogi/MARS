### 1. Model: DeBERTa-v3-Large with Adversarial Robustness
**Cite Lesson ID: solution_lesson_node_00002, solution_lesson_node_00005**

We will retain the **`microsoft/deberta-v3-large`** backbone within a **Cross-Encoder** architecture, as this has proven significantly superior to Bi-Encoders for capturing fine-grained semantic nuances (**Cite Lesson 00002**).
*   **Architecture:** We will instantiate the model using **`AutoModelForSequenceClassification`** with a single regression output node. This adheres to **Lesson 00005**, utilizing the pre-optimized classification head (including pooling and dropout) rather than a custom, potentially unstable implementation.
*   **Structural Innovation (Training Wrapper):** To address the training instability and "fold collapse" observed in **Lesson 00011**, we will wrap the model training with **Adversarial Weight Perturbation (AWP)**. AWP injects worst-case perturbations into the model's weights during the forward pass and minimizes the loss with respect to these perturbed weights. This explicitly flattens the loss landscape, preventing the model from converging into sharp, non-robust minima that lead to instability on unseen data.

### 2. Data: Semantic Context Expansion & Group Stratification
**Cite Lesson ID: solution_lesson_node_00001, solution_lesson_node_00010**

*   **Semantic Context Injection (Evolution of Lesson 00001):** We will evolve the context injection strategy. Instead of injecting the raw CPC code (e.g., "H04"), which is merely a symbolic identifier, we will parse the provided `input/description.md` file to map these codes to their full natural language descriptions (e.g., "H04" $\rightarrow$ "ELECTRIC COMMUNICATION TECHNIQUE").
    *   **Input Format:** `[CLS] Context Description [SEP] Anchor [SEP] Target [SEP]`
    *   This allows the Cross-Encoder to attend to the *definition* of the domain, providing a richer semantic signal than the code alone.
*   **Stratified Group K-Fold:** We will strictly employ **Stratified Group K-Fold Cross-Validation** (5 folds). We will group by the `anchor` text to prevent leakage (ensuring no anchor appears in both train and val) and stratify by `score` to maintain class balance (**Cite Lesson 00010**).

### 3. Training: Stabilized Short-Horizon Fine-Tuning
**Cite Lesson ID: solution_lesson_node_00007, solution_lesson_node_00011**

*   **Objective:** We will optimize **Mean Squared Error (MSE)** loss.
*   **Optimization Schedule:**
    *   **Duration:** We will limit training to **3-4 epochs**. As identified in **Lesson 00011**, large models converge rapidly on this dataset, and extending the schedule beyond this point yields diminishing returns and increases the risk of instability.
    *   **Precision:** We will use **Mixed Precision (FP16)** and **Gradient Accumulation** to accommodate the large batch sizes required for stable batch normalization statistics on the A100 GPU (**Cite Lesson 00010**).
    *   **AWP Trigger:** AWP will be activated after the first epoch (warmup) to ensure the model has learned a reasonable trajectory before adversarial constraints are applied.

### 4. Evaluation: Robust Ensemble Inference
**Cite Lesson ID: solution_lesson_node_00010**

*   **Inference:** We will generate predictions using all 5 models trained during the Cross-Validation phase.
*   **Ensembling:** The final similarity score will be the **arithmetic mean** of the predictions from all folds. This ensemble approach mitigates the variance of individual models and leverages the full diversity of the training data (**Cite Lesson 00010**).
