To establish a highly efficient baseline, I propose a solution based on **Model 1**, utilizing a distilled transformer architecture formulated as a Question-Answering task. This approach leverages the specific characteristics of the dataset (specifically the high overlap in neutral tweets) to minimize computational overhead while maintaining competitive accuracy.

### Model
The architecture will be a **DistilRoBERTa-based Question Answering model**.
*   **Backbone:** We will use `distilroberta-base`, a lighter and faster version of RoBERTa (6 transformer layers instead of 12), which fits the efficiency requirement.
*   **Head:** A simple linear layer will be added on top of the transformer's hidden states. This layer will output two logits for each token in the sequence: one representing the probability of being the **start** of the selected span, and the other representing the **end**.
*   **Input Structure:** The input will be formatted to condition the extraction on the sentiment. The sequence will be constructed as: `<s> [Sentiment] </s> </s> [Tweet Text] </s>`. This allows the self-attention mechanism to utilize the sentiment token as a query to identify the relevant span in the text.

### Data
*   **Tokenization:** We will use the pre-trained RoBERTa tokenizer (Byte-Level BPE). Crucially, we will generate **offset mappings** during tokenization. These mappings link each token back to its character positions in the original string, which is necessary for extracting the exact substring for the final submission.
*   **Target Creation:** For the training set, we will calculate the start and end token indices that correspond to the `selected_text`.
*   **The "Neutral" Heuristic:** Data analysis shows that for "neutral" tweets, the `selected_text` is nearly identical to the full `text` (Jaccard score ~0.97). To simplify the learning task and reduce training time, we will **filter out all neutral samples from the training set**. The model will only be trained to distinguish spans for "positive" and "negative" sentiments.

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** We will use **Cross Entropy Loss**. The loss will be calculated as the sum of the loss for the start index prediction and the loss for the end index prediction.
*   **Optimizer:** We will use the **AdamW** optimizer with a linear learning rate scheduler (with warmup).
*   **Precision:** We will utilize **Mixed Precision (FP16)** training. This takes advantage of the A100 GPU's tensor cores to significantly speed up training and reduce memory usage.
*   **Strategy:** We will train for a limited number of epochs (e.g., 3-5) on the filtered (positive/negative only) dataset.

### Evaluation
*   **Inference Logic:**
    1.  **Check Sentiment:** For every sample in the test set, we first check the `sentiment` column.
    2.  **Neutral Case:** If the sentiment is "neutral", we bypass the model entirely and predict the full `text` as the answer. This is a zero-cost operation that maximizes the score for this class.
    3.  **Positive/Negative Case:** If the sentiment is non-neutral, we feed the tokenized text into the model. We select the start and end tokens with the highest logits (applying a constraint that `start_index <= end_index`).
*   **Reconstruction:** Using the stored offset mappings, we convert the predicted token span back into the original string. This ensures that the predicted `selected_text` includes the correct punctuation and spacing required by the Jaccard metric.
