Based on **Lesson 2 (Regression Precision Over Classification Confidence)**, the primary driver for improving Mean Average Precision (mAP) in 3D object detection is the geometric accuracy of the bounding boxes (dimensions, depth, and orientation) rather than just the classification confidence. The previous solution used low weights (`0.1`) for regression tasks (`WH`, `Z`, `ROT`), allowing the model to focus predominantly on the heatmap classification loss.

My strategy is to **rebalance the loss function** to prioritize regression accuracy. I will increase the weights for width/height/length, Z-coordinate, and rotation to `1.0`, making them equal in importance to the heatmap and offset losses. This forces the lightweight ResNet-18 backbone (validated as superior for this sparse data in **Lesson 3**) to learn more precise spatial features. To accommodate this more challenging optimization landscape, I will also extend the training duration to 25 epochs and increase the patience for early stopping.
