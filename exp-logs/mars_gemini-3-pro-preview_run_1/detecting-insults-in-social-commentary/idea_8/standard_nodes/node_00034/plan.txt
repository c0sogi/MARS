The issue is a "Gradient Unscaling Mismatch" caused by calling `scaler.unscale_(optimizer)` prematurely inside the Adversarial Weight Perturbation (AWP) block. In the original code, when AWP is active, `unscale_` is called before the second backward pass. This causes the second backward pass to accumulate *scaled* gradients into *unscaled* gradients. Since `scaler` tracks that `unscale_` has already been called, it skips unscaling during `scaler.step()`, resulting in the optimizer applying massive, scaled gradients to the weights, leading to `NaN` values and model divergence.

To fix this, I will modify `library/engine.py` to defer the call to `scaler.unscale_(optimizer)` until **after** all backward passes (both the initial one and the AWP one) are complete. This ensures that the optimizer always receives consistently scaled gradients before the final unscaling and clipping step. Cite debug_lesson_7.
