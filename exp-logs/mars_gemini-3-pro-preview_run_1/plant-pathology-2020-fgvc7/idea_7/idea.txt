Based on the analysis of the previous experiments and the specific lessons learned, I propose a solution that implements **Progressive Resizing** with a **ResNet34** backbone. This strategy introduces a structural innovation in the training pipeline—evolving the input resolution over stages—to combine the generalization benefits of lower-dimensional feature learning with the fine-grained discriminative power of high-resolution imagery.

# Model
I will utilize the **ResNet34** architecture initialized with ImageNet weights.
*   **Rationale:** This backbone is retained as the core architecture because it has consistently outperformed deeper (ResNeXt) and more complex (EfficientNet) models on this specific dataset, offering the optimal balance of capacity and convergence speed (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003, solution_lesson_node_00007, solution_lesson_node_00018).
*   **Head Design:** The classification head will consist of a Global Average Pooling layer followed by a single fully connected layer with 4 outputs. I will explicitly avoid complex heads like Multi-Sample Dropout or GeM Pooling, which were shown to cause underfitting due to over-regularization (Cite solution_lesson_node_00023).

# Data
*   **Preprocessing (Structural Innovation - Progressive Resizing):** I will implement a two-stage data loading strategy.
    *   **Stage 1:** Images will be resized to **256x256** pixels. This resolution allows the model to learn coarse-grained structural features and converges rapidly (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
    *   **Stage 2:** Images will be resized to **512x512** pixels. Fine-tuning on this higher resolution allows the model to resolve small disease artifacts (rust spots, scab lesions) that are lost at 256px, leveraging the original high-resolution data (2048px) more effectively.
*   **Augmentation:** I will employ a robust `albumentations` pipeline including **HorizontalFlip**, **VerticalFlip**, **ShiftScaleRotate**, and **RandomBrightnessContrast**. This maintains the high augmentation intensity required to prevent overfitting, ensuring that the "upgrade" in resolution is supported by sufficient data diversity (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003, solution_lesson_node_00016).
*   **Splitting:** I will use **Stratified 5-Fold Cross-Validation** to ensure robust evaluation and mitigate selection bias (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).

# Training
*   **Loss Function:** I will use **Weighted Cross-Entropy Loss**, with weights inversely proportional to class frequencies. This is preserved to maximize the Macro-Averaged AUC by prioritizing the minority `multiple_diseases` class (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
*   **Optimizer:** The **Adam** optimizer will be used.
*   **Procedure (Progressive Fine-Tuning):**
    1.  **Phase 1 (Coarse):** Train on 5 folds using 256x256 images for 15 epochs with a learning rate of `1e-4`.
    2.  **Phase 2 (Fine):** Load the best weights from Phase 1. Fine-tune on the same folds using **512x512** images for an additional 15 epochs with a reduced learning rate (e.g., `1e-5`). This ensures the model adapts to the new receptive field scale without destroying previously learned features.
*   **Scheduler:** I will use a **Cosine Annealing** scheduler. Crucially, the scheduler will be reset for each phase, and the cycle length (`T_max`) will be strictly synchronized with the number of epochs in that phase (15) to ensure the learning rate decays fully for optimal convergence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015).
*   **Regularization:** I will **exclude** Mixup/CutMix. Given the added complexity of progressive resizing and the risk of underfitting observed previously (Cite solution_lesson_node_00011), standard geometric augmentation is sufficient and safer.

# Evaluation
*   **Resolution:** Inference will be performed strictly at **512x512** pixels, matching the resolution of the final training stage to ensure pipeline consistency (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
*   **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):** Predictions will be generated on the original image and a horizontally flipped version, then averaged. This leverages the model's learned invariance to reduce variance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
*   **Ensemble Aggregation:** The final submission will be the average of the probabilities from the **5 models** (one from each fold) trained through the progressive pipeline.
