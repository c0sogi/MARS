Based on the lessons learned from previous iterations, I propose a **Dual-Architecture Heterogeneous Ensemble** strategy. This approach structurally evolves the pipeline by combining the reliable convergence of ResNet34 with the superior feature extraction capabilities of ResNeXt50, while explicitly correcting the optimization and augmentation errors that caused previous ensemble attempts to fail.

# Model
I will implement a **Heterogeneous Ensemble** comprising two distinct backbone architectures to maximize feature diversity:
1.  **ResNet34:** Retained as the "anchor" model. It has proven to be the most reliable architecture for this small dataset, offering an optimal balance of depth and convergence speed (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
2.  **ResNeXt50-32x4d:** Introduced as the "high-capacity" expert. Unlike standard ResNets, ResNeXt uses grouped convolutions ("cardinality"), allowing it to learn more diverse feature representations. This architecture previously underperformed (Idea 3) only because it was paired with Mixup regularization, which required a longer training budget than available (Cite solution_lesson_node_00011). By removing Mixup, we can leverage its superior capacity within the standard training timeframe.

Both models will use a standard classification head: Global Average Pooling followed by a fully connected layer with 4 outputs.

# Data
-   **Resolution:** All images will be resized to **256x256** pixels for both training and inference. This strictly enforces pipeline consistency to prevent the receptive field mismatches that degraded performance in previous experiments (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
-   **Splitting:** I will use **Stratified 5-Fold Cross-Validation**. This is essential to mitigate the selection bias inherent in single-split validations on this small, heterogeneous dataset (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
-   **Augmentation:**
    -   I will employ the robust `albumentations` pipeline including **ShiftScaleRotate** and **RandomBrightnessContrast** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
    -   **Correction:** I will explicitly ensure **HorizontalFlip** and a wide rotation range are included. Previous ensemble attempts failed because these standard augmentations were weakened, reducing the data manifold (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016).
    -   **Exclusion:** I will **strictly exclude Mixup**. Given the short training budget (~10-15 epochs), we cannot afford the "convergence tax" imposed by strong regularization techniques (Cite solution_lesson_node_00011).

# Training
-   **Loss Function:** I will use **Weighted Cross-Entropy Loss**, with weights inversely proportional to class frequencies. This is critical to maximizing the Macro-Averaged AUC by ensuring the model prioritizes the minority `multiple_diseases` class (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
-   **Optimizer:** The **Adam** optimizer will be used for both architectures.
-   **Scheduler:** I will implement **Cosine Annealing Warm Restarts**.
    -   **Correction:** I will explicitly **synchronize the cycle length (`T_0`) with the total number of epochs**. This ensures the learning rate decays fully to the fine-tuning range within the training budget, correcting the mismatch that caused the previous ensemble to under-converge (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015).
-   **Procedure:** Both architectures will be trained independently on all 5 folds, resulting in 10 total models.

# Evaluation
-   **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):** For every test image, predictions will be generated on the original and a horizontally flipped version, then averaged. This leverages the model's geometric invariance to reduce variance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
-   **Ensemble Aggregation:** The final submission will be the average of the probability outputs from all **10 models** (5 folds $\times$ 2 architectures). This combines the statistical robustness of Stratified K-Fold with the representational power of the ResNeXt architecture, stabilized by the reliable ResNet34.
