To improve upon the previous results and address the identified weaknesses, I propose a solution that structurally evolves the pipeline by adopting a **ResNeXt** architecture and integrating **Mixup Regularization**. This strategy balances increased model capacity with advanced regularization to prevent overfitting on the small dataset.

# Model
I will upgrade the backbone from ResNet34 to **ResNeXt-50-32x4d**.
*   **Architecture:** While ResNets rely on depth, ResNeXt leverages "cardinality" (parallel paths), which allows it to learn a more diverse set of features with similar parameter efficiency. This provides a structural innovation over the previous ResNet backbones while maintaining the stable optimization landscape of the ResNet family, avoiding the convergence issues observed with EfficientNet (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
*   **Head:** The classification head will remain a Global Average Pooling layer followed by a fully connected layer with 4 outputs.

# Data
*   **Preprocessing:** Images will be resized to **256x256** pixels. This resolution will be strictly maintained for both training and inference to ensure pipeline consistency and avoid receptive field mismatches (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
*   **Splitting:** I will employ **Stratified 5-Fold Cross-Validation**. This ensures that the model is evaluated on the entire dataset, providing a robust performance estimate and mitigating the selection bias inherent in single-split validations on small datasets (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
*   **Augmentation:**
    *   **Geometric/Photometric:** I will retain the successful `albumentations` pipeline (ShiftScaleRotate, RandomBrightnessContrast) to generate diverse training samples (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
    *   **Mixup (Innovation):** I will introduce **Mixup** augmentation. This technique trains the model on convex combinations of pairs of images and their labels. By forcing the model to behave linearly in-between training examples, Mixup acts as a strong regularizer, which is critical for leveraging the increased capacity of ResNeXt-50 without overfitting the small dataset.

# Training
*   **Loss Function:** I will use **Cross-Entropy Loss** adapted for soft targets (generated by Mixup). Crucially, I will retain the **Inverse Class Frequency Weighting** strategy. The weights will be applied to the component labels before mixing, ensuring the model continues to prioritize the minority `multiple_diseases` class to maximize the Macro-Averaged AUC (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
*   **Optimization:**
    *   **Optimizer:** The Adam optimizer will be used.
    *   **Scheduler:** I will implement a **Cosine Annealing Warm Restarts** scheduler. Previous experiments demonstrated that a dynamic schedule significantly outperforms a constant learning rate for this task (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008), and the "Warm Restarts" mechanism specifically helps the model escape local minima better than standard decay (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).

# Evaluation
*   **Ensemble:** The final submission will be an ensemble of the **5 models** trained during the K-Fold process.
*   **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):** For each test image, predictions will be generated on the original and a horizontally flipped version, then averaged. This reduces variance and leverages the geometric invariance learned during training.
