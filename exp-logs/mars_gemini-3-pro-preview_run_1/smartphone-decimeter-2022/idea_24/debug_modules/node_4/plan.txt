The issue is that the raw GNSS logs contain invalid WLS baseline coordinates that are numerically close to zero (e.g., `1e-10`) but not exactly `0.0`. The current preprocessing pipeline only filters out exact `0.0` values. These near-zero coordinates place the reference point near the center of the Earth, resulting in massive target distances (~6,371 km) when compared to ground truth. These outliers trigger the sanity check filter (`< 1,000 km`), causing the entire dataset to be discarded.

To fix this, I will modify `library/preprocessing.py` to implement a physical validity check for the WLS coordinates. Specifically, I will calculate the geocentric radius of the WLS position and discard any rows where the radius is less than 6,000 km (Earth's radius is ~6,371 km). This ensures that only physically plausible coordinates are used as baselines, preventing the generation of invalid targets and the subsequent dropping of data. This applies **Debug Lesson 30: Filter Coordinates by Physical Validity, Not Just Sentinel Zeros**.
