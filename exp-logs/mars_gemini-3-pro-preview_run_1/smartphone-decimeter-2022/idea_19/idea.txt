Based on the lessons learned, particularly the importance of **Local Inductive Bias** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00031), **Feature Completeness** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00033), and the limitations of 1D architectures in capturing angular relationships, I propose the **Cyclic Spatio-Temporal 2D ResUNet**.

This strategy introduces a fundamental structural innovation by transforming the problem from a 1D time-series regression into a **2D Spatio-Temporal** task. By representing the satellite constellation as a "Sky Heatmap" (Time $\times$ Azimuth), the model can use 2D convolutions to exploit **angular continuity**—learning that signal degradation in one azimuthal sector implies likely degradation in adjacent sectors (e.g., a large building blocking a range of angles)—while simultaneously modeling temporal dependencies.

### Model
**Architecture: 2D Residual U-Net with Circular Padding**
The backbone is a sequence-to-sequence Encoder-Decoder operating on 2D feature maps.
*   **Input Representation:** A 4D tensor of shape `(Batch, Channels, Time, Azimuth)`. The `Azimuth` dimension represents the 360-degree view of the sky binned into sectors.
*   **Cyclic Convolution (Structural Innovation):** All 2D Convolutional layers apply **Circular Padding** along the `Azimuth` dimension and standard padding along the `Time` dimension. This enforces the physical reality that $359^\circ$ is adjacent to $0^\circ$, preventing boundary artifacts and allowing the model to learn continuous blockage patterns that wrap around the horizon.
*   **Residual Encoder:** A series of **2D Residual Blocks** followed by 2D Max Pooling. This progressively downsamples the Time (to capture drift) and Azimuth (to capture broad blockage sectors) dimensions.
*   **Bottleneck:** A stack of dilated 2D convolutions or a 2D ASPP module to capture global context across the entire drive and sky view.
*   **Residual Decoder:** Upsampling layers concatenated with **Skip Connections** from the encoder (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018) to preserve high-frequency local signal details.
*   **Output Head:**
    *   The decoder outputs a feature map of shape `(Time, Azimuth, Features)`.
    *   A **Global Average Pooling** layer collapses the `Azimuth` dimension, aggregating the learned spatial corrections into a temporal sequence `(Time, Features)`.
    *   A final fully connected layer projects this to the 2D residual vector $(\Delta North, \Delta East)$.

### Data
**Preprocessing Pipeline**
1.  **Temporal Quantization:** Align raw GNSS logs and ground truth by **rounding timestamps to the nearest second (1Hz)**. This prevents data loss from clock jitter (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016).
2.  **Spatio-Temporal Heatmap Construction (Innovation):**
    *   **Binning:** Divide the sky into $K$ azimuthal bins (e.g., 36 bins of $10^\circ$).
    *   **Channel Aggregation:** For each bin at each timestamp, calculate robust statistics for the satellites falling within it:
        *   **Signal Quality:** Maximum and Mean `Cn0DbHz`.
        *   **Geometry:** Mean `SvElevationDegrees`.
        *   **Density:** `SatCount` (number of satellites in the bin).
    *   **Handling Sparsity:** Empty bins are filled with zeros, creating a distinct "no signal" signature.
    *   **Global Context:** Broadcast global metadata (Total `SatCount`, `RawPseudorangeUncertaintyMeters`) across all azimuth bins to provide system-level context (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
3.  **Translation Invariance:** Absolute `Latitude` and `Longitude` are strictly excluded (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
4.  **Target Transformation:** Regression targets are transformed from Degrees to **Meters (East-North)** relative to the WLS baseline (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** **Mean Absolute Error (MAE)** is used for its robustness to outliers (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
*   **Scaled Deep Supervision:** Auxiliary output heads are attached to intermediate decoder layers. Crucially, the ground truth targets for these heads are **downsampled (Average Pooled)** to match the temporal resolution of the specific layer, preventing gradient conflicts (Cite solution_lesson_node_00037).
*   **Optimization:** AdamW optimizer with **Cosine Annealing** learning rate schedule and **Gradient Clipping** to ensure stability (Cite solution_lesson_node_00042).

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** The preprocessed "Sky Heatmap" tensor for a full test drive is fed into the model. The model outputs the sequence of Cartesian residuals $(\Delta North, \Delta East)$.
2.  **Reconstruction:** These Cartesian offsets are converted back into angular offsets $(\Delta Lat, \Delta Lon)$ using local WGS84 approximations.
3.  **Correction:** The predicted offsets are added to the baseline WLS coordinates provided in the test set to generate the final submission.
