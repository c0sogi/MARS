Based on the lessons learned, specifically the efficiency of sequence-to-sequence models (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018), the importance of boundary statistics (Cite solution_lesson_node_00020), and the need for robust residual learning (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001), I propose the **Cascaded 1D ResUNet with Signal Profile Aggregation**.

This strategy introduces a structural innovation by stacking two U-Net models in series (Cascaded refinement) to iteratively correct positioning errors, and evolves the feature engineering by using histogram-based binning to capture the full shape of the signal quality distribution.

### Model
The architecture consists of two **1D Residual U-Nets** arranged in a cascade:
1.  **Stage 1 (Coarse Estimator):** The first U-Net takes the time-series sequence of aggregated features and predicts an initial residual vector $(\Delta North_1, \Delta East_1)$. This stage is responsible for correcting large-scale biases and low-frequency drifts (e.g., atmospheric delays).
2.  **Residual Mixing:** The predicted residuals from Stage 1 are concatenated channel-wise with the original input feature sequence.
3.  **Stage 2 (Fine Refinement):** The second U-Net processes this enriched input to predict a refinement vector $(\Delta North_2, \Delta East_2)$. This stage focuses on high-frequency noise and correcting local errors made by the first stage.
4.  **Final Output:** The model outputs the sum of both stages: $\Delta_{Final} = \Delta_1 + \Delta_2$.
5.  **Backbone:** Both stages use **1D Residual Blocks** in the encoder and decoder to allow for deeper networks without gradient degradation, and **Skip Connections** to preserve local temporal details (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).

### Data
The preprocessing pipeline emphasizes robust alignment and rich statistical descriptors:
1.  **Temporal Quantization:** Raw GNSS logs and ground truth are aligned by rounding timestamps to the nearest second (1Hz). This prevents data loss from exact matching failures (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016).
2.  **Signal Profile Aggregation (Innovation):** Instead of simple means, we construct a descriptive profile of the satellite constellation at each timestamp:
    *   **Histogram Binning:** `Cn0DbHz` and `SvElevationDegrees` are discretized into fixed bins (e.g., Low/Medium/High quality). We calculate the **count of satellites** falling into each bin. This explicitly models the proportion of "healthy" vs. "noisy" signals.
    *   **Boundary Statistics:** We retain the **Minimum** and **Maximum** values for signal strength and elevation (Cite solution_lesson_node_00020). The minimums serve as critical indicators of multipath-prone epochs.
    *   **Reliability Metadata:** `SatCount` and aggregated `RawPseudorangeUncertaintyMeters` are included to quantify baseline confidence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
3.  **Translation Invariance:** Absolute `Latitude` and `Longitude` are strictly excluded from the input features to ensure the model learns error dynamics rather than memorizing map locations (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
4.  **Target Transformation:** Regression targets are transformed from Degrees (Lat/Lon) to **Meters (East-North)** relative to the WLS baseline (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** **Deep Supervision with Mean Absolute Error (MAE)**. The loss is calculated as $L = \text{MAE}(\text{Stage}_1, \text{GT}) + \text{MAE}(\text{Stage}_1 + \text{Stage}_2, \text{GT})$. This forces the first network to learn a valid correction independently, stabilizing the training of the cascaded system. MAE is used for its robustness to outliers (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with weight decay is used to prevent overfitting.
*   **Validation:** A `GroupShuffleSplit` based on `drive_id` is used to ensure the model is evaluated on entirely unseen trips, preventing data leakage.

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** The preprocessed feature sequence for a full test drive is fed into the cascaded model. The model outputs the final summed Cartesian residuals $(\Delta North, \Delta East)$.
2.  **Reconstruction:** These Cartesian offsets are converted back into angular offsets $(\Delta Lat, \Delta Lon)$ using local WGS84 approximations.
3.  **Correction:** The predicted offsets are added to the baseline WLS coordinates provided in the test set to generate the final submission.
