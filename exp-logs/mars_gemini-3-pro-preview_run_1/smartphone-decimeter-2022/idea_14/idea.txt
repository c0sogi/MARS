Based on the lessons learned, I propose the **Multi-Scale 1D ResUNet with Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling (ASPP)**.

This strategy structurally evolves the successful **1D U-Net** approach (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018) by integrating an **ASPP** module at the bottleneck. This addresses the need to capture error dynamics at multiple temporal scales (e.g., instantaneous multipath spikes vs. slow atmospheric drift) while strictly adhering to the **Feature Completeness** principle (Cite solution_lesson_node_00033) by retaining geometric and statistical signal descriptors that were previously discarded.

### Model
**Architecture: 1D ResUNet with ASPP**
The model utilizes a sequence-to-sequence Encoder-Decoder architecture optimized for time-series signal denoising.
*   **Residual Encoder:** A series of downsampling stages using **1D Residual Blocks** (ResNet style). This allows for a deeper network that can extract hierarchical features without gradient degradation.
*   **ASPP Bottleneck (Structural Innovation):** The deepest feature map is processed by an **Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling** module. This consists of parallel 1D Convolutional branches with different dilation rates (e.g., 1, 6, 12, 24).
    *   *Why:* This allows the model to simultaneously "look" at the immediate neighborhood (for local noise) and a wide temporal context (for trajectory drift) without the training instability or data hunger of Transformers (Cite solution_lesson_node_00031).
*   **Residual Decoder:** Upsampling layers that concatenate features from the Encoder via **Skip Connections**. This preserves the high-frequency local information necessary for precise timestamp-level corrections (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).
*   **Output Head:** A final projection layer predicts the 2D residual vector $(\Delta North, \Delta East)$ in meters.

### Data
**Preprocessing Pipeline**
1.  **Temporal Quantization:** Raw GNSS logs and ground truth are aligned by **rounding timestamps to the nearest second (1Hz)**. This prevents the massive data loss associated with exact timestamp matching due to clock jitter (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016).
2.  **Complete Statistical Aggregation:** For each timestamp, aggregate raw satellite features into a dense vector. Crucially, we ensure **Feature Completeness** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00033) by calculating:
    *   **Signal Strength (`Cn0DbHz`):** Mean, **Standard Deviation**, **Minimum**, and Maximum. The "Min" and "Std" are vital for detecting signal instability and multipath (Cite solution_lesson_node_00020).
    *   **Elevation (`SvElevationDegrees`):** Mean, Std, Min, Max.
    *   **Azimuth (`SvAzimuthDegrees`):** **Mean Sin** and **Mean Cos**. This captures the geometric distribution of the constellation (e.g., "are all satellites to the North?") which is critical for vector correction but was missing in previous failed attempts.
    *   **Metadata:** `SatCount` and aggregated `RawPseudorangeUncertaintyMeters` (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
3.  **Translation Invariance:** Absolute `Latitude` and `Longitude` are strictly excluded from the input features to prevent map-memorization (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
4.  **Target Transformation:** Regression targets are transformed from Degrees (Lat/Lon) to **Meters (East-North)** relative to the WLS baseline to ensure physical consistency (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** **Mean Absolute Error (MAE)** with **Deep Supervision**. Auxiliary loss heads are attached to intermediate decoder layers, and the total loss is a weighted sum. This forces the encoder to learn robust representations early in the network. MAE is used for its robustness to heavy-tailed outliers (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with weight decay to regularize the residual blocks.
*   **Validation Strategy:** A `GroupShuffleSplit` based on `drive_id` is used to ensure the model is evaluated on entirely unseen trips, preventing data leakage.

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** The preprocessed feature sequence for a full test drive is fed into the model. The model outputs the sequence of Cartesian residuals $(\Delta North, \Delta East)$.
2.  **Reconstruction:** These Cartesian offsets are converted back into angular offsets $(\Delta Lat, \Delta Lon)$ using local WGS84 approximations.
3.  **Correction:** The predicted offsets are added to the baseline WLS coordinates provided in the test set to generate the final submission.
