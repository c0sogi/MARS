Based on the lessons learned, specifically the superior efficiency of 1D U-Nets (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018), the critical importance of feature completeness (Cite solution_lesson_node_00033), and the need for multi-scale context to handle both local noise and global drift (Cite solution_lesson_node_00012), I propose the **Multi-Scale Directional 1D ResUNet**.

This strategy structurally evolves the previous best approach (1D U-Net) by integrating an **Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling (ASPP)** bottleneck to capture diverse temporal receptive fields and introducing **Directional Signal Aggregation** to explicitly model the geometry of signal blockage (e.g., urban canyons), correcting the feature deficiencies that caused previous multi-scale attempts to fail.

### Model
**Architecture: 1D ResUNet with ASPP and Deep Supervision**
The model retains the sequence-to-sequence U-Net structure but upgrades the internal blocks to handle complex error dynamics better.
*   **Residual Encoder:** A series of downsampling stages using **1D Residual Blocks** (two 1D convolutions with a skip connection). This allows for a deeper network to extract hierarchical features without gradient degradation.
*   **Multi-Scale Bottleneck (ASPP):** Instead of a standard convolution at the bottleneck, the model employs **1D Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling**. The feature map is processed by parallel 1D convolutions with different dilation rates (e.g., 1, 6, 12, 24). This allows the model to simultaneously attend to immediate signal spikes (multipath) and long-term trajectory trends (atmospheric drift) (Cite solution_lesson_node_00012), preserving the local inductive bias of CNNs (Cite solution_lesson_node_00031).
*   **Residual Decoder:** Upsampling layers concatenated with skip connections from the encoder to preserve high-frequency details (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).
*   **Deep Supervision:** Auxiliary output heads are attached to intermediate decoder layers. The loss is calculated as a weighted sum of the final output and these auxiliary outputs. This forces earlier layers to learn meaningful residual representations, stabilizing the training of the deeper architecture.

### Data
**Preprocessing Pipeline**
1.  **Temporal Quantization:** Raw GNSS logs and ground truth are aligned by rounding timestamps to the nearest second (1Hz) to prevent data loss from clock jitter (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016).
2.  **Global Feature Aggregation:** For each timestamp, calculate robust statistics for all visible satellites:
    *   **Signal Quality:** Mean, Standard Deviation, **Minimum**, and Maximum for `Cn0DbHz` and `SvElevationDegrees` (Cite solution_lesson_node_00020, solution_lesson_node_00033).
    *   **Reliability:** `SatCount` and aggregated `RawPseudorangeUncertaintyMeters` (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
3.  **Directional Feature Engineering (Innovation):** To model the *geometry* of blockage (e.g., signals blocked on the left but open on the right), divide the sky into 4 azimuthal quadrants (NE, SE, SW, NW). For each quadrant, calculate:
    *   **Sector SatCount:** Number of satellites in that quadrant.
    *   **Sector Signal Quality:** Mean `Cn0DbHz` of satellites in that quadrant.
    *   *Note:* If a sector is empty, fill with 0. This provides the model with a coarse "sky visibility mask."
4.  **Translation Invariance:** Absolute `Latitude` and `Longitude` are strictly excluded to prevent map memorization (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
5.  **Target Transformation:** Regression targets are transformed from Degrees to **Meters (East-North)** relative to the WLS baseline (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** **Mean Absolute Error (MAE)** with Deep Supervision weights (e.g., 1.0 for final, 0.5 for auxiliary). MAE is chosen for robustness to heavy-tailed outliers (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with weight decay to regularize the increased parameter count of the residual blocks.
*   **Validation Strategy:** A `GroupShuffleSplit` based on `drive_id` is used to ensure the model is evaluated on entirely unseen trips, preventing data leakage.

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** The preprocessed feature sequence (Global + Directional stats) for a full test drive is fed into the model. The model outputs the sequence of Cartesian residuals $(\Delta North, \Delta East)$.
2.  **Reconstruction:** These Cartesian offsets are converted back into angular offsets $(\Delta Lat, \Delta Lon)$ using local WGS84 approximations.
3.  **Correction:** The predicted offsets are added to the baseline WLS coordinates provided in the test set to generate the final submission.
