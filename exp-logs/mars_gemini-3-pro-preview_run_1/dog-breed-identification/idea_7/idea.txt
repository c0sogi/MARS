Based on the lessons learned, particularly the success of the **ConvNeXt-Base** architecture with **SWA** and the failure of the **Heterogeneous Ensemble** due to the "weak link" problem, I propose a **Regularized Heterogeneous Ensemble with Augmentation Annealing**.

This strategy addresses the core conflict identified in the lessons: high-capacity models (like Swin or Large variants) require aggressive regularization (Mixup/CutMix) to prevent overfitting `Cite solution_lesson_node_00016`, but aggressive regularization destroys the probability calibration required for minimizing Log Loss `Cite solution_lesson_node_00004`. My solution introduces a "Cooldown" phase to resolve this.

# Model
We will construct a **Heterogeneous Ensemble** consisting of two distinct backbone architectures to leverage diverse feature representations:
1.  **ConvNeXt-Base:** Retained for its proven performance on this dataset `Cite solution_lesson_node_00014`.
2.  **Swin-Base (Swin Transformer):** Re-introduced to capture global context. We will explicitly address its previous underperformance `Cite solution_lesson_node_00021` by applying the advanced regularization strategy described below, transforming it from a "weak link" into a robust contributor.

Both models will be initialized with **ImageNet-1k** weights to avoid the alignment and resolution issues observed with ImageNet-22k models `Cite solution_lesson_node_00017`. Each model will use a linear head with **Dropout** `Cite solution_lesson_node_00006`.

# Data
We will utilize the **High-Resolution Geometric Pipeline** that proved optimal for fine-grained classification.
*   **Preprocessing:** `Resize(274)` (integer scaling) followed by `CenterCrop(256)`. This preserves the aspect ratio, which is critical for breed differentiation `Cite solution_lesson_node_00018`.
*   **Augmentation:**
    *   **Spatial:** Only **Random Horizontal Flips** `Cite solution_lesson_node_00002`.
    *   **Pixel-Level (New):** We will introduce **Mixup** and **CutMix** during the main training phase. This provides the "aggressive regularization" needed to train high-capacity models without overfitting `Cite solution_lesson_node_00016`.

# Training
We will implement a **Stratified 5-Fold Cross-Validation** for *each* architecture (10 total training runs). The training pipeline is structurally evolved into a **Four-Phase Regime** to balance regularization with calibration:

1.  **Phase 1: Head Adaptation**
    *   Freeze backbone, train head. No Mixup/CutMix.
    *   Goal: Align weights `Cite solution_lesson_node_00009`.

2.  **Phase 2: Regularized Fine-Tuning**
    *   Unfreeze backbone. Apply **Discriminative Learning Rates** `Cite solution_lesson_node_00006`.
    *   **Action:** Enable **Mixup** and **CutMix**.
    *   **Loss:** Soft-Target Cross Entropy.
    *   Goal: Learn robust, generalizable features and prevent the memorization/overfitting observed in previous high-capacity experiments.

3.  **Phase 3: Regularization Cooldown (New)**
    *   **Action:** **Disable Mixup and CutMix**. Continue training for a few epochs with a low learning rate.
    *   **Loss:** Standard Cross Entropy.
    *   **Goal:** "Sharpen" the model's predictions. This directly addresses the lesson that label smoothing/soft targets penalize Log Loss `Cite solution_lesson_node_00004`. It allows the model to transition from learning robust features (Phase 2) to outputting high-confidence, calibrated probabilities on real images.

4.  **Phase 4: Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA)**
    *   Apply SWA on the "cooled" model to find the flat minimum `Cite solution_lesson_node_00024`.

# Evaluation
*   **Inference:** Apply **Test Time Augmentation (TTA)** (Original + Horizontal Flip) for every test image `Cite solution_lesson_node_00014`.
*   **Ensemble:** Average the probabilities from all 10 models (5 ConvNeXt folds + 5 Swin folds). By fixing the Swin model's performance via Mixup, we ensure a robust ensemble that reduces variance `Cite solution_lesson_node_00026` without diluting the prediction quality `Cite solution_lesson_node_00021`.
