# Model: Cross-Validated DenseNet121 with GeM Pooling

I propose a **Homogeneous Ensemble of DenseNet121** models, structurally enhanced with **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling**.

*   **Backbone:** I will utilize **DenseNet121**. As established in **Lesson 16**, the dense connectivity pattern (feature concatenation) is structurally superior to residual addition (ResNet) or large-kernel convolution (ConvNeXt) for preserving the high-frequency, fine-grained textural details required to identify tumor cells in low-resolution pathology patches.
*   **Stem Modification:** To prevent the immediate loss of spatial fidelity, I will replace the standard input stem ($7 \times 7$ stride-2 convolution + max pooling) with a **single $3 \times 3$ convolution with stride 1 and padding 1**. This ensures the deep layers process a feature map that retains the spatial resolution of the input. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00003**. The subsequent layers will be initialized with **ImageNet pretrained weights**. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00008**.
*   **Structural Innovation (GeM Pooling):** I will replace the standard Global Average Pooling (GAP) layer with **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling**. The task requires detecting "at least one pixel" of tumor tissue (a max-pooling-like operation) within a texture-based classification task (an average-pooling-like operation). GeM pooling introduces a learnable parameter $p$ that allows the network to dynamically interpolate between Max and Average pooling ($f(x) = (\text{mean}(x^p))^{1/p}$), enabling it to better capture sparse tumor signals within the patch than a fixed GAP layer.

# Data: Hard Attention and Conservative Augmentation

*   **Preprocessing:** I will apply **Hard Attention via ROI Cropping**, extracting the central **48x48 pixel region** from the source images. This focuses the model on the relevant 32x32 target area plus a minimal 8-pixel context border, removing irrelevant background noise. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00001**.
*   **Dataset Usage:** I will utilize the **full training dataset** for all folds. I will strictly avoid subsampling, as high-capacity models require the full data distribution to learn robust invariants. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00014**.
*   **Augmentation:** I will apply random horizontal flips, vertical flips, and 90-degree rotations. Crucially, I will restrict color augmentation to **mild brightness and contrast adjustments only**, explicitly **excluding hue and saturation jittering**. This preserves the H&E stain color signatures that are critical for identifying tumor tissue. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00004**.

# Training: Fully Converged 5-Fold Cross-Validation

To address the failure of the previous ensemble attempt (Idea 6), which underperformed due to insufficient training time, I will implement a **Fully Converged 5-Fold Cross-Validation** strategy utilizing the full 24-hour runtime budget.

*   **Protocol:** The training data will be split into 5 stratified folds. I will train 5 independent DenseNet121 models, one for each fold.
*   **Convergence:** Each model will be trained for **20 epochs**. I will maintain a **relaxed Early Stopping patience of 6 epochs** to ensure the models survive volatile transient phases and reach true convergence, prioritizing the quality of the base learners over raw speed. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00007** and **Cite solution_lesson_node_00021**.
*   **Optimization:** I will use the **AdamW** optimizer with a **Cosine Annealing** learning rate scheduler. I will also introduce **Label Smoothing (0.05)** to the Binary Cross-Entropy loss to prevent overconfidence and improve generalization on the noisy boundary cases.

# Evaluation: TTA-Ensemble

For the final submission, I will employ a robust inference pipeline that marginalizes out both model variance and orientation noise:

1.  **Test Time Augmentation (TTA):** For each test image, every model in the ensemble will generate predictions for 4 views: original, horizontal flip, vertical flip, and 90-degree rotation. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**.
2.  **Global Averaging:** The final prediction will be the average of the 20 probability outputs (5 models $\times$ 4 TTA views). This maximizes the Area Under the ROC Curve by smoothing out stochastic errors.
