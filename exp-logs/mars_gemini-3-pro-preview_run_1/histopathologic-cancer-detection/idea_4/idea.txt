# Model: ConvNeXt-Tiny with Resolution-Preserving Stem

I propose replacing the DenseNet121 backbone with a **ConvNeXt-Tiny** architecture. ConvNeXt modernizes standard CNNs with Vision Transformer (ViT) design principles—such as larger kernels ($7 \times 7$), inverted bottlenecks, and fewer activation functions—offering superior feature extraction capabilities for complex textures.

To ensure this architecture functions effectively on the small $48 \times 48$ input patches, I will apply the **Partial Transfer Learning with Stem Modification** strategy. The standard ConvNeXt input stem (a $4 \times 4$ convolution with stride 4) is too aggressive, as it would immediately downsample the image to $12 \times 12$. I will replace this layer with a **$3 \times 3$ convolution with stride 1 and padding 1**. This modification preserves the full $48 \times 48$ spatial resolution entering the deep stages of the network, preventing the "spatial dimensionality collapse" observed with standard backbones on small inputs. I will initialize the rest of the network with **ImageNet pretrained weights** to leverage learned feature detectors.

This ensures that the training process does not terminate prematurely during the volatile high-learning-rate phases, allowing the model to reach the stable convergence zone.

# Evaluation: Test Time Augmentation (TTA)

For the final inference, I will employ **Test Time Augmentation**. The model will predict probabilities for the original crop and its three geometric transformations (horizontal flip, vertical flip, and 90-degree rotation). The final submission score will be the average of these four predictions, reducing variance and improving robustness.
