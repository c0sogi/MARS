Based on the lessons learned, I propose a solution that evolves the architecture from a Residual Network to a Densely Connected Network while retaining the successful regularization and inference strategies.

# Model
I propose replacing the previous Residual Network (ResNet) architecture with a **Custom Shallow DenseNet (Densely Connected Convolutional Network)** augmented with **Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) blocks**.

*   **Structural Innovation (Dense Connectivity):** Unlike the ResNet used previously (which sums features), the DenseNet architecture concatenates feature maps from all preceding layers within a block. This maximizes information flow and feature reuse, which is particularly effective for small-resolution images (32x32) where preserving every bit of signal is crucial.
*   **Structural Innovation (Channel Attention):** I will integrate Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) blocks after the dense layers. This introduces a mechanism for the network to explicitly model channel interdependencies and recalibrate feature maps, a capability absent in the previous baseline.
*   **Conservative Downsampling:** The model will retain the specialized "stem" design (3x3 convolution with stride 1, no initial pooling) to preserve the 32x32 spatial resolution in the early stages, avoiding the signal destruction caused by standard backbones **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00001)**.
*   **Architecture Layout:** The network will consist of a stem, followed by 3 Dense Blocks separated by Transition Layers (1x1 convolution + 2x2 Average Pooling). The final classification head will use Global Average Pooling followed by a Linear layer.

# Data
*   **Preprocessing:** Images will be normalized using the dataset's channel-wise mean and standard deviation.
*   **Augmentation:**
    *   **Geometric:** The training pipeline will apply both **RandomHorizontalFlip** and **RandomVerticalFlip**, exploiting the rotational invariance of the aerial photography **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00002)**.
    *   **Regularization:** To further smooth decision boundaries and close the generalization gap, **Mixup** augmentation will be applied. This involves training on linear interpolations of image pairs and their labels **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00004)**.

# Training
*   **Loss Function:** **Binary Cross-Entropy with Logits** will be used. Since Mixup creates soft probability labels (e.g., 0.9 vs 1.0), the loss function must handle continuous targets rather than just discrete integers.
*   **Optimizer:** The **AdamW** optimizer will be used for its superior weight decay handling.
*   **Scheduler:** A **Cosine Annealing** scheduler will be employed to gradually decay the learning rate.
*   **Duration:** The training duration will be set to at least **30 epochs**. This extended duration is critical to accommodate the slower convergence required by Mixup regularization and to ensure the Cosine Annealing schedule has a sufficiently gradual decay curve **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00003)**.

# Evaluation
*   **Inference Strategy:** To maximize performance and robustness, predictions will be generated using **4-view Test Time Augmentation (TTA)**. The final probability for each test image will be the average of the predictions for the original image, a horizontally flipped version, a vertically flipped version, and a 180-degree rotated version (horizontal + vertical flip) **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00002)**.
