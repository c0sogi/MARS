# Solution: Domain-Adapted DeBERTa-v3 Ensemble with AWP and Hybrid Pooling

## Model
I propose a high-performance architecture that prioritizes semantic understanding and signal preservation while maintaining computational feasibility.

1.  **Backbone (Strategic Retention):** We will utilize **DeBERTa-v3-base**. This model has proven superior to RNNs (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007) and standard RoBERTa (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018) due to its disentangled attention mechanism. We explicitly select the "Base" variant to ensure we can train on the **full dataset** using Cross-Validation within the 24-hour limit, avoiding the performance degradation caused by subsampling required for "Large" models (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014).
2.  **Hybrid Pooling Head (Strategic Retention):** We will attach the highly effective custom pooling head to the final hidden state of the transformer:
    *   **Global Max Pooling:** To extract the strongest local toxic signal (e.g., a specific slur) regardless of its position (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
    *   **Linear Attention Pooling:** To compute a context-aware weighted average of the sequence, prioritizing relevant tokens while filtering noise (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002). We will strictly use **Linear Attention** ($w^T h_t$) rather than complex additive attention to ensure efficient convergence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
    *   These two vectors will be concatenated to form the final representation.
3.  **Multi-Sample Dropout (Strategic Retention):** The concatenated representation will pass through 5 parallel dropout masks before the final classification layer. This smooths the loss landscape and improves generalization, provided the model is trained for sufficient epochs (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016).

## Data
1.  **Tokenization (Strategic Retention):** We will use the **DeBERTa-v3 Tokenizer** (SentencePiece). This preserves punctuation and special characters as distinct tokens, which are critical features for detecting emotional intensity and obfuscated toxicity often lost in standard cleaning (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).
2.  **Unsupervised Corpus Construction:** We will concatenate the text from both `train.csv` and `test.csv` to create a comprehensive domain-specific corpus for the pre-training stage.
3.  **Supervised Dataset:** We will use the **Full Training Dataset** for the classification stage (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014).

## Training
I propose a **Two-Stage Training Pipeline** (Structural Innovation) to address the lexical adaptation challenges identified in previous lessons.

**Stage 1: Domain-Adaptive Pre-training (DAPT)**
*   **Objective:** We will fine-tune the DeBERTa-v3 backbone on the combined (train + test) corpus using the **Masked Language Modeling (MLM)** objective for 3 epochs.
*   **Rationale:** Lesson 00009 highlighted that lower layers fail to adapt to toxic vocabulary (slang, profanity) when learning rates are dampened or when training is purely supervised. DAPT explicitly adapts the tokenizer embeddings and lower-layer features to the specific distribution of Wikipedia comments *before* the classification task begins, resolving the lexical gap without the risks of Layer-wise Learning Rate Decay (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).

**Stage 2: Robust Supervised Fine-Tuning (5-Fold CV)**
*   **Strategy:** We will fine-tune the domain-adapted backbone using **5-Fold Cross-Validation**.
*   **Adversarial Weight Perturbation (AWP) (Strategic Retention):** We will implement AWP starting from the second epoch. By perturbing weights to maximize loss during training, AWP forces the model to find flatter, more robust minima, squeezing out the final performance gains in this high-AUC regime (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).
*   **Optimization:** We will use the **AdamW** optimizer with a **Uniform Learning Rate**. We will explicitly **reject Layer-wise Learning Rate Decay (LLRD)** to allow the model to fully utilize the domain-adapted features (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
*   **Scheduling:** We will use a **OneCycleLR** scheduler with a duration of **4 to 5 epochs per fold**. This extended duration is critical to allow the regularization effects of Multi-Sample Dropout and AWP to stabilize, avoiding the underfitting observed with compressed schedules (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006, solution_lesson_node_00016).

## Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** We will generate predictions for the test set using each of the 5 trained fold models.
2.  **Ensembling:** The final prediction for each comment will be the arithmetic mean of the probabilities output by the 5 models. This ensemble approach reduces variance and is standard practice for maximizing ROC AUC.
3.  **Submission:** The averaged probabilities will be formatted into the required CSV structure.
