# Context-Aware DeBERTa-v3-Base with Adversarial Weight Perturbation (AWP)

## Model
I propose a structural evolution that combines the superior feature extraction of **DeBERTa-v3-base** with the proven **Hybrid Pooling** head, enhanced by a new **Adversarial Weight Perturbation (AWP)** training paradigm.

1.  **Backbone (Strategic Retention):** We will utilize **DeBERTa-v3-base**. Transformers have been proven to significantly outperform RNNs in this domain (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007). We select the "Base" variant over "Large" to ensure we can train on the full dataset within the time limit, prioritizing data coverage over parameter count (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014).
2.  **Hybrid Pooling Head (Strategic Retention):** We will attach the highly effective custom pooling head to the sequence of hidden states from the transformer:
    *   **Global Max Pooling:** To extract the strongest local toxic signal (e.g., a specific slur) regardless of its position (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
    *   **Linear Attention Pooling:** To compute a context-aware weighted average of the sequence, prioritizing relevant tokens while filtering noise (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002). We will use simple **Linear Attention** ($w^T h_t$) rather than complex additive attention to ensure efficient convergence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
    *   These two vectors will be concatenated to form the final representation.

## Data
1.  **Tokenization (Strategic Retention):** We will use the **DeBERTa-v3 Tokenizer** (SentencePiece). This tokenizer preserves punctuation and special characters as distinct tokens, which are critical features for detecting emotional intensity and obfuscated toxicity that are often lost in standard cleaning (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).
2.  **Preprocessing:** We will use the **Full Training Dataset**. We will strictly avoid subsampling, as exposing the model to the full data distribution is more valuable than increasing model size (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014). Inputs will be padded/truncated to a fixed length (e.g., 300 tokens).

## Training
1.  **Adversarial Weight Perturbation (Structural Innovation):** We will introduce **Adversarial Weight Perturbation (AWP)** into the training loop.
    *   **Mechanism:** Instead of standard minimization, we will formulate the training as a min-max game. For each batch, after computing the forward pass and gradients, we will perturb the model's weights in the direction that *maximizes* the loss (adversarial attack). We then perform a second forward/backward pass to minimize this "worst-case" loss.
    *   **Rationale:** This technique flattens the loss landscape, forcing the model to find a solution that is robust to small perturbations. This directly improves generalization and calibration, which is critical for maximizing the ROC AUC metric.
    *   **Implementation:** AWP will be activated after the first epoch (warmup) to avoid destabilizing the model during the initial learning phase.
2.  **Optimization:** We will use the **AdamW** optimizer with a **Uniform Learning Rate**. We will explicitly **reject Layer-wise Learning Rate Decay (LLRD)**, as lower layers must update at the full rate to adapt to the specific toxic vocabulary (slang, profanity) of this dataset (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
3.  **Scheduling:** We will employ a **OneCycleLR** scheduler with a duration of **4 to 5 epochs**. We will strictly avoid compressing the schedule, ensuring the decay phase is gradual. This is necessary to accommodate the additional regularization overhead introduced by AWP and prevent premature convergence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006, solution_lesson_node_00016).

## Evaluation
The evaluation pipeline remains consistent. The test set will be processed using the DeBERTa-v3 tokenizer. The model will predict the probability for each of the six toxicity classes. These probabilities will be formatted into the required submission CSV.
