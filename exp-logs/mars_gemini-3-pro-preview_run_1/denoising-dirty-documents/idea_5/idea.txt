Based on the comprehensive analysis of the provided lessons and the specific constraints of the text denoising task (small dataset, high-frequency details), I propose the following optimized strategy.

# Model
**Architecture: Residual Shallow U-Net with Dilated Bottleneck**
I propose a structural evolution of the successful "Shallow U-Net" (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00018}) that incorporates **Residual Learning** and **Dilated Convolutions**.
*   **Backbone:** We will retain the **3-level Encoder-Decoder** structure (32 $\to$ 64 $\to$ 128 filters). This lightweight design prevents overfitting on the small 92-image dataset, adhering to the finding that simpler architectures generalize better here (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00011}).
*   **Residual Learning (Structural Innovation):** Instead of predicting the clean image directly, the network will be trained to predict the **noise residual** (i.e., `Input - Target`). The final clean image will be obtained by subtracting the predicted noise from the input. This formulation simplifies the optimization landscape for denoising tasks, as the noise manifold is often less complex than the structured text manifold.
*   **Dilated Bottleneck (Structural Innovation):** We will replace the standard convolutions in the central bottleneck with **Dilated Convolutions** (e.g., dilation rates of 2 and 4). This expands the receptive field without reducing resolution (pooling) or adding parameters (depth), allowing the model to better distinguish between large noise artifacts and actual text strokes while respecting the parameter constraints of the small dataset.
*   **Upsampling:** We will strictly use **Bilinear Upsampling followed by Convolution** to avoid checkerboard artifacts (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00009}).

# Data
**Preprocessing & Augmentation**
*   **Maximized Patch Size:** We will train on **large random crops** (e.g., $160 \times 160$) extracted from the native resolution images. This maximizes spatial context, which was proven to significantly reduce error compared to smaller patches (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00016}).
*   **Aggressive Geometric Augmentation:** We will apply random horizontal flips, vertical flips, and 90-degree rotations. This exploits the rotational invariance of the noise to virtually multiply the dataset size (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00003}).
*   **Deterministic Loading:** We will implement a `worker_init_fn` to seed the data loader workers. This ensures that the random cropping and augmentation pipeline is fully reproducible and deterministic, eliminating the variance observed in previous multi-process data loading (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00015}).

# Training
**Procedure & Optimization**
*   **Strategy: Converged K-Fold Cross-Validation Ensemble.**
    *   We will implement a **5-Fold Cross-Validation** scheme. Instead of a single train/val split, we will train 5 independent models, each on a different 80% subset of the data.
    *   Crucially, we will train **each of the 5 models to full convergence** (e.g., 1000 epochs). Since the optimal training time per model is short (~15 minutes), the total training time (~75 minutes) is well within the 24-hour limit. This strategy combines the low bias of fully converged models (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00012}) with the variance reduction of ensembling, utilizing 100% of the available training data for the final inference.
*   **Scheduler:** We will use **Cosine Annealing** with `T_max` set to the full number of epochs (1000). This ensures the learning rate decays fully to zero, allowing the model to settle into a sharp minimum without being disrupted by premature stopping or high learning rate floors (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00007}, Cite {solution_lesson_node_00016}).
*   **Optimization Velocity:** We will use the **Adam** optimizer with a **high learning rate (1e-3)** and **small batch size (16)** to maximize the frequency of weight updates (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00010}).
*   **Loss Function:** Mean Squared Error (MSE) computed on the residual (Predicted Noise vs. Actual Noise).

# Evaluation
**Inference Pipeline**
*   **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):** For each test image, we will generate predictions for 8 geometric views (Original, Rot90, Rot180, Rot270, and their horizontal flips). These will be inversely transformed and averaged to smooth out prediction noise (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00006}).
*   **Ensemble Aggregation:** The TTA-averaged predictions from all **5 fully converged fold models** will be averaged together. This ensures the final submission benefits from the patterns learned across the entire dataset, not just a single training split.
*   **Submission:** The final averaged pixel intensities will be flattened and mapped to the required `image_row_col` format.
