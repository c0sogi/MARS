Based on the success of the Multi-Scale 1D CNN in extracting frequency-specific features from raw waveforms, and recognizing the limitation of its narrow 50-second observation window, I propose a **Spectrogram-Guided Attentive Dual-Stream Network**. This strategy structurally evolves the previous "Hybrid" concept by replacing simple concatenation with a Cross-Attention mechanism, allowing the model to dynamically utilize the long-term context (10 minutes) to resolve ambiguities in the short-term signal (50 seconds).

### 1. Model
The architecture consists of two parallel encoders connected by an attention-based fusion module:

*   **Stream A (Local EEG Encoder):** This branch processes the 50-second raw EEG signal. It retains the **Multi-Scale 1D Convolutional (Inception)** architecture Cite {solution_lesson_node_00002}. Crucially, instead of applying Global Average Pooling immediately at the end of the encoder, we preserve the **temporal sequence of feature vectors**. This allows the network to maintain the high-resolution phase and frequency information extracted by the parallel kernel branches.
*   **Stream B (Global Context Encoder):** This branch processes the 10-minute spectrograms using a lightweight 2D CNN backbone (e.g., EfficientNet-B0). The output is a feature map where the spatial dimensions are flattened to form a sequence representing the long-term evolution of brain activity.
*   **Attentive Fusion Module:** A **Cross-Attention** layer is introduced to fuse the streams. The high-fidelity EEG features from Stream A act as **Queries**, while the long-range Spectrogram features from Stream B serve as **Keys and Values**. This mechanism allows the model to explicitly "attend" to relevant historical context (e.g., the onset of a seizure minutes prior) to better classify the current 50-second window, effectively grounding the local waveform data in the global study context.
*   **Classification Head:** The attention-refined features are pooled and passed through a final Dense layer with Softmax activation.

### 2. Data
*   **Raw EEG:** Extract the central 50-second window. Downsample the signal from 200 Hz to **100 Hz** to reduce dimensionality while preserving the Nyquist frequency for relevant brain rhythms Cite {solution_lesson_node_00002}. Apply channel-wise instance normalization.
*   **Spectrograms:** Load the full 10-minute spectrograms to provide the global context. Apply a Log transformation (dB scale) and resize to a standard resolution (e.g., $512 \times 512$) to fit the 2D CNN input.
*   **Augmentation:** Apply **SpecAugment** (time/frequency masking) to the spectrograms and **Channel Dropout** to the EEG signals to encourage robustness.

### 3. Training
*   **Loss Function:** Use **Kullback-Leibler (KL) Divergence** to optimize the model against the soft probability targets provided by the expert annotators, as this has been proven to be the most effective metric for this task Cite {solution_lesson_node_00002}.
*   **Optimizer & Schedule:** Utilize **AdamW** with a **OneCycle Learning Rate** policy. This schedule, which rapidly increases and then decays the learning rate, facilitates super-convergence and was a key factor in the efficiency of the previous 1D CNN experiments.
*   **Strategy:** Train the entire architecture end-to-end. Use mixed-precision (FP16) to offset the computational cost of the added image encoder and attention layers.

### 4. Evaluation
*   **Preprocessing:** For each test sample, generate the two required inputs: the processed 50-second raw EEG crop and the corresponding 10-minute spectrogram image.
*   **Inference:** Pass the paired inputs through the dual-stream network. The model will output the probability distribution over the 6 classes.
*   **Post-Processing:** Ensure the predicted probabilities sum to 1.0 before saving to the submission file.
