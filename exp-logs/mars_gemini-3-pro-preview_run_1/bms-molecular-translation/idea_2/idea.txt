Based on the provided model architectures and the previous baseline attempt, I propose the **"Show, Attend, and Tell" (Attention-Based Encoder-Decoder)** approach. This method advances beyond the global compression of Idea 1 by incorporating a spatial attention mechanism, which is critical for correctly transcribing the detailed graph structure of molecules, while remaining computationally efficient.

### Model
The architecture is designed to align visual features with textual tokens dynamically:
*   **Encoder**: The backbone will be **EfficientNet-B0**, selected for its optimal balance of parameters and accuracy. Unlike Idea 1, we will **remove the final Global Average Pooling layer**. This preserves the output as a spatial feature grid (tensor of shape $H \times W \times C$), retaining the local positional information of atoms and bonds necessary for detailed transcription.
*   **Attention Mechanism**: A lightweight **Bahdanau (Additive) Attention** layer will be inserted between the encoder and decoder. At each generation step, this mechanism calculates a "context vector" by computing a weighted sum of the encoder's spatial features. The weights are learned dynamically, allowing the model to "look" at specific parts of the chemical image (e.g., a specific ring or side chain) relevant to the character currently being predicted.
*   **Decoder**: A **single-layer LSTM** (Long Short-Term Memory) network. It receives the attention-derived context vector concatenated with the embedding of the previously generated token. The LSTM effectively manages the long-range dependencies in InChI strings (which can exceed 300 characters) better than simple RNNs/GRUs while remaining lighter than Transformer decoders.

### Data
*   **Image Preprocessing**: Images will be resized to a fixed resolution of **$256 \times 256$ pixels**. This resolution is sufficient for the attention mechanism to distinguish structural components while keeping the feature map size small for fast processing. Pixel values will be normalized to the $[0, 1]$ range.
*   **Text Preprocessing**: The InChI strings will be tokenized at the character level using the vocabulary identified in the EDA (approx. 38 unique characters). Special tokens (`<SOS>`, `<EOS>`, `<PAD>`) will be added. Sequences will be padded to a fixed length (e.g., 400) to handle the longest strings found in the dataset.

### Training
*   **Loss Function**: **Cross-Entropy Loss** will be used to minimize the error between the predicted character probabilities and the ground truth characters.
*   **Optimizer**: The **Adam** optimizer will be employed for its efficiency in handling sparse gradients and rapid convergence.
*   **Strategy**: **Teacher Forcing** will be used throughout training. The decoder will receive the ground-truth character from the previous time step as input, rather than its own prediction. This stabilizes the training of the attention alignment and prevents error propagation during the early learning phases.

### Evaluation
*   **Inference Process**: For test data, the model runs in autoregressive mode. The image is encoded once into spatial features. The decoder is initialized with the `<SOS>` token.
*   **Decoding Strategy**: **Greedy Decoding** will be utilized for maximum inference speed. At each step, the model selects the token with the highest probability, and this prediction is fed back as the input for the next step. Generation terminates when `<EOS>` is predicted or the maximum sequence length is reached.
