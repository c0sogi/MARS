Based on the accumulated lessons and the available dataset metadata, I propose a **Hierarchical Multi-Task ResNet-50** strategy. This approach evolves the architecture from a simple flat classifier to a hierarchy-aware system that leverages the provided category taxonomy to stabilize training and improve feature discriminability.

### Model
I will upgrade the backbone from ResNet-18 to **ResNet-50** to increase the model's capacity for distinguishing between the 5,270 classes. The architecture will retain the effective **Multi-View Aggregation** strategy, where images are processed independently by the backbone and aggregated via **Global Max Pooling** to form a single product feature vector **Cite solution_lesson_node_00001**.

However, I will introduce a structural innovation in the classification head. Instead of a single output layer, the model will branch into **three parallel Linear Heads** **Cite solution_lesson_node_00003** from the aggregated feature vector:
1.  **Coarse Head:** Predicts the Level 1 Category (e.g., "SPORT").
2.  **Intermediate Head:** Predicts the Level 2 Category (e.g., "CYCLES").
3.  **Fine Head (Target):** Predicts the Level 3 Category (e.g., "VELO DE VILLE").

This hierarchical structure forces the backbone to learn features that respect the semantic taxonomy of the products, using the coarse labels as auxiliary supervision to guide the optimization of the fine-grained target.

### Data
1.  **Metadata Indexing:** I will continue to use the CSV-based metadata index to perform random-access reads from the BSON files, avoiding the I/O bottleneck of extracting images to disk **Cite solution_lesson_node_00001**.
2.  **Label Engineering:** I will preprocess `category_names.csv` to create a mapping from the target `category_id` (Level 3) to its corresponding Level 1 and Level 2 parent IDs. The custom Dataset class will return the image tensor along with this triplet of labels `(y_lvl1, y_lvl2, y_lvl3)`.
3.  **Preprocessing:** Images will be decoded to RGB and normalized. No complex augmentations (like ColorJitter) will be used to maintain convergence speed **Cite solution_lesson_node_00003**.

### Training
1.  **Loss Function:** The total loss will be a weighted sum of the losses from all three heads: $L_{total} = L_{fine} + \lambda_1 L_{intermediate} + \lambda_2 L_{coarse}$. I will use **Label Smoothing** on the fine-grained target head to prevent overfitting on the noisy 5,270 classes **Cite solution_lesson_node_00003**.
2.  **Batch Scaling:** I will use a large batch size (e.g., 512) to saturate the A100 GPU and ensure stable BatchNorm statistics, utilizing the maximum available CPU workers (12) to prevent data starvation **Cite solution_lesson_node_00004**.
3.  **Optimization:** I will employ the **OneCycleLR** scheduler. Given the massive dataset size, I will limit training to a short duration (e.g., 2-3 epochs), relying on the aggressive learning rate schedule to achieve convergence quickly **Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**. I will ensure the learning rate is scaled appropriately for the large batch size to maintain effective weight updates **Cite solution_lesson_node_00006**.

### Evaluation
During inference on the test set, the auxiliary heads (Level 1 and Level 2) will be ignored. The predictions will be generated solely from the **Fine Head (Level 3)** using the same max-pooling aggregation pipeline. The final output will be the argmax of the Level 3 logits.
