The `torch.OutOfMemoryError` is caused by the excessive memory footprint of the dense heatmap tensors during the loss calculation. The model outputs a tensor of shape `(Batch, Classes, Height, Width)`. With `Batch=2`, `Classes=4782`, and `Height=Width=256`, a single tensor occupies approximately **2.33 GiB** of VRAM. The `ModifiedFocalLoss` creates multiple intermediate copies of this tensor (e.g., for `log`, `pow`, and weighting) during the forward pass, which rapidly exhausts the 40GB GPU memory.

To resolve this, I will apply two fixes:
1.  **Reduce Batch Size**: In `runfile.py`, I will decrease `Config.BATCH_SIZE` from 2 to 1. This halves the size of all dense tensors to ~1.16 GiB, providing a significant safety margin.
2.  **Optimize Loss Calculation**: In `library/loss.py`, I will optimize the `ModifiedFocalLoss` to compute the positive loss component sparsely. As per **debug_lesson_3**, we should avoid materializing massive dense tensors when targets are sparse. By using boolean masking (`pred[pos_mask]`) instead of dense multiplication (`loss * pos_inds`), we avoid allocating a 2.33 GiB tensor for the positive loss term.
