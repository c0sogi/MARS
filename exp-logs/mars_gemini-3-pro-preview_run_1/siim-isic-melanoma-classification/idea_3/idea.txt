Based on the accumulated lessons and the limitations of previous attempts, I propose a structural evolution that shifts from Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) to a **Hierarchical Vision Transformer** architecture, specifically designed to capture global context (e.g., lesion borders and asymmetry) more effectively than local convolutions.

# Proposed Solution: Swin Transformer V2 with Stochastic Depth and Balanced Sampling

## Model
**Architecture:**
Replace the EfficientNet backbone with a **Swin Transformer V2 (Tiny or Small)** pre-trained on ImageNet.
- **Structural Innovation:** Unlike CNNs (EfficientNet) which rely on translation-invariant convolutions, Swin Transformers utilize **shifted window self-attention**. This allows the model to model long-range dependencies and global shape characteristics of the lesion (e.g., irregular borders, global asymmetry) which are critical for malignancy detection but often lost in local receptive fields.
- **Tabular Fusion:** Retain the tabular branch but upgrade the fusion mechanism. Instead of simple concatenation, apply a **Gated Linear Unit (GLU)** at the fusion point. The tabular embedding (processed via MLP) will act as a "gate" to re-weight the image feature vector before the final classification layer, allowing patient metadata (age, site) to suppress or amplify specific visual signals.

## Data
**Preprocessing:**
- **Resolution:** Resize images to **256x256**. This resolution aligns with the standard window sizes of Swin Transformers (multiples of 32) and balances texture detail with computational efficiency.
- **Augmentation (Training):** Apply robust geometric and color augmentations (Flip, Rotate, ColorJitter). Crucially, include **Random Erasing** to force the model to learn distributed features rather than relying on specific visual artifacts Cite {solution_lesson_node_00007}.
- **Normalization:** Use standard ImageNet mean and standard deviation.

## Training
**Strategy:**
- **Backbone Fine-Tuning:** Unfreeze the entire Swin Transformer backbone to adapt the pre-trained features to the dermatological domain Cite {solution_lesson_node_00001}.
- **Imbalance Handling:** Use a **WeightedRandomSampler** to ensure every mini-batch contains a balanced ratio of malignant to benign samples, preventing the model from ignoring the minority class Cite {solution_lesson_node_00005}.
- **Optimizer & Scheduling:** Use **AdamW** optimizer combined with a **OneCycleLR** scheduler. The warm-up phase of OneCycleLR is essential to stabilize the training of the Transformer (which is sensitive to initialization), while the decay phase allows for fine convergence Cite {solution_lesson_node_00007}.
- **Regularization:**
    - **Random Erasing:** Applied **only** during the training phase to prevent overfitting Cite {solution_lesson_node_00008}.
    - **Stochastic Depth (DropPath):** Implement DropPath (a regularization technique specific to Transformers that drops entire paths/layers during training) to prevent the high-capacity Swin model from co-adapting too strongly to the training data.

## Evaluation
**Inference:**
- **Deterministic Pipeline:** Ensure that the validation and test data loaders **do not** apply Random Erasing or stochastic augmentations, providing a clean and reliable benchmark Cite {solution_lesson_node_00008}.
- **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):** Perform inference on the original image and geometric variations (horizontal/vertical flips). Average the predicted probabilities to smooth out noise and improve robustness.
