Based on the accumulated lessons, I propose a structural evolution from the EfficientNet family to a **ConvNeXt** architecture. This represents a shift from Neural Architecture Search (NAS) based models to a modern, manually designed architecture that integrates Vision Transformer design principles (like large kernel sizes) into a Convolutional Network. This change aims to capture the fine-grained dependencies required for species classification more effectively than standard CNNs.

### Model
- **Architecture:** Adopt **ConvNeXt-Tiny** as the backbone.
    - **Rationale:** ConvNeXt modernizes the standard ResNet by incorporating "Transformer-style" blocks, specifically utilizing **7x7 depthwise convolutions**. This large kernel size increases the effective receptive field, allowing the model to better integrate global shape information with local textural details—critical for distinguishing between 1,010 fine-grained species—while avoiding the quadratic complexity of self-attention.
    - **Capacity:** With approximately 28M parameters, this model offers significantly higher capacity than the previously used EfficientNet-B1 (~7.8M) or MobileNetV3, addressing the underfitting tendencies observed in earlier experiments.
- **Initialization:** Initialize with weights pre-trained on **ImageNet-1k**.

### Data
- **Input Resolution:** Increase the training resolution to **256x256**.
    - **Rationale:** Following the principle of **Compound Scaling**, higher resolution inputs are essential for fine-grained tasks to resolve subtle discriminative features (Cite solution_lesson_node_00011).
- **Training Preprocessing:**
    - **Augmentation:** Apply **`RandomResizedCrop`** (scale 0.4-1.0) to force the model to learn from partial views and local details (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
    - **Flips:** Use `RandomHorizontalFlip`.
    - **Color:** Apply only **mild `ColorJitter`** (brightness/contrast/saturation=0.1) and explicitly **avoid hue rotation** or aggressive distortion, as specific color patterns are vital for species identification (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
- **Evaluation Preprocessing:**
    - **Geometry:** Resize the image's shortest edge to **292 pixels** (approx 1.14x target) and perform a **CenterCrop to 256x256**. This strictly preserves the aspect ratio during inference, preventing geometric distortion that degrades performance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).

### Training
- **Loss Function:** Use **Cross Entropy Loss with Label Smoothing (0.1)**.
    - **Rationale:** With the increased capacity of ConvNeXt and the high class cardinality (1,010 classes), Label Smoothing is necessary to prevent the model from becoming over-confident on noisy labels and to improve cluster separation in the embedding space (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
- **Optimizer:** Use **AdamW** (standard for ConvNeXt) with a weight decay of 0.05.
- **Scheduler:** Employ a **Cosine Annealing** schedule.
- **Duration:** Train for **20 Epochs**.
    - **Rationale:** Previous experiments showed that compressing the schedule (e.g., to 6 or 10 epochs) leads to underfitting. The heavier ConvNeXt model and stronger `RandomResizedCrop` augmentation require a larger budget of gradient updates to converge (Cite solution_lesson_node_00012, solution_lesson_node_00013).
- **Efficiency:** Utilize **Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP)** to manage the memory footprint of the larger model and resolution, ensuring the batch size remains efficient (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).

### Evaluation
- **Inference:** Perform a single forward pass on the center-cropped images.
- **Prediction:** Apply Softmax to the logits and select the category with the highest probability (Top-1) for the primary metric, while outputting sorted indices for the submission format.
