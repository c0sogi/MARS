The strategy is to address the performance degradation observed in the previous solution (`idea_46`, MCRMSE ~0.744) by reverting the **Distance Embedding** strategy to the one used in the best-performing models (`idea_25`/`idea_44`, MCRMSE ~0.620).

The previous solution used **Discrete Embeddings** for topological distances with a clipping range of 32. According to **Lesson 114**, aggressive clipping destroys the resolution required to model long-range interactions in RNA, and **functional encodings (like Sinusoidal)** are preferred. Additionally, **Lesson 113** suggests that while functional encodings are good, they should be applied to **discrete tokenized positions** (indices) rather than raw float values to handle the non-linear nature of structural distances.

Therefore, I will:
1.  **Increase `DIST_CLIP`** in `library/config.py` from 32 to 128 to cover the full sequence length (107), ensuring no loss of long-range structural information (Cite Lesson 114).
2.  **Update `library/model.py`** to replace the learnable `nn.Embedding` for distances with a **Fixed Sinusoidal Embedding** initialized with standard positional encoding weights. This combines the benefits of functional encoding with discrete indexing.
3.  **Update `library/data.py`** to use a new cache version (`v2`), forcing the data pipeline to re-process the structure strings with the new, larger clipping range.

The rest of the architecture (Wide-Stream BiGRU, Scalar Mixture, No Stem Dropout) remains unchanged as it has proven effective in previous iterations (Cite Lesson 109).
