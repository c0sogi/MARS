Based on the comprehensive analysis of the previous experiments and the derived lessons, I propose a **Hybrid Dilated ResNet-BiGRU** strategy. This approach is a structural evolution that optimizes the "Hybrid ResNet-BiGRU" concept from Idea 6 by integrating **Dilated Convolutions** for multi-scale feature extraction and correcting the critical training flaws (Loss Function and Sample Weighting) identified in Ideas 5 and 6.

# Model
**Architecture: Dilated Residual Convolutional-Recurrent Network**
The model is designed as a hierarchical encoder that processes the RNA sequence at varying scales of context before aggregating global information.

1.  **Input Embeddings**: We will retain the effective multi-channel embedding strategy. The `sequence`, `structure`, and `predicted_loop_type` inputs will be tokenized, embedded into dense vectors, and concatenated. This preserves the "Sequence-Native" approach which outperforms graph representations **Cite solution_lesson_node_00009**.
2.  **Stage 1: Dilated 1D-ResNet Encoder**: The embeddings will pass through a stack of **Dilated Residual Blocks**.
    *   **Innovation**: Unlike the standard convolutions in Idea 6, we will use **exponentially increasing dilation rates** (e.g., $d=1, 2, 4$) in successive layers.
    *   **Rationale**: Standard CNNs have limited receptive fields, and Bi-GRUs process tokens sequentially. Dilated convolutions allow the model to exponentially expand its receptive field to capture intermediate-range motifs (e.g., large loops or stems) without reducing spatial resolution (pooling) or increasing parameter count. This provides the downstream RNN with richer, context-aware local features.
3.  **Stage 2: Bidirectional GRU**: The output of the Dilated ResNet will be fed into a multi-layer **Bidirectional GRU**.
    *   **Rationale**: While dilated convolutions capture wider local context, they cannot easily model the global folding stability of the entire 107-nucleotide chain. The Bi-GRU ensures a true **Global Receptive Field** **Cite solution_lesson_node_00004** and provides the strong **Sequential Inductive Bias** necessary for small-data regimes **Cite solution_lesson_node_00010**.
4.  **Output Head**: A shared Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) will project the final hidden states to the 5 target degradation values.

# Data
*   **Full-Context Processing**: We will process the complete 107-nucleotide sequence for every sample. We will **not crop** the inputs to the scored length (68), ensuring the model utilizes downstream structural information (e.g., 3' stem halves) that causally affects the 5' scored region **Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**.
*   **Preprocessing**: Inputs will be integer-encoded. No graph topology will be constructed.

# Training
*   **Objective Function: Masked Mean Squared Error (MSE)**.
    *   **Correction**: We will strictly use **MSE (L2) Loss**, rejecting the "Robust" Huber/L1 loss used in Idea 6. Lesson 00012 demonstrated that when the evaluation metric is RMSE, using robust losses causes a misalignment that degrades performance because the model under-predicts outliers **Cite solution_lesson_node_00012**.
    *   **Masking**: The loss will be calculated only for the first 68 positions where ground truth is available **Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**.
*   **No Sample Weighting**: We will **not** weight samples by their signal-to-noise ratio. Lesson 00011 proved that weighting the loss function creates a distribution mismatch with the unweighted evaluation metric, leading to poor generalization on noisy test samples **Cite solution_lesson_node_00011**.
*   **Optimizer**: AdamW optimizer with a Cosine Annealing scheduler to stabilize the hybrid architecture.

# Evaluation
*   **Inference**: The test sequences (length 107) will be passed through the model to generate a `(N, 107, 5)` prediction tensor.
*   **Formatting**: The predictions will be flattened to match the `id_seqpos` format. Values for unscored positions (69-107) will be included in the submission file to meet format requirements, even though they are not scored.
