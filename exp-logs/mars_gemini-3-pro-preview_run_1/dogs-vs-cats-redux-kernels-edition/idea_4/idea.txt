Based on the progression of experiments and the comprehensive analysis of lessons learned, I propose a **Tri-Architecture Heterogeneous Ensemble** strategy. This solution structurally evolves the previous "Heterogeneous Ensemble" by integrating a Transformer-based architecture to maximize the diversity of inductive biases, while strictly enforcing reproducibility and stability protocols identified in recent failures.

# Model
I propose expanding the ensemble to include three distinct architectural families, creating a **Tri-Model Ensemble**:
1.  **EfficientNetV2-M:** Retained for its efficiency and MBConv-based feature extraction (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).
2.  **ConvNeXt-Base:** Retained for its modern, isotropic CNN design that mimics transformers (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015).
3.  **Swin Transformer V2 (Base):** Introduced as the **Structural Innovation**. Unlike the previous two CNN-based models, Swin utilizes **Shifted Window Self-Attention**. This introduces a fundamentally different inductive bias—focusing on global context and long-range dependencies—which complements the local texture bias of CNNs.

By aggregating predictions from these three diverse families (MBConv, Modern CNN, and Hierarchical Transformer), we aim to further reduce variance and error beyond what was achieved with the dual-CNN approach (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015).

# Data
*   **Resolution:** All models will be trained at **384x384** resolution. This has been identified as the optimal saturation point for this dataset; higher resolutions (e.g., 480p) incur computational penalties without performance gains (Cite solution_lesson_node_00013).
*   **Augmentation:** We will apply the **Heavy Augmentation** pipeline (RandomResizedCrop, ShiftScaleRotate, ColorJitter) to prevent overfitting in these high-capacity models (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
*   **Constraints:** We will explicitly **exclude Mixup and CutMix**, as the high domain overlap makes these regularization techniques detrimental to convergence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
*   **Reproducibility:** To address the variance issue identified in previous runs, we will implement a **deterministic worker initialization function** (`worker_init_fn`) in the DataLoaders. This ensures that random augmentations are applied identically across runs, guaranteeing that performance gains are due to model improvements rather than data variance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).

# Training
*   **Strategy:** We will employ a **5-Fold Cross-Validation** scheme for all three architectures, resulting in 15 total trained models.
*   **Optimization:**
    *   **Optimizer:** AdamW with standard parameters.
    *   **Scheduler:** Cosine Annealing learning rate schedule.
    *   **Duration:** Training will be strictly limited to **3 epochs per fold**. We observed that 2 epochs was insufficient (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016), while stretching to 4 epochs with a coupled scheduler diluted the learning rate decay and degraded performance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00017).
    *   **Batch Size:** We will maintain a batch size of **32** (using gradient accumulation if necessary for the Swin model) to ensure Batch Normalization statistics remain stable (Cite solution_lesson_node_00012).

# Evaluation
*   **Test Time Augmentation (TTA):** Inference will be performed on both the original and horizontally flipped versions of each test image (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).
*   **Ensemble Aggregation:** The final submission will be the arithmetic mean of the probabilities from all 15 models (3 architectures × 5 folds). This maximizes the "Bagging" effect across the widest possible range of feature extraction mechanisms.
