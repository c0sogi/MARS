Based on the analysis of previous experiments and the extracted lessons, I propose the **Local-Chemical-Environment Wide Deep Sets (LCE-WDS)** strategy.

This strategy structurally evolves the successful "Augmented Point-Cloud Deep Sets" (Idea 4). It addresses the "Interaction Blindness" of standard Deep Sets (where the model knows *where* atoms are but not *who* surrounds them) by injecting **Local Chemical Environment** descriptors. Unlike previous failed attempts that used sparse vectors (Idea 18, Lesson 58) or discontinuous categorical indices (Idea 24, Lesson 64), this strategy uses **distance-weighted averages of neighbor physical properties**. This provides a smooth, dense, and chemically informative signal about the local bonding environment (e.g., "am I surrounded by electronegative atoms?"). The architecture strictly enforces the **Wide MLP**, **Batch Normalization**, and **Dropout** safeguards identified as critical in Lessons 17, 24, and 36.

### Model
The architecture consists of two parallel processing streams that merge via Late Fusion:

1.  **Atomic Stream (Chemically-Contextualized Processor):**
    *   **Input Features:** For each atom, construct a dense feature vector (13 dimensions):
        *   **Atomic Identity:** One-hot encoding (Al, Ga, In, O) (4 dims).
        *   **Spatial Context:** Centered Cartesian coordinates $(x, y, z)$ relative to the unit cell centroid. This retains critical node-level spatial variance (**Cite Lesson 10**).
        *   **Nearest Neighbor Distance:** The scalar distance to the closest neighbor (PBC-corrected) (**Cite Lesson 15**).
        *   **Local Chemical Environment (LCE):** Three scalar values representing the **Inverse-Distance Weighted Average** of the **Atomic Mass**, **Covalent Radius**, and **Electronegativity** of the $K=12$ nearest neighbors.
            *   Formula: $LCE_p = \frac{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_{12}} (1/d_{ij}) \cdot P_j}{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_{12}} (1/d_{ij})}$, where $P_j$ is the property of neighbor $j$.
            *   This captures the chemical nature of the surrounding cage (e.g., steric bulk, electron affinity) in a continuous, permutation-invariant manner. Unlike the atom's own properties (which are redundant with one-hot encoding per **Lesson 23**), the *neighbor* properties provide unique interaction data not captured by the atom's identity alone.
    *   **Encoder:** A **Wide MLP** (e.g., 512 hidden units) to project features into a high-dimensional latent space via **Immediate Expansion** (**Cite Lesson 6**).
    *   **Regularization:** **Batch Normalization** (**Cite Lesson 36**) and **Dropout** (e.g., 0.1) (**Cite Lesson 24**) are applied after every ReLU activation to handle the heterogeneous scales of the inputs and prevent overfitting.
    *   **Output:** A **Linear** projection (no activation) to the final embedding space (**Cite Lesson 18**).
    *   **Aggregation:** **Dual Pooling** (concatenation of Global Mean and Global Max) to capture both average properties and salient features (**Cite Lesson 5**). Sum pooling is excluded (**Cite Lesson 7**).

2.  **Global Stream (Thermodynamic Context):**
    *   **Input:** Macroscopic features: Lattice vector lengths, lattice angles, unit cell volume, atomic density, **explicit stoichiometry** (**Cite Lesson 13**), and the **Total Number of Atoms** (**Cite Lesson 35**).
    *   **Encoder:** A **High-Capacity MLP** (e.g., 256 units) with Batch Normalization and Dropout to model non-linear boundary condition effects (**Cite Lesson 9**).

3.  **Fusion Head:**
    *   **Late Fusion:** Concatenate the aggregated atomic embedding and the global embedding (**Cite Lesson 19**).
    *   **Regressor:** A final MLP predicts `formation_energy_ev_natom` and `bandgap_energy_ev`.

### Data
1.  **Geometry Preprocessing:**
    *   Parse `geometry.xyz` files.
    *   **Centering:** Subtract the unit cell centroid from all atomic coordinates.
    *   **PBC Neighbor Search:** Compute the pairwise distance matrix using lattice vectors to respect Periodic Boundary Conditions. Identify indices and distances of the 12 nearest neighbors for each atom.
    *   **Feature Extraction:**
        *   Extract $d_{min}$ (NN Dist).
        *   Look up physical properties (Mass, Radius, Electronegativity) for neighbor atoms.
        *   Compute the inverse-distance weighted average for each property to form the LCE vector.
2.  **Tabular Preprocessing:**
    *   Extract lattice parameters, stoichiometry, and **Total Atoms**.
    *   Calculate Unit Cell Volume and Atomic Density.
    *   **Scaling:** Apply Standard Scaling (z-score) to all continuous features (coords, NN dist, LCE features, global features).
3.  **Target Transformation:**
    *   Apply `log(1+y)` to both target variables to align the training objective (MSE) with the evaluation metric (RMSLE).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** Mean Squared Error (MSE) on the log-transformed targets.
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with weight decay ($1e-4$) to regularize the wide layers.
*   **Scheduler:** `ReduceLROnPlateau` monitoring validation loss.
*   **Strategy:** Train for **200 epochs** with early stopping (patience ~20 epochs). The wide, regularized architecture allows for stable convergence over a longer schedule (**Cite Lesson 17**).

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** Process the test set using the identical pipeline (Centering, PBC-LCE calculation, Scaling).
2.  **Prediction:** Pass the processed data through the trained model.
3.  **Inverse Transformation:** Apply `exp(x) - 1` to the model outputs to recover the original energy scale.
4.  **Submission:** Format the results as `id,formation_energy_ev_natom,bandgap_energy_ev`.
