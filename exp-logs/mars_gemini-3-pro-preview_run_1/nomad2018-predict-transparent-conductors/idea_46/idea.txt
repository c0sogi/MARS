Based on the lessons learned from the previous experiments, I propose the **Gated Physics-Informed Multi-Scale Deep Sets (GPI-MS-DS)** strategy.

This strategy represents a structural evolution of the current state-of-the-art **Anisotropic Multi-Scale Physics-Aware Deep Sets** (Idea 44). It refines the feature engineering by introducing **Bond Hardness** (a physics-normalized geometric descriptors) and **Angular Distortion** (a global symmetry descriptor). Furthermore, it evolves the fusion mechanism from simple concatenation to **Gated Conditioning**, allowing the global thermodynamic context to dynamically modulate the importance of local atomic features before regression. It strictly enforces the **Selective Scaling**, **Sparse Batching**, and **Wide/Regularized** architectural constraints that have proven essential for stability.

### Model
The architecture consists of two parallel processing streams that merge via a Gated Fusion mechanism:

1.  **Atomic Stream (Physics-Normalized Point Processor):**
    *   **Input Features:** For each atom, construct a dense feature vector (18 dimensions):
        *   **Atomic Identity:** One-hot encoding (Al, Ga, In, O) (4 dims). **[Unscaled]**
        *   **Spatial Context:** Centered Cartesian coordinates $(x, y, z)$ relative to the unit cell centroid (**Cite Lesson 10**).
        *   **Nearest Neighbor Distance ($d_{min}$):** The scalar distance to the single closest neighbor (PBC-corrected) (**Cite Lesson 15**).
        *   **Local Packing Ratio:** $R = d_{min} / d_{mean\_12}$. A proxy for local structural distortion (**Cite Lesson 82**).
        *   **Bond Hardness Proxy (New):** A dimensionless scalar calculated as $H = d_{min} / (r_{cov} + r_{avg\_neigh})$. Here, $r_{cov}$ is the covalent radius of the atom, and $r_{avg\_neigh}$ is the weighted average covalent radius of the $K=6$ nearest neighbors (derived from the Context vector). This normalizes the geometric bond length by the physical size of the atoms involved, providing a chemical-agnostic measure of "bond tightness" or strain.
        *   **Multi-Scale Chemical Contexts:** Two 4-dimensional vectors representing the **Inverse-Distance Weighted Composition** of the **$K=6$** and **$K=24$** nearest neighbors (**Cite Lesson 92**).
    *   **Encoder:** A **Wide MLP** (e.g., 3 layers of 512 hidden units) to project features into a high-dimensional latent space via **Immediate Expansion** (**Cite Lesson 6**).
    *   **Regularization:** **Batch Normalization** (**Cite Lesson 36**) and **Dropout** (e.g., 0.1) (**Cite Lesson 24**) applied after activations.
    *   **Aggregation:** **Dual Pooling** (concatenation of Global Mean and Global Max) (**Cite Lesson 5**).

2.  **Global Stream (Distortion-Aware Context):**
    *   **Input:**
        *   **Standard:** Lattice vector lengths, lattice angles, unit cell volume, atomic density, **explicit stoichiometry** (**Cite Lesson 13**), and **Total Number of Atoms** (**Cite Lesson 35**).
        *   **Lattice Aspect Ratios:** ($a/b, b/c, c/a$) to capture anisotropy (**Cite Lesson 100**).
        *   **Weighted Physics:** Composition-weighted mean Mass, Radius, and Electronegativity (**Cite Lesson 29**).
        *   **Angular Distortion (New):** A scalar representing the deviation of the lattice from orthogonality: $\Delta_{angle} = \sum |\theta - 90^\circ|$ for $\theta \in \{\alpha, \beta, \gamma\}$. This provides a direct measure of lattice shear strain.
    *   **Encoder:** A **High-Capacity MLP** (e.g., 256 units) with Batch Normalization and Dropout (**Cite Lesson 9**).

3.  **Gated Fusion Head (New):**
    *   Instead of simple concatenation, use the encoded global vector to **gate** the aggregated atomic vector.
    *   Let $Z_{atom}$ be the pooled atomic features and $Z_{global}$ be the encoded global features.
    *   Compute a gate vector $G = \sigma(W \cdot Z_{global} + b)$, where $\sigma$ is the Sigmoid function.
    *   Compute modulated atomic features: $Z'_{atom} = Z_{atom} \odot G$ (element-wise product).
    *   Concatenate $Z'_{atom}$ and $Z_{global}$ and pass to the final Regressor MLP. This allows the global thermodynamic state (e.g., high density/strain) to suppress or amplify specific local geometric signals (e.g., bond lengths) (**Cite Lesson 19** - evolution of fusion).

### Data
1.  **Geometry Preprocessing:**
    *   Parse `geometry.xyz` files.
    *   **Centering:** Subtract unit cell centroid.
    *   **PBC Neighbor Search:** Compute pairwise distances using lattice vectors.
    *   **Feature Extraction:**
        *   Compute $d_{min}$, $d_{mean\_12}$, Packing Ratio.
        *   Compute Weighted Contexts ($K=6, 24$).
        *   **Compute Bond Hardness:** Use a lookup table for covalent radii. Calculate $r_{avg\_neigh}$ using the $K=6$ context weights and the radius lookup. Compute $H$.
2.  **Tabular Preprocessing:**
    *   Extract lattice parameters, stoichiometry, Total Atoms.
    *   Calculate Volume, Density, Aspect Ratios, Weighted Physics.
    *   Calculate **Angular Distortion**.
    *   **Selective Scaling:** Apply Standard Scaling **ONLY** to continuous features. Leave one-hot encodings unscaled (**Cite Lesson 94**).
3.  **Batching:**
    *   Use **Sparse (Flattened) Batching** (**Cite Lesson 89**).
4.  **Target Transformation:**
    *   Apply `log(1+y)` to targets.

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** Mean Squared Error (MSE) on log-transformed targets.
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with weight decay ($1e-4$).
*   **Scheduler:** `ReduceLROnPlateau` monitoring validation loss.
*   **Strategy:** Train for **200 epochs** with early stopping (patience ~20 epochs).

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** Process test set with the identical pipeline.
2.  **Prediction:** Pass data through the trained model.
3.  **Inverse Transformation:** Apply `exp(x) - 1`.
4.  **Submission:** Format as `id,formation_energy_ev_natom,bandgap_energy_ev`.
