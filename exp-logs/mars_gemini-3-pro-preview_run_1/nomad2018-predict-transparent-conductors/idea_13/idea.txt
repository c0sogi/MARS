Based on the lessons learned from the previous experiments, specifically the failure of **Idea 12** due to the omission of system cardinality (Lesson 35) and the failure of **Idea 11** due to improper pooling despite promising features, I propose the **Multi-Coordinate Potential Deep Sets (MC-PDS)** strategy.

This strategy structurally evolves the "Augmented Point-Cloud Deep Sets" (Idea 4) by enriching the atomic representation with **Dual Coordinate Systems** and a **Scalar Potential Proxy**. It strictly enforces the architectural safeguards (Wide MLPs, Dropout, Late Fusion) that have proven necessary for generalization on this dataset.

### Model
The architecture consists of two parallel streams that process local and global information, merging via Late Fusion:

1.  **Atomic Stream (Dual-Coordinate Point Processor):**
    *   **Input Features:** For each atom, construct a feature vector containing:
        *   **Atomic Identity:** One-hot encoding (Al, Ga, In, O).
        *   **Centered Cartesian Coordinates:** $(x, y, z)$ relative to the centroid. Captures absolute spatial scale and bond lengths (**Cite Lesson 10**).
        *   **Fractional Coordinates:** $(u, v, w)$ relative to the unit cell basis vectors. Unlike Cartesian coordinates, these are normalized to the unit cell geometry. Since the atomic branch does not see the lattice vectors until fusion, providing fractional coordinates explicitly injects knowledge of the atom's position relative to the global boundary conditions without the redundancy of full context injection (**Cite Lesson 19**).
        *   **Nearest Neighbor Distance:** Scalar distance to the closest neighbor (**Cite Lesson 15**).
        *   **Local Potential Proxy:** The scalar sum of inverse distances to neighbors ($P_i = \sum_{j \neq i} \frac{1}{d_{ij}}$) using Periodic Boundary Conditions. This captures the local atomic density and electrostatic-like environment more robustly than a single neighbor distance, adhering to the principle of parsimonious scalar descriptors (**Cite Lesson 21**).
    *   **Encoder:** A **Wide MLP** (e.g., 512 hidden units) with **Dropout** after activations. The width prevents information bottlenecks (**Cite Lesson 17**), while Dropout prevents the overfitting observed in high-capacity models (**Cite Lesson 24**).
    *   **Output:** A **Linear** projection to the latent dimension (**Cite Lesson 18**).
    *   **Aggregation:** **Dual Pooling** (concatenation of Global Mean and Global Max). Sum pooling is excluded to maintain intensivity (**Cite Lesson 5, Lesson 7**).

2.  **Global Stream (Thermodynamic Context):**
    *   **Input:** Macroscopic features: Lattice vector lengths, lattice angles, unit cell volume, atomic density, **explicit stoichiometry** (**Cite Lesson 13**), and crucially, the **Total Number of Atoms** (**Cite Lesson 35**).
    *   **Encoder:** A **High-Capacity MLP** (e.g., 256 units) to model non-linear boundary condition effects (**Cite Lesson 9**).

3.  **Fusion Head:**
    *   **Late Fusion:** Concatenate the aggregated atomic embedding and the global embedding.
    *   **Regressor:** A final MLP predicts `formation_energy_ev_natom` and `bandgap_energy_ev`.

### Data
1.  **Geometry Preprocessing:**
    *   Parse `geometry.xyz` files.
    *   **Centering:** Subtract the unit cell centroid from Cartesian coordinates.
    *   **Fractionalization:** Compute fractional coordinates by multiplying Cartesian coordinates by the inverse of the lattice matrix ($X_{frac} = X_{cart} \cdot L^{-1}$).
    *   **PBC Calculations:** Compute the pairwise distance matrix using lattice vectors to respect Periodic Boundary Conditions.
    *   **Feature Extraction:** Extract minimum non-zero distance (NN Dist) and compute the sum of inverse distances (Potential Proxy) for each atom.
2.  **Tabular Preprocessing:**
    *   Extract lattice parameters, stoichiometry, and **Total Atoms**.
    *   Calculate Unit Cell Volume and Atomic Density.
    *   **Scaling:** Apply Standard Scaling (z-score) to all continuous features (coords, distances, potentials, global features).
3.  **Target Transformation:**
    *   Apply `log(1+y)` to both target variables to align the training objective (MSE) with the evaluation metric (RMSLE).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** Mean Squared Error (MSE) on the log-transformed targets.
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with weight decay ($1e-4$) to regularize the wide layers.
*   **Scheduler:** `ReduceLROnPlateau` monitoring validation loss.
*   **Strategy:** Train for **200 epochs** with early stopping (patience ~20 epochs). The wide, regularized architecture allows for stable convergence.

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** Process the test set using the identical pipeline (Centering, Fractionalization, Potential calculation, Scaling).
2.  **Prediction:** Pass the processed data through the trained model.
3.  **Inverse Transformation:** Apply `exp(x) - 1` to the model outputs.
4.  **Submission:** Format the results as `id,formation_energy_ev_natom,bandgap_energy_ev`.
