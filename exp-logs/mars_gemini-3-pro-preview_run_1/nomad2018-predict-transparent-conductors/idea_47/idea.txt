Based on the comprehensive analysis of previous experiments and the extracted lessons, I propose the **Chemically-Explicit Anisotropic Deep Sets (CEADS)** strategy.

This strategy represents a structural synthesis of the most effective components from previous iterations (Idea 44, Idea 45) while integrating novel global descriptors to capture lattice strain. It refines the atomic feature engineering by injecting **Explicit Nearest Neighbor Identity** to resolve specific bonding partners (addressing Interaction Blindness) and enhances the global stream with **Physical Property Variance** and **Angular Distortion** to capture chemical heterogeneity and lattice shear. Crucially, it strictly enforces **Selective Feature Scaling** (Lesson 94) and **Sparse Batching** (Lesson 89) to maintain stability and generalization.

### Model
The architecture consists of two parallel processing streams that merge via Late Fusion:

1.  **Atomic Stream (Chemically-Explicit Point Processor):**
    *   **Input Features:** For each atom, construct a dense feature vector (21 dimensions):
        *   **Atomic Identity:** One-hot encoding (Al, Ga, In, O) (4 dims). **[Unscaled]**
        *   **Nearest Neighbor Identity (New):** One-hot encoding of the single closest neighbor atom (4 dims). This provides explicit chemical context for the $d_{min}$ scalar, allowing the model to learn specific bond-length potentials (e.g., distinguishing a short Al-O bond from a short In-In bond) without the sparsity issues of split distances (**Cite Lesson 58**). **[Unscaled]**
        *   **Spatial Context:** Centered Cartesian coordinates $(x, y, z)$ relative to the unit cell centroid (**Cite Lesson 10**).
        *   **Nearest Neighbor Distance ($d_{min}$):** The scalar distance to the single closest neighbor (PBC-corrected) (**Cite Lesson 15**).
        *   **Local Packing Ratio:** The dimensionless ratio $R = d_{min} / d_{mean\_12}$ (**Cite Lesson 82**).
        *   **Multi-Scale Chemical Contexts:** Two 4-dimensional vectors representing the **Inverse-Distance Weighted Composition** of the **$K=6$** (Short-Range) and **$K=24$** (Medium-Range) nearest neighbors (**Cite Lesson 92**).
    *   **Encoder:** A **Wide MLP** (e.g., 3 layers of 512 hidden units) to project features into a high-dimensional latent space via **Immediate Expansion** (**Cite Lesson 6**).
    *   **Regularization:** **Batch Normalization** (**Cite Lesson 36**) and **Dropout** (e.g., 0.1) (**Cite Lesson 24**) are applied after every ReLU activation.
    *   **Aggregation:** **Dual Pooling** (concatenation of Global Mean and Global Max) (**Cite Lesson 5**).

2.  **Global Stream (Variance & Distortion Context):**
    *   **Input:**
        *   **Geometric:** Lattice vector lengths, lattice angles, unit cell volume, **Lattice Aspect Ratios** (anisotropy) (**Cite Lesson 100**).
        *   **Angular Distortion (New):** A scalar quantifying lattice shear: $\sum |\theta - 90^\circ|$ for $\theta \in \{\alpha, \beta, \gamma\}$.
        *   **Structural:** Atomic density, **Total Number of Atoms** (**Cite Lesson 35**).
        *   **Chemical:** **Explicit Stoichiometry** (composition percentages) (**Cite Lesson 13**).
        *   **Physical Statistics (New):** Calculate the **Mean** AND **Standard Deviation** of Atomic Mass, Covalent Radius, and Electronegativity based on the unit cell composition. The variance explicitly encodes **chemical disorder** and size mismatch (e.g., high variance in radius implies lattice strain), serving as a strong inductive bias (**Cite Lesson 29**).
    *   **Encoder:** A **High-Capacity MLP** (e.g., 256 units) with Batch Normalization and Dropout (**Cite Lesson 9**).

3.  **Fusion Head:**
    *   **Late Fusion:** Concatenate the aggregated atomic embedding and the global embedding (**Cite Lesson 19**).
    *   **Regressor:** A final MLP predicts `formation_energy_ev_natom` and `bandgap_energy_ev`.

### Data
1.  **Geometry Preprocessing:**
    *   Parse `geometry.xyz` files.
    *   **Centering:** Subtract the unit cell centroid from all atomic coordinates.
    *   **PBC Neighbor Search:** Compute pairwise distances using lattice vectors (Periodic Boundary Conditions).
    *   **Feature Extraction:**
        *   Identify neighbors for $K=1$ (Nearest), $K=12$, $K=6$, $K=24$.
        *   Extract $d_{min}$, NN Identity, Packing Ratio, and Weighted Context Vectors.
2.  **Tabular Preprocessing:**
    *   Extract lattice parameters, stoichiometry, and Total Atoms.
    *   Calculate Unit Cell Volume, Atomic Density, Lattice Aspect Ratios, and Angular Distortion.
    *   **Physics Injection:** Calculate Mean and Standard Deviation of physical properties.
    *   **Selective Scaling:** Apply Standard Scaling (z-score) **ONLY** to continuous features. **Do NOT scale** the Atomic Identity or NN Identity one-hot vectors (**Cite Lesson 94**).
3.  **Batching Strategy:**
    *   Use **Sparse (Flattened) Batching** to preserve Batch Normalization statistics (**Cite Lesson 89**).
4.  **Target Transformation:**
    *   Apply `log(1+y)` to both target variables.

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** Mean Squared Error (MSE) on the log-transformed targets.
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with weight decay ($1e-4$).
*   **Scheduler:** `ReduceLROnPlateau` monitoring validation loss.
*   **Strategy:** Train for **200 epochs** with early stopping (patience ~20 epochs). The wide, regularized architecture allows for stable convergence (**Cite Lesson 17**).

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** Process the test set using the identical pipeline (Centering, PBC-Neighbor/Context calculation, Physics Variance, Selective Scaling).
2.  **Prediction:** Pass the processed data through the trained model using sparse batching.
3.  **Inverse Transformation:** Apply `exp(x) - 1` to the model outputs.
4.  **Submission:** Format the results as `id,formation_energy_ev_natom,bandgap_energy_ev`.
