Based on the comprehensive analysis of previous experiments and the extracted lessons, I propose the **Density-Calibrated Chemically-Contextualized Wide Deep Sets (DC3-WDS)** strategy.

This strategy represents a structural synthesis and evolution of **Idea 39** (Soft-Chemical Context) and **Idea 36** (Parsimonious Geometric). It addresses the "Interaction Blindness" of standard Deep Sets by injecting a dense, continuous descriptor of the **Local Chemical Environment** (from Idea 39), while simultaneously capturing **Local Packing Density** (from Idea 36) to resolve geometric ambiguities (e.g., distinguishing between loose and tight packing of the same chemical neighborhood). Crucially, it rigorously enforces the architectural safeguards—**Wide MLPs**, **Batch Normalization**, **Dropout**, and **System Cardinality**—identified as critical for stability and generalization in Lessons 17, 24, 35, and 36.

### Model
The architecture consists of two parallel processing streams that merge via Late Fusion:

1.  **Atomic Stream (Context-Aware Point Processor):**
    *   **Input Features:** For each atom, construct a dense, information-rich feature vector (13 dimensions):
        *   **Atomic Identity:** One-hot encoding (Al, Ga, In, O) (4 dims).
        *   **Spatial Context:** Centered Cartesian coordinates $(x, y, z)$ relative to the unit cell centroid. This retains critical node-level spatial variance (**Cite Lesson 10**).
        *   **Nearest Neighbor Distance ($d_{min}$):** The scalar distance to the single closest neighbor (PBC-corrected). This serves as a sharp, high-signal proxy for immediate steric constraints (**Cite Lesson 15**).
        *   **Local Packing Density ($d_{mean}$):** The arithmetic mean of the distances to the $K=12$ nearest neighbors (PBC-corrected). This provides a robust scalar proxy for local crowding, complementing the sharp $d_{min}$ with a smoother environmental descriptor (**Cite Lesson 22**).
        *   **Soft-Chemical Context:** A 4-dimensional vector representing the **Inverse-Distance Weighted Composition** of the $K=12$ nearest neighbors.
            *   Weights $w_{ij} = 1 / d_{ij}$.
            *   Normalized Context $C_i = \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i} (w_{ij} / \sum w) \cdot \text{OneHot}(type_j)$.
            *   This vector sums to 1.0 and smoothly encodes the chemical identity of the surrounding cage (e.g., "rich in Oxygen") without the sparsity of split distances (**Cite Lesson 58**) or the discontinuity of categorical neighbors (**Cite Lesson 64**).
    *   **Encoder:** A **Wide MLP** (e.g., 3 layers of 512 hidden units) to project features into a high-dimensional latent space via **Immediate Expansion** (**Cite Lesson 6**).
    *   **Regularization:** **Batch Normalization** (**Cite Lesson 36**) and **Dropout** (e.g., 0.1) (**Cite Lesson 24**) are applied after every ReLU activation. This is essential to handle the heterogeneous scales of the inputs and prevent overfitting.
    *   **Output:** A **Linear** projection (no activation) to the final embedding space (**Cite Lesson 18**).
    *   **Aggregation:** **Dual Pooling** (concatenation of Global Mean and Global Max) to capture both average properties and salient features (**Cite Lesson 5**). Sum pooling is excluded (**Cite Lesson 7**).

2.  **Global Stream (Thermodynamic Context):**
    *   **Input:** Macroscopic features: Lattice vector lengths, lattice angles, unit cell volume, atomic density, **explicit stoichiometry** (**Cite Lesson 13**), and the **Total Number of Atoms** (**Cite Lesson 35**).
    *   **Encoder:** A **High-Capacity MLP** (e.g., 256 units) with Batch Normalization and Dropout to model non-linear boundary condition effects (**Cite Lesson 9**).

3.  **Fusion Head:**
    *   **Late Fusion:** Concatenate the aggregated atomic embedding and the global embedding (**Cite Lesson 19**).
    *   **Regressor:** A final MLP predicts `formation_energy_ev_natom` and `bandgap_energy_ev`.

### Data
1.  **Geometry Preprocessing:**
    *   Parse `geometry.xyz` files.
    *   **Centering:** Subtract the unit cell centroid from all atomic coordinates.
    *   **PBC Neighbor Search:** Compute the pairwise distance matrix using lattice vectors to respect Periodic Boundary Conditions. Identify the $K=12$ nearest neighbors for each atom.
    *   **Feature Extraction:**
        *   Extract $d_{min}$ (minimum distance) and $d_{mean}$ (mean of 12 distances).
        *   Compute the **Soft-Chemical Context** vector using normalized inverse-distance weighting of the neighbors' identities.
2.  **Tabular Preprocessing:**
    *   Extract lattice parameters, stoichiometry, and **Total Atoms**.
    *   Calculate Unit Cell Volume and Atomic Density.
    *   **Scaling:** Apply Standard Scaling (z-score) to all continuous features (atomic coords, geometric scalars, context vector, global features).
3.  **Target Transformation:**
    *   Apply `log(1+y)` to both target variables to align the training objective (MSE) with the evaluation metric (RMSLE).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** Mean Squared Error (MSE) on the log-transformed targets.
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with weight decay ($1e-4$) to regularize the wide layers.
*   **Scheduler:** `ReduceLROnPlateau` monitoring validation loss.
*   **Strategy:** Train for **200 epochs** with early stopping (patience ~20 epochs). The wide, regularized architecture allows for stable convergence over a longer schedule (**Cite Lesson 17**).

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** Process the test set using the identical pipeline (Centering, PBC-Neighbor/Context calculation, Scaling).
2.  **Prediction:** Pass the processed data through the trained model.
3.  **Inverse Transformation:** Apply `exp(x) - 1` to the model outputs to recover the original energy scale.
4.  **Submission:** Format the results as `id,formation_energy_ev_natom,bandgap_energy_ev`.
