Based on the comprehensive analysis of previous experiments and the extracted lessons, I propose the **Symmetry-Informed Residual Deep Sets with Statistical Pooling (SI-RDS-SP)** strategy.

This strategy evolves the successful "Augmented Point-Cloud Deep Sets" (Idea 4) by upgrading the atomic encoder to a Residual architecture to support deeper feature extraction, enriching the aggregation mechanism with statistical variance (Standard Deviation), and explicitly modeling crystallographic symmetry. It strictly adheres to the successful feature engineering of Idea 4 (Nearest Neighbor distance, Explicit Stoichiometry) while addressing the regularization and capacity issues identified in Lesson 24 and Lesson 17.

### Model
The architecture consists of three converging streams that process local, global, and symmetry information independently before a late fusion:

1.  **Atomic Stream (Residual Point Processor):**
    *   **Input Features:** A compact, non-redundant feature vector for each atom: **One-hot encoding** of the element (Al, Ga, In, O), **Centered Cartesian coordinates** (Cite Lesson 10), and the **PBC-corrected Nearest Neighbor Distance** (Cite Lesson 15). Physical constants are excluded to avoid redundancy (Cite Lesson 23).
    *   **Backbone:** A **Wide Residual MLP** consisting of stacked Residual Blocks (Linear $\to$ BatchNorm $\to$ ReLU $\to$ Dropout $\to$ Linear $\to$ BatchNorm $\to$ Add Input $\to$ ReLU). This structural innovation allows the model to learn deeper, more complex non-linear transformations of the local geometry without suffering from gradient degradation or the overfitting observed in Idea 6 (Cite Lesson 24).
    *   **Aggregation (Tri-Pooling):** The atomic embeddings are aggregated into a global vector using **Mean**, **Max**, and **Standard Deviation** pooling. While Mean and Max capture average properties and salient features (Cite Lesson 5), the novel addition of **Std Dev pooling** explicitly encodes the *variance* or heterogeneity of the local atomic environments (e.g., structural distortion), providing a richer fingerprint for energy prediction without the extensivity problems of Sum pooling (Cite Lesson 7).

2.  **Global Stream (Thermodynamic Context):**
    *   **Input:** Macroscopic features: Lattice vector lengths, lattice angles, unit cell volume, atomic density, and **explicit stoichiometry** (Cite Lesson 13).
    *   **Backbone:** A **High-Capacity MLP** (e.g., 3 layers, 256 units) to process these boundary conditions, ensuring the model has sufficient capacity to resolve energy variations driven by macroscopic constraints (Cite Lesson 9).

3.  **Symmetry Stream (Crystallographic Prior):**
    *   **Input:** The Spacegroup ID (integer).
    *   **Backbone:** A learnable **Embedding Layer** followed by a linear projection. This injects high-level crystallographic constraints that provide a strong prior for the potential energy surface, complementing the raw geometric data.

4.  **Fusion Head:**
    *   **Late Fusion:** Concatenate the Tri-Pooled atomic vector, the Global embedding, and the Symmetry embedding. This ensures local features are fully aggregated before being conditioned on global contexts (Cite Lesson 19).
    *   **Regressor:** A final MLP predicts `formation_energy_ev_natom` and `bandgap_energy_ev`.

### Data
1.  **Geometry Preprocessing:**
    *   Parse `geometry.xyz` files.
    *   **Centering:** Subtract the unit cell centroid from all atomic coordinates.
    *   **PBC Neighbor Calculation:** Compute the pairwise distance matrix using the lattice vectors to strictly respect **Periodic Boundary Conditions**. Extract the minimum non-zero distance for each atom as the "Nearest Neighbor" feature.
2.  **Tabular Preprocessing:**
    *   Extract lattice parameters and stoichiometry.
    *   Calculate derived features: **Unit Cell Volume** and **Atomic Density**.
    *   Extract Spacegroup ID.
    *   **Scaling:** Apply Standard Scaling (z-score) to all continuous features (atomic coords, NN distance, global features) to ensure balanced gradients.
3.  **Target Transformation:**
    *   Apply `log(1+y)` to both target variables to align the training objective (MSE) with the evaluation metric (RMSLE).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** Mean Squared Error (MSE) on the log-transformed targets.
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with weight decay (e.g., 1e-4) to regularize the wide layers.
*   **Scheduler:** `ReduceLROnPlateau` monitoring validation loss.
*   **Strategy:** Train for **200 epochs** with early stopping (patience ~20 epochs). The use of Residual blocks and Dropout enables deeper training without the rapid overfitting seen in previous high-capacity attempts.

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** Process the test set using the identical pipeline (Centering, PBC-Neighbor calculation, Scaling).
2.  **Prediction:** Pass the processed data through the trained model.
3.  **Inverse Transformation:** Apply `exp(x) - 1` to the model outputs to recover the original energy scale.
4.  **Submission:** Format the results as `id,formation_energy_ev_natom,bandgap_energy_ev`.
