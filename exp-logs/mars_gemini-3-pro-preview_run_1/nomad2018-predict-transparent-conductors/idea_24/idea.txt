Based on the accumulated lessons, I propose the **Anisotropic Chemically-Contextualized Wide Deep Sets (ACC-WDS)** strategy.

This strategy structurally evolves the successful "Augmented Point-Cloud Deep Sets" (Idea 4) by resolving two specific blind spots in its feature engineering: the lack of *chemical specificity* in the nearest-neighbor interaction and the lack of *geometric shape* information in the local environment. It addresses these by introducing **Nearest Neighbor Identity** (avoiding the sparsity issues of Idea 18/Lesson 58) and **Local Inertia Eigenvalues** (providing rotationally invariant shape descriptors). Crucially, the architecture rigorously enforces the regularization and capacity constraints (Wide MLPs, Batch Normalization, Dropout) that were identified as essential for stability in Lessons 17, 24, and 36.

### Model
The architecture consists of two parallel processing streams that merge via Late Fusion:

1.  **Atomic Stream (Anisotropy-Aware Point Processor):**
    *   **Input Features:** For each atom, construct a dense, low-dimensional feature vector (15 dimensions):
        *   **Atomic Identity:** One-hot encoding of the atom's element (Al, Ga, In, O) (4 dims).
        *   **Nearest Neighbor Identity (New):** One-hot encoding of the element type of the single closest neighbor (PBC-corrected). This injects explicit chemical bond context (e.g., distinguishing Al-O from In-In) without the sparsity/placeholder problems of splitting distances (**Cite Lesson 58**).
        *   **Spatial Context:** Centered Cartesian coordinates $(x, y, z)$ relative to the unit cell centroid to retain node-level spatial variance (**Cite Lesson 10**).
        *   **Nearest Neighbor Distance:** The scalar distance to the closest neighbor (**Cite Lesson 15**).
        *   **Local Inertia Eigenvalues (New):** The three sorted eigenvalues $(\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3)$ of the covariance matrix computed from the relative positions of the $K=12$ nearest neighbors. These scalars provide a rotationally invariant proxy for the local packing shape (linear, planar, or spherical) without the high dimensionality of raw neighbor vectors (**Cite Lesson 21**).
    *   **Encoder:** A **Wide MLP** (e.g., 512 hidden units) to project these features into a high-dimensional latent space via **Immediate Expansion** (**Cite Lesson 6**).
    *   **Regularization:** **Batch Normalization** (**Cite Lesson 36**) and **Dropout** (**Cite Lesson 24**) are applied after every ReLU activation. This is critical to normalize the heterogeneous input scales (one-hot vs. eigenvalues vs. coords) and prevent overfitting in the wide layers.
    *   **Output:** A **Linear** projection (no activation) to the final embedding space (**Cite Lesson 18**).
    *   **Aggregation:** **Dual Pooling** (concatenation of Global Mean and Global Max) to capture both average properties and salient features (**Cite Lesson 5**). Sum pooling is explicitly excluded (**Cite Lesson 7**).

2.  **Global Stream (Thermodynamic Context):**
    *   **Input:** Macroscopic features: Lattice vector lengths, lattice angles, unit cell volume, atomic density, **explicit stoichiometry** (**Cite Lesson 13**), and the **Total Number of Atoms** (**Cite Lesson 35**).
    *   **Encoder:** A **High-Capacity MLP** (e.g., 256 units) with Batch Normalization and Dropout to model non-linear boundary condition effects (**Cite Lesson 9**).

3.  **Fusion Head:**
    *   **Late Fusion:** Concatenate the aggregated atomic embedding and the global embedding (**Cite Lesson 19**).
    *   **Regressor:** A final MLP predicts `formation_energy_ev_natom` and `bandgap_energy_ev`.

### Data
1.  **Geometry Preprocessing:**
    *   Parse `geometry.xyz` files.
    *   **Centering:** Subtract the unit cell centroid from all atomic coordinates.
    *   **PBC Calculations:** Compute the pairwise distance matrix using lattice vectors to respect Periodic Boundary Conditions.
    *   **Feature Extraction:**
        *   Identify the nearest neighbor index for each atom to extract **NN Distance** and **NN Identity**.
        *   Identify the $K=12$ nearest neighbors to compute the $3 \times 3$ covariance matrix of their relative positions and extract **3 Eigenvalues**.
2.  **Tabular Preprocessing:**
    *   Extract lattice parameters, stoichiometry, and **Total Atoms**.
    *   Calculate Unit Cell Volume and Atomic Density.
    *   **Scaling:** Apply Standard Scaling (z-score) to all continuous features (coords, distances, eigenvalues, global features).
3.  **Target Transformation:**
    *   Apply `log(1+y)` to both target variables to align the training objective (MSE) with the evaluation metric (RMSLE).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** Mean Squared Error (MSE) on the log-transformed targets.
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with weight decay ($1e-4$) to regularize the wide layers.
*   **Scheduler:** `ReduceLROnPlateau` monitoring validation loss.
*   **Strategy:** Train for **200 epochs** with early stopping (patience ~20 epochs). The wide, regularized architecture allows for stable convergence over a longer schedule (**Cite Lesson 17**).

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** Process the test set using the identical pipeline (Centering, PBC-Neighbor/Eigenvalue calculation, Scaling).
2.  **Prediction:** Pass the processed data through the trained model.
3.  **Inverse Transformation:** Apply `exp(x) - 1` to the model outputs.
4.  **Submission:** Format the results as `id,formation_energy_ev_natom,bandgap_energy_ev`.
