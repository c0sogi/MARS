Based on the comprehensive history of experiments and the specific lessons learned, I propose the **Time-Preserving Selective Kernel (SK) ResNet-18 CRNN**.

This solution introduces a **Structural Innovation** by replacing the standard residual blocks of the ResNet backbone with **Selective Kernel (SK) Units**. This design evolves the "Attention" concept from the previous best solution (Coordinate Attention) by applying it to the **kernel scale** (receptive field) rather than just spatial positioning. This allows the model to dynamically adapt its receptive field size to capture both the sharp, transient onset of the whale call (requiring small kernels) and the longer, smooth frequency sweep (requiring larger kernels), while strictly adhering to the successful temporal modeling and data constraints established in prior lessons.

### Model

The architecture is a **Convolutional Recurrent Neural Network (CRNN)** that integrates a dynamic-receptive-field backbone with a robust temporal aggregator.

1.  **Backbone: Time-Preserving SK-ResNet-18**
    *   **Structural Innovation (Selective Kernel Units)**: We replace the standard `BasicBlock` of the ResNet-18 with **Selective Kernel (SK) Blocks**.
        *   **Mechanism**: Each SK block splits the input feature map into two branches with different kernel sizes (e.g., $3\times3$ and $5\times5$, or $3\times3$ and dilated $3\times3$). The outputs are summed and passed through a channel-wise attention mechanism (Global Average Pooling + FC + Softmax) that generates selection weights. These weights dynamically fuse the branches, allowing the network to adjust its receptive field size per channel based on the input signal content.
        *   **Relevance**: This is critical for right whale calls, which exhibit multi-scale features: sharp, transient clicks (best captured by small kernels) and elongated frequency up-sweeps (best captured by large/dilated kernels).
    *   **Time-Preservation**: We strictly maintain the **Asymmetric Strides `(2, 1)`** (Frequency, Time) in the final two residual stages (Layers 3 and 4). This ensures the temporal sequence length remains sufficient (~50-60 steps) for the downstream RNN, preventing the signal collapse observed in standard backbones (Cite solution_lesson_node_00012).
    *   **Initialization**: Initialize with **ImageNet pre-trained weights** (e.g., from `timm`'s `skresnet18`). Programmatically average the weights of the first convolutional layer to adapt the 3-channel RGB weights to the 1-channel spectrogram input (Cite solution_lesson_node_00020).

2.  **Temporal Modeling: Bi-Directional GRU**
    *   The output feature maps are pooled along the frequency dimension and permuted to a sequence format $(T \times C)$. This sequence is fed into a **Bi-directional GRU**, retaining the component proven most effective for modeling the specific temporal contour of the right whale call (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).

3.  **Aggregation: Attention Pooling**
    *   We utilize an **Attention Pooling** layer to compute a weighted sum of the GRU outputs. This focuses the final classification decision on the specific timeframe containing the call and suppresses background noise (Cite Idea 3).

### Data

1.  **Preprocessing**:
    *   **Input Window**: Generate Log-Mel Spectrograms covering the **full 2.0-second duration** of the clip. Do not truncate to 1.0s, ensuring the signal is never discarded (Cite solution_lesson_node_00023).
    *   **Spectrogram Parameters**: Use **128 Mel bins** to support the deeper backbone and a hop length tuned to produce ~200 time frames.
    *   **Channel Format**: Use a **1-channel** input representation. Do not repeat the spectrogram to 3 channels (Cite solution_lesson_node_00042).

2.  **Augmentation**:
    *   **Mixup**: Apply Mixup ($\alpha=0.4$) to training batches to regularize the model and accelerate convergence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00020).
    *   **SpecAugment**: Apply Frequency and Time Masking.
    *   **Constraint**: Strictly limit the **Time Mask width to a maximum of 200ms** (approx. 20 frames). This ensures the augmentation does not occlude the entire short-duration whale call, preventing the introduction of label noise (Cite solution_lesson_node_00011).

### Training

1.  **Loss Function**: Use **Binary Cross-Entropy with Logits Loss**.
    *   **Class Weighting**: Apply a **Positive Class Weight** (e.g., 9.0) to explicitly handle the 1:9 class imbalance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00025).
    *   **Target Handling**: Compute loss against the **mixed soft targets** generated by Mixup.

2.  **Optimizer**: Use **Adam** with a standard learning rate (e.g., 1e-3).

3.  **Scheduler**: Implement **ReduceLROnPlateau** to decay the learning rate when Validation AUC stagnates (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).

4.  **Metric Validity**: Monitor **Validation AUC** on unmixed data. Do not rely on training metrics computed against hard labels during Mixup, as they are mathematically invalid (Cite solution_lesson_node_00022).

### Evaluation

1.  **Inference**: Process test clips into 2.0s Log-Mel Spectrograms (128 bins).
2.  **Prediction**: Pass inputs through the Time-Preserving SK-ResNet-18 model.
3.  **Output**: Apply a Sigmoid activation to the final output of the Attention Pooling layer to generate the probability `p` for the submission file.
