Based on the comprehensive failure analysis and the successes of previous iterations, I propose the **Time-Preserving EfficientNet-BiGRU with Attention Pooling**.

This solution structurally evolves the "Current Best" ResNet-based approach (Lesson 00020) by transitioning to an **EfficientNet** backbone. It specifically corrects the fatal data-loading flaw that caused the previous EfficientNet attempt to fail (Lesson 00023) and strictly adheres to the temporal resolution and augmentation constraints identified in the lessons.

### Model
The architecture is a **Convolutional Recurrent Neural Network (CRNN)** designed to combine efficient spectral feature extraction with robust temporal sequence modeling.

1.  **Backbone: Time-Preserving EfficientNet-B0**
    We replace the ResNet-18 backbone with an **EfficientNet-B0**.
    *   **Why EfficientNet**: It employs **Inverted Residual Blocks (MBConv)** with **Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE)** modules. The SE blocks explicitly model channel interdependencies, allowing the network to selectively emphasize frequency bands relevant to the whale call while suppressing noise, offering a structural advantage over ResNet's standard convolutions.
    *   **Weight Initialization**: Initialize with **ImageNet pre-trained weights**. Programmatically average the weights of the first convolutional layer (3 channels) to 1 channel to adapt to the spectrogram input, enabling rapid convergence (Cite Lesson 00020).
    *   **Time-Preservation (Critical)**: Standard EfficientNet downsamples the input by 32x, which destroys the temporal structure of the 2-second clip. We will modify the strides of the final three stages (blocks) to be **Asymmetric `(2, 1)`** (Frequency, Time). This compresses the frequency dimension to extract abstract features while maintaining a temporal sequence length of ~50 steps (downsampling time by only 4x), ensuring the downstream RNN receives a sequence with sufficient resolution to detect the "up-sweep" motion (Cite Lesson 00012).

2.  **Temporal Modeling: Bi-Directional GRU**
    The output feature maps are pooled along the frequency dimension and permuted to a sequence format $(T \times C)$. This sequence is fed into a **Bi-directional GRU**. We retain this component as it is proven to be the most effective method for modeling the specific temporal contour of the right whale call (Cite Lesson 00001).

3.  **Aggregation: Attention Pooling**
    Instead of Global Max Pooling, we utilize **Attention Pooling**. A learnable context vector computes a weight for each time step in the GRU output, creating a weighted sum. This allows the model to dynamically focus on the specific timeframe containing the call and ignore the background noise sections.

### Data
1.  **Preprocessing**:
    *   Generate **Log-Mel Spectrograms** with **128 Mel bins** (to support the deeper backbone), a window size of 25ms, and a hop length of 10ms.
    *   **Critical Correction**: Ensure the input window covers the **full 2.0-second duration** of the audio clips. Do not truncate to 1.0s or fixed frame counts that are shorter than the maximum clip length, as this was the root cause of the previous EfficientNet failure (Cite Lesson 00023).

2.  **Augmentation**:
    *   **Mixup**: Apply Mixup ($\alpha=0.4$) to training batches to regularize the model and accelerate convergence (Cite Lesson 00020).
    *   **SpecAugment**: Apply Frequency and Time Masking.
    *   **Constraint**: Strictly limit the **Time Mask width to a maximum of 200ms** (approx. 20 frames). This ensures the augmentation does not occlude the entire short-duration whale call, preventing the introduction of label noise (Cite Lesson 00011).

### Training
*   **Loss Function**: Use **Binary Cross-Entropy with Logits Loss**.
*   **Target Handling**: Compute the loss against the **mixed soft targets** generated by Mixup.
*   **Metric Validity**: Do not rely on training metrics (Accuracy/AUC) calculated against original hard labels, as they are misleading under Mixup. Monitor progress solely via **Validation AUC** computed on unmixed data (Cite Lesson 00022).
*   **Optimizer**: Use **Adam** with a standard learning rate.
*   **Scheduler**: Implement **ReduceLROnPlateau** to decay the learning rate when Validation AUC stagnates (Cite Lesson 00006).

### Evaluation
*   **Inference**: Process the test set clips into 2.0s Log-Mel Spectrograms (128 bins).
*   **Prediction**: Pass the spectrograms through the Time-Preserving EfficientNet-BiGRU model.
*   **Output**: Apply a Sigmoid activation to the final output of the Attention Pooling layer to generate the probability `p` for the submission file.
