The strategy focuses on improving the input signal resolution and computational efficiency, supported by the provided lessons.

1.  **Enhance Input Resolution (Cite Lesson 00025, Lesson 00064):** The previous solution used a `HOP_LENGTH` of 512, resulting in a temporal resolution of ~256ms and a sequence length of only ~8 frames for a 2-second clip. This is too coarse to capture the "up-call" morphology of right whale calls. I will reduce `HOP_LENGTH` to 64 (32ms), increasing the sequence length to ~63 frames. This provides the CRNN with sufficient temporal detail to model the signal's evolution.
2.  **Scale Augmentation (Cite Lesson 00011):** With the finer resolution (32ms per frame), the previous `TIME_MASK_PARAM` of 1 is negligible. To maintain effective regularization while respecting the "mask < 200ms" constraint (to avoid occluding the entire call), I will increase `TIME_MASK_PARAM` to 6 (approx. 192ms).
3.  **Optimize Compute:** Processing higher-resolution spectrograms increases computational load. To fit within the runtime limit on the A100 GPU, I will increase `BATCH_SIZE` from 32 to 128. This improves GPU utilization and training throughput.
