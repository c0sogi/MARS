Based on the comprehensive analysis of previous experiments and lessons learned, I propose the **Deep Residual U-Net with Coordinate Attention and SWA-Lovasz Curriculum**.

This strategy synthesizes the robust **Deep Residual Backbone** (proven in `Cite solution_lesson_node_00021`) with a **Substitutive Loss Curriculum** (proven in `Cite solution_lesson_node_00053`) and introduces **Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA)** to resolve the "sharpness-calibration" conflict identified in `Cite solution_lesson_node_00047`. Additionally, it upgrades the attention mechanism to **Coordinate Attention** to better capture the stratified nature of seismic data.

### 1. Model: Deep Residual U-Net with Coordinate Attention
I will employ a custom **Deep Residual U-Net** that balances capacity with regularization, specifically designed to handle the anisotropic textures of salt deposits.

*   **Backbone (Encoder):** A custom Deep Residual Encoder initialized with **64 filters** and expanding to a bottleneck of **512 filters**. I will explicitly avoid the 1024-filter bottleneck, as `Cite solution_lesson_node_00049` demonstrated that excessive capacity hinders convergence on this dataset.
*   **Decoder (Coordinate Attention):**
    *   **Architecture:** I will replace the scSE modules with **Coordinate Attention (CA)** blocks. While scSE (`Cite solution_lesson_node_00017`) is effective, CA factorizes attention into two parallel 1D feature encodings (height and width). This is structurally superior for seismic images, where salt boundaries often follow stratified, horizontal patterns, allowing the model to capture long-range dependencies in one direction while preserving precise position in the other.
    *   **Placement:** These modules will be placed *only* in the decoder to avoid disrupting the encoder's feature extraction `Cite solution_lesson_node_00017`.
    *   **Upsampling:** I will strictly use **Bilinear Upsampling** followed by a convolution, avoiding Transpose Convolutions to prevent checkerboard artifacts `Cite solution_lesson_node_00026`.
*   **Deep Supervision:** Auxiliary prediction heads will be attached at resolutions $32 \times 32$, $64 \times 64$, and $128 \times 128$ `Cite solution_lesson_node_00021`.
*   **Input Fusion:** Depth information (`z`) will be fused as a dense channel at the input `Cite solution_lesson_node_00002`.

### 2. Data: Maximum Utilization and Integrity
*   **Preprocessing:** Images will be padded to $128 \times 128$ using **Reflection Padding** to handle the $101 \times 101$ dimensions without introducing edge artifacts `Cite solution_lesson_node_00002`.
*   **Data Partitioning:** I will use a single **90/10 Stratified Split** (Train/Val). I will explicitly avoid K-Fold Cross-Validation to maximize the training signal for the single model `Cite solution_lesson_node_00019`.
*   **Augmentation:** I will restrict augmentation to **Horizontal Flips** only. I will strictly avoid interpolation-heavy transformations (rotation, scaling) to preserve the edge sharpness required for the high-IoU metric `Cite solution_lesson_node_00024`.

### 3. Training: Substitutive Curriculum with Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA)
This pipeline addresses the trade-off where Lovasz loss optimizes the metric but degrades ensemble calibration (`Cite solution_lesson_node_00047`).

*   **Optimizer & Scheduler:** **AdamW** with **Cosine Annealing Warm Restarts**.
    *   **Schedule:** **3 constant cycles of 50 epochs** (Total: 150 epochs). The constant cycle length allows the model to settle into robust minima `Cite solution_lesson_node_00023`.
*   **Phase 1 (Cycles 1 & 2, Epochs 0-100):**
    *   **Loss:** **BCE + Sample-Wise Dice Loss**. Dice is calculated per-image to correctly penalize empty masks `Cite solution_lesson_node_00025`.
    *   **Goal:** Establish a robust, calibrated feature extractor.
*   **Phase 2 (Cycle 3, Epochs 101-150):**
    *   **Loss Substitution:** Switch to **BCE + Lovasz-Hinge Loss**. I will *remove* the Dice term to avoid gradient conflict, adhering to the "Substitutive" strategy proved superior in `Cite solution_lesson_node_00053`.
    *   **Innovation (SWA):** During the final 20 epochs of this cycle, I will implement **Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA)**. Instead of saving a single "best" checkpoint (which might be a sharp, unstable minimum on the jagged Lovasz surface `Cite solution_lesson_node_00018`), I will average the model weights at the end of each epoch. This creates a "centroid" model that retains the metric-optimizing properties of Lovasz while restoring the smoothness and generalization required for effective ensembling.

### 4. Evaluation: Hybrid Snapshot Ensembling
*   **Inference Strategy:** I will ensemble (average) the predictions of:
    1.  **Best Model from Cycle 2:** A robust, probabilistically calibrated model trained with Dice.
    2.  **SWA Model from Cycle 3:** A metric-tuned, weight-averaged model trained with Lovasz.
    This combination leverages the diversity of the optimization landscape (`Cite solution_lesson_node_00035`) while avoiding the "weak+strong" degradation (`Cite solution_lesson_node_00050`) by ensuring the Lovasz model is stabilized via SWA.
*   **TTA:** Test-Time Augmentation (Horizontal Flip) will be applied to each snapshot `Cite solution_lesson_node_00004`.
*   **Post-Processing:** Predictions will be center-cropped to $101 \times 101$ `Cite solution_lesson_node_00002`.
