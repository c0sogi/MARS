### Proposed Strategy: Hypercolumn Depth-Aware U-Net with Lovasz Fine-Tuning

Based on the analysis of previous experiments and lessons learned, I propose a structural evolution that combines the efficiency of the custom lightweight architecture with a multi-scale aggregation mechanism (Hypercolumns) and a robust optimization pipeline (Lovasz loss + Ensembling).

#### 1. Model: Hypercolumn Depth-Aware U-Net
I will retain the **Custom Lightweight U-Net** backbone rather than the ResNet34 variant to avoid the over-parameterization risks identified in **Cite solution_lesson_node_00015**. However, I will fundamentally alter the reconstruction head to improve boundary precision.

*   **Encoder:** A custom 4-block convolutional encoder. Depth information (`z`) will be fused at the input level as a dense channel **Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**.
*   **Decoder:** A symmetrical decoder equipped with **Concurrent Spatial and Channel Squeeze & Excitation (scSE)** modules. Crucially, these attention modules will be placed *only* in the decoder to focus on semantic feature recalibration without disrupting the encoder's feature extraction **Cite solution_lesson_node_00007**, **Cite solution_lesson_node_00017**.
*   **Structural Innovation (Hypercolumns):** Instead of generating the final mask solely from the last decoder block, I will implement a **Hypercolumn** head. The output feature maps from *all* decoder levels (after the scSE block) will be upsampled to the input resolution and concatenated along the channel dimension. A final $1 \times 1$ convolution will process this aggregated tensor. This allows the classifier to simultaneously reason using high-level semantic "blobs" (from deep layers) and low-level texture edges (from shallow layers), directly addressing the need for pixel-perfect precision.

#### 2. Data: Stratified Cross-Validation and Preprocessing
*   **Preprocessing:** Images will be padded to $128 \times 128$ using **Reflection Padding** to handle the irregular $101 \times 101$ dimensions without introducing edge artifacts **Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**.
*   **Pipeline Structure:** I will move from a single train/val split to **5-Fold Stratified Cross-Validation**. This ensures that the model is evaluated on all available data and reduces the variance caused by the small dataset size. Stratification will be based on the salt coverage class.

#### 3. Training: Two-Stage Optimization with Metric Alignment
The training strategy will be overhauled to align the optimization objective with the competition metric.

*   **Stage 1 (Convergence):** Train the model using the composite **BCE + Dice Loss** to achieve stable convergence and general object localization **Cite solution_lesson_node_00006**.
*   **Stage 2 (Fine-Tuning):** Switch the loss function to **Lovasz-Softmax Loss** for the final epochs. Lovasz-Softmax is a differentiable surrogate for the Jaccard index (IoU). Fine-tuning with this loss will directly optimize the intersection-over-union metric, refining boundaries more effectively than BCE/Dice alone.
*   **Scheduling:** The learning rate scheduler (ReduceLROnPlateau) will monitor the **Validation mAP (Metric)** rather than Validation Loss. This ensures that learning rate decays are triggered by stagnation in the actual target metric, preventing premature optimization stops **Cite solution_lesson_node_00016**.
*   **Duration:** Training will run for an extended period (e.g., 100 epochs) to capture the "long tail" of convergence where boundary sharpening occurs **Cite solution_lesson_node_00011**.

#### 4. Evaluation: Ensemble and TTA
*   **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):** Inference will be performed on the original image and a horizontally flipped version, with predictions averaged **Cite solution_lesson_node_00004**.
*   **Ensembling:** The final submission will be an average of the probability maps generated by the 5 models trained during the Cross-Validation phase. This ensemble approach significantly improves robustness and generalization on unseen test data.
*   **Post-Processing:** The $128 \times 128$ predictions will be center-cropped to $101 \times 101$ before thresholding **Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**.
