### Proposed Strategy: Deep Residual U-Net with Lovasz-Cycle Snapshot Ensembling

Based on the analysis of the provided lessons, I propose a structural evolution that combines the robust feature extraction of the **Deep Residual U-Net** with a **Multi-Stage Loss Curriculum** and **Snapshot Ensembling**. This strategy directly addresses the identified failure modes of previous attempts: it incorporates the metric-optimizing Lovasz loss *only after* convergence (fixing the failure in `Cite solution_lesson_node_00018`) and leverages ensembling without reducing the effective training set size (fixing the failure in `Cite solution_lesson_node_00019`).

#### 1. Model: Deep Residual U-Net with scSE and Deep Supervision
I will retain the high-performing architecture from the current best solution, as it has proven to balance capacity and generalization effectively.

*   **Backbone:** A custom **Deep Residual U-Net (DeepResUNet)**. The encoder will consist of Residual Blocks (two $3 \times 3$ convolutions with a skip connection) starting with **64 filters** to maintain channel capacity `Cite solution_lesson_node_00020`, `Cite solution_lesson_node_00021`.
*   **Attention:** **Concurrent Spatial and Channel Squeeze & Excitation (scSE)** modules will be integrated into the decoder blocks. This focuses the model on relevant salt textures during upsampling `Cite solution_lesson_node_00007`.
*   **Deep Supervision:** Auxiliary prediction heads will be attached at resolutions $32 \times 32$, $64 \times 64$, and $128 \times 128$. These will be weighted with a decaying schedule (`[1.0, 0.5, 0.25]`) to enforce multi-scale semantic discrimination `Cite solution_lesson_node_00021`, `Cite solution_lesson_node_00022`.
*   **Input Fusion:** Depth information (`z`) will be normalized and fused as a dense channel at the input level `Cite solution_lesson_node_00002`.

#### 2. Data: Reflection Padding and Minimal Augmentation
*   **Preprocessing:** Images will be padded to $128 \times 128$ using **Reflection Padding** to handle the $101 \times 101$ dimensions without introducing artificial edge artifacts `Cite solution_lesson_node_00002`.
*   **Augmentation:** To preserve edge precision for the high-IoU metric, I will restrict augmentation to **Horizontal Flips** only. I will strictly avoid interpolation-heavy transformations (rotation, scaling) which were shown to degrade performance `Cite solution_lesson_node_00024`.
*   **Data Usage:** I will use a single stratified Train/Validation split (e.g., 90/10) rather than K-Fold Cross-Validation. This maximizes the data available for the model, avoiding the performance drop observed when fragmenting the small dataset `Cite solution_lesson_node_00019`.

#### 3. Training: Cyclic Curriculum Learning
The core innovation lies in the training pipeline, which implements a **Loss Curriculum** synchronized with a **Cosine Annealing** schedule.

*   **Optimizer & Scheduler:** I will use **AdamW** with **Cosine Annealing Warm Restarts (SGDR)**. The schedule will consist of **3 constant cycles of 50 epochs** (Total: 150 epochs). This long, stable cycle length allows the model to settle into robust minima `Cite solution_lesson_node_00022`, `Cite solution_lesson_node_00023`.
*   **Phase 1 (Cycles 1 & 2, Epochs 0-100):** Train using **BCE + Dice Loss**. This phase ensures stable convergence and robust feature extraction, establishing a strong "basin" of attraction `Cite solution_lesson_node_00018`.
*   **Phase 2 (Cycle 3, Epochs 101-150):** Switch the primary loss function to **Lovasz-Hinge Loss** (combined with a small BCE weight for stability). Unlike previous failed attempts that switched too early, this fine-tuning phase occurs *after* the model has fully converged. It directly optimizes the Jaccard index (IoU), refining the decision boundaries to maximize the competition metric `Cite solution_lesson_node_00018`.
*   **Snapshotting:** Instead of saving only one model, I will save the "Best Model" (based on mAP) from the end of **Cycle 2** (Robust BCE/Dice baseline) and **Cycle 3** (Lovasz-refined).

#### 4. Evaluation: Snapshot Ensembling
*   **Inference Strategy:** I will implement **Snapshot Ensembling** by averaging the predictions of the best models from Cycle 2 and Cycle 3. This captures the diversity of the optimization landscape—combining a robust generalist (Cycle 2) with a metric-optimized specialist (Cycle 3)—without the computational cost or data reduction of training separate fold models `Cite solution_lesson_node_00019`.
*   **TTA:** Test-Time Augmentation (Horizontal Flip) will be applied to each snapshot before averaging `Cite solution_lesson_node_00004`.
*   **Post-Processing:** The final ensemble prediction ($128 \times 128$) will be center-cropped to $101 \times 101$ `Cite solution_lesson_node_00002`.
*   **Metric Alignment:** All checkpoint selection and learning rate decisions will be driven by the **Validation mAP**, not the loss `Cite solution_lesson_node_00016`.
