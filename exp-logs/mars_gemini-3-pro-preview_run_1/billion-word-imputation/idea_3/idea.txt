# Optimized Strategy: Decoupled Localization-Classification Transformer

## Model
To address the limitations of the unified classification approach, I propose a **Decoupled Localization-Classification Transformer**. While the previous "Dense Gap Classification" strategy (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003) correctly identified the efficiency of single-pass prediction, treating the "No Insertion" case as just another class in a massive vocabulary conflates two distinct sub-problems: detecting *where* a word is missing (syntactic/structural anomaly) and determining *what* word is missing (semantic infilling).

- **Backbone**: A **Transformer Encoder** (e.g., 8-12 layers, 512 hidden size) trained from scratch. This provides the global context modeling lacking in the CNN approach while retaining the parallel processing capability.
- **Decoupled Heads**: Instead of a single output layer, the model features two distinct heads sharing the same encoder representations:
    1.  **Localization Head**: A dense layer projecting to a single scalar with a Sigmoid activation. It predicts the probability $P(Gap_i)$ that a word is missing at the position *after* token $i$. This head focuses purely on structural coherence.
    2.  **Identification Head**: A dense layer projecting to the full vocabulary size $|V|$ (excluding the `[NO_INSERT]` token). It predicts the conditional probability distribution $P(Word | Gap_i)$.
- **Inference Logic**: The final score for inserting word $w$ at position $i$ is computed as the product of the two probabilities: $Score(i, w) = P(Gap_i) \times P(Word=w | Gap_i)$. This allows the model to leverage strong structural signals even if the semantic prediction is uncertain, and vice versa.

## Data
- **Vocabulary**: Construct a fixed vocabulary of the top $K$ (e.g., 50,000) most frequent words from the training set.
- **Dynamic Sample Generation**: Continue using the online generation strategy. For each training step:
    1.  Sample a sentence and remove one word at a random index $k$.
    2.  **Localization Target**: A binary vector of length $L$ (sequence length), with a 1 at index $k$ and 0 elsewhere.
    3.  **Identification Target**: The integer ID of the removed word.
- **Input Representation**: Use standard learnable word embeddings combined with absolute positional embeddings.

## Training
- **Multi-Task Loss Function**: The objective is a weighted sum of two losses: $L = \lambda_{loc} L_{BCE} + \lambda_{id} L_{CE}$.
    - $L_{BCE}$: **Weighted Binary Cross-Entropy** applied to the Localization Head outputs across all positions. We apply class weighting (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003) to handle the imbalance (1 positive gap vs $N-1$ negative gaps).
    - $L_{CE}$: **Cross-Entropy Loss** applied to the Identification Head. Crucially, this loss is **masked** and calculated *only* at the ground-truth gap index $k$. This prevents the classifier from wasting capacity trying to predict "placeholder" words for valid gaps, allowing it to focus entirely on the semantics of the missing word.
- **Optimization**: Use AdamW with a One-Cycle learning rate scheduler to ensure convergence within the 24-hour limit.

## Evaluation
- **Single-Pass Inference**:
    1.  Feed the test sentence into the model to obtain the Localization vector ($L \times 1$) and Identification matrix ($L \times |V|$).
    2.  Compute the global score matrix via broadcasting: $S = P_{loc} \cdot P_{id}$.
    3.  Apply the "Dense Gap Classification" principle (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003) by selecting the single $(position, word\_id)$ tuple with the maximum value in $S$.
    4.  Reconstruct the sentence using the predicted word and location.
