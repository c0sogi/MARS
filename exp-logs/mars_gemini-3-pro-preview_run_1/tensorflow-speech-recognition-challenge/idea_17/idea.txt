# Model
**Architecture: Dilated EfficientNet-B2 with Multi-Sample Dropout**

I propose a structural evolution of the classification head to approximate ensembling within a single network pass.

*   **Backbone (Dilated EfficientNet-B2):** We will retain the **EfficientNet-B2** backbone due to its proven inductive bias for this dataset (Cite solution_lesson_node_00024).
    *   **Input Adaptation:** The first layer will be initialized by **averaging the pretrained ImageNet RGB weights** to accept 1-channel Log-Mel Spectrograms (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016).
    *   **Resolution Preservation:** We will strictly apply **Dilated Convolutions** (stride=1, dilation=2) in the final backbone stage. This preserves a temporal resolution of ~6-7 frames, preventing feature map collapse (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009, Cite solution_lesson_node_00025).
*   **Pooling:** We will use **Attentive Pooling** to dynamically weight active speech segments (Cite solution_lesson_node_00017).
*   **Structural Innovation (Multi-Sample Dropout Head):** We will replace the standard Dropout-Linear head with a **Multi-Sample Dropout** block.
    *   The pooled feature vector will be duplicated and passed through **8 parallel Dropout masks** (rate=0.5).
    *   All 8 masked vectors will pass through the **same shared Linear layer**.
    *   The final output is the average of the 8 logit vectors.
    *   **Benefit:** This creates an implicit ensemble that accelerates convergence and smooths the loss landscape, addressing the brittleness of the "Unknown" class decision boundary (Cite solution_lesson_node_00053).
*   **Output:** The model will output probabilities for **31+ Fine-Grained Classes** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00028).

# Data
**Preprocessing:**
*   **High-Fidelity Spectrograms:** Generate Log-Mel Spectrograms with **128 Mel bands**, **N_FFT of 1024** (64ms), and a **Hop Length of 160** (10ms) (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005, Cite solution_lesson_node_00008).
*   **Normalization:** **Strictly avoid** Instance Normalization to preserve absolute energy cues (Cite solution_lesson_node_00039).

**Augmentation (GPU):**
*   **Waveform Noise Injection:** Inject background noise into raw waveforms (SNR 10-30dB) to force invariance to environmental conditions (Cite solution_lesson_node_00029).
*   **Regularization:** Apply **SpecAugment** (Time/Freq masking) (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003) and **Mixup** with **$\alpha = 1.0$** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016).

**Balancing (Correction):**
*   **Target-Centric Balancing:** We will correct the sampling error identified in Lesson 53. Instead of flattening the fine-grained distribution (which dilutes targets), we will employ **Variance-Aware Balancing** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00026):
    *   **Upsample** the 10 Target Commands to ~2,000 samples each.
    *   **Keep** the 20+ Auxiliary Classes (Unknowns) at their natural counts (~9,000 total).
    *   This ensures the batch distribution is roughly **50% Targets / 50% Unknowns**, allocating sufficient capacity to the classes that matter for the metric while preserving the variance of the negative class.

# Training
**Strategy: Smooth Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA)**
We will implement an SWA pipeline explicitly designed to avoid the "Learning Rate Shock" observed in Lesson 50.

*   **Optimizer:** **AdamW**.
*   **Schedule:**
    *   **Phase 1 (Convergence):** Train for **30 epochs** using a Cosine Annealing schedule that decays from $1e^{-3}$ to a floor of $0.05 \times 1e^{-3}$.
    *   **Phase 2 (SWA Collection):** Train for an additional **10 epochs** (Epochs 31-40).
    *   **Transition:** We will set the constant SWA learning rate to **match the learning rate at Epoch 30** exactly. This ensures a seamless transition into the weight averaging phase without destabilizing the weights (Cite solution_lesson_node_00050).
    *   **Update:** At the end of training, perform a forward pass to update Batch Normalization statistics using the averaged weights (Cite solution_lesson_node_00051).

# Evaluation
