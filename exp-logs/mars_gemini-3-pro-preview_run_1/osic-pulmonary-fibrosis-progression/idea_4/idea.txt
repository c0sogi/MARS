Based on the comprehensive analysis of the previous experiments and the derived lessons, I propose the following optimized strategy. This solution introduces a **Siamese Dual-Axis Architecture** with **Multi-Task Regularization** to address the primary challenge: overfitting on a small dataset while maximizing the extraction of 3D spatial information.

# Model: Siamese Dual-Axis Tri-Slab Network with Auxiliary Regularization

**Architecture Design**
The model evolves the successful "Dual-Axis" approach by enforcing weight sharing and adding a regularization objective.
1.  **Siamese Visual Backbone:** Instead of using two separate CNNs for the Axial and Coronal views (which doubles the parameter count and risks overfitting), we utilize a **single** `EfficientNet-B0` backbone (initialized with ImageNet weights) to process both views.
    *   The **Axial Tri-Slab** image (representing Top-down depth) is passed through the backbone to generate feature vector $V_{axial}$.
    *   The **Coronal Tri-Slab** image (representing Front-back depth) is passed through the *same* backbone to generate feature vector $V_{coronal}$.
    *   **Rationale:** The visual texture of pulmonary fibrosis (e.g., honeycombing, reticulation) is largely isotropic (view-invariant). Sharing weights forces the model to learn robust, generalized texture filters applicable to any orientation, significantly reducing the parameter space for this small dataset (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008, solution_lesson_node_00011).
2.  **Tabular Fusion:** The two visual vectors are concatenated with the encoded clinical metadata (Age, Sex, SmokingStatus, Baseline_Percent).
3.  **Multi-Task Heads:**
    *   **Trajectory Head (Primary):** Predicts the three trajectory parameters: $\alpha$ (Slope), $\sigma_{base}$ (Base Confidence), and $\sigma_{growth}$ (Uncertainty Growth) (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).
    *   **Auxiliary Head (Regularization):** Predicts the patient's `Baseline_Percent` from the fused features.
    *   **Rationale:** By forcing the network to accurately regress the known disease severity at $t=0$, we ensure the extracted features represent the actual physiological state of the lung, preventing the model from learning spurious correlations solely for the slope prediction.

# Data: Orthogonal Tri-Slab Generation

**Preprocessing**
For each patient, we generate two distinct "2.5D" inputs to capture 3D spatial heterogeneity without the cost of 3D CNNs (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
1.  **Axial Tri-Slab (RGB):** The CT volume is split along the Z-axis (Head-to-Feet) into three equal overlapping slabs. The Maximum Intensity Projection (MIP) of each slab is mapped to the R, G, and B channels (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
2.  **Coronal Tri-Slab (RGB):** The CT volume is split along the Y-axis (Anterior-Posterior) into three equal slabs. The MIP of each slab is computed and mapped to R, G, B channels. This view explicitly captures the vertical (Z-axis) distribution of fibrosis in the image plane (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).
3.  **Normalization:** Both images are resized to **224x224** (native resolution) to avoid noise artifacts from upscaling (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).

**Augmentation**
*   **Spatial:** Apply random horizontal flips, slight rotations, and shifts to both views. This is critical for regularizing the small dataset (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
*   **Intensity:** Strictly **avoid** brightness/contrast augmentations. The pixel values represent physical Hounsfield Units (density), and altering them destroys the calibration required to measure fibrosis severity (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).

# Training: Robust Multi-Objective Optimization

**Loss Function**
The total loss is a weighted sum of the primary metric and the auxiliary task:
$$ L_{total} = L_{metric} + \lambda L_{aux} $$
*   **$L_{metric}$:** The **Modified Laplace Log Likelihood** calculated on the parametric prediction: $\text{FVC}_{pred} = \text{Base} + \alpha t$. Crucially, we **retain the error clipping** (thresholding error at 1000ml) in the loss function. As found in previous experiments, this saturation acts as a necessary filter for outliers in the noisy medical data (Cite solution_lesson_node_00013).
*   **$L_{aux}$:** Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the predicted percent and the true `Baseline_Percent`.
*   **$\lambda$:** A weighting factor (e.g., 0.2) to balance the tasks.

**Strategy**
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with Cosine Annealing scheduler.
*   **Early Stopping:** Strict monitoring of the validation metric, with a patience of roughly 5-8 epochs, as performance on this small cohort peaks early (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008).

# Evaluation: Parametric Inference

For every `Patient_Week` in the test set:
1.  Generate the Axial and Coronal Tri-Slab images from the baseline CT.
2.  Pass both through the Siamese network to predict $\alpha$, $\sigma_{base}$, and $\sigma_{growth}$.
3.  Calculate the final predictions using the time delta $t$ (Week - Baseline_Week):
    *   $$ \text{FVC} = \text{Baseline\_FVC} + \alpha \times t $$
    *   $$ \text{Confidence} = \sigma_{base} + \sigma_{growth} \times |t| $$
4.  Clip the Confidence at 70 ml as required by the metric.
