Based on the comprehensive analysis of the previous experiments and the derived lessons, I propose the following optimized strategy. This solution introduces a **FiLM-Conditioned Dual-Axis Network**, a structural evolution that changes how strong clinical priors interact with visual features—moving from "late fusion" to "deep feature modulation."

# Model: FiLM-Conditioned Dual-Axis Network

**Architecture Design**
The model retains the successful **Dual-Axis** approach (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010) with **Independent Backbones** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014) but introduces **Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM)** to condition the visual feature extraction on the clinical metadata.

1.  **Independent Visual Backbones:** Two separate **EfficientNet-B0** backbones (initialized with ImageNet weights) process the orthogonal views.
    *   **Branch A:** Processes the **Axial Tri-Slab** input.
    *   **Branch B:** Processes the **Coronal Tri-Slab** input.
    *   *Output:* Instead of immediately pooling, we extract the final convolutional feature maps $F_{axial}$ and $F_{coronal}$ (shape $C \times H \times W$).

2.  **Tabular Modulator Network (Structural Innovation):**
    *   A Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) processes the clinical metadata (Age, Sex, SmokingStatus, and crucially **Percent** - Cite solution_lesson_node_00017).
    *   This MLP outputs two vectors, $\gamma$ (scale) and $\beta$ (shift), for each visual branch.
    *   **FiLM Layer:** We apply a channel-wise affine transformation to the visual feature maps *before* pooling:
        $$ F_{modulated} = \gamma_{tab} \cdot F_{visual} + \beta_{tab} $$
    *   *Rationale:* This allows the strong clinical priors (e.g., a patient's age or baseline disease severity) to explicitly highlight or suppress specific visual feature channels (e.g., emphasizing fibrosis patterns relevant to older demographics) effectively creating a patient-specific feature extractor. This avoids the complexity of spatial attention (Cite solution_lesson_node_00020) while integrating metadata deeper than simple concatenation.

3.  **Global Pooling & Skip-Connected Fusion:**
    *   The modulated feature maps are Global Average Pooled.
    *   **Skip Connection:** The pooled vectors are concatenated with the **raw tabular embedding**. This ensures the regression head has direct access to the strong scalar priors (like Baseline FVC), preventing them from being diluted by the modulation process (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).

4.  **Parametric Prediction Head:**
    *   The head predicts three scalars: $\alpha$ (Slope), $\sigma_{base}$ (Base Confidence), and $\sigma_{growth}$ (Uncertainty Growth) (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).
    *   **Architectural Constraint:** We apply a **Softplus** activation to the $\sigma$ outputs to strictly enforce positivity within the network architecture, avoiding "dead zones" caused by clipping in the loss function (Cite solution_lesson_node_00021).

# Data: Overlapping Orthogonal Tri-Slabs

**Preprocessing**
1.  **Dual-View Generation:** For each patient, generate two inputs to capture 3D spatial heterogeneity (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).
    *   **Axial Tri-Slab (RGB):** Split the CT volume along the Z-axis (Depth) into three slabs with **15% overlap**. Compute the Maximum Intensity Projection (MIP) of each slab and map to RGB channels (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
    *   **Coronal Tri-Slab (RGB):** Split along the Y-axis (Anterior-Posterior) into three overlapping slabs, compute MIPs, and map to RGB.
2.  **Resolution:** Resize all images to **224x224** (native resolution) to strictly avoid overfitting caused by upscaling artifacts (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).

**Augmentation**
1.  **Spatial Only:** Apply random horizontal flips, shifts, and slight rotations. This is critical for regularizing the small dataset (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
2.  **No Intensity Changes:** Strictly **disable** brightness/contrast augmentations to preserve the physical Hounsfield Unit density signal required to measure fibrosis (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).

# Training: Robust Metric Optimization

**Loss Function**
The model is trained to minimize the negative **Modified Laplace Log Likelihood** on the parametric trajectory.
*   $$ L = -\text{Metric}(\text{True\_FVC}, \text{Base} + \alpha t, \sigma_{base} + \sigma_{growth}|t|) $$
*   **Robustness:** We explicitly **retain the error clipping** (thresholding absolute error at 1000 ml) within the loss calculation. This saturation acts as a necessary filter for large outliers in the noisy medical data (Cite solution_lesson_node_00013).
*   **No Auxiliary Loss:** We avoid adding MSE auxiliary targets (like predicting Percent) to prevent interference with the probabilistic objective (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016).

**Strategy**
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with Cosine Annealing scheduler.
*   **Early Stopping:** Use a strict patience (e.g., 5-8 epochs). Training on this small cohort exhausts the generalizable signal rapidly (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008).

# Evaluation: Parametric Inference

For every `Patient_Week` in the test set:
1.  Generate the Axial and Coronal Tri-Slab images.
2.  Pass inputs and baseline metadata through the **FiLM-Conditioned Network** to predict $\alpha$, $\sigma_{base}$, and $\sigma_{growth}$.
3.  Calculate the final predictions using the time delta $t$ (Week - Baseline_Week):
    *   $$ \text{FVC} = \text{Baseline\_FVC} + \alpha \times t $$
    *   $$ \text{Confidence} = \sigma_{base} + \sigma_{growth} \times |t| $$
4.  Clip the Confidence at 70 ml as required by the metric.
