Based on the lessons learned, I propose the **Tabular-Query Spatial-Attention Network (TQ-SAN)**. This strategy fundamentally evolves the fusion mechanism by shifting from "late fusion" (concatenating pooled vectors) to "guided feature extraction," where the strong clinical priors explicitly direct the model's focus to relevant spatial regions within the CT scans.

# Model: Tabular-Query Spatial-Attention Network (TQ-SAN)

**Architecture Design**
The model utilizes a multi-stream architecture that leverages the strong correlation of clinical metadata (specifically `Percent`) to query visual features before they are compressed by pooling.

1.  **Independent Visual Backbones:** Two separate **EfficientNet-B0** backbones (initialized with ImageNet weights) process the orthogonal views.
    *   **Stream A (Axial):** Processes the Axial Tri-Slab input. Instead of global pooling, we extract the final convolutional feature map of size $C \times H \times W$ (e.g., $1280 \times 7 \times 7$).
    *   **Stream B (Coronal):** Processes the Coronal Tri-Slab input to extract a similarly sized spatial feature map.
    *   *Rationale:* We maintain independent backbones to capture distinct anatomical textures in orthogonal planes (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014).

2.  **Tabular Query Generation:** A Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) encodes the clinical metadata (Age, Sex, SmokingStatus, Baseline_Percent) into a latent vector $d_{tab}$. This vector serves two roles: as a **Query** for attention and as a **Prior** for the final prediction.

3.  **Cross-Modal Spatial Attention (Structural Innovation):**
    *   Instead of global average pooling (which destroys spatial localization), we treat the flattened visual feature maps ($HW \times C$) as **Keys** and **Values**.
    *   The Tabular vector $d_{tab}$ is projected to act as the **Query**.
    *   We compute **Cross-Attention** scores between the clinical state (Query) and the spatial grid (Keys). This allows the model to dynamically weight specific regions of the lung (e.g., focusing on basal honeycombing vs. diffuse reticulation) based on the patient's known disease severity (`Percent`) and demographics.
    *   The output is a context-aware visual vector $v_{fused}$ that aggregates spatial information weighted by clinical relevance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00017).

4.  **Skip-Connected Parametric Head:**
    *   The attention-derived visual vector $v_{fused}$ is concatenated with the original tabular vector $d_{tab}$ via a **skip connection**.
    *   *Rationale:* This ensures the regression head has direct access to the strong scalar priors (like Baseline FVC), preventing them from being diluted by the high-dimensional visual features (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).
    *   The head predicts three scalars: $\alpha$ (Slope), $\sigma_{base}$ (Base Confidence), and $\sigma_{growth}$ (Uncertainty Growth) (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).

# Data: Dual-Axis Overlapping Tri-Slabs

**Preprocessing**
1.  **Overlapping Depth Slabs:** For each patient, we generate two inputs.
    *   **Axial:** Split the CT volume along the Z-axis into three slabs with **15% overlap**. Compute the Maximum Intensity Projection (MIP) for each and map to RGB channels (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003, solution_lesson_node_00006).
    *   **Coronal:** Split the volume along the Y-axis into three overlapping slabs, compute MIPs, and map to RGB. This captures the vertical distribution of fibrosis (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).
2.  **Resolution:** Resize all images to **224x224**, the native resolution of the backbone, to strictly avoid overfitting caused by upscaling artifacts (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).

**Augmentation**
1.  **Spatial Only:** Apply random horizontal flips, shifts, and slight rotations. This is critical for regularizing the small dataset (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
2.  **No Intensity Changes:** Strictly **disable** brightness/contrast augmentations to preserve the physical Hounsfield Unit density signal required to measure fibrosis (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).

# Training: Robust Metric Optimization

**Loss Function**
We optimize the **Modified Laplace Log Likelihood** directly.
*   $$ L = -\text{Metric}(\text{True\_FVC}, \text{Base} + \alpha t, \sigma_{base} + \sigma_{growth}|t|) $$
*   **Critical:** We explicitly **retain the error clipping** (thresholding absolute error at 1000 ml) within the loss function. This saturation is necessary to filter out large outliers in the noisy medical data, as removing it was shown to degrade performance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00013).
*   We avoid auxiliary losses (like MSE on Percent) to prevent interference with the probabilistic objective (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016).

**Strategy**
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with Cosine Annealing.
*   **Schedule:** Use a short training schedule with aggressive **Early Stopping** (patience ~5-8 epochs), as the model exhausts the generalizable signal in this small cohort very rapidly (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008).

# Evaluation: Parametric Trajectory Inference

For every `Patient_Week` in the test set:
1.  Generate the Axial and Coronal Tri-Slab inputs.
2.  Pass inputs and baseline metadata through the **TQ-SAN** to predict the trajectory parameters $\alpha$, $\sigma_{base}$, and $\sigma_{growth}$.
3.  Compute the final submission values:
    *   $$ \text{FVC} = \text{Baseline\_FVC} + \alpha \times (\text{Week} - \text{Baseline\_Week}) $$
    *   $$ \text{Confidence} = \sigma_{base} + \sigma_{growth} \times |\text{Week} - \text{Baseline\_Week}| $$
4.  Clip the Confidence at 70 ml as required by the metric.
