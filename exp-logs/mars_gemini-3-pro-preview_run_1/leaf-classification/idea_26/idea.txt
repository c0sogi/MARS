Based on the comprehensive analysis of the accumulated lessons—specifically the "near-perfect" performance of the **OAS-LDA backbone** [Cite solution_lesson_node_00047], the constraints of **Small Sample Size** ($N \approx 7$) on covariance estimation [Cite solution_lesson_node_00015], and the potential for **Sharpening** to break the log-loss floor—I propose the **Factorized High-Precision OAS Discriminant**.

This strategy introduces a **Semantic Feature Factorization** as a structural innovation. Instead of estimating a single, massive $192 \times 192$ covariance matrix (which requires estimating ~18,000 parameters from sparse data) or using Random Subspaces (which breaks signal [Cite solution_lesson_node_00024]), we decompose the problem into three independent "Experts" based on the semantic feature groups: **Margin**, **Shape**, and **Texture**. By modeling the joint likelihood as the product of these group-wise likelihoods (Naive Bayes assumption at the group level), we reduce the dimensionality per estimator to $64$, effectively tripling the statistical power ($N/P$) available for covariance estimation. Furthermore, this allows the **OAS Estimator** to apply distinct shrinkage intensities to each feature type, adapting to their specific noise profiles.

# Model
The architecture is a **Bayesian Ensemble of Linear Discriminants** operating in strict **Double Precision**:

1.  **Factorized Backbones:** Three independent **Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA)** models, each assigned to a specific feature group:
    *   **Expert M:** Trained on `margin_1`...`margin_64`.
    *   **Expert S:** Trained on `shape_1`...`shape_64`.
    *   **Expert T:** Trained on `texture_1`...`texture_64`.
2.  **Group-Wise OAS Covariance:** Each expert estimates its own covariance matrix $\Sigma_g$ using **Oracle Approximating Shrinkage (OAS)** with `assume_centered=True`.
    *   *Innovation:* This allows the model to learn, for example, that "Texture" requires heavy shrinkage (noisy) while "Shape" requires little shrinkage (clean), a flexibility lost in the Global model.
3.  **Bayesian Aggregation (Sharpening):** We combine the experts by summing their **Linear Logits** (equivalent to multiplying likelihoods).
    *   *Formulation:*
        $$ \log P(y|x) \propto \left( \sum_{g \in \{M, S, T\}} \log P(x_g|y) \right) + \log P(y) $$
    *   *Implementation:* Since each LDA expert implicitly calculates $Z_g = \log P(x_g|y) + \log P(y)$, the sum $\sum Z_g$ would triple-count the prior. We explicitly correct this:
        $$ Z_{final} = Z_{margin} + Z_{shape} + Z_{texture} - 2 \cdot \log(\pi) $$
    *   *Rationale:* Unlike averaging probabilities (smoothing), summing logits **sharpens** the distribution. If all three experts agree on a class, the resulting probability is pushed significantly closer to 1.0 than any individual expert, helping to minimize Log Loss in the high-confidence regime.

# Data
1.  **Input:** The solution uses the 192 pre-extracted tabular features.
2.  **Semantic Splitting:** The features are sliced into three matrices ($X_M, X_S, X_T$) based on their column name prefixes.
3.  **Inductive Preprocessing:**
    *   **High-Precision Loading:** Data is processed in **`float64`** [Cite solution_lesson_node_00046].
    *   **Transformation:** Apply **Yeo-Johnson Power Transformation** (`standardize=False`) followed by **Standard Scaling**.
    *   **Fit:** Fitted **only on the Training set** [Cite solution_lesson_node_00041].
    *   *Note:* The transformation is applied globally or per-group (mathematically equivalent since these are element-wise/column-wise operations).

# Training
1.  **Parameter Estimation (Per Group):**
    *   For each group $g \in \{M, S, T\}$:
        *   Compute class means $\mu_{g,k}$ and global priors $\pi_k$ (Arithmetic Mean [Cite solution_lesson_node_00066]).
        *   Compute residuals $R_g = X_g - \mu_{g,y}$.
        *   Estimate $\Sigma_g$ using `OAS(assume_centered=True)` [Cite solution_lesson_node_00061].
        *   Compute weights $W_g = \Sigma_g^{-1} \mu_g$ and bias $b_g = -0.5 \text{diag}(\mu_g^T W_g) + \log(\pi)$ [Cite solution_lesson_node_00055].
2.  **No Optimization:** The aggregation is analytical (Bayesian Sum), requiring no weights to be learned, thus avoiding overfitting.

# Evaluation
1.  **Inference:**
    *   Preprocess test data to get $X_{test, M}, X_{test, S}, X_{test, T}$.
    *   Compute logits for each group in **`float64`**: $Z_g = X_{test, g} W_g^T + b_g$.
    *   Aggregated Logits: $Z_{total} = Z_M + Z_S + Z_T - 2 \log(\pi)$.
    *   Apply Softmax to $Z_{total}$ in **`float64`** [Cite solution_lesson_node_00057].
2.  **Robustness:** This strategy maximizes the statistical power for covariance estimation (addressing Small N) and utilizes logit-summation to achieve the extreme probability saturation required to break the current metric floor.
