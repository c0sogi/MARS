Based on the progression of experiments and the specific lessons learned, I propose a **Bayesian Fisher-Gaussian Process Pipeline**.

### Model
The solution introduces a sequential "Filter-then-Refine" architecture that fundamentally changes the role of Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) from a final classifier to a supervised feature extractor.
1.  **Backbone (Supervised Dimensionality Reduction):** A **Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA)** model is used as a transformer rather than a predictor. It projects the 192-dimensional input space into a compact $(C-1)$-dimensional "Fisher Subspace" (98 dimensions) that maximizes the ratio of between-class variance to within-class variance. This step effectively compresses the signal and filters out the redundant/noisy features identified in previous iterations [Cite solution_lesson_node_00001].
2.  **Head (Bayesian Classification):** A **Gaussian Process Classifier (GPC)** is trained on the projected Fisher features. Unlike the rigid linear decision boundaries and point-estimate probabilities of standard LDA, the GPC employs a kernel-based approach (using an RBF + WhiteKernel) to model the class probabilities. This introduces a Bayesian framework that integrates over parameter uncertainty, providing naturally calibrated and robust probability estimates—critical for minimizing log-loss in small-sample regimes.

### Data
1.  **Input:** The model uses the 192 extracted tabular features (`margin`, `shape`, `texture`).
2.  **Preprocessing Pipeline:**
    *   **Power Transformation:** Apply the **Yeo-Johnson** transformation to all features. This is strictly retained to enforce the multivariate Gaussian distribution that LDA relies upon [Cite solution_lesson_node_00006].
    *   **Scaling:** Apply **Standard Scaling** (zero mean, unit variance) to ensure numerical stability for both the LDA projection and the GPC kernel computations.
    *   **Note:** We explicitly avoid data fragmentation strategies (like hierarchical splitting) to preserve the effective sample size [Cite solution_lesson_node_00015].

### Training
1.  **Pipeline Training:** The entire pipeline is fitted on the full training dataset ($N=712$).
    *   The **LDA** component computes the shared covariance matrix and class means to define the projection matrix.
    *   The **GPC** component optimizes the kernel hyperparameters (length-scale and noise level) by maximizing the Log Marginal Likelihood of the projected data.
2.  **Safety Mechanism:** To prevent any potential degradation from the non-linear kernel in the GPC, the final probability output will be a weighted average ($0.5 \times P_{LDA} + 0.5 \times P_{GPC}$), where $P_{LDA}$ is the probability from the standard LDA classifier. This "Hybrid Generative-Discriminative" ensemble retains the "near-perfect" baseline of the generative LDA [Cite solution_lesson_node_00006] while adding the discriminative, probabilistic refinement of the GPC.
3.  **No Bagging:** We explicitly reject bagging, as previous experiments showed it degrades performance for stable estimators in this domain [Cite solution_lesson_node_00011].

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** Test samples are passed through the transformation pipeline (Yeo-Johnson $\rightarrow$ Scaler $\rightarrow$ LDA Projection).
2.  **Prediction:** The projected samples are classified by the GPC (and the parallel LDA path).
3.  **Aggregation:** The final probabilities are averaged and submitted. This approach ensures that the "perfect" linear separation found by LDA is preserved, while the GPC provides a "Bayesian Safety Net" to better calibrate the confidence of predictions on unseen test data.
