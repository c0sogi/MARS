Based on the lessons learned from the previous attempts, I propose a **Two-Stage Region-of-Interest (ROI) Pipeline** designed to preserve high-frequency fracture details while maintaining a stable training objective.

### Model
The solution introduces a cascaded architecture consisting of two distinct models:

1.  **Stage 1: Spine Localization Network (U-Net):**
    Instead of processing the entire 512x512 CT slice where the spine occupies only a small fraction of the area, this model performs semantic segmentation to locate the cervical vertebrae (C1-C7). It is a lightweight 2D U-Net trained on the provided segmentation subset. Its sole purpose is to output a coordinate (center of mass) for the spinal column in each slice.

2.  **Stage 2: High-Resolution 2.5D Fracture Classifier:**
    This is the primary classification model. It utilizes a **2.5D input structure** (stacking 3 consecutive axial slices as RGB channels) and a robust backbone (e.g., EfficientNet-V2 or ResNet50).
    *   **Structural Innovation:** Unlike the previous approach which resized the full image, this model takes a **fixed-size crop (e.g., 224x224 or 256x256) centered on the spine** coordinates predicted by Stage 1. By cropping the Region of Interest (ROI) from the original 512x512 image without downsampling, the model retains the native pixel resolution required to detect fine hairline fractures. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**

### Data
1.  **Preprocessing:**
    *   **Windowing:** Apply specific "bone window" settings (e.g., Center 400, Width 1800) to all DICOM images to maximize contrast for osseous structures.
    *   **Localization Generation:** Train the Stage 1 U-Net on the subset of data with segmentation masks. Run inference on the entire dataset (train and test) to generate `(x, y)` center coordinates for the spine in every slice.
    *   **Patch Extraction:** For the Stage 2 classifier, extract square patches centered on the generated coordinates. This ensures the input contains the relevant anatomy at full resolution. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**

2.  **Labeling:**
    *   Use `train_bounding_boxes.csv` to assign positive labels to specific slices containing fractures.
    *   Assign negative labels to slices without bounding boxes (background).

### Training
1.  **Loss Function:**
    *   Use standard **Binary Cross Entropy (BCE)** or Focal Loss for the Stage 2 classifier.
    *   Crucially, **do not apply the competition's metric weights** (which heavily favor `patient_overall`) to the slice-level training loss. The classifier must remain conservative and precise at the slice level to prevent the accumulation of False Positives during aggregation. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**

2.  **Sampling Strategy:**
    *   Employ balanced sampling (e.g., 1:1 ratio of fractured slices to non-fractured slices) within each batch to ensure the model sees sufficient positive examples despite the class imbalance.

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference Pipeline:**
    *   For each test scan, first run the Stage 1 Localizer to determine the spine's location in 3D space.
    *   Extract high-resolution 2.5D crops for the Stage 2 Classifier.
    *   Predict probabilities for all 8 targets (C1-C7, `patient_overall`) for each slice.

2.  **Aggregation:**
    *   Apply **Max Pooling** across all slices for a given patient to determine the final study-level probabilities. Because the model is trained with unweighted BCE on high-resolution crops, the individual slice predictions will be more reliable, making Max Pooling a valid aggregation strategy without the risk of amplifying noise. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**
