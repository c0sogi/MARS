Based on the comprehensive analysis of the previous ideas and the critical lessons learned, I propose the **Anatomically-Grouped Recurrent Pipeline**. This solution synthesizes the high-resolution feature extraction of Idea 4, the anatomical awareness attempted in Idea 5, and the robust sequence modeling mandated by Lesson 00009.

# Model

The architecture is a three-stage cascade that enforces anatomical consistency while leveraging robust sequence modeling.

1.  **Stage 1: Multi-Class Anatomical Localizer (2D U-Net)**
    *   We deploy a lightweight 2D U-Net trained on the segmentation subset.
    *   **Innovation:** Unlike previous iterations that output a single "spine" class, this model performs **multi-class segmentation** (Background, C1, C2, ..., C7).
    *   **Outputs:** For every slice, it provides:
        1.  **ROI Coordinates:** The center of the vertebral mass for cropping.
        2.  **Binary Bone Mask:** A merged mask of all vertebrae to guide the encoder.
        3.  **Anatomical ID:** A one-hot vector indicating which vertebra (if any) is present in the slice.

2.  **Stage 2: Mask-Conditioned Feature Encoder (2.5D CNN)**
    *   A robust 2D backbone (e.g., EfficientNet-V2) extracts visual features.
    *   **Input:** To preserve fine-grained fracture details, we extract **high-resolution crops** (e.g., 256x256) from the *original* 512x512 images **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00002)**. The input is a 4-channel tensor: 3 consecutive CT slices (RGB) + the **Binary Bone Mask** (Alpha) from Stage 1.
    *   **Function:** The mask channel forces the network to focus feature extraction strictly on osseous structures, suppressing soft-tissue noise.

3.  **Stage 3: Anatomically-Grouped Recurrent Aggregator (Bi-GRU)**
    *   This stage replaces the generic pooling of previous ideas with a structured, anatomically-aware mechanism.
    *   **Input:** A sequence of feature vectors from Stage 2. Crucially, we concatenate the **Anatomical ID** (from Stage 1) to the visual features at each step.
    *   **Backbone:** A **Bidirectional GRU** processes the sequence. We choose a GRU over a Transformer because the dataset size (~160 studies) favors the strong inductive bias of RNNs **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00009)**.
    *   **Structural Innovation (Anatomical Pooling):** Instead of a single output head, the network uses **Anatomical Pooling**.
        *   The RNN outputs a sequence of hidden states $H = \{h_1, ..., h_N\}$.
        *   To predict the probability for vertebra $C_k$, the model aggregates (e.g., via Mean or Attention pooling) *only* the hidden states $h_i$ where the Stage 1 Anatomical ID corresponds to $C_k$.
        *   To predict `patient_overall`, the model aggregates the entire sequence $H$ using a global attention mechanism **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00008)**.
    *   This ensures that the prediction for "C1 Fracture" is derived specifically from the visual evidence of C1 slices (plus temporal context), rather than a noisy global average.

# Data

1.  **Preprocessing:**
    *   **Windowing:** Apply "bone window" (Center: 400, Width: 1800) to all DICOMs.
    *   **Inference Generation:** Run the Stage 1 U-Net on the entire dataset (train/test) to generate crops, masks, and Anatomical IDs.
    *   **Cropping:** Extract fixed-size crops centered on the spine from the **full-resolution** source images. Do not downsample the source image **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00002)**.

2.  **Labeling:**
    *   **Stage 2:** Use `train_bounding_boxes.csv` to create binary slice-level targets.
    *   **Stage 3:** Use `train.csv` for patient-level multi-label targets.

# Training

The training is divided into three phases to decouple feature learning from metric optimization, preventing the amplification of noise **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00005)**.

1.  **Phase 1 (Localizer):** Train the Multi-Class U-Net using Dice Loss + Cross Entropy to accurately segment and identify C1-C7 levels.

2.  **Phase 2 (Encoder):** Train the Stage 2 CNN on the slice-level bounding box targets.
    *   **Loss:** Use **Unweighted Binary Cross Entropy (BCE)**. We explicitly **do not** apply the competition's heavy `patient_overall` weights here. This forces the encoder to be conservative and precise, learning to detect fractures based on visual evidence rather than class bias **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00005)**.

3.  **Phase 3 (Aggregator):** Freeze the Stage 2 Encoder and pre-compute feature sequences for all training patients. Train the Stage 3 Bi-GRU on these sequences.
    *   **Loss:** Use the **Weighted Multi-label Logarithmic Loss**. Since the RNN aggregates evidence across the volume and uses Anatomical Pooling to route signals correctly, it can safely optimize the weighted metric without the risk of amplifying single-slice noise inherent to Max Pooling **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00008)**.

# Evaluation

1.  **Inference Pipeline:**
    *   **Step 1:** Pass the test scan through the Stage 1 U-Net to get ROI coordinates, Binary Masks, and Anatomical IDs for every slice.
    *   **Step 2:** Extract high-resolution, mask-concatenated crops and pass them through the Stage 2 Encoder to generate a sequence of visual features.
    *   **Step 3:** Construct the sequence (Visual Features + Anatomical IDs) and pass it to the Stage 3 Bi-GRU.
    *   **Step 4:** The Bi-GRU performs Anatomical Pooling to generate specific probabilities for C1-C7 and a global probability for `patient_overall`.
