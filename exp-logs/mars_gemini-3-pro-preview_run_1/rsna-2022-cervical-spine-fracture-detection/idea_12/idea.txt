Based on the comprehensive analysis of the previous ideas and the critical lessons learned, I propose the **Dual-Stream Graph-Recurrent Network (DS-GRN)**. This solution structurally evolves the "Dual-Stream" concept (Idea 11) by integrating a **Spinal Graph Module** to explicitly model the anatomical dependencies between vertebrae, while strictly adhering to the resolution and stability constraints identified in the lessons.

# Model

The architecture is a three-stage cascade that reconciles high-resolution fracture detection with global context, aggregated via a structurally aware graph-recurrent mechanism.

**Stage 1: Multi-Class Anatomical Localizer (2D U-Net)**
*   **Purpose:** To provide spatial localization and anatomical indexing.
*   **Architecture:** A lightweight 2D U-Net trained on the segmentation subset.
*   **Outputs:** For every slice, it generates:
    1.  **ROI Coordinates:** Center-of-mass of the spinal column for cropping.
    2.  **Binary Bone Mask:** A merged mask of all vertebrae to guide the local encoder.
    3.  **Anatomical Probability Profile:** A probability vector ($P_{C1}, ..., P_{C7}$) indicating the likelihood of each vertebra being present in the slice.

**Stage 2: Dual-Resolution Feature Encoder**
*   **Innovation:** A dual-branch CNN that processes visual information at two distinct scales, addressing the need for both detail and context **Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**.
    *   **Branch A (Local Stream):** Accepts **High-Resolution Crops** (e.g., 256x256) extracted from the *original* 512x512 images centered on the spine. It includes the Binary Bone Mask as a 4th channel. This branch preserves the high-frequency spatial frequencies required to detect hairline fractures **Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**.
    *   **Branch B (Global Stream):** Accepts downsampled full-field-of-view slices (e.g., 256x256). This branch captures gross anatomical context, such as spinal alignment and subluxations.
*   **Fusion:** The outputs of both branches are concatenated to form a comprehensive **Slice Feature Vector**.

**Stage 3: Graph-Recurrent Aggregator**
*   **Sequence Modeling:** A **Bidirectional GRU** processes the sequence of Slice Feature Vectors for the entire patient. We select a GRU over a Transformer to leverage its inductive bias for sequential data on this small dataset **Cite solution_lesson_node_00009**.
*   **Anatomical Pooling:** The model converts the variable-length sequence of GRU hidden states into 7 fixed-size **Vertebra Node Vectors** ($V_{C1}, ..., V_{C7}$). This is done by computing a weighted average of the states, where weights are derived from the Stage 1 **Anatomical Probability Profile**.
*   **Spinal Graph Refinement (Structural Innovation):** These 7 vectors form the nodes of a **Spinal Graph**, where edges represent physical adjacency (C1-C2, C2-C3, etc.). A lightweight **Graph Convolutional Network (GCN)** (using `torch_geometric`) processes this graph. This explicitly models inter-vertebral dependencies (e.g., a dislocation at C6 affecting C7) which generic dense layers might struggle to learn from small data.
*   **Prediction Heads:**
    *   **Node Classifiers:** Predict probabilities for C1-C7 from the refined node vectors.
    *   **Graph Readout:** A global pooling operation over the graph predicts `patient_overall`, explicitly modeling the logical hierarchy **Cite solution_lesson_node_00008**.

# Data

**Preprocessing:**
*   **Windowing:** Apply a standard "bone window" (Center: 400, Width: 1800) to all DICOM pixel arrays.
*   **Stage 1 Inference:** Run the trained U-Net on the entire dataset (train and test) to generate ROI coordinates, Masks, and Anatomical Profiles.
*   **Dual-Resolution Extraction:**
    *   For the **Local Stream**, extract crops from the **original full-resolution** (512x512) images. **Do not downsample the source image** before cropping to avoid destroying fracture signals **Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**.
    *   For the **Global Stream**, resize the full slice to 256x256.

**Labeling:**
*   **Stage 2 Targets:** Use `train_bounding_boxes.csv` to generate binary slice-level targets (Fracture vs. Background) for the subset of data where they exist.
*   **Stage 3 Targets:** Use `train.csv` for patient-level multi-label targets.

# Training

The training strategy is divided into three phases to decouple feature learning from metric optimization.

**Phase 1 (Localizer):**
*   Train the Stage 1 U-Net on the segmentation subset using Dice Loss + Cross Entropy.

**Phase 2 (Encoder):**
*   Train the Stage 2 Dual-Resolution CNN on the slice-level bounding box targets.
*   **Loss Function:** Use **Unweighted Binary Cross Entropy (BCE)**. We explicitly **do not** apply the competition's heavy `patient_overall` weights here. This forces the encoder to be conservative and precise, learning to detect fractures based on visual evidence rather than class bias **Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**.

**Phase 3 (Aggregator):**
*   Freeze the Stage 2 Encoder. Pre-compute feature sequences for all training patients. Train the Stage 3 Graph-Recurrent Aggregator on these sequences.
*   **Loss Function:** Use the **Weighted Multi-label Logarithmic Loss**. Since the Aggregator processes the entire volume and uses Graph Refinement to route signals, it can safely optimize the weighted metric without the risk of amplifying single-slice noise inherent to Max Pooling **Cite solution_lesson_node_00008**.

# Evaluation

**Inference Pipeline:**
1.  **Localization:** Pass the test scan through Stage 1 to obtain ROI coordinates, Masks, and Anatomical Profiles.
2.  **Feature Extraction:** Extract High-Res Crops (Local) and Downsampled Slices (Global). Pass them through the Stage 2 Encoder to generate a sequence of fused features.
3.  **Aggregation:** Pass the sequence (concatenated with Anatomical Profiles) to the Stage 3 Bi-GRU.
4.  **Graph Reasoning:** The Bi-GRU outputs are pooled into Vertebra Nodes and refined by the GCN.
5.  **Prediction:** The Node Classifiers and Graph Readout output the final probabilities for C1-C7 and `patient_overall`.
