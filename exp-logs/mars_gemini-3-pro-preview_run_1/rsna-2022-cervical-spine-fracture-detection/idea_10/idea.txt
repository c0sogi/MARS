Based on the comprehensive analysis of the previous ideas and the critical lessons learned, I propose the **Hybrid-Context Hierarchical Recurrent Network (HCH-RN)**. This solution structurally evolves the "Dual-Stream" concept (Idea 9) by optimizing the global context extraction mechanism and refining the aggregation strategy to be anatomically robust without computational redundancy.

# Model

The architecture is a three-stage cascade that integrates global anatomical awareness with high-resolution local fracture detection, aggregated via a structured recurrent network.

1.  **Stage 1: Multi-Task Anatomical Segmentor (U-Net)**
    *   **Input:** Downsampled axial slices (e.g., 256x256) covering the full field of view.
    *   **Architecture:** A 2D U-Net with a standard encoder backbone (e.g., EfficientNet-B0).
    *   **Innovation (Feature Reuse):** Unlike previous ideas that treated the U-Net solely as a cropper, we extract the **Global Context Vector** directly from the U-Net's bottleneck (Global Average Pooling of the encoder output). This vector captures gross spinal alignment and deformities, fulfilling the "Dual-Branch" recommendation for global context **Cite solution_lesson_node_00002** without training a separate global CNN.
    *   **Outputs:**
        1.  **ROI Coordinates:** Center-of-mass for cropping.
        2.  **Binary Bone Mask:** To guide the local encoder.
        3.  **Anatomical Probability Profile:** A probability vector ($P_{C1}, ..., P_{C7}$) indicating the presence of specific vertebrae in the slice.
        4.  **Global Context Vector:** The latent representation of the full slice.

2.  **Stage 2: Mask-Guided High-Resolution Encoder (2.5D CNN)**
    *   **Input:** **High-Resolution Crops** (e.g., 256x256) extracted from the *original* 512x512 images centered on the Stage 1 coordinates. We strictly avoid downsampling the source image to preserve hairline fracture details **Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**.
    *   **Composition:** A 4-channel tensor: 3 consecutive CT slices (RGB) + the **Binary Bone Mask** (Alpha) from Stage 1.
    *   **Backbone:** A robust 2D CNN (e.g., EfficientNet-V2) that outputs a dense **Local Fracture Embedding**.

3.  **Stage 3: Hybrid-Feature Recurrent Aggregator (Bi-GRU)**
    *   **Input:** A sequence representing the patient scan. At each step $t$, the input is the concatenation of the **Local Fracture Embedding** (Stage 2), the **Global Context Vector** (Stage 1), and the **Anatomical Probability Profile** (Stage 1).
    *   **Sequence Modeling:** A **Bidirectional GRU** processes the sequence. We select a GRU over a Transformer to leverage its inductive bias for sequential data on this small dataset **Cite solution_lesson_node_00009**.
    *   **Anatomically-Biased Attention Pooling:** Instead of simple pooling, the model uses 7 distinct Attention Heads.
        *   For vertebra $k$, the attention weights are computed as a learnable query over the GRU states, explicitly **biased** (masked) by the Stage 1 Anatomical Probability $P_{Ck}$. This ensures the model attends only to slices physically corresponding to C1, while the learnable component selects the most salient fracture features within that region.
    *   **Hierarchical Heads:**
        *   **Vertebrae Heads:** Predict C1-C7 probabilities using the pooled vectors.
        *   **Patient Head:** Predicts `patient_overall` using the concatenation of all vertebra-specific vectors, explicitly modeling the logical hierarchy **Cite solution_lesson_node_00008**.

# Data

1.  **Preprocessing:**
    *   **Windowing:** Apply "bone window" (Center: 400, Width: 1800) to all DICOMs.
    *   **Stage 1 Inference:** Run the U-Net on the entire dataset to generate ROI coordinates, Masks, Global Vectors, and Anatomical Profiles.
    *   **Cropping:** Extract fixed-size crops centered on the spine from the **full-resolution** source images **Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**.

2.  **Labeling:**
    *   **Stage 2:** Use `train_bounding_boxes.csv` to generate binary slice-level targets (Fracture vs. Background).
    *   **Stage 3:** Use `train.csv` for patient-level multi-label targets.

# Training

The training strategy is decoupled to prevent metric-induced noise amplification **Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**.

1.  **Phase 1 (Localizer):** Train the Stage 1 U-Net on the segmentation subset using Dice Loss + Cross Entropy.

2.  **Phase 2 (Encoder):** Train the Stage 2 CNN on the slice-level bounding box targets.
    *   **Loss:** Use **Unweighted Binary Cross Entropy (BCE)**. We explicitly **exclude** the competition's heavy `patient_overall` weights here. This forces the encoder to be conservative and precise, learning to detect fractures based on visual evidence rather than class bias **Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**.

3.  **Phase 3 (Aggregator):** Freeze Stage 1 and Stage 2. Pre-compute feature sequences for all training patients. Train the Stage 3 Bi-GRU on these sequences.
    *   **Loss:** Use the **Weighted Multi-label Logarithmic Loss**. Since the RNN aggregates evidence across the volume and uses Anatomically-Biased Attention to route signals, it can safely optimize the weighted metric without the risk of amplifying single-slice noise inherent to Max Pooling **Cite solution_lesson_node_00008**.

# Evaluation

1.  **Inference Pipeline:**
    *   **Step 1:** Pass the test scan through Stage 1 to obtain ROI coordinates, Masks, Global Context Vectors, and Anatomical Profiles.
    *   **Step 2:** Extract high-resolution crops and pass them through Stage 2 to get Local Fracture Embeddings.
    *   **Step 3:** Construct the sequence (Local + Global + Anatomical) and feed it to the Stage 3 Bi-GRU.
    *   **Step 4:** The Bi-GRU uses Anatomically-Biased Attention to pool features and predict probabilities for C1-C7 and `patient_overall`.
