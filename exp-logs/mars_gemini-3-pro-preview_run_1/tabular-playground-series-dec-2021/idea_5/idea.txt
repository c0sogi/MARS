Based on the comprehensive analysis of previous attempts and the derived lessons, I propose a **Full-Scale Homogeneous Ensemble** strategy. This approach synthesizes the successful components of the baseline (high-capacity XGBoost, geometric features) with a robust Cross-Validation architecture to maximize data utilization without the pitfalls of subsampling or weak-learner dilution.

### Model
The architecture will evolve from a single hold-out model to a **Stratified K-Fold Homogeneous Ensemble**.
*   **Backbone:** We will employ the **XGBoost** classifier with **GPU acceleration** (`tree_method='hist'`, `device='cuda'`). This model has proven to be the strongest individual performer, significantly outperforming LightGBM and Neural Networks in this specific domain. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00006**.
*   **Ensemble Structure:** Instead of mixing disparate models (which diluted performance previously), we will train **5 independent instances** of the optimized XGBoost architecture. Each instance will be trained on a distinct subset of the data defined by a Stratified 5-Fold split.
*   **Configuration:** We will strictly adhere to the high-capacity configuration validated in previous experiments: `max_depth=10` and `eta=0.05`. This ensures the model has sufficient complexity to capture boundaries in the full dataset while avoiding the inefficiency of aggressive fine-tuning. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00002, solution_lesson_node_00004**.
*   **Diversity Induction:** To ensure the ensemble members are not identical, we will introduce **Feature Subsampling** (e.g., `colsample_bytree=0.8`) and **Row Subsampling** (e.g., `subsample=0.8`). This decorrelates the trees, allowing the ensemble to reduce variance more effectively than a single model.

### Data
*   **Data Volume:** We will load and utilize the **Full Training Dataset** (2.8M+ samples). We will explicitly reject any form of subsampling, as previous experiments demonstrated that data volume is the primary driver of performance and should not be sacrificed for architectural complexity. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00009**.
*   **Feature Engineering (Exploitation):** We will apply the physics-informed geometric transformations that yielded definitive gains. This includes:
    1.  **Euclidean Distance to Hydrology:** $\sqrt{\text{Horizontal\_Distance}^2 + \text{Vertical\_Distance}^2}$.
    2.  **Relative Elevation:** `Elevation` - `Vertical_Distance_To_Hydrology`.
    3.  **Cyclic Aspect Encoding:** Transforming the angular `Aspect` feature into its sine and cosine components.
    **Cite solution_lesson_node_00007**.
*   **Preprocessing:** The data will be split into 5 stratified folds. This ensures that across the ensemble, **100% of the available training data** is utilized for learning, whereas the previous single-split approach left 20% of the data unseen by the model.

### Training
*   **Procedure:** We will execute a loop to train 5 separate models, one for each fold.
*   **Optimization:** Each model will be trained using the `multi:softmax` objective (or `multi:softprob` for internal probability storage).
*   **Convergence Control:** We will use **Early Stopping** (patience=50) monitoring the validation fold (Out-Of-Fold data). We will set a high hard limit for iterations (e.g., 3000) but rely entirely on the early stopping mechanism to determine the optimal training duration, decoupling the compute budget from hyperparameter tuning. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00003**.
*   **Hardware Efficiency:** All training will be performed on the NVIDIA A100 GPU using histogram-based binning to ensure the 5-fold training completes well within the time limit.

### Evaluation
*   **Inference:** For the test set, we will generate class probability vectors using all 5 trained models.
*   **Aggregation:** We will compute the **Unweighted Average** (Soft Voting) of the 5 probability vectors. This method effectively averages out the variance of the individual models.
*   **Prediction:** The final class label will be selected by applying `argmax` to the averaged probability vector.
*   **Validation:** We will calculate the overall Out-Of-Fold (OOF) accuracy by aggregating the predictions on the validation folds. This provides a robust estimate of test performance using the entire dataset, unlike the single-split validation.
