Based on the accumulated empirical evidence and the specific failure modes identified in previous iterations, I propose the following optimized strategy. This solution introduces a **Hybrid CNN-Transformer** architecture to synthesize the strengths of previous approaches while strictly adhering to the data and training protocols proven to work.

### Model
I propose replacing the pure CNN (EfficientNet) and pure Transformer (Swin) backbones with a **MaxViT-Tiny (Multi-Axis Vision Transformer)** architecture.
*   **Structural Innovation:** MaxViT represents a structural evolution that bridges the gap between the architectures used in **Lesson 00002** (EfficientNet) and **Lesson 00011** (Swin). It utilizes **MBConv** blocks (from EfficientNet) in early stages to efficiently capture high-frequency local textures (critical for identifying "rust" or "scab"), and **Block/Grid Attention** mechanisms in later stages to capture global context without the window-shifting complexity that failed at lower resolutions. This hybrid design explicitly targets the dual nature of the problem: distinguishing fine-grained lesions while understanding the global leaf structure.
*   **Pooling:** We will utilize the model's standard **Global Average Pooling (GAP)** head. We explicitly reject the use of learnable pooling layers (like GeM), as previous experiments demonstrated that the optimization latency of learning the pooling parameter hinders convergence in short training schedules (Cite **Lesson 00017**).

### Data
*   **Resolution:** Input images will be resized to **384x384 pixels**. This resolution has been empirically identified as the optimal trade-off, providing sufficient pixel density to resolve small disease artifacts where 256px failed (Cite **Lesson 00011**), while remaining computationally feasible compared to full resolution.
*   **Augmentation Strategy:**
    *   **Geometric:** We will strictly enforce **RandomRotate90** and **ShiftScaleRotate**. This addresses the critical failure mode where models trained with only horizontal/vertical flips failed to generalize to rotation-invariant leaf orientations (Cite **Lesson 00015**).
    *   **Regularization:** We will employ **CoarseDropout (Cutout)** and moderate **RandomBrightnessContrast**. These are necessary to regularize the model capacity without the optimization difficulty of pixel-mixing strategies (Cite **Lesson 00002**).
    *   **Exclusion:** We will explicitly **exclude Mixup and CutMix**. Empirical evidence confirms that these techniques induce underfitting when used within limited training epochs (<20), as the model struggles to learn from the noisy, mixed targets (Cite **Lesson 00005**).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** We will use **BCEWithLogitsLoss** combined with **Label Smoothing** ($\epsilon=0.05$). This replaces hard binary targets to prevent the model from becoming overconfident on ambiguous images (especially the "complex" class), which was shown to improve validation F1 scores even when training loss was higher (Cite **Lesson 00007**).
*   **Precision:** **Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP)** will be enabled. This is strictly required to manage the memory footprint of the MaxViT attention blocks at 384x384 resolution (Cite **Lesson 00002**).
*   **Optimization:** The model will be trained using **AdamW** with a **Cosine Annealing** scheduler. We will use **ImageNet-1k** pretrained weights rather than 21k to minimize the risk of overfitting, as we are not using the heavy Mixup regularization required to stabilize 21k models (Cite **Lesson 00013**).

### Evaluation
*   **Inference:** Predictions will be generated using a **single crop** at 384x384 pixels.
*   **TTA Strategy:** We will **disable Test-Time Augmentation (TTA)**. Previous experiments demonstrated that averaging predictions across flipped images blurs the precision of high-confidence detections in this specific domain, resulting in a lower F1-score compared to the single-crop baseline (Cite **Lesson 00007**).
*   **Post-Processing:** A threshold of **0.5** will be applied to the sigmoid probabilities to generate the final space-delimited labels.
