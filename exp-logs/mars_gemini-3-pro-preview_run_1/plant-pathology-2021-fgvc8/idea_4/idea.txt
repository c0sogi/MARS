Based on the comprehensive analysis of previous experiments and the specific "Lessons Learned," I propose the following optimized strategy. This solution pivots to a **ConvNeXt** architecture but fundamentally restructures the training pipeline to correct the specific failure modes observed in previous attempts (specifically Lesson 00005 and Lesson 00013).

### Model
I propose replacing the EfficientNet backbone with a **ConvNeXt-Small** architecture, modified with a custom pooling head.
*   **Architecture:** We will utilize **ConvNeXt-Small** pre-trained on **ImageNet-21k**. ConvNeXt modernizes the CNN design with large kernels (7x7) and LayerNorm, offering a superior balance of local texture extraction (critical for disease spots) and global context compared to the MBConv blocks of EfficientNet (Cite Lesson 00001, Lesson 00011).
*   **Structural Innovation (GeM Pooling):** We will replace the standard Global Average Pooling (GAP) layer with **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling**. Standard GAP dilutes the signal of small disease lesions by averaging them with the healthy leaf background. GeM Pooling (a trainable parameter $p$) interpolates between Max and Average pooling, allowing the model to focus on high-activation regions (the disease spots) while ignoring the background. This directly addresses the fine-grained nature of the task.
*   **Head Design:** The output of the GeM layer will pass through a BatchNorm layer, a Dropout layer, and finally a Linear projection to the 6 class logits.

### Data
*   **Resolution:** Input images will be resized to **384x384 pixels**. This resolution was empirically identified as the "sweet spot," outperforming 256px and 320px by resolving smaller artifacts (Cite Lesson 00007, Lesson 00011).
*   **Augmentation Strategy:**
    *   **Geometric:** We will strictly enforce **RandomRotate90** and **ShiftScaleRotate**. Previous experiments proved that simple horizontal/vertical flips are insufficient for rotation-invariant objects like leaves, leading to overfitting (Cite Lesson 00015).
    *   **Color/Regularization:** We will include **HueSaturationValue**, **RandomBrightnessContrast**, and **CoarseDropout**. These strong augmentations are mandatory to prevent the higher-capacity ImageNet-21k model from overfitting (Cite Lesson 00013, Lesson 00002).
    *   **Exclusion:** We will explicitly **exclude Mixup and CutMix**. Empirical evidence showed that these techniques cause underfitting when used with short training schedules (<20 epochs) on this specific dataset (Cite Lesson 00005).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** We will use **BCEWithLogitsLoss** combined with **Label Smoothing** ($\epsilon=0.05$). This replaces hard binary targets to prevent overconfidence in the "complex" class and improves generalization on the validation set (Cite Lesson 00007).
*   **Optimization:** The model will be trained using **AdamW** with a **Cosine Annealing** scheduler.
*   **Precision:** **Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP)** will be enabled. This is strictly required to handle the memory footprint of the ConvNeXt backbone at 384x384 resolution (Cite Lesson 00002).
*   **Strategy:** We will train for approx 10-15 epochs. The combination of ImageNet-21k initialization and the removal of Mixup will ensure rapid convergence, while the strong geometric/color augmentations will ensure robust generalization.

### Evaluation
*   **Inference:** Predictions will be generated using a **single crop** at 384x384 pixels.
*   **TTA Strategy:** We will **disable Test-Time Augmentation (TTA)**. Previous findings demonstrated that averaging predictions across flips blurs the precision of high-confidence detections, resulting in a lower F1-score compared to the single-crop baseline (Cite Lesson 00007).
*   **Post-Processing:** A threshold of **0.5** will be applied to the sigmoid probabilities to generate the final space-delimited labels.
