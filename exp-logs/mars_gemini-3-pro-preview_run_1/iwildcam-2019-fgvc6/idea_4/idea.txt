Based on the comprehensive analysis of previous attempts and the specific lessons learned, I propose the following optimized strategy. This solution structurally evolves from the previous ideas by integrating the **Full Dataset** (unlike Idea 2) with **Standard Augmentations** (unlike Idea 3) and a **Robust Loss Function** (unlike Idea 1), centered around a more powerful backbone architecture.

# Model
I will upgrade the architecture from ConvNeXt-Tiny to **ConvNeXt-Small**.
*   **Rationale:** The A100 GPU has sufficient compute and VRAM (40GB) to handle a larger model than the Tiny variant used in Idea 2. ConvNeXt-Small provides a deeper and wider feature extraction capability, which is crucial for distinguishing between visually similar animal species and the complex background.
*   **Initialization:** The model will be initialized with **ImageNet-1k** pretrained weights to leverage learned visual primitives.
*   **EMA:** I will implement **Exponential Moving Average (EMA)** of the model weights. While Idea 3 failed, it was due to Mixup/No-RRC; EMA itself remains a valid technique to improve generalization and stability on the test set by smoothing out the optimization trajectory.

# Data
The data pipeline will be fundamentally restructured to resolve the conflicts identified in the lessons.
1.  **Full Dataset Utilization:** I will train on the **entire 196,157 image training set**. **Cite Lesson 00008** established that subsampling (used in Idea 2) discards critical rare examples that no amount of loss weighting can recover.
2.  **No Sampling:** I will **not** use a WeightedRandomSampler. **Cite Lesson 00002** demonstrated that mechanically oversampling location-grouped data leads to background memorization. The model will see the natural distribution of data.
3.  **Augmentation Strategy:**
    *   **RandomResizedCrop (RRC):** I will rigorously apply RRC. **Cite Lesson 00016** showed that removing RRC in favor of global resizing destroys the pixel information of small animals. RRC is essential to "zoom in" on animals during training.
    *   **Standard Augmentations Only:** I will use HorizontalFlip, ColorJitter, and GaussianBlur. I will **explicitly exclude** Mixup and CutMix. **Cite Lesson 00015** proved that these heavy regularizers retard convergence too much for this specific dataset and training horizon.
    *   **Input Size:** Images will be resized to 224x224 pixels.

# Training
The training strategy focuses on handling the long-tail distribution mathematically via the loss function rather than mechanically via sampling.
1.  **Loss Function:** I will use **Class-Balanced Focal Loss**.
    *   **Correction:** The class weights (calculated as $N_{total}/N_{class}$) will be applied as an **external multiplier** to the loss output, not inside the probability computation. **Cite Lesson 00004** identified that internal weighting distorts probabilities and prevents learning.
    *   **Background Preservation:** By using the full dataset and Focal Loss, we avoid the "aggressive undersampling" of the empty class that hurt precision in **Lesson 00014**. The model will see plenty of "empty" negatives to learn the background, while the Focal term focuses the gradient on the hard/rare positives.
2.  **Optimizer:** AdamW with a weight decay of 1e-2.
3.  **Scheduler:** Cosine Annealing LR scheduler.
4.  **Batch Size:** I will maximize the batch size (e.g., 128 or 256) to ensure stable gradient estimation given the class imbalance.

# Evaluation
*   **Inference:** Images will be resized to 224x224 (no cropping) and normalized.
*   **Prediction:** The final predictions will be generated using the **EMA weights** of the model, which typically offer a more robust decision boundary than the final checkpoint.
*   **Metric:** The model will be evaluated on the validation set using Macro F1, but the primary signal for success will be the balance between Recall on rare classes and Precision on the "Empty" class.
