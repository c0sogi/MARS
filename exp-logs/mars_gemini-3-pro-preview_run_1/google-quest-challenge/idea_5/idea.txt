Based on the comprehensive analysis of previous experiments and the identified "Lessons," I propose the following optimized strategy.

# Model
I propose a **Lightweight DeBERTa-v3 Dual-Encoder with Multi-Sample Dropout**. This architecture is designed to resolve the "diminishing returns of complexity" observed in **Cite solution_lesson_node_00023** by "right-sizing" the backbone, while introducing advanced regularization to maximize generalization on the small dataset.

*   **Backbone Architecture:** Utilize **`microsoft/deberta-v3-small`**.
    *   *Rationale:* Previous experiments showed that `deberta-v3-base` (184M params) underperformed `distilroberta-base` (82M params) due to overfitting and optimization difficulties on the small training set (~4.4k samples) (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00023**). The `small` variant (~44M params) provides the advanced disentangled attention mechanisms of DeBERTa (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00008**) but with a parameter count significantly better suited to the data volume, preventing the "Starved Batch" issues by allowing larger physical batch sizes (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00022**).
*   **Dual-Branch Structure:** Maintain the Siamese design, processing "Question" and "Answer" streams independently to preserve their distinct semantic properties (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00001**).
*   **Masked Hybrid Pooling:** Apply **Masked Global Average and Max Pooling** to the final hidden state of both branches (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00003**). Avoid "Weighted Layer Pooling" to reduce architectural complexity (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00023**).
*   **Interaction-Aware Fusion:**
    1.  Compute explicit interactions (Element-wise Product and Absolute Difference) *only* on the **Average Pooled** vectors to capture semantic alignment (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00007**).
    2.  Concatenate the Average Pooled vectors, Max Pooled vectors, and interaction terms.
    3.  Apply **Layer Normalization** to the fused vector to stabilize the heterogeneous feature scales (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00009**).
*   **Multi-Sample Dropout Head:** Replace the standard MLP with a **Multi-Sample Dropout** head.
    *   *Mechanism:* Instead of a single dropout pass, create $K$ (e.g., 5) different dropout masks. Pass the fused vector through the MLP $K$ times (once for each mask) and average the outputs *before* calculating the loss.
    *   *Benefit:* This acts as a strong regularizer and ensemble-within-a-model, accelerating convergence and improving generalization on small datasets, directly addressing the overfitting risks of previous iterations.
*   **Monolithic Prediction:** Predict all 30 targets with a single head to leverage inter-label correlations (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00016**).

# Data
*   **Preprocessing:** Concatenate `question_title` and `question_body` for the Question input; use `answer` for the Answer input.
*   **Tokenization:** Use the `microsoft/deberta-v3-small` tokenizer.
*   **Dynamic Padding:** Pad sequences to the longest length in the batch (not fixed 512) to optimize training speed (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00018**).

# Training
*   **Differential Learning Rates:** Apply a high learning rate (e.g., `1e-3`) to the Multi-Sample Dropout MLP head and a low learning rate (e.g., `2e-5`) to the backbone. This ensures the head escapes random initialization without destroying the pre-trained features (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00015**).
*   **Parameter Grouping:** Explicitly exclude `bias` and `LayerNorm` parameters from weight decay in the AdamW optimizer to prevent underfitting (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00018**).
*   **Batch Size:** Use a batch size of **16**. The smaller backbone allows this to fit in memory easily, ensuring stable gradient estimation without the need for aggressive gradient accumulation (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00012**).
*   **Schedule:** Train for **5 epochs** using a continuous linear decay schedule with warmup. Do not reset the optimizer after the initial head warmup; allow the momentum to carry over (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00019**, **Cite solution_lesson_node_00011**).
*   **Loss Function:** Binary Cross Entropy (BCE) with Logits Loss.

# Evaluation
*   **Inference:** Pass the test data through the model to generate 30 logits per sample.
*   **Prediction:** Apply Sigmoid activation to map logits to [0, 1].
*   **Validation:** Monitor Mean Column-wise Spearman's Correlation. Accept the model if it exceeds the previous best (`~0.408`) plus a small noise margin, treating the metric as a distribution rather than a point estimate (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00010**).
