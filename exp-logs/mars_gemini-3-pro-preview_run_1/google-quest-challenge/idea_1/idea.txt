To establish a highly efficient baseline that prioritizes speed and simplicity while remaining distinct from the provided architectures, I propose a **Dual-Branch Deep Averaging Network (DAN)**. This approach strips away the computational complexity of Transformers (Model 1), Recurrent layers (Model 2), and Convolutions (Model 3) in favor of a lightweight, averaging-based neural architecture.

### Model
The architecture consists of two parallel branches: one for the Question (concatenating Title and Body) and one for the Answer.
1.  **Embedding Layer**: Both branches share a trainable embedding layer initialized with random weights or lightweight pre-trained vectors (e.g., FastText). This maps input tokens to dense vectors.
2.  **Global Average Pooling**: Instead of using sequential processing (RNNs) or self-attention (Transformers), the model simply computes the mean of the embedding vectors across the sequence length for each branch. This results in a fixed-size representation vector for the Question ($u$) and the Answer ($v$).
3.  **Concatenation**: The two vectors $u$ and $v$ are concatenated directly ($[u; v]$) to form a combined representation. Unlike Model 2, we avoid manually engineered interaction features (like difference or element-wise product), relying on the subsequent layers to learn necessary interactions.
4.  **MLP Head**: The combined vector is passed through a simple Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) consisting of two fully connected layers with ReLU activation and Dropout for regularization.
5.  **Output**: A final linear layer projects the features to 30 dimensions, followed by a Sigmoid activation to ensure predictions fall within the $[0,1]$ range.

### Data
1.  **Text Preprocessing**: Concatenate `question_title` and `question_body` into a single "Question" field. Keep `answer` separate. Perform basic normalization (lowercasing) and simple tokenization (splitting by whitespace).
2.  **Vocabulary Construction**: Build a vocabulary from the training corpus, keeping the top $N$ most frequent words (e.g., 20,000) and assigning them integer IDs. Rare words are mapped to an `<UNK>` token.
3.  **Sequence Formatting**: Convert text sequences into fixed-length integer arrays by truncating or padding with zeros. This allows for efficient batch processing on the GPU.

### Training
1.  **Loss Function**: Use **Binary Cross Entropy (BCE) Loss**. Since the target labels are continuous probabilities between 0 and 1, BCE is often more numerically stable and appropriate than Mean Squared Error (MSE) for this specific range.
2.  **Optimizer**: Utilize the **Adam** optimizer with default parameters, which generally converges faster than SGD for embedding-based models.
3.  **Strategy**: Train for a small number of epochs (e.g., 5-10) with a standard batch size (e.g., 32 or 64). The simplicity of the model ensures extremely fast epoch times compared to Transformer or RNN baselines.

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference**: Pass the preprocessed `test.csv` data (Question and Answer sequences) through the trained model to generate 30 probability scores for each `qa_id`.
2.  **Post-processing**: Ensure no values fall outside the $[0,1]$ interval (guaranteed by Sigmoid, but good to verify).
3.  **Submission**: Format the output with the `qa_id` and the 30 target columns as specified in the submission sample.
