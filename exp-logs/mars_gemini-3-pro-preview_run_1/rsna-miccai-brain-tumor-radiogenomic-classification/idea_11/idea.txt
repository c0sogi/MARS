# Independent-Instance 2.5D Volumetric Ensemble (I2VE)

This solution introduces a **Data-Centric Structural Innovation**. Instead of increasing model complexity (which consistently failed in Ideas 5, 6, 8, and 10), we fundamentally alter the training paradigm from "Subject-Level Classification" to **"Slice-Level Instance Learning"**. This approach leverages the "Early Fusion" stability of 2D backbones (Cite solution_lesson_node_00031) while addressing the "Small Data" bottleneck and "Middle Slice Fallacy" (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002) by effectively tripling the dataset size and enforcing independent feature learning.

### Model
*   **Architecture:** We utilize a standard **EfficientNet-B0** backbone initialized with ImageNet weights.
*   **Input:** A 3-channel 2D composite image $(224 \times 224 \times 3)$.
    *   Channel 1: **FLAIR** slice.
    *   Channel 2: **T1wCE** slice.
    *   Channel 3: **T2w** slice.
*   **Design Rationale:** We strictly adhere to **Early Fusion** (stacking modalities) rather than Multi-Stream architectures, as the latter tripled the parameter count and caused severe overfitting on this small dataset (Cite solution_lesson_node_00031). We also avoid learnable input projections (Cite solution_lesson_node_00025) and Siamese aggregation layers (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018) to preserve the pretrained backbone's feature extraction capabilities.

### Data
The innovation lies in the **Preprocessing and Sampling Pipeline**:

1.  **Brain-Centric ROI Definition:**
    *   To address the "Middle Slice Fallacy" where naive geometric centers often land on the neck or skull (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002), we first compute the **Z-axis Bounding Box** of the brain. We load the middle file of the sequence, determine a silence threshold (pixel > 0), and scan the file list to identify the start and end indices of actual brain tissue.
2.  **Deterministic Data Expansion (The Innovation):**
    *   From this Brain-Centric ROI, we deterministically extract **3 distinct slices** at relative depths of **45%, 50%, and 55%**.
    *   **Crucially**, we treat these 3 slices as **independent training examples**, assigning the patient's global `MGMT_value` label to each.
    *   *Rationale:* This effectively triples the training set size (~1200 samples), forcing the model to learn robust features from multiple cross-sections. This avoids the "Signal Dilution" observed in Siamese networks (where max-pooling discarded useful features, Cite solution_lesson_node_00018) and the "Label Noise" of stochastic sampling (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015).
3.  **High-Fidelity Preprocessing:**
    *   **Precision:** All operations are performed in **float32**. We strictly avoid casting to `uint8` to preserve the high dynamic range of the MRI sensors (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016).
    *   **Normalization:** We apply **Independent Channel Min-Max Scaling** to the $[0, 1]$ range. We normalize FLAIR, T1wCE, and T2w separately *before* stacking to prevent high-intensity modalities from suppressing low-intensity ones (Cite solution_lesson_node_00023). We avoid ImageNet mean/std normalization (Cite solution_lesson_node_00017).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) with Logits.
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with a learning rate of $1e-4$.
*   **Regularization:**
    *   **Aggressive Weight Decay:** Set to **$1e-2$**. Since we are training on a relatively small dataset, strong regularization is critical to force feature generalization (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).
    *   **Dropout:** We utilize the default dropout rate of the EfficientNet backbone, avoiding manual overrides that may degrade performance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00012).
    *   **Augmentation:** We apply **Elastic Transformations** and **Grid Distortions**. Non-rigid augmentations are essential for modeling anatomical variance in medical imaging (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).
*   **Validation Strategy:** We employ **5-Fold Cross-Validation** grouped by **Subject ID**. This ensures that slices from the same patient never appear in both train and validation sets simultaneously, preventing data leakage (Cite solution_lesson_node_00028).

### Evaluation
*   **Inference:** For each test subject, we deterministically generate the same 3 inputs (Brain-Centric slices at 45%, 50%, 55%).
*   **Consensus Aggregation:** We pass each slice through the model independently to obtain 3 probability scores. The final prediction for the subject is the **mean** of these scores.
    *   *Rationale:* This acts as a deterministic "Test-Time Ensemble". It captures the volumetric context without the complexity of 3D models (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008) and ensures that if the tumor is visible in *any* of the central slices, the prediction is boosted, providing robustness against positioning errors.
