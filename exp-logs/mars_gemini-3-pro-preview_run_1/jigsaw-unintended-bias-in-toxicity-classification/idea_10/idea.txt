Based on the lessons learned and the progression of previous ideas, I propose the following optimized strategy. This solution introduces a **Curriculum Fine-Tuning Pipeline** as the primary structural innovation. Instead of attempting to optimize all objectives simultaneously (as in Idea 9), this strategy decomposes the training into distinct phases: Domain Adaptation, General Feature Learning, and Robust Metric Optimization. This allows the model to stabilize its representation before applying computationally expensive and aggressive techniques like Adversarial Weight Perturbation (AWP) and Ranking Loss.

# Proposed Solution: DeBERTa-v3-Large with Curriculum Fine-Tuning and Multi-Task EMA

### Model
**Architecture:**
We utilize **DeBERTa-v3-Large** as the backbone. Its disentangled attention mechanism is critical for distinguishing the content of an identity term from its context.
**Multi-Task Learning (MTL) Heads:**
The encoder feeds into three distinct classification heads to enforce semantic disentanglement **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00004)**:
1.  **Primary Toxicity Head:** Predicts the main `target`.
2.  **Auxiliary Identity Head:** Predicts the presence of identity attributes (e.g., `male`, `muslim`).
3.  **Auxiliary Identity Attack Head:** Predicts the `identity_attack` subtype to explicitly model the difference between "Identity" and "Attack".
**Exponential Moving Average (EMA):**
We maintain a shadow copy of the model parameters using EMA. This stabilizes the decision boundary and contributes to finding a flatter minimum in the loss landscape, complementing the AWP strategy.

### Data
**Preprocessing:**
1.  **DAPT Corpus:** Concatenate `comment_text` from Train and Test sets for unsupervised learning.
2.  **Target Engineering:** Generate soft targets for Toxicity and binary targets for Identity/Attack heads.
3.  **Dual Samplers:**
    *   **Uniform Stratified Sampler:** Preserves the natural data distribution (used in Stage 2).
    *   **Bias-Focused Weighted Sampler:** Heavily oversamples the "Bias Trap" subgroups (Non-Toxic + Identity, Toxic + Identity) to ensure dense batches of hard examples **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00007)**.

### Training
We implement a **Three-Stage Curriculum Pipeline**:

**Stage 1: Domain-Adaptive Pretraining (DAPT)**
We perform Masked Language Modeling (MLM) on the combined Train + Test corpus. This adapts the DeBERTa-Large backbone to the specific linguistic distribution (slang, obfuscations) of the competition data **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00010)**.

**Stage 2: General Multi-Task Fine-Tuning**
We fine-tune the model using the **Uniform Stratified Sampler** to learn general toxicity features without overfitting to the metric yet.
*   **Loss:** Weighted Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) on the Primary Head + Standard BCE on Auxiliary Heads.
*   **Weighting:** We apply **Bias-Centric Sample Weights** (e.g., 5.0x for bias traps) to the Primary Loss. This embeds the "cost" of bias into the loss surface while maintaining global distribution diversity **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00002)**.

**Stage 3: Robust Metric Optimization (Curriculum Shift)**
We switch to the **Bias-Focused Weighted Sampler** and introduce advanced optimization objectives to refine the decision boundary for the specific AUC metric.
*   **Pairwise Ranking Loss:** We add a Margin Ranking Loss that penalizes the model when it ranks a "Non-Toxic Identity" example higher than a "Toxic Background" example. The weighted sampler ensures these hard pairs exist in every batch **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00007)**.
*   **Adversarial Weight Perturbation (AWP):** We enable AWP to inject worst-case noise into the weights during training. This explicitly flattens the loss landscape, preventing the model from relying on brittle features (like specific identity tokens) and ensuring robustness **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00010)**.

### Evaluation
**Inference:**
Predictions are generated using the **EMA weights** of the **Primary Toxicity Head**. The auxiliary heads and ranking logic are discarded, as they served their purpose of shaping the encoder's representation during the curriculum training.
