# EfficientNet-B3 with Context-Preserving Square Crops and Focal Loss

### Model
I propose upgrading the architecture to **EfficientNet-B3**, replacing the ResNet18 used in previous iterations. EfficientNet-B3 offers a superior balance of accuracy and computational efficiency, providing the necessary capacity to distinguish between the 185 fine-grained animal classes.
*   **Backbone:** EfficientNet-B3 pretrained on ImageNet.
*   **Input Resolution:** The input size will be increased to **300x300** (the native resolution for B3), significantly higher than the previous 224x224. This higher resolution is critical for preserving texture details in animal fur and patterns after cropping.
*   **Head:** A standard global average pooling layer followed by a dense classification layer for the 185 classes.

### Data
The data processing pipeline will be refined to maximize feature quality and geometric consistency, addressing the limitations of simple resizing.
1.  **Context-Preserving Square Crops:** Instead of a tight crop that requires distortion (stretching) or zero-padding to fit a square input, I will implement a **square-context cropping strategy**.
    *   For each MegaDetector bounding box, determine the maximum dimension (width or height).
    *   Expand this dimension by a margin (e.g., 10-20%) to define the side length of a square.
    *   Crop this square region from the original image, centered on the animal. This ensures the animal's aspect ratio is preserved (preventing distortion) and automatically includes valuable background context (habitat information) along the shorter dimension. This adheres to the core principle of focusing on the object (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
2.  **Full Dataset Utilization:** The model will be trained on the entire training dataset (125,760 images) rather than a subset, ensuring exposure to the long tail of rare classes (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
3.  **Aggressive Augmentation:** I will maintain the successful aggressive augmentation policy (ShiftScaleRotate, ColorJitter) to encourage invariance to camera trap variations (lighting, pose, blur) (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).

### Training
The training strategy is designed to handle extreme class imbalance without the stability issues encountered previously.
1.  **Loss Function (Focal Loss):** I will replace the "Inverse Frequency Weighted Cross-Entropy" with **Focal Loss**.
    *   *Rationale:* Previous attempts showed that combining aggressive static class weights with regularization (like label smoothing) caused divergence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006). Focal Loss dynamically down-weights easy examples (like the abundant "Empty" class) and focuses gradients on hard, misclassified examples. This achieves the goal of balancing the dataset without creating a high "loss floor" that destabilizes optimization.
2.  **Optimizer & Schedule:** Use the Adam optimizer. The training duration will be set to **15-20 epochs**. This extended duration is necessary to allow the larger EfficientNet-B3 backbone to converge given the increased input resolution and the difficulty introduced by aggressive augmentations (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).

### Evaluation
1.  **Test-Time Processing:** For each test image, extract the highest-confidence MegaDetector bounding box. Apply the same **Context-Preserving Square Crop** logic used in training to generate a 300x300 input.
2.  **Inference:** Pass the tensor through the trained EfficientNet-B3.
3.  **Prediction:** Select the class with the highest probability. If the MegaDetector finds no object, or the confidence is extremely low, predict "Empty" (Category 0) directly, or process the full image if the pipeline allows, though reliance on the detector is preferred.
