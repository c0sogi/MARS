This approach synthesizes the successful data engineering and training pipelines from previous iterations while introducing a **structural innovation** in the model architecture to further capitalize on the available compute resources (NVIDIA A100).

# Model: ConvNeXt-Small with High-Resolution Input
I propose replacing the EfficientNet-B3 backbone with a **ConvNeXt-Small** architecture.
*   **Structural Innovation**: Unlike the EfficientNet family (which relies on Neural Architecture Search and MBConv blocks), ConvNeXt modernizes the standard ResNet structure by incorporating design choices from Vision Transformers (e.g., non-overlapping "patchify" stem, larger 7x7 depthwise kernels, LayerNorm, and GELU activations). This architecture is designed to capture long-range dependencies and texture details more effectively than standard CNNs, which is critical for distinguishing fine-grained species features in cluttered camera trap environments.
*   **Capacity**: ConvNeXt-Small (~50M parameters) is moderately heavier than EfficientNet-B3 (~12M parameters) but remains well within the computational budget of the A100 GPU. This increased capacity allows the model to learn more complex feature representations from the large-scale dataset.
*   **Input Resolution**: I will increase the input resolution to **384x384**. This aligns with the native resolution often used for fine-tuning ConvNeXt and directly addresses the need to maximize pixel density on the target animal (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002), ensuring that even small animals in the crop retain sufficient detail.

# Data: Context-Aware Cropping on the Full Dataset
The data pipeline will strictly adhere to the "Context-Aware" and "Full Data" principles established in the lessons.
1.  **Preprocessing**: For every image, I will use the MegaDetector bounding box to generate a **Context-Preserving Square Crop**. I will apply a **20% margin** around the detection (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009) to include immediate habitat context (vegetation, ground texture) which aids classification, while avoiding the "loss of resolution" caused by using full images (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
2.  **Dataset Scope**: I will train on the **full training dataset (125,760 images)**. Previous experiments proved that subsets are poor proxies for model capacity in high-cardinality tasks (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003), and the full diversity is required to handle the 185 classes.
3.  **Augmentation**: I will employ **aggressive data augmentation** (ShiftScaleRotate, ColorJitter) to encourage invariance to lighting and pose (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).

# Training: Focal Loss and Extended Cosine Schedule
The training strategy combines the stability of Focal Loss with a fully utilized learning rate schedule.
1.  **Loss Function**: I will use **Focal Loss** instead of Weighted Cross-Entropy. This dynamically down-weights the "easy" examples (such as the abundant "Empty" class) and focuses gradients on hard, misclassified examples, effectively handling the extreme class imbalance without the instability observed with static weighting schemes (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009). *Note: I will explicitly avoid Label Smoothing, as it caused divergence when combined with imbalance handling techniques (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).*
2.  **Optimizer & Schedule**: I will use the AdamW optimizer with a **Cosine Annealing** scheduler. Crucially, I will set the scheduler's horizon (`T_max`) to match the full number of epochs (e.g., 15-20) to stretch the decay profile (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014).
3.  **Convergence Strategy**: I will **disable early stopping** (or set patience to the full epoch count). This ensures the model benefits from the final phase of the Cosine schedule where the learning rate is minimal and stability is highest (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015).
4.  **Batch Size**: I will maximize the batch size (e.g., 64 or 128) to fit the A100 memory, ensuring stable Batch Normalization statistics and smoother gradient estimates (Cite solution_lesson_node_00011).
5.  **Coupled Duration**: Since I am using aggressive augmentation, I will ensure the training duration is sufficient (15+ epochs) to allow convergence on the harder data distribution (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).

# Evaluation: TTA-Enhanced Inference
To maximize robustness and accuracy without additional training cost:
1.  **Test Time Augmentation (TTA)**: During inference, I will perform two forward passes for each test image: one on the original crop and one on a horizontally flipped version. The predictions will be averaged. This simple ensemble technique has been proven to consistently boost performance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00012).
2.  **Pipeline**: The test images will undergo the same MegaDetector-guided cropping (with 20% margin) and resizing to 384x384 as the training set.
