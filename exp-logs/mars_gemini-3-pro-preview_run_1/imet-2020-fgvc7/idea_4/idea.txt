Based on the comprehensive analysis of previous experiments and lessons learned, I propose an optimized strategy that evolves the architecture to a higher-capacity backbone while integrating robust stabilization techniques to handle label noise.

### Model
I propose upgrading the backbone to **ConvNeXt-Small**.
*   **Architecture:** Moving from ConvNeXt-Tiny to **ConvNeXt-Small** increases the network's depth and width. This added capacity is necessary to capture the fine-grained artistic details and disentangle the complex correlations among 3,474 attributes, building on the success of the ConvNeXt family observed previously (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
*   **Pooling:** I will retain the **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling** layer. This is structurally essential for this dataset to dynamically focus on salient objects in "centered" images while aggregating features effectively in "scenic" room images, avoiding the signal dilution caused by standard Global Average Pooling (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
*   **Structural Innovation (Model EMA):** I will implement **Model Exponential Moving Average (EMA)**. By maintaining a shadow copy of the model parameters that decays slowly (e.g., decay rate 0.9999) towards the trained weights, we create a temporal ensemble. This is a critical structural addition to improve robustness against the "noisy" and "unverified" annotations explicitly mentioned in the dataset description, ensuring the final model is less prone to overfitting specific label errors (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).

### Data
*   **Preprocessing:** I will maintain the input resolution of **320x320** pixels. This resolution has proven superior to 224x224 for preserving the brushstrokes and textures required for artwork classification (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
*   **Pipeline:** I will strictly configure the DataLoader with `num_workers=4` and `pin_memory=True`. This parallelization is non-negotiable to prevent GPU starvation and ensure the training throughput required to converge the larger ConvNeXt-Small model within the time limit (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).
*   **Augmentation:** I will apply the **aggressive augmentation strategy** (including `ShiftScaleRotate` and `ColorJitter`) established in prior successful runs. High-capacity models like ConvNeXt-Small require difficult training samples to learn robust features and prevent memorization (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** I will use **BCEWithLogitsLoss** with a **moderate positive weight (e.g., 10-12)**. This balances the need to amplify the signal for sparse positive labels (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001) without degrading precision through excessive false positives, as observed with aggressive weighting (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003). I will also retain **Label Smoothing (0.05)** to further mitigate the impact of noisy ground truth labels (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
*   **Optimization:** I will use the **AdamW** optimizer combined with a **Cosine Annealing** learning rate scheduler, which has proven effective for convergence in this domain (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
*   **Strategy:** I will train for **15-20 epochs**. Leveraging the 24-hour runtime, this extended duration ensures the heavier ConvNeXt-Small backbone fully converges. This explicitly avoids the pitfall of "undertraining" where a superior architecture fails because the epoch count was reduced to save time (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008).

### Evaluation
*   **Inference:** Predictions will be generated using the **EMA weights**, which typically offer better generalization than the final training snapshot.
*   **Threshold Tuning:** I will perform a grid search for the optimal probability threshold on the validation set, strictly searching the **full probability range (0.01 to 0.99)**. This ensures the selected operating point is mathematically optimal and not artificially constrained by a narrow search window (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
