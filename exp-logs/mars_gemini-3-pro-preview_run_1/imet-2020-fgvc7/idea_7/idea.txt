Based on the comprehensive analysis of the provided lessons and the progression of previous experiments, I propose the following optimized strategy. This solution structurally evolves the architecture from the successful ConvNeXt-Tiny baseline to a higher-capacity **ConvNeXt-Small** model, while introducing **Mixup/CutMix** regularization and **Model EMA** to specifically address the challenges of label noise and model scaling identified in the lessons.

### Model
I propose a high-capacity architecture designed to disentangle the complex correlations among the 3,474 attributes while remaining robust to label noise.
*   **Backbone:** I will utilize **ConvNeXt-Small** initialized with pre-trained weights. The progression from ResNet34 to ResNet50 demonstrated that increasing model capacity yields significant performance gains when properly regularized (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007). ConvNeXt-Small provides the necessary depth and width to capture fine-grained artistic details that the lighter ConvNeXt-Tiny may miss.
*   **Pooling:** I will strictly retain the **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling** layer at the head of the network. This component is structurally essential for this dataset, as it allows the model to dynamically focus on salient objects in "centered" images while effectively aggregating features in "scenic" room images, avoiding the signal dilution caused by standard Global Average Pooling (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
*   **Structural Innovation (Model EMA):** I will implement **Model Exponential Moving Average (EMA)**. By maintaining a "shadow" copy of the model parameters that decays slowly (e.g., decay rate 0.9999) towards the training weights, we create a temporal ensemble. This is a critical structural addition to improve robustness against the "noisy" and "unverified" annotations explicitly mentioned in the dataset description, ensuring the final model is less prone to overfitting specific label errors (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).

### Data
*   **Preprocessing:** I will maintain the input resolution of **320x320** pixels. This resolution was empirically shown to provide a significant performance boost over 224x224 by preserving the fine brushstrokes and textures required for artwork classification (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
*   **Pipeline:** I will configure the DataLoader with `num_workers=4` and `pin_memory=True`. This parallelization is non-negotiable to prevent GPU starvation and maximize training throughput, which is particularly critical when training the computationally heavier ConvNeXt-Small model (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).
*   **Augmentation (Structural Innovation):** I will introduce **Mixup and CutMix** augmentations into the training pipeline. Since higher-capacity models require stronger regularization to prevent overfitting (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007), Mixup/CutMix provides a more robust signal than standard geometric augmentations alone. This forces the model to learn interpolatable features and reduces the memorization of noisy labels.

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** I will use **BCEWithLogitsLoss** with a **moderate positive weight (e.g., 10-12)**. This balances the need to amplify the signal for sparse positive labels (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001) without degrading precision through excessive false positives, as observed with aggressive weighting (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003). I will also retain **Label Smoothing (0.05)** to further mitigate the impact of noisy ground truth labels (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
*   **Optimizer:** I will use the **AdamW** optimizer combined with a **Cosine Annealing** learning rate scheduler. This schedule has proven effective for facilitating convergence and generalization in high-capacity models for this domain (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
*   **Duration:** I will extend the training duration to **20 epochs**. Previous experiments showed that heavier models/resolutions can underperform if the epoch count is reduced to save time (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008). Leveraging the 24-hour runtime limit, this duration ensures the ConvNeXt-Small backbone fully converges.

### Evaluation
*   **Inference:** Predictions will be generated using the **EMA weights**, which typically offer better generalization and robustness to label noise than the final training snapshot.
*   **Threshold Tuning:** I will perform a grid search for the optimal probability threshold on the validation set, strictly searching the **full probability range (0.01 to 0.99)**. This ensures the selected operating point is mathematically optimal and prevents the sub-optimality caused by arbitrary search boundaries (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
