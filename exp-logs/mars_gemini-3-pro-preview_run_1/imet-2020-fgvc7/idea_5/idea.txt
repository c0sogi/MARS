Based on the lessons learned and the analysis of previous ideas, I propose the following optimized strategy. This approach structurally evolves the architecture to **ConvNeXt-Small** while introducing **Mixup/CutMix** regularization and **Model EMA** to address label noise and capacity scaling.

### Model
I propose upgrading the backbone to **ConvNeXt-Small**.
*   **Architecture:** I will replace the ConvNeXt-Tiny backbone with **ConvNeXt-Small**. The lessons demonstrated that increasing model capacity (e.g., ResNet34 to ResNet50) improves performance when properly regularized (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007). ConvNeXt-Small offers a necessary capacity increase to disentangle the 3,474 attributes, addressing the limitations of lighter backbones.
*   **Pooling:** I will retain the **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling** layer. This component was proven to outperform Global Average Pooling by dynamically focusing on salient objects in "centered" images while effectively aggregating features in "scenic" room images (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
*   **Robustness (Structural Innovation):** I will implement **Model Exponential Moving Average (EMA)**. By maintaining a shadow copy of the model weights that decays slowly (e.g., 0.9999) towards the training weights, we create a temporal ensemble. This structural change is critical to mitigate the impact of the "noisy" and "unverified" annotations explicitly mentioned in the dataset description, providing greater stability than the raw weights.

### Data
*   **Preprocessing:** I will maintain the input resolution of **320x320** pixels, which was shown to capture fine artistic details better than 224x224 (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
*   **Pipeline:** I will configure the DataLoader with `num_workers=4` and `pin_memory=True`. This parallelization is essential to prevent GPU starvation and maximize throughput for the larger model (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).
*   **Augmentation (Structural Innovation):** I will introduce **Mixup and CutMix** augmentations into the training pipeline. Previous lessons showed that higher-capacity models require aggressive regularization to prevent overfitting (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007). Mixup/CutMix provides a stronger regularization signal than standard geometric augmentations (`ShiftScaleRotate`), forcing the model to learn more robust, interpolatable features, which is particularly effective for the ConvNeXt architecture.

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** I will use **BCEWithLogitsLoss** with a **moderate positive weight (e.g., 10-12)** and **Label Smoothing (0.05)**. This configuration balances the need to amplify the signal for sparse positive labels (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001) without causing the precision collapse observed with aggressive weighting (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003), while smoothing prevents overconfidence in noisy labels (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
*   **Optimizer:** I will use the **AdamW** optimizer combined with a **Cosine Annealing** learning rate scheduler, which facilitates better convergence for high-capacity models (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
*   **Duration:** I will extend the training duration to **20 epochs**. Previous experiments with ConvNeXt-Tiny failed because they were undertrained (4 epochs) due to time constraints (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008). Allocating sufficient epochs ensures the heavier ConvNeXt-Small backbone fully converges within the 24-hour limit.

### Evaluation
*   **Inference:** Predictions will be generated using the **EMA weights**, which typically yield better generalization and robustness to noise than the final training snapshot.
*   **Threshold Tuning:** I will perform a grid search for the optimal probability threshold on the validation set, strictly searching the **full probability range (0.01 to 0.99)**. This ensures the selected operating point is mathematically optimal and prevents the sub-optimality caused by arbitrary search boundaries (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
