Based on the progression of empirical findings and the lessons learned, I propose the following optimized strategy. This approach structurally evolves the architecture to a higher-capacity backbone while integrating advanced regularization and weight averaging techniques to address the identified challenges of label noise and model scaling.

### Model
I propose upgrading the backbone architecture to **ConvNeXt-Small**.
*   **Architecture:** I will replace the ConvNeXt-Tiny backbone with **ConvNeXt-Small**. Previous experiments demonstrated that increasing model capacity (e.g., ResNet34 to ResNet50) yields significant performance gains when accompanied by appropriate regularization (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007). The ConvNeXt-Small architecture provides the necessary depth and width to better disentangle the complex correlations among the 3,474 attributes compared to the lighter Tiny variant.
*   **Pooling:** I will strictly retain the **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling** layer at the head of the network. This component has been proven to outperform Global Average Pooling by dynamically focusing on salient objects in "centered" images while effectively aggregating features in "scenic" room images, preventing signal dilution (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
*   **Structural Innovation (Model EMA):** I will implement **Model Exponential Moving Average (EMA)**. By maintaining a "shadow" copy of the model parameters that decays slowly (e.g., decay rate 0.9999) towards the training weights, we create a temporal ensemble. This is a critical structural addition to improve robustness against the "noisy" and "unverified" annotations explicitly mentioned in the dataset description, providing greater stability than the raw weights from the final epoch.

### Data
*   **Preprocessing:** I will maintain the input resolution of **320x320** pixels. This resolution was empirically shown to provide a significant performance boost over 224x224 by preserving the fine artistic details required for this task (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
*   **Pipeline:** I will configure the DataLoader with `num_workers=4` and `pin_memory=True`. This parallelization is essential to prevent GPU starvation and maximize training throughput, which is particularly critical when training the computationally heavier ConvNeXt-Small model (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).
*   **Augmentation (Structural Innovation):** I will introduce **Mixup and CutMix** augmentations into the training pipeline. Since higher-capacity models require stronger regularization to prevent overfitting (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007), Mixup/CutMix provides a more robust signal than standard geometric augmentations alone, forcing the model to learn interpolatable features.

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** I will use **BCEWithLogitsLoss** with a **moderate positive weight (e.g., 10-12)**. This balances the need to amplify the signal for sparse positive labels (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001) without degrading precision through excessive false positives (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003). I will also retain **Label Smoothing (0.05)** to further mitigate the impact of noisy ground truth labels (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
*   **Optimizer:** I will use the **AdamW** optimizer combined with a **Cosine Annealing** learning rate scheduler. This schedule has proven effective for facilitating convergence and generalization in high-capacity models for this domain (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
*   **Duration:** I will extend the training duration to **20 epochs**. Previous experiments showed that heavier models/resolutions can underperform if the epoch count is reduced to save time (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008). Leveraging the 24-hour runtime limit, this duration ensures the ConvNeXt-Small backbone fully converges.

### Evaluation
*   **Inference:** Predictions will be generated using the **EMA weights**, which typically offer better generalization and robustness to label noise than the final training snapshot.
*   **Threshold Tuning:** I will perform a grid search for the optimal probability threshold on the validation set, strictly searching the **full probability range (0.01 to 0.99)**. This ensures the selected operating point is mathematically optimal and prevents the sub-optimality caused by arbitrary search boundaries (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
