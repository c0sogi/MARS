Based on the accumulated "Lessons Learned" and the analysis of previous experiments, I propose the **Morphology-Enhanced Quantile-Elastic Pipeline**.

This solution structurally evolves the previous "Spatially-Aware" and "MIP-Based" approaches by introducing **1D Morphological Profiling** as a feature engineering innovation. While previous ideas attempted to capture the 3D distribution of fibrosis using 2D image projections (MIPs) or zonal sampling, this solution explicitly models the **Apical-Basal Gradient**—the hallmark physical signature of IPF—as a compact set of polynomial coefficients derived from the Z-axis density curves. This strictly adheres to the "Inductive Bias" lesson by enforcing physical priors and maintains the successful "Deep Feature + Regularized Linear Solver" architecture.

### Model
The architecture consists of a **Dual-Branch Feature Extractor** feeding into a **Mathematically Specialized Linear System**.

1.  **Branch 1: Stratified Texture Analysis (Deep Features)**
    *   **Visual Backbone:** We utilize **EfficientNet-B0** (pre-trained on ImageNet) to extract texture descriptors.
    *   **Stratified Sampling:** We divide the CT scan into three anatomical zones (Upper, Middle, Lower) and select the slice with the highest pixel variance from each. This captures the "worst-case" local heterogeneity (e.g., honeycombing) while preserving coarse spatial context **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00008)**.
    *   **Dimensionality Reduction:** The features from the three slices are concatenated and compressed using **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)** to ~30 dimensions. This is mandatory to prevent the curse of dimensionality when feeding deep features into linear solvers **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00014)**.

2.  **Branch 2: Morphological Profiling (Structural Innovation)**
    *   **1D Signal Generation:** Instead of treating the lung volume as a bag of pixels, we compute two 1D signals along the Z-axis (from apex to base):
        *   **Area Profile:** The cross-sectional area of lung tissue per slice.
        *   **Density Profile:** The mean Hounsfield Unit (HU) of lung tissue per slice.
    *   **Polynomial Encoding:** We fit a **3rd-degree polynomial** to each of these 1D signals. The resulting coefficients (4 for Area, 4 for Density) compactly encode the **shape** (e.g., basal shrinkage) and **gradient** (e.g., increasing density towards the base) of the lungs.
    *   **Rationale:** This explicitly provides the linear model with the "Apical-Basal Gradient" as a low-dimensional physical prior, enforcing the inductive bias that lung capacity is defined by the 3D distribution of healthy tissue **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00013)**.

3.  **FVC Predictor (Linear Quantile Regressor)**
    *   A **Linear Quantile Regressor** targeting the **Median (q=0.5)**.
    *   **Inputs:** The concatenated vector $[X_{PCA\_Texture}, X_{Morphology}, X_{Clinical}]$.
    *   **Structure:** We employ a **Varying-Coefficient** design by generating explicit **Interaction Terms**: $X_{all} \times \text{Weeks}$. This allows the model to learn patient-specific rates of decline based on both their texture patterns and morphological gradients **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00008)**, preserving the linear prior required for trend extrapolation **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00006)**.
    *   **Objective:** Minimizing L1 loss (Median) aligns perfectly with the Laplace Log Likelihood metric **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00016)**.

4.  **Uncertainty Predictor (Elastic Net)**
    *   An **Elastic Net Regressor** (L1+L2 Regularization).
    *   **Inputs:** The static vector $[X_{PCA\_Texture}, X_{Morphology}, X_{Clinical}]$ plus the **Time Horizon** ($|t - t_{baseline}|$).
    *   **Rationale:** We use Elastic Net to predict the magnitude of errors, as unregularized GLMs have proven unstable **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00023)**. We include the Time Horizon to explicitly model **Temporal Heteroscedasticity** (uncertainty growing over time) **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00017)**, while excluding complex interactions to maintain **Parsimony** **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00011)**.

### Data
1.  **Preprocessing:**
    *   **Volume Loading & Masking:** Load DICOMs, stack into 3D volume, and threshold (HU -1000 to -400) to isolate lung tissue.
    *   **Morphological Extraction:** Iterate through the Z-axis to calculate `count_pixels` (Area) and `mean_hu` (Density) for every slice containing lung. Normalize the Z-index to [0, 1].
    *   **Curve Fitting:** Fit a cubic polynomial (`np.polyfit`) to the Area(z) and Density(z) series. Extract the 8 coefficients.
    *   **Texture Extraction:** Select the max-variance slice from the top, middle, and bottom thirds of the scan. Resize to 224x224 and normalize.
    *   **Tabular:** Standard scale continuous features; One-Hot encode categorical features.

2.  **Feature Engineering:**
    *   Extract EfficientNet features for the 3 slices, concatenate, and apply **PCA** (fitted on training data).
    *   Fuse PCA features with Polynomial Coefficients and Clinical Data.
    *   **FVC Set:** Generate interactions: $X_{Fused} \times \text{Weeks}$.
    *   **Uncertainty Set:** Append horizon feature: $|\text{Weeks} - \text{Baseline\_Weeks}|$.

### Training
The training utilizes **Decoupled Residual Regression (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001)**:
1.  **Stage 1 (FVC Optimization):**
    *   Train the **Linear Quantile Regressor (q=0.5)** on the FVC feature set.
    *   **Objective:** Minimize Mean Absolute Error (L1).
2.  **Stage 2 (Residual Computation):**
    *   Generate median predictions for the training set.
    *   Compute absolute residuals: $r = |y_{true} - y_{pred}|$.
3.  **Stage 3 (Uncertainty Optimization):**
    *   Train the **Elastic Net** on the Uncertainty feature set with target $r$.
    *   **Objective:** Minimize Mean Squared Error (L2) + Regularization.

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:**
    *   Process the test patient's CT to extract Stratified Texture and Morphological Coefficients.
    *   Predict Median FVC using the Quantile Regressor.
    *   Predict the expected Mean Absolute Deviation (MAD) using the Elastic Net.
2.  **Analytical Scaling:**
    *   Convert the predicted MAD into the final confidence ($\sigma$) using the formula: $\sigma = \text{MAD} \times \sqrt{2}$. This analytically maps the predicted error to the optimal scale parameter for the Laplace metric **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00009)**.
3.  **Formatting:**
    *   Clip $\sigma$ at 70 ml.
