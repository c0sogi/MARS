Based on the comprehensive analysis of the previous experiments and the distilled lessons, I propose the **Morphologically-Augmented Gaussian Dynamic Ensemble (MAGDE)**.

This strategy structurally evolves the "Gaussianized Generative" baseline (Lesson 70, 73) and the "Dynamic Ensemble Selection" framework (Lesson 56). It resolves the conflict between Lesson 50 (Geometric features help) and Lesson 52 (Geometric features are redundant) by treating explicit morphological descriptors as a distinct, Gaussianized "view" of the data. This allows a Greedy Selector to mathematically determine their contribution, rather than forcing a manual feature concatenation that might dilute the signal.

# Model
The solution utilizes a **Dynamic Ensemble Selection** architecture. We generate a pool of experts, each operating on a specific "view" of the data with a specific inductive bias, and use Greedy Forward Selection to construct the optimal weighted ensemble.

### 1. Expert Pool Generation
All experts operate in **double precision (`float64`)** (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00073`) and utilize **PowerTransformer** (Yeo-Johnson) preprocessing (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00070`) to strictly satisfy the normality assumptions of the generative models.

*   **Expert A: The Global Anchor (Baseline)**
    *   **Input:** The original 192 extracted features (Margin, Shape, Texture).
    *   **Algorithm:** **Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA)** with Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage.
    *   **Role:** Preserves the current state-of-the-art performance (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00063`).

*   **Expert B: The Morphological Expert (Innovation)**
    *   **Input:** A newly engineered set of **Explicit Geometric Descriptors** extracted from the raw binary images (see Data section).
    *   **Algorithm:** **LDA** with Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage.
    *   **Rationale:** Handcrafted histograms (original features) capture shape *implicitly* via frequency distributions. Explicit descriptors (Hu Moments, Solidity) capture shape *invariantly* and globally. By training a dedicated expert on this view, we isolate this high-signal physical information (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00050`) without it being drowned out by the 192 histogram features in a concatenated vector (addressing Lesson 52).

*   **Expert C: The Combined Expert**
    *   **Input:** Concatenation of Original (192) + Morphological features.
    *   **Algorithm:** **LDA** with Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage.
    *   **Role:** Captures potential cross-covariance between the histogram textures and the global geometry.

*   **Expert D: The Calibrated Discriminative Backup**
    *   **Input:** Original 192 features.
    *   **Algorithm:** **Logistic Regression** (L2, `lbfgs`) wrapped in **CalibratedClassifierCV** (Isotonic).
    *   **Rationale:** Provides a discriminative safety net. The Isotonic calibration ensures that if the Gaussian assumption of LDA fails for specific classes, this model can correct the probabilities (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00009` context).

### 2. Selection Mechanism
*   **Greedy Forward Selection:** We employ the selection strategy from Lesson 56. We iteratively add experts to the ensemble *if and only if* they maximize the improvement in Validation Log Loss. This ensures that if the Global Anchor is truly optimal, the ensemble converges to it; if the Morphological view adds orthogonal signal, it is incorporated without dilution.

# Data
1.  **Feature Engineering (Structural Innovation):**
    *   **Source:** Raw binary images.
    *   **Extraction:** For each image, extract **Hu Moments** (7 invariant moments describing global shape) and **Basic Geometric Scalars** (Aspect Ratio, Solidity, Extent, Eccentricity).
    *   **Rationale:** These features provide a "Physical View" of the leaf that is orthogonal to the "Statistical View" of the provided histograms.
2.  **Preprocessing:**
    *   **Precision:** Cast all features (Original and New) to `float64` to prevent numerical noise in density estimation (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00073`).
    *   **Gaussianization:** Apply `PowerTransformer(method='yeo-johnson')` to all feature sets. This is critical to making the geometric scalars (which may be skewed) compatible with LDA (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00070`).
    *   **Leakage Prevention:** Transformers are fit on the training split and applied to the test split.

# Training
The training procedure rigorously separates the Selection phase from the Final Retraining phase to maximize data utilization (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00010`).

1.  **Phase 1: Selection (Train/Val Split)**
    *   Split the training data (80/20).
    *   **Train Expert Pool:** Train Experts A, B, C, and D on the 80% split.
    *   **Generate Predictions:** Compute `float64` probability matrices on the 20% hold-out set.
    *   **Run Selection:** Apply Greedy Forward Selection (with replacement) to determine the optimal subset of experts and their integer weights (e.g., $3 \times \text{Expert A} + 1 \times \text{Expert B}$).

2.  **Phase 2: Final Retraining (Full Data)**
    *   Retrain *only* the selected experts on the **Combined Training + Validation Set**.
    *   For Logistic Regression, use the exact hyperparameters (`C`) found in Phase 1 to prevent configuration drift (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00028`).

# Evaluation
1.  **Inference:**
    *   Extract morphological features from test images.
    *   Process the test set through the retrained pipelines of the selected experts.
2.  **Aggregation:**
    *   Compute the final submission probabilities using the **Weighted Average** determined in Phase 1.
    *   This strategy combines the "Super-Expert" performance of Gaussianized LDA with the domain-specific robustness of explicit shape descriptors, strictly controlled by validation performance to prevent the redundancy issues observed in previous lessons.
