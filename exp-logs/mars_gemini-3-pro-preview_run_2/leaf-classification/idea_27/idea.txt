Based on the comprehensive analysis of the previous experiments and the distilled lessons, I propose the **Orthogonal-Basis Hybrid-Complexity Ensemble (OBHCE)**.

This strategy structurally evolves the "Generative Library" (Idea 23) and "Morphological Augmentation" (Idea 22) concepts. It addresses the limitation that while Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) is excellent for the high-dimensional provided features (Lesson 63), it forces a "shared covariance" assumption that ignores class-specific shape variations. By introducing **Zernike Moments**—a complete orthogonal basis for shape representation—we create a low-dimensional, high-fidelity view that enables the use of **Quadratic Discriminant Analysis (QDA)**. This allows the ensemble to combine a **Linear Global Baseline** with a **Non-Linear Shape Expert**, strictly managed by Greedy Selection.

# Model
The solution utilizes a **Dynamic Ensemble Selection** architecture composed of two distinct tiers of probabilistic experts, differing in both **Inductive Bias** (Linear vs. Quadratic) and **Input Representation** (Statistical vs. Spectral).

1.  **Tier 1: High-Dimensional Linear Anchors (The Baseline)**
    *   **Input:** The 192 provided features (Margin, Shape, Texture).
    *   **Algorithm:** **Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA)** with a library of shrinkage estimators: **Ledoit-Wolf** (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00063`), **OAS**, and **Fixed Shrinkage** (`[0.01, 0.1]`, Cite `solution_lesson_node_00081`).
    *   **Role:** Provides robust, low-variance density estimation on the high-dimensional global view ($P=192$), where $N \approx P$ necessitates linear boundaries and shared covariance.

2.  **Tier 2: Low-Dimensional Quadratic Experts (The Innovation)**
    *   **Input:** Newly engineered **Zernike Moment Magnitudes** (Order 10 $\approx$ 36 features).
    *   **Algorithm:** **Regularized Quadratic Discriminant Analysis (QDA)** with a grid of regularization parameters (`reg_param` $\in [0.0, 0.1, 0.5]$).
    *   **Rationale:** Unlike simple scalars (Hu Moments), Zernike polynomials form a complete orthogonal basis, allowing for higher-fidelity shape reconstruction. Crucially, the low dimensionality of this view ($P \approx 36 \ll N \approx 700$) allows us to deploy **QDA**, which models **class-specific covariance matrices**. This captures heteroscedasticity (e.g., some species vary wildly in shape, others are rigid) and non-linear boundaries, which the Linear Anchors miss. This avoids the pitfalls of Kernels (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00061`) by using a parametric non-linear model supported by the sample size.

3.  **Selection Mechanism:**
    *   **Greedy Forward Selection:** We employ the selection strategy from Lesson 56. We iteratively add experts to the ensemble *if and only if* they maximize the improvement in Validation Log Loss. This ensures that the QDA experts are included only if their non-linear signal outweighs their estimation variance (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00056`).

# Data
1.  **Feature Engineering (Structural Innovation: Spectral Descriptors):**
    *   **Source:** Raw binary images.
    *   **Preprocessing:** Center the image centroid to the origin and scale the maximum radius to 1.0 to ensure Translation and Scale invariance.
    *   **Extraction:** Compute **Zernike Moments** up to Order 10. Calculate the **Magnitude** of the complex moments to ensure Rotation invariance. This yields a vector of ~36 orthogonal shape descriptors (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00050`).
2.  **Preprocessing:**
    *   **Precision:** Cast all data to `float64` to prevent numerical noise in density calculations (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00073`).
    *   **Gaussianization:** Apply `PowerTransformer(method='yeo-johnson')` to all views (Global and Zernike). This is critical for maximizing the performance of both LDA and QDA (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00070`).
    *   **Leakage Prevention:** Transformers are fit on the training split and applied to the test split.

# Training
The training procedure rigorously separates the Selection phase from the Final Retraining phase to maximize data utilization (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00010`).

1.  **Phase 1: Selection (Train/Val Split)**
    *   Split training data (80/20).
    *   **Train Library:** Train Tier 1 experts on Global features and Tier 2 experts on Zernike features using the 80% split.
    *   **Generate Predictions:** Compute `float64` probability matrices on the 20% hold-out set.
    *   **Run Selection:** Apply Greedy Forward Selection (with replacement) to determine the optimal subset of experts and their integer weights.

2.  **Phase 2: Final Retraining (Full Data)**
    *   Retrain *only* the selected experts on the **Combined Training + Validation Set**.
    *   Use the exact hyperparameters identified in Phase 1 to prevent configuration drift (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00028`).

# Evaluation
1.  **Inference:**
    *   Extract Zernike features from Test images.
    *   Process the test set through the retrained pipelines of the selected experts (applying `PowerTransformer` and `float64` casting).
2.  **Aggregation:**
    *   Compute the final submission probabilities using the **Weighted Average** determined in Phase 1.
