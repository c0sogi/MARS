Based on the comprehensive analysis of previous experiments and the distilled lessons, I propose the **Selective Multi-View Hybrid Ensemble**.

This strategy structurally evolves the previous "Soft Voting" approaches (Idea 2, 6, 8) by moving from a monolithic feature representation to a **Multi-View Learning** architecture. This addresses the "Small N, High D" challenge by creating subspaces with highly favorable sample-to-feature ratios while preserving the holistic modeling of the global baseline.

# Model
The solution utilizes a **Soft-Voting Ensemble** composed of estimators trained on different "views" (subsets) of the data. This diversity allows the ensemble to capture both global cross-feature correlations and localized, high-signal patterns without the noise of the full dimensionality.

1.  **View Definitions:**
    *   **Global View:** All 192 features. Captures interactions between shape, margin, and texture.
    *   **Margin View:** The 64 margin features.
    *   **Shape View:** The 64 shape features.
    *   **Texture View:** The 64 texture features.

2.  **Base Estimators (Per View):**
    For *each* of the four views, we will train two complementary linear models, resulting in a pool of up to 8 potential estimators:
    *   **Discriminative Component:** **Logistic Regression** with L2 regularization. This optimizes the decision boundary directly (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00001`).
    *   **Generative Component:** **Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA)** with Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage. This provides superior density estimation and sample efficiency, particularly in the lower-dimensional (D=64) views where the $N/D$ ratio improves to ~11 (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00006`).

3.  **Selective Ensembling Mechanism:**
    To strictly prevent **Ensemble Dilution** (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00009`), we will not blindly average all 8 models. Instead, we will employ a **performance-based gating mechanism**. During cross-validation, any View-Estimator that fails to achieve a Log Loss within a specific tolerance of the Global Baseline (e.g., within 0.05) will be excluded. This ensures that weak views (e.g., if Texture proves non-discriminative) do not contaminate the high-confidence predictions of the Global models.

# Data
1.  **Feature Engineering:**
    *   Load the 192 extracted features.
    *   Create the sub-views by slicing the column vectors corresponding to `margin`, `shape`, and `texture`.
2.  **Preprocessing:**
    *   **Global Scaling:** Apply **StandardScaler** to all views. We explicitly reject non-linear transformations like `QuantileTransformer` (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00003`).
    *   **Leakage Prevention:** The scaler will be fit on the training data and applied to the test data.

# Training
The models will be trained on the **combined Training and Validation sets** to maximize sample utilization (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00010`), using the following rigorous configuration:

1.  **Logistic Regression (All Views):**
    *   **Estimator:** `LogisticRegressionCV` with **3-fold Cross-Validation** (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00010`).
    *   **Metric Alignment:** Explicitly set `scoring='neg_log_loss'` to ensure hyperparameters are selected to minimize the evaluation metric, not accuracy (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00031`).
    *   **Constrained Search:** Use a manually constrained grid for regularization `Cs` (e.g., `np.logspace(-2, 4, 20)`) to focus on the high-signal regime and prevent search space dilution (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00021`).
    *   **Solver:** `lbfgs` with L2 regularization (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00005`).

2.  **LDA (All Views):**
    *   Train with **Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage** to handle potential multicollinearity and high dimensionality.

# Evaluation
1.  **Inference:**
    *   Process the test set to generate the Global, Margin, Shape, and Texture feature matrices.
    *   Generate probability predictions from all selected (non-filtered) estimators.
2.  **Aggregation:**
    *   Compute the final predictions using **Soft Voting** (averaging the probability vectors).
    *   This combines the holistic power of the Global models with the specialized, high-stability signals of the Local models, while the selection mechanism protects against noise.
