Based on the comprehensive analysis of the previous experiments and the distilled lessons, I propose the **Multi-Resolution Gaussianized Dynamic Ensemble (MRGDE)**.

This strategy structurally evolves the "Generative Library" (Idea 23) and "Precision-Gaussianized" (Idea 30) concepts by addressing the specific failure mode of Non-Parametric Gaussianization (Lesson 76). Instead of viewing "Gaussianization" as a binary choice (Parametric vs. Non-Parametric) or a risky overfitting hazard (High-Quantile Transform), we formalize it as a **Distributional Resolution Spectrum**. By generating experts that view the data through **Parametric** (Yeo-Johnson), **Robust Non-Parametric** (Low-Quantile), and **Flexible Non-Parametric** (Medium-Quantile) lenses, we allow the Greedy Selector to mathematically determine the optimal normalization strategy for the 99-class manifold, strictly constrained to prevent the overfitting observed in Lesson 76.

# Model
The solution utilizes a **Dynamic Ensemble Selection** architecture. We generate a library of probabilistic experts that vary in **Gaussianization Resolution**, **Covariance Estimation**, and **Feature View**.

### 1. The Multi-Resolution Expert Library
All experts operate in **double precision (`float64`)** to strictly minimize numerical noise at the metric floor (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00073`).

**Dimension A: The Gaussian Resolution Spectrum (Preprocessing)**
We project the data into three distinct latent spaces representing different trade-offs between distributional flexibility and regularization:
1.  **Parametric Basis:** `PowerTransformer(method='yeo-johnson')`.
    *   **Role:** Stabilizes variance using a smooth, global power law. This is the proven high-performance baseline (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00070`).
2.  **Robust Non-Parametric Basis (Coarse Resolution):** `QuantileTransformer(output_distribution='normal', n_quantiles=20)`.
    *   **Role:** A highly constrained rank-based transform (using only ~3% of $N$). It handles outliers and skew that Yeo-Johnson misses but uses very few breakpoints to strictly prevent the overfitting/memorization observed in Lesson 76.
3.  **Flexible Non-Parametric Basis (Fine Resolution):** `QuantileTransformer(output_distribution='normal', n_quantiles=100)`.
    *   **Role:** A balanced transform (using ~14% of $N$) that captures more local density variations while still maintaining a regularization buffer compared to the sample size.

**Dimension B: The Estimators**
For each Basis and View, we train:
*   **Generative Experts (LDA):**
    *   **Algorithm:** **Linear Discriminant Analysis** with **OAS** (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00090`) and **Fixed Shrinkage** (`[0.01]`, Cite `solution_lesson_node_00081`).
    *   **Role:** Models joint probability $P(X, Y)$ using robust covariance estimation.
*   **Discriminative Anchor (LogReg):**
    *   **Algorithm:** **Logistic Regression** (L2) on the **Parametric Basis** only.
    *   **Configuration:** `LogisticRegressionCV` optimizing `neg_log_loss` (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00031`).
    *   **Role:** Provides a discriminative fallback if the generative assumptions fail.

**Dimension C: The Feature Views**
1.  **Global View:** The 192 provided features.
2.  **Combined View:** Global + Explicit Morphometrics (Hu Moments + Scalars). (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00050`).

### 2. Selection Mechanism
*   **Greedy Forward Selection:** We employ the selection strategy from Lesson 56. We iteratively add experts to the ensemble *if and only if* they maximize the improvement in Validation Log Loss. This ensures that if the Parametric OAS-LDA is sufficient (Lesson 90), the ensemble converges to it; if the Robust Non-Parametric basis adds orthogonal signal, it is incorporated (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00056`).

# Data
1.  **Feature Engineering:**
    *   **Global View:** Load the provided 192 feature vectors.
    *   **Morphometric View:** Load raw binary images. Extract **Deterministic** Morphometrics: **Hu Moments** (7 invariant descriptors) and **Geometric Scalars** (Aspect Ratio, Solidity, Extent, Eccentricity). We explicitly reject Monte-Carlo augmentation as it is redundant for stable invariants (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00089`).
    *   **Combined View:** Concatenate Global and Morphometric matrices.
2.  **Preprocessing (The Rigor):**
    *   **Precision:** Cast **all** feature matrices to `float64` (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00073`).
    *   **Basis Generation:** Apply the three transforms (Parametric, Coarse-Quantile, Fine-Quantile) to generate transformed versions of the Global and Combined views.
    *   **Leakage Prevention:** Transformers are fit on the training split and applied to the test split.

# Training
The training procedure rigorously separates the Selection phase from the Final Retraining phase to maximize data utilization (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00010`).

1.  **Phase 1: Selection (Train/Val Split)**
    *   Split training data (80/20).
    *   **Train Library:** Train the full Cartesian product of (Bases $\times$ Estimators $\times$ Views) on the 80% split.
    *   **Generate Predictions:** Compute `float64` probability matrices on the 20% hold-out set.
    *   **Run Selection:** Apply Greedy Forward Selection (with replacement) to determine the optimal subset of experts and their integer weights.

2.  **Phase 2: Final Retraining (Full Data)**
    *   Retrain *only* the selected experts on the **Combined Training + Validation Set**.
    *   Use the exact hyperparameters (e.g., shrinkage, n_quantiles) identified in Phase 1 to prevent configuration drift (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00028`).

# Evaluation
1.  **Inference:**
    *   Extract Morphometric features for the Test set.
    *   Process the test set through the retrained pipelines of the selected experts (applying the specific Basis transform and `float64` casting).
2.  **Aggregation:**
    *   Compute the final submission probabilities using the **Weighted Average** determined in Phase 1.
