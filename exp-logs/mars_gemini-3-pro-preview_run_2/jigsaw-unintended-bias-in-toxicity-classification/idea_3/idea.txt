Based on the lessons learned from the LSTM experiments and the previous Transformer proposal, I propose the following optimized strategy. This solution evolves the architecture by replacing the LSTM backbone with a Transformer (as explored in Idea 2) but structurally modifies the Transformer's aggregation and regularization mechanisms based on the specific empirical wins from the LSTM lessons.

### Model
We will implement a **Multi-Task RoBERTa model with Attention Pooling and Spatial Dropout**.

*   **Backbone:** We will utilize **RoBERTa-base** as the feature extractor. This provides the necessary "Capacity Scaling" required to disentangle complex identity mentions from toxicity, which Lesson 4 identified as critical when using aggressive auxiliary weighting (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).
*   **Aggregation Layer (Structural Innovation):** Standard Transformers typically rely on the `[CLS]` token for classification. However, Lesson 2 demonstrated that **Attention Pooling** (weighted sum of hidden states) significantly outperforms static pooling by allowing the model to dynamically emphasize relevant tokens (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002). Therefore, we will discard the `[CLS]` token output and instead apply a custom Attention Pooling layer to the full sequence of hidden states generated by RoBERTa.
*   **Regularization:** Before the pooling layer, we will apply **Spatial Dropout** (dropping entire feature channels across the sequence) to the Transformer's hidden states. This retains the robust regularization strategy that improved generalization in the LSTM experiments (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
*   **Multi-Task Heads:** The output of the Attention Pooling layer will feed into two separate fully connected heads:
    1.  **Toxicity Head:** Predicts the main `target` variable.
    2.  **Identity Head:** Predicts the 9 identity attributes to force the backbone to learn these as distinct concepts (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).

### Data
We will employ the **"Pad-then-Trim" (Device-Side Trimming)** strategy to maximize training throughput, enabling the use of the computationally heavier Transformer backbone.

*   **Preprocessing:** Text will be tokenized using the RoBERTa tokenizer and pre-padded to a fixed length (e.g., 512) into contiguous `int32` arrays.
*   **Device-Side Trimming:** We will strictly avoid CPU-side collation. We will load the fixed-length batches to the GPU and dynamically slice the tensors to the maximum non-padding length of that specific batch just before the forward pass. This method was proven to match the efficiency of custom collation without the data-loading overhead (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).

### Training
The training process focuses on aggressive bias mitigation and efficiency.

*   **Loss Function:** We will use the **Aggressive Multi-Task Loss** strategy. The total loss will be a weighted sum of the Toxicity BCE and Identity BCE, with a high weight (e.g., $\lambda=0.8$) assigned to the Identity task. This forces the high-capacity Transformer to explicitly model identities, preventing them from becoming latent proxies for toxicity (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).
*   **Optimization:** We will use the **OneCycleLR** scheduler with the AdamW optimizer. This scheduler was shown to accelerate convergence significantly, allowing us to train this larger model effectively within the time limits (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
*   **Strategy:** We will perform full fine-tuning of the model (rather than just LoRA) because the efficiency gains from Device-Side Trimming (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007) provide sufficient computational headroom to leverage the full capacity of the backbone.

### Evaluation
*   **Inference:** The test set will be processed using the same Device-Side Trimming pipeline.
*   **Prediction:** We will extract predictions solely from the **Toxicity Head** for the submission, discarding the Identity Head outputs.
