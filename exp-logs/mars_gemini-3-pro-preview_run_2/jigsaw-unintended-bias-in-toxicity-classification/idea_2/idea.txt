To solve the task of toxicity recognition with bias mitigation, I propose a structural evolution from the previous LSTM-based approach to a **Transformer-based architecture optimized with Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)**. This strategy fundamentally shifts the mechanism of context understanding from learning-from-scratch (LSTM) to leveraging deep pre-trained linguistic knowledge, while strictly retaining the efficient training and bias-mitigation strategies validated in the previous lessons.

### Model
We will replace the Bidirectional LSTM backbone with a **pre-trained Transformer encoder** (specifically `RoBERTa-base` or `RoBERTa-large`), which offers superior capability in handling the nuance and context required to disentangle identity mentions from toxicity.

*   **Backbone:** A pre-trained RoBERTa model will serve as the feature extractor. To manage the computational budget and prevent overfitting, we will inject **Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)** matrices into the query and value projection layers of the attention blocks. This allows us to scale up the effective model capacity significantly (Cite Lesson 4) without the memory cost of full fine-tuning.
*   **Multi-Task Heads:** We will retain the successful decoupled head architecture (Cite Lesson 1). The `[CLS]` token embedding from the Transformer will feed into two distinct classification heads:
    1.  **Toxicity Head:** A dense layer predicting the primary toxicity target.
    2.  **Identity Head:** A separate dense layer predicting the 9 identity attributes.
*   **Regularization:** We will apply Dropout to the pooled `[CLS]` output before the classification heads to maintain the regularization benefits observed previously (Cite Lesson 2).

### Data
The data pipeline will be engineered for maximum throughput using the "Pad-then-Trim" strategy validated in the previous experiments.

*   **Preprocessing:** Instead of a simple whitespace tokenizer, we will use the specific Subword Tokenizer associated with the pre-trained model (e.g., Byte-Pair Encoding). All text sequences will be tokenized and pre-padded to a fixed maximum length (e.g., 512 tokens) and stored as contiguous `int32` Numpy arrays (`input_ids` and `attention_mask`).
*   **Device-Side Trimming:** We will strictly avoid CPU-side collation. We will load the fixed-length pre-padded batches onto the GPU and dynamically slice the tensors to the maximum non-padding length defined by the attention mask of that specific batch (Cite Lesson 7). This ensures we do not waste compute on padding tokens while maintaining high I/O throughput.

### Training
The training procedure focuses on forcing the model to distinguish between identity concepts and toxicity signals.

*   **Loss Function:** We will employ the **Aggressive Multi-Task Loss** strategy (Cite Lesson 4). The total loss will be a weighted sum of the Toxicity BCE and the Identity BCE, with a high weight (e.g., $\lambda=0.8$) assigned to the Identity task. This forces the powerful Transformer backbone to retain distinct representations for identities, preventing them from being conflated with toxicity.
*   **Optimization:** We will use the AdamW optimizer to update the LoRA parameters and the classification heads. We will utilize the **OneCycleLR** scheduler (Cite Lesson 6) to achieve rapid convergence, allowing us to effectively fine-tune the model within the runtime constraints.

### Evaluation
*   **Inference:** The test data will be processed through the same tokenizer and Device-Side Trimming pipeline.
*   **Prediction:** We will extract the sigmoid output solely from the **Toxicity Head** for the final submission. The Identity Head is used exclusively during training to enforce feature disentanglement and is discarded during inference.
