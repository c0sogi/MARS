Based on the comprehensive analysis of previous experiments and the derived lessons, I propose a **Dual-Stream Spatio-Temporal Residual Network (DST-ResNet)**. This architecture is designed to disentangle the two primary sources of GNSS error: dynamic signal noise (best captured by temporal sequences) and static environmental bias (best captured by absolute spatial coordinates).

### Model: Dual-Stream Spatio-Temporal Residual Network (DST-ResNet)

The model architecture consists of two parallel processing streams that fuse their representations to predict the residual correction.

1.  **Temporal Stream (1D-CNN):**
    *   **Input:** A time-series window of size $N$ (e.g., 11 epochs, representing $\pm 5$ seconds) containing **dynamic features only**: signal quality metrics (Cn0, Uncertainty), satellite counts, and trajectory dynamics (Velocity/Deltas).
    *   **Architecture:** A stack of **1D Convolutional layers** with BatchNorm and ReLU activation.
    *   **Purpose:** Unlike the flattened MLP (Idea 1), the CNN preserves the local temporal structure of the sensor data (e.g., detecting a sudden drop in signal strength followed by an erratic jump). Unlike the Bi-LSTM (Idea 2), the CNN has a fixed receptive field, preventing the model from overfitting to long-term trajectory signatures unique to the training set **Cite solution_lesson_node_00010**.

2.  **Spatial Stream (MLP):**
    *   **Input:** The **absolute baseline coordinates** (WLS Latitude, Longitude) for the *center* timestamp of the window only.
    *   **Architecture:** A simple Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP).
    *   **Purpose:** This stream explicitly learns the state-dependent error biases (e.g., multipath effects specific to a street intersection). This addresses the critical failure of Idea 2, which removed absolute coordinates and lost spatial context **Cite solution_lesson_node_00011**.

3.  **Fusion & Output Head:**
    *   The outputs of the Temporal Stream (flattened or pooled) and the Spatial Stream are concatenated.
    *   A final series of Dense layers projects this fused representation to a 2D output vector: the residual correction in local metric units ($\Delta$East, $\Delta$North).

### Data

**Preprocessing Steps:**
1.  **Target Generation:** Calculate the ground truth residuals in a local metric frame (ENU - East, North, Up). The target is the difference in meters between the Ground Truth and the baseline WLS position. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00001**.
2.  **Feature Engineering:**
    *   **Dynamics:** Compute the first-order differences (deltas) for Latitude, Longitude, and **Altitude** between consecutive timestamps. The inclusion of vertical dynamics serves as a robust indicator of signal anomalies. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**, **Cite solution_lesson_node_00006**.
    *   **Signal Metrics:** Aggregate standard GNSS fields (Cn0, Uncertainty, Svid count).
3.  **Windowing & Splitting:**
    *   Create sliding windows of length $N$ (e.g., 11) centered on the target timestamp.
    *   Split the features into two sets: **Dynamic Sequence** (for the CNN stream) and **Static Center State** (Absolute Lat/Lon for the MLP stream).
4.  **Normalization:** Apply Standard Scaling to all inputs.

### Training

**Procedure:**
*   **Loss Function:** **Mean Absolute Error (MAE)**. This L1 loss is chosen for its robustness to the extreme outliers inherent in urban GNSS data, aligning with the competition's percentile-based evaluation.
*   **Optimizer:** **AdamW** with a learning rate scheduler (e.g., ReduceLROnPlateau) to ensure stable convergence.
*   **Regularization:** Apply Dropout within the Fusion MLP layers to prevent co-adaptation between the spatial and temporal features.

### Evaluation

**Inference Process:**
1.  **Preprocessing:** Apply the same windowing and feature splitting to the test data.
2.  **Prediction:** Feed the test windows into the DST-ResNet to predict the metric residuals ($\Delta$Meters).
3.  **Reconstruction:** Convert the predicted metric residuals back to degrees (using local approximation factors derived from the WLS latitude) and add them to the baseline WLS position to obtain the final Latitude/Longitude predictions.
