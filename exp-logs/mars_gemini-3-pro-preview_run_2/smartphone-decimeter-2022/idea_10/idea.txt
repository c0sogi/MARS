Based on the accumulated lessons, specifically the conflict between the need for state-dependent bias correction (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00011**) and the risk of spatial overfitting when using global coordinates (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00017**), I propose the **Sky-Conditioned Relative-Motion 1D-CNN**.

This solution introduces a structural innovation by replacing absolute geospatial coordinates with a **Satellite Geometry Context** stream. This allows the model to infer environmental error conditions (e.g., urban canyon effects indicated by low satellite elevation or poor geometry) without memorizing specific map locations, while a parallel convolutional stream handles local trajectory smoothing.

### Model
The architecture is a dual-stream neural network designed to disentangle kinematic smoothing from environmental bias correction.

1.  **Trajectory Stream (1D-CNN Backbone):**
    *   **Input:** A time-series window of size $N$ (e.g., 15 epochs).
    *   **Architecture:** A stack of **Residual 1D Convolutional Blocks** (Conv1D $\to$ BatchNorm $\to$ ReLU). This backbone acts as a learnable filter, smoothing the trajectory based on the coherence of the movement and signal quality sequence (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00013**).
    *   **Output:** A feature vector obtained via Global Average Pooling.

2.  **Sky Context Stream (MLP):**
    *   **Input:** A vector of aggregated **Satellite Geometry Statistics** derived from the window (e.g., Mean Satellite Elevation, Azimuth Spread/Diversity, and Mean Signal Strength).
    *   **Purpose:** This stream provides the necessary "state" context to predict systematic biases (e.g., multipath is more likely when mean elevation is low) without using absolute Latitude/Longitude, thereby strictly preventing spatial overfitting (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00017**).

3.  **Fusion Head:**
    *   The outputs of the Trajectory and Sky streams are concatenated.
    *   A final Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) projects this fused representation to a 2D output vector: the residual correction ($\Delta$East, $\Delta$North) in meters.

### Data
**Preprocessing Pipeline:**
1.  **Target Transformation:** Calculate ground truth residuals in **meters** relative to the WLS baseline. This ensures the target magnitude is compatible with network initialization (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00001**).
2.  **Sequence Feature Engineering (Trajectory Stream):**
    *   **Relative Coordinates:** For each window, subtract the WLS Latitude/Longitude/Altitude of the *center* timestamp from all steps. Convert these differences to meters using **Simple Element-wise Scaling** (e.g., $\Delta Lat^\circ \times 111,320$). This preserves the local geometric shape for smoothing (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00013**) while ensuring translation invariance and avoiding the instability of complex projections (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00020**).
    *   **Dynamics:** Explicitly include first-order differences (velocity) for Latitude, Longitude, and **Altitude**. Vertical dynamics are retained as a critical indicator of signal anomalies (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**, **Cite solution_lesson_node_00006**).
    *   **Signal Metrics:** Include standardized Cn0 (Signal Strength) and Uncertainty estimates.
3.  **Context Feature Engineering (Sky Stream):**
    *   Aggregate `SvElevationDegrees` and `SvAzimuthDegrees` from the raw logs. Calculate the mean and standard deviation for the window to characterize the "view of the sky."
4.  **Windowing:** Use a short sliding window (e.g., 15 seconds) to prevent overfitting to long-term trajectory signatures unique to the training set (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00010**).
5.  **Normalization:** Apply Standard Scaling (Z-score) to all inputs.

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** **Mean Absolute Error (MAE)**. This L1 loss is selected for its robustness to the extreme outliers (multipath effects) inherent in urban GNSS data.
*   **Optimizer:** **AdamW** optimizer with a `ReduceLROnPlateau` learning rate scheduler to ensure stable convergence.
*   **Regularization:** Apply Batch Normalization within the CNN blocks and Dropout in the Fusion MLP to prevent co-adaptation.

### Evaluation
**Inference Process:**
1.  **Preprocessing:** Apply the same windowing, relative-centering, simple scaling, and sky-context aggregation steps to the test data.
2.  **Prediction:** Feed the processed windows into the model to predict the metric residuals ($\Delta$East, $\Delta$North).
3.  **Reconstruction:** Convert the predicted metric residuals back to decimal degrees using the local approximation factors derived from the test sample's latitude. Add these corrections to the baseline WLS Latitude/Longitude to generate the final submission.
