Based on the empirical success of the custom ResNet and the ensemble strategies in previous experiments, I propose a **Heterogeneous Ensemble of Specialized Architectures**. This strategy evolves the solution from a homogeneous ensemble (identical ResNets) to a heterogeneous one, introducing structural diversity to capture different feature representations while adhering to the resolution constraints identified in the lessons.

### Model
I propose an ensemble consisting of two distinct convolutional architectures, both designed specifically for 32x32 inputs to avoid the downsampling pitfalls of standard backbones (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).

1.  **Custom Lightweight ResNet (Retained):** We will retain the high-performing ResNet architecture from the previous best solution. This model features residual connections to facilitate gradient flow and maintains a final feature map size of 8x8 to preserve spatial hierarchy (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005). It concludes with a **Global Average Pooling (GAP)** layer to enforce translation invariance and minimize parameter count (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).
2.  **Custom Lightweight DenseNet (New):** To introduce structural diversity, we will implement a custom Densely Connected Convolutional Network (DenseNet). Unlike ResNets which sum features, DenseNets concatenate feature maps from all preceding layers. This architecture encourages feature reuse and is particularly effective for small images, as it maintains high-frequency information throughout the network.
    *   **Design:** The DenseNet will consist of 3 dense blocks. To respect the lesson on spatial preservation, transition layers between blocks will use strictly controlled pooling (e.g., 2x2 average pooling) to ensure the final feature map before classification is also 8x8, matching the ResNet's resolution strategy (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).
    *   **Head:** Like the ResNet, this model will use **Global Average Pooling** instead of flattening, followed by a single dense classification layer (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).

### Data
*   **Preprocessing:** Images will be loaded in their native 32x32 resolution and normalized to the [0, 1] range.
*   **Augmentation:** We will strictly adhere to the "light augmentation" protocol. We will apply only **Random Horizontal Flips** and **Random Vertical Flips**. We will explicitly avoid geometric rotations, color jittering, or cutout, as previous experiments demonstrated that these aggressive regularizations degrade performance on this high-signal dataset (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).

### Training
*   **Optimizer & Scheduler:** Both architectures will be trained using **AdamW** combined with a **Cosine Annealing** learning rate scheduler. This combination has been proven to eliminate residual ranking errors and improve convergence stability compared to standard Adam (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
*   **Loss Function:** Binary Cross-Entropy with Logits Loss.
*   **Ensembling Strategy:** We will employ **Seed Averaging** for *both* architectures. We will train multiple independent instances (seeds) of the ResNet and multiple independent instances of the DenseNet. This addresses stochastic noise inherent in training (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007) while the use of two different architectures provides inductive bias diversity.

### Evaluation
*   **Test Time Augmentation (TTA):** During inference, we will generate predictions for the original image, a horizontally flipped version, and a vertically flipped version for every model instance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).
*   **Aggregation:** The final submission probability for each image will be the arithmetic mean of the TTA-averaged predictions across all ResNet seeds and all DenseNet seeds. This combines the benefits of noise reduction (via TTA and seed averaging) with the robustness of heterogeneous modeling.
