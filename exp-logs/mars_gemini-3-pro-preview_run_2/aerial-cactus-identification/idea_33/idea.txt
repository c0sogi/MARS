Based on the comprehensive analysis of the provided lessons and the progression of previous experiments—specifically the dominance of the "Wide SE-ResNeXt" in metric saturation (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00064**), the inference efficiency of "Structural Re-parameterization" (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00103**), and the proven benefit of "Multi-Scale Aggregation" (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00016**)—I propose the **Custom Wide SE-RepNeXt with Multi-Scale Aggregation**.

This strategy represents a structural synthesis that creates a "Super-Model" combining the best attributes of the previous champions: the massive representational capacity of ResNeXt, the training dynamics of ResNet, and the inference speed of VGG. By applying re-parameterization to a grouped convolution architecture, we can afford the "Super-Wide" channel configuration necessary for accuracy without paying the latency penalty at test time.

# Model
I propose a **Custom Wide SE-RepNeXt Architecture**.

*   **Backbone (Wide RepNeXt):** We will implement a 3-stage backbone using **SE-RepNeXt Blocks**.
    *   **Training Topology (Multi-Branch):** Each block will consist of parallel branches to facilitate gradient flow: a **Grouped $3 \times 3$ Convolution**, a **Grouped $1 \times 1$ Convolution**, and an **Identity Mapping** (where dimensions match). This combines the optimization benefits of ResNet with the capacity of ResNeXt.
    *   **Inference Topology (Fused):** Before inference, we will mathematically fuse the parallel branches and Batch Normalization layers into a single **Grouped $3 \times 3$ Convolution**. This structural re-parameterization eliminates the memory access cost of residual addition, significantly accelerating inference **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00103)**.
    *   **Width & Cardinality:** We will strictly adhere to the **"Super-Wide" Channel Configuration** (`[64, 128, 256]`) with high cardinality (e.g., 32 groups). This ensures the model has the raw capacity to saturate the metric **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00064)**.
    *   **Downsampling:** We will use a re-parameterizable downsampling block consisting of a parallel Stride-2 $3 \times 3$ convolution and Stride-2 $1 \times 1$ convolution. This inherently applies the $3 \times 3$ strided projection strategy proven to accelerate convergence on low-resolution data **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00060)**.
    *   **Attention:** We will integrate **Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) Blocks** after the fused convolution in each block. This accelerates convergence by adaptively recalibrating the rich, multi-group features **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00012)**.

*   **Head (Multi-Scale Aggregation):** We will implement a concatenation-based aggregation head.
    *   We will extract feature maps from the end of **Stage 2** (16x16) and **Stage 3** (8x8).
    *   We will apply **Global Average Pooling (GAP)** to both. We explicitly reject Covariance Pooling and GeM, as standard GAP is sufficient for this high-signal task and avoids statistical instability **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00053)**.
    *   The concatenated vector will be passed to a single dense classification layer. This ensures the classifier leverages both the texture-rich mid-level features and the semantic high-level features **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00016)**.

# Data
*   **Preprocessing:** Images will be loaded in their native **32x32** resolution and normalized to the [0, 1] range. We will strictly avoid resizing or upscaling to prevent interpolation artifacts **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00001)**.
*   **Augmentation:** We will strictly adhere to the "light augmentation" strategy, applying only **Random Horizontal Flips** and **Random Vertical Flips**. We will explicitly avoid geometric rotations, color jittering, or cutout, as these techniques act as noise on this high-signal dataset **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00002)**.

# Training
*   **Ensembling Strategy:** We will employ **Homogeneous Seed Averaging**. We will train **5 independent instances** of this architecture using different random seeds. This strategy eliminates stochastic residual ranking errors **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00007)** while avoiding the instability risks of heterogeneous ensembles **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00008)**.
*   **Optimizer & Scheduler:** We will use **AdamW** combined with a **Cosine Annealing** learning rate scheduler. This combination ensures robust convergence and generalization **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00007)**.
*   **Training Duration:** We will utilize a reduced training schedule (e.g., 20 epochs). The combination of the "Super-Wide" capacity, SE blocks, and RepVGG-style gradient flow facilitates rapid convergence.
*   **Loss Function:** Binary Cross-Entropy with Logits Loss.

# Evaluation
*   **Structural Re-parameterization:** Prior to inference, the model will be converted from its training form (multi-branch) to its inference form (single-branch grouped convolutions) to maximize throughput **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00103)**.
*   **Test Time Augmentation (TTA):** During inference, we will generate predictions for the original image, a horizontally flipped version, and a vertically flipped version for each of the 5 model instances **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00005)**.
*   **Aggregation:** The final submission probability for each image will be the arithmetic mean of all TTA predictions across all 5 seeds. This maximizes robustness and minimizes stochastic noise.
