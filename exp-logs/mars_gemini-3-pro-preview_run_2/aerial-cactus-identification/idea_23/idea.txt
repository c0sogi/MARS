Based on the comprehensive analysis of the provided lessons and the progression of previous ideas, I propose the **Custom Wide Dual-Pooling SE-ResNeXt with Multi-Scale Aggregation**.

This strategy represents a structural synthesis of the "Wide Capacity" paradigm (Lesson 64), the "Multi-Scale Efficiency" paradigm (Lesson 16), and a refined "Texture-Sensitive Attention" mechanism. It explicitly addresses the limitation of standard Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) blocks—which rely solely on Average Pooling and may smooth out high-frequency texture details like cactus spines—by integrating Max Pooling directly into the attention mechanism, while avoiding the computational instability of second-order statistics (Lesson 53).

### Model
I propose a **Custom Wide Dual-Pooling SE-ResNeXt Architecture**.

*   **Backbone (Wide ResNeXt):** We will utilize the **"Super-Wide" Channel Configuration** (`[64, 128, 256]`) with **Grouped Convolutions** (Cardinality = 32). This structure was proven to maximize representational capacity and saturation speed for this dataset **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00064)**.
    *   **Structure:** A 3-stage network designed for 32x32 inputs.
    *   **Kernels:** We will exclusively use **3x3 convolutions** to match the receptive field to the input resolution **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00011)**.
    *   **Shortcuts:** We will strictly utilize **1x1 convolutions** for projection shortcuts, avoiding the performance degradation observed with dense 3x3 shortcuts **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00063)**.
    *   **Spatial Preservation:** The network will downsample 32x32 $\to$ 16x16 $\to$ 8x8. We strictly preserve the 8x8 resolution to prevent the loss of fine-grained spatial details **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00005)**.

*   **Structural Innovation (Dual-Pooling SE Blocks):** We will replace standard SE blocks with **Dual-Pooling Squeeze-and-Excitation Blocks** within each residual unit.
    *   **Standard SE Limitation:** Standard SE blocks use Global Average Pooling (GAP) to squeeze spatial information. GAP computes the mean intensity, which effectively suppresses high-frequency anomalies like sharp spines or texture variations critical for cactus identification.
    *   **Dual-Pooling Mechanism:** In the "Squeeze" phase, we will compute *both* **Global Average Pooling** (for background context) and **Global Max Pooling** (for salient texture/spine detection). These two descriptors will be summed (or concatenated) before being passed to the shared MLP.
    *   **Benefit:** This allows the network to adaptively recalibrate channels based on the *presence* of distinctive features (Max) as well as the *global state* (Average), accelerating convergence beyond standard SE **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00012)** without the computational cost of Covariance Pooling **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00053)**.

*   **Head (Multi-Scale Aggregation):** We will implement the concatenation-based aggregation head.
    *   We will extract feature maps from the end of **Stage 2** (16x16) and **Stage 3** (8x8).
    *   We will apply **Global Average Pooling (GAP)** to both.
    *   The concatenated vector will be passed to a single dense classification layer. This ensures the classifier leverages both the texture-rich mid-level features and the semantic high-level features **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00016)**.

### Data
*   **Preprocessing:** Images will be loaded in their native **32x32** resolution and normalized to the [0, 1] range. We will strictly avoid resizing or upscaling to prevent interpolation artifacts **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00001)**.
*   **Augmentation:** We will strictly adhere to the "light augmentation" strategy, applying only **Random Horizontal Flips** and **Random Vertical Flips**. We will explicitly avoid geometric rotations, color jittering, or cutout, as these techniques act as noise on this high-signal dataset **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00002)**.

### Training
*   **Ensembling Strategy:** We will employ **Homogeneous Seed Averaging**. We will train **5 independent instances** of this architecture using different random seeds. This strategy is chosen to eliminate stochastic residual ranking errors **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00007)** while avoiding the instability risks of heterogeneous ensembles **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00008)**.
*   **Optimizer & Scheduler:** We will use **AdamW** combined with a **Cosine Annealing** learning rate scheduler. This combination ensures robust convergence and generalization **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00007)**.
*   **Training Duration:** We will utilize a reduced training schedule (e.g., 20 epochs). The combination of the "Wide" capacity **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00064)** and the enhanced "Dual-Pooling" attention facilitates rapid convergence.
*   **Loss Function:** Binary Cross-Entropy with Logits Loss.

### Evaluation
*   **Test Time Augmentation (TTA):** During inference, we will generate predictions for the original image, a horizontally flipped version, and a vertically flipped version for each of the 5 model instances **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00005)**.
*   **Aggregation:** The final submission probability for each image will be the arithmetic mean of all TTA predictions across all 5 seeds. This maximizes robustness and minimizes stochastic noise.
