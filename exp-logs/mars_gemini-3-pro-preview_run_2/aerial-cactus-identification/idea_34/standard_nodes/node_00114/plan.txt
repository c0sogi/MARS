To optimize the performance of the solution, I will implement a strategy focused on "right-sizing" the model capacity and improving computational efficiency while maintaining the perfect AUC of 1.0.

1.  **Reduce Model Width**: I will reduce the `STAGE_CHANNELS` from `[96, 192, 384]` to `[32, 64, 128]`. The previous "Ultra-Wide" configuration was significantly over-parameterized for 32x32 images. As established in **Lesson `solution_lesson_node_00019`** and **Lesson `solution_lesson_node_00049`**, a standard width starting at 32 filters is sufficient to saturate the metric on this dataset, offering a better balance of speed and accuracy than extremely wide or extremely narrow architectures.
2.  **Switch to RepVGG (Groups=1)**: I will change `GROUPS` from 32 to 1. This transitions the architecture from a RepNeXt design (grouped convolutions) to a RepVGG design (dense convolutions). According to **Lesson `solution_lesson_node_00103`**, structural re-parameterization with dense convolutions allows for highly efficient inference by fusing multi-branch blocks into single 3x3 kernels, decoupling training dynamics from inference speed.
3.  **Optimize Training Schedule**: I will reduce `NUM_EPOCHS` from 20 to 15 and increase `BATCH_SIZE` from 128 to 256. The use of Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) blocks accelerates convergence (**Lesson `solution_lesson_node_00012`**), allowing us to shorten the training schedule without compromising the final metric. The increased batch size leverages the A100 GPU more effectively.
