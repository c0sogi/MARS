Based on the comprehensive analysis of the provided lessons and the progression of previous ideas, I propose an optimized strategy: the **Custom Narrow SE-Multi-Scale ResNet**.

This strategy synthesizes the efficiency of narrow channel widths, the convergence speed of attention mechanisms, and the robustness of multi-scale feature aggregation into a single, highly optimized architecture. It explicitly rejects the use of heavy backbones, large kernels, and encoder-decoder structures, which were proven ineffective for this specific task.

### Model
I propose a **Custom Narrow SE-Multi-Scale ResNet** designed specifically for high-efficiency classification of 32x32 images.

*   **Backbone Structure:** The network will follow a custom ResNet topology with three stages. To strictly preserve spatial hierarchy, the downsampling schedule will be controlled to ensure the final feature map resolution is **8x8** (e.g., 32x32 $\to$ 16x16 $\to$ 8x8). This prevents the loss of fine-grained details caused by aggressive pooling **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00005)**.
*   **Kernel Selection:** We will exclusively use **3x3 convolutions** throughout the network. We will explicitly avoid larger kernels (e.g., 7x7), as empirical evidence showed they increase computational cost without improving performance on small input images **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00011)**.
*   **Channel Configuration:** We will adopt a **Narrow Channel Width** configuration, scaling from 16 to 64 channels (`[16, 32, 64]`). Previous experiments demonstrated that this reduced capacity is sufficient for the dataset and significantly lowers computational overhead compared to standard widths **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00019)**.
*   **Attention Mechanism:** We will integrate **Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) Blocks** within each residual unit. While standard convolutions handle spatial processing, the SE blocks will accelerate convergence by adaptively recalibrating channel importance, allowing the model to reach optimal performance in fewer epochs **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00012)**.
*   **Head (Multi-Scale Aggregation):** Instead of relying solely on the final stage, we will implement **Multi-Scale Feature Aggregation**. We will apply **Global Average Pooling (GAP)** to the output of **Stage 2 (16x16)** and **Stage 3 (8x8)** independently. These two feature vectors will be concatenated before passing to the final classification layer. This allows the classifier to leverage both mid-level texture details and high-level semantic abstractions, compensating for the narrower channel width **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00016)**. We explicitly reject the U-Net decoder approach, as upsampling is redundant for global classification **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00027)**.

### Data
*   **Preprocessing:** Images will be loaded in their native **32x32** resolution and normalized to the [0, 1] range. We will not perform resizing or upscaling **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00001)**.
*   **Augmentation:** We will strictly adhere to the "light augmentation" strategy, applying only **Random Horizontal Flips** and **Random Vertical Flips**. We will explicitly avoid geometric rotations, color jittering, or cutout, as these techniques act as noise on this high-signal dataset **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00002)**.

### Training
*   **Ensembling Strategy:** We will employ **Homogeneous Seed Averaging**. We will train **5 independent instances** of the Custom Narrow SE-Multi-Scale ResNet using different random seeds. This strategy is chosen to eliminate stochastic residual ranking errors **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00007)** while avoiding the instability observed with heterogeneous ensembles **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00008)**.
*   **Optimizer & Scheduler:** We will use the **AdamW** optimizer combined with a **Cosine Annealing** learning rate scheduler. This combination ensures robust convergence and generalization **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00007)**.
*   **Training Duration:** We will utilize a reduced training schedule (e.g., 12-15 epochs). The combination of the narrow architecture **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00019)** and SE blocks **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00012)** facilitates rapid convergence, making long training runs unnecessary.
*   **Loss Function:** Binary Cross-Entropy with Logits Loss.

### Evaluation
*   **Test Time Augmentation (TTA):** During inference, we will generate predictions for the original image, a horizontally flipped version, and a vertically flipped version for each of the 5 model instances **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00005)**.
*   **Aggregation:** The final submission probability for each image will be the arithmetic mean of all TTA predictions across all 5 seeds. This maximizes robustness and minimizes stochastic noise.
