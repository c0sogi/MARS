Based on the comprehensive analysis of the provided lessons and the progression of previous experiments—specifically the dominance of "Wide" architectures (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00064**), the inference efficiency of "Structural Re-parameterization" (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00103**), and the proven benefit of "Multi-Scale Aggregation" (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00016**)—I propose the **Custom Ultra-Wide ECA-RepNeXt with Multi-Scale Aggregation**.

This strategy represents a structural synthesis that optimizes the "Wide SE-RepNeXt" concept (Idea 35). It retains the massive representational capacity and inference speed but refines the attention mechanism. By replacing Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) with **Efficient Channel Attention (ECA)**, we address the potential bottleneck of dimensionality reduction in SE blocks, ensuring that the rich, fine-grained feature interactions preserved by the "Ultra-Wide" grouped convolutions are fully utilized without the parameter overhead of fully connected layers.

# Model
I propose a **Custom Ultra-Wide ECA-RepNeXt Architecture**.

*   **Backbone (Ultra-Wide RepNeXt):** We will implement a 3-stage backbone using **ECA-RepNeXt Blocks**.
    *   **Channel Configuration:** We will strictly adhere to the **"Ultra-Wide" Channel Configuration** (`[96, 192, 384]`). This massive capacity is the primary driver of performance (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00049**, **Cite solution_lesson_node_00064**), and it is made computationally feasible by the re-parameterized inference structure.
    *   **Training Topology (Multi-Branch):** Each block will consist of parallel branches to facilitate gradient flow and optimization:
        1.  **Grouped $3 \times 3$ Convolution** (Cardinality = 32).
        2.  **Grouped $1 \times 1$ Convolution** (Cardinality = 32).
        3.  **Identity Mapping** (where dimensions match).
        This combines the optimization benefits of ResNet with the parameter efficiency of ResNeXt.
    *   **Inference Topology (Fused):** Before inference, we will mathematically fuse the parallel branches and Batch Normalization layers into a single **Grouped $3 \times 3$ Convolution**. This eliminates the memory access overhead of residual additions, maximizing throughput (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00103**).
    *   **Downsampling:** We will use a re-parameterizable downsampling block consisting of a parallel Stride-2 $3 \times 3$ convolution and Stride-2 $1 \times 1$ convolution. This inherently applies the **$3 \times 3$ strided convolution** strategy proven to accelerate convergence on low-resolution data (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00060**).

*   **Structural Innovation (ECA Attention):** We will replace SE blocks with **Efficient Channel Attention (ECA)** blocks after the fused convolution in each unit.
    *   **Rationale:** While SE blocks accelerate convergence (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00012**), they rely on dimensionality reduction ($C \to C/r \to C$) via fully connected layers. In an "Ultra-Wide" grouped network, this reduction can destroy the specific channel-wise information preserved by the groups. ECA uses a local 1D convolution to model cross-channel interactions *without* dimensionality reduction, preserving the full fidelity of the wide feature maps while being computationally lighter.

*   **Head (Multi-Scale Aggregation):** We will implement a concatenation-based aggregation head.
    *   We will extract feature maps from the end of **Stage 2** (16x16) and **Stage 3** (8x8) (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00016**).
    *   We will apply **Global Average Pooling (GAP)** to both. We explicitly reject Covariance Pooling or GeM, as standard GAP is sufficient for this high-signal task and avoids the statistical instability of second-order methods on small grids (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00053**, **Cite solution_lesson_node_00098**).
    *   The concatenated vector will be passed to a single dense classification layer.

# Data
*   **Preprocessing:** Images will be loaded in their native **32x32** resolution and normalized to the [0, 1] range. We will strictly avoid resizing or upscaling to prevent interpolation artifacts (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00001**).
*   **Augmentation:** We will strictly adhere to the "light augmentation" strategy, applying only **Random Horizontal Flips** and **Random Vertical Flips**. We will explicitly avoid geometric rotations, color jittering, or cutout, as these techniques act as noise on this high-signal dataset (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**).

# Training
*   **Ensembling Strategy:** We will employ **Homogeneous Seed Averaging**. We will train **5 independent instances** of this architecture using different random seeds. This strategy eliminates stochastic residual ranking errors (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00007**) while avoiding the instability risks of heterogeneous ensembles (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00008**).
*   **Optimizer & Scheduler:** We will use **AdamW** combined with a **Cosine Annealing** learning rate scheduler. This combination ensures robust convergence and generalization (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00007**).
*   **Training Duration:** We will utilize a reduced training schedule (e.g., 20 epochs). The combination of the "Ultra-Wide" capacity, ECA blocks, and RepVGG-style gradient flow facilitates rapid convergence.
*   **Loss Function:** Binary Cross-Entropy with Logits Loss.

# Evaluation
*   **Structural Re-parameterization:** Prior to inference, the model will be converted from its training form (multi-branch) to its inference form (single-branch grouped convolutions) to maximize throughput (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00103**).
*   **Test Time Augmentation (TTA):** During inference, we will generate predictions for the original image, a horizontally flipped version, and a vertically flipped version for each of the 5 model instances (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**).
*   **Aggregation:** The final submission probability for each image will be the arithmetic mean of all TTA predictions across all 5 seeds. This maximizes robustness and minimizes stochastic noise.
