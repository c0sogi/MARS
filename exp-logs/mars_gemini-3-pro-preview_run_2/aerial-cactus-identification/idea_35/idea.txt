Based on the comprehensive analysis of the provided lessons and the progression of previous experiments—specifically the dominance of "Wide" architectures (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00064**), the inference efficiency of "Structural Re-parameterization" (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00103**), and the proven benefit of "Multi-Scale Aggregation" (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00016**)—I propose the **Custom Ultra-Wide SE-RepNeXt with Multi-Scale Aggregation**.

This strategy represents the ultimate structural synthesis. It leverages the **Inference Efficiency** of RepVGG to afford an **Ultra-Wide Channel Configuration** that exceeds previous limits, maximizing the **Representational Capacity** identified as the primary performance driver (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00049**). It resolves the "Shortcut Kernel" debate (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00060** vs. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00063**) by using re-parameterizable blocks that ensemble both strategies during training and fuse them for inference.

# Model
I propose a **Custom Ultra-Wide SE-RepNeXt Architecture**.

*   **Backbone (Ultra-Wide RepNeXt):** We will implement a 3-stage backbone using **SE-RepNeXt Blocks**.
    *   **Channel Configuration:** We will scale the width to **"Ultra-Wide"** dimensions: `[96, 192, 384]`. This represents a 50% increase over the previous "Super-Wide" champion (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00064**). We can afford this massive capacity because the re-parameterized inference model eliminates the memory access overhead of residual additions (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00103**).
    *   **Training Topology (Multi-Branch):** Each block will consist of parallel branches to facilitate gradient flow:
        1.  **Grouped $3 \times 3$ Convolution** (Cardinality = 32).
        2.  **Grouped $1 \times 1$ Convolution** (Cardinality = 32).
        3.  **Identity Mapping** (where dimensions match).
        This combines the optimization benefits of ResNet with the capacity of ResNeXt.
    *   **Inference Topology (Fused):** Before inference, we will mathematically fuse the parallel branches and Batch Normalization layers into a single **Grouped $3 \times 3$ Convolution**. This creates a simple feed-forward network with high throughput (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00103**).
    *   **Downsampling:** We will use a re-parameterizable downsampling block consisting of a parallel Stride-2 $3 \times 3$ convolution and Stride-2 $1 \times 1$ convolution. This inherently applies the $3 \times 3$ strided projection strategy proven to accelerate convergence (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00060**) while retaining the precision of $1 \times 1$ projections (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00063**).
    *   **Attention:** We will integrate **Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) Blocks** after the fused convolution in each block. This accelerates convergence by adaptively recalibrating the rich, multi-group features (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00012**).
    *   **Spatial Preservation:** The network will downsample 32x32 $\to$ 16x16 $\to$ 8x8. We strictly preserve the 8x8 resolution to avoid the loss of fine-grained spatial details (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**).

*   **Head (Multi-Scale Aggregation):** We will implement a concatenation-based aggregation head.
    *   We will extract feature maps from the end of **Stage 2** (16x16) and **Stage 3** (8x8) (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00016**).
    *   We will apply **Global Average Pooling (GAP)** to both. We explicitly reject Covariance Pooling, as the channel count ($C=384$) significantly exceeds the spatial resolution ($N=64$) at Stage 3, rendering second-order statistics unstable (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00044**). GAP is sufficient and robust (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00053**).
    *   The concatenated vector will be passed to a single dense classification layer.

# Data
*   **Preprocessing:** Images will be loaded in their native **32x32** resolution and normalized to the [0, 1] range. We will strictly avoid resizing or upscaling to prevent interpolation artifacts (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00001**).
*   **Augmentation:** We will strictly adhere to the "light augmentation" strategy, applying only **Random Horizontal Flips** and **Random Vertical Flips**. We will explicitly avoid geometric rotations, color jittering, or cutout, as these techniques act as noise on this high-signal dataset (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**).

# Training
*   **Ensembling Strategy:** We will employ **Homogeneous Seed Averaging**. We will train **5 independent instances** of this architecture using different random seeds. This strategy eliminates stochastic residual ranking errors (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00007**) while avoiding the instability risks of heterogeneous ensembles (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00008**).
*   **Optimizer & Scheduler:** We will use **AdamW** combined with a **Cosine Annealing** learning rate scheduler. This combination ensures robust convergence and generalization (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00007**).
*   **Training Duration:** We will utilize a reduced training schedule (e.g., 20 epochs). The combination of the "Ultra-Wide" capacity, SE blocks, and RepVGG-style gradient flow facilitates rapid convergence.
*   **Loss Function:** Binary Cross-Entropy with Logits Loss.

# Evaluation
*   **Structural Re-parameterization:** Prior to inference, the model will be converted from its training form (multi-branch) to its inference form (single-branch grouped convolutions) to maximize throughput (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00103**).
*   **Test Time Augmentation (TTA):** During inference, we will generate predictions for the original image, a horizontally flipped version, and a vertically flipped version for each of the 5 model instances (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**).
*   **Aggregation:** The final submission probability for each image will be the arithmetic mean of all TTA predictions across all 5 seeds. This maximizes robustness and minimizes stochastic noise.
