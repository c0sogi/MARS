Based on the progression of experiments and the specific lessons learned, I propose the **Custom Narrow SE-ResNet with Multi-Scale Global Covariance Pooling**.

This strategy represents a structural evolution from the "Learnable Pooling" of Idea 12. While previous iterations moved from Average Pooling to Max Pooling (Idea 11) and then to Generalized Mean Pooling (Idea 12) to better capture salient features, they all remain **first-order** statistical methods. Cacti identification in low-resolution aerial imagery is fundamentally a **texture recognition** task (distinguishing spines and vegetation patterns from background noise). Texture is best described by **second-order** statistics (correlations between features). Therefore, I propose replacing the first-order pooling layers with Global Covariance Pooling to explicitly model these texture descriptors while retaining the highly efficient backbone established in prior successful runs.

### Model
I propose a **Custom Narrow SE-ResNet with Multi-Scale Global Covariance Pooling**.

*   **Backbone (Narrow SE-ResNet):** We will utilize the optimized backbone structure validated in previous experiments to ensure efficiency and spatial preservation.
    *   **Channel Configuration:** We will strictly adhere to the "Narrow" width (`[16, 32, 64]`). This reduced capacity has been proven sufficient for the dataset and significantly lowers computational cost **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00019)**.
    *   **Kernel Selection:** We will exclusively use **3x3 convolutions**, matching the receptive field to the 32x32 input resolution and avoiding the inefficiency of large kernels **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00011)**.
    *   **Spatial Preservation:** The network will downsample the input from 32x32 $\to$ 16x16 $\to$ 8x8, strictly preserving the 8x8 resolution to avoid the loss of fine-grained details **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00005)**.
    *   **Attention:** Each residual block will include a **Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) module** to accelerate convergence by adaptively recalibrating channel importance **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00012)**.

*   **Structural Innovation (Global Covariance Pooling):** Instead of GAP, GMP, or GeM, we will implement **Global Covariance Pooling (GCP)** at the aggregation stages.
    *   **Multi-Scale Extraction:** We will extract feature maps from **Stage 2 (16x16, 32 channels)** and **Stage 3 (8x8, 64 channels)** to leverage both mid-level and high-level features **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00016)**.
    *   **Covariance Computation:** For each feature map of size $C \times H \times W$, we will reshape it to $C \times N$ (where $N=H \times W$) and compute the covariance matrix $\Sigma = X X^T$ (size $C \times C$). This matrix captures the correlation between different feature channels (e.g., how often a "vertical edge" filter activates simultaneously with a "green color" filter), which is a powerful descriptor for texture.
    *   **Normalization:** To ensure numerical stability and gradient flow, we will apply **Matrix Power Normalization** (specifically, Newton-Schulz iteration or element-wise sign-sqrt) to the covariance matrices.
    *   **Flattening:** We will flatten the upper triangular portion of the covariance matrices (to remove redundancy) and concatenate the vectors from Stage 2 and Stage 3.
    *   **Head:** The fused second-order feature vector will be passed to a single dense classification layer. This explicitly targets the "texture" nature of the problem in a way that first-order pooling cannot, without the overhead of decoder architectures **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00027)**.

### Data
*   **Preprocessing:** Images will be loaded in their native **32x32** resolution and normalized to the [0, 1] range. We will strictly avoid resizing or upscaling to prevent interpolation artifacts **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00001)**.
*   **Augmentation:** We will strictly adhere to the "light augmentation" strategy, applying only **Random Horizontal Flips** and **Random Vertical Flips**. We will explicitly avoid geometric rotations, color jittering, or cutout, as these techniques act as noise on this high-signal dataset **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00002)**.

### Training
*   **Ensembling Strategy:** We will employ **Homogeneous Seed Averaging**. We will train **5 independent instances** of this architecture using different random seeds. This strategy is chosen to eliminate stochastic residual ranking errors **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00007)** while avoiding the instability risks of heterogeneous ensembles **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00008)**.
*   **Optimizer & Scheduler:** We will use **AdamW** combined with a **Cosine Annealing** learning rate scheduler. This combination ensures robust convergence and generalization **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00007)**.
*   **Training Duration:** We will utilize a reduced training schedule (e.g., 15 epochs). The combination of the narrow architecture **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00019)** and SE blocks **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00012)** facilitates rapid convergence.
*   **Loss Function:** Binary Cross-Entropy with Logits Loss.

### Evaluation
*   **Test Time Augmentation (TTA):** During inference, we will generate predictions for the original image, a horizontally flipped version, and a vertically flipped version for each of the 5 model instances **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00005)**.
*   **Aggregation:** The final submission probability for each image will be the arithmetic mean of all TTA predictions across all 5 seeds. This maximizes robustness and minimizes stochastic noise.
