# Proposed Strategy: Heterogeneous Ensemble with Adversarial Training

## Model
To maximize the Area Under the Curve (AUC) and address the variance issues inherent in small-dataset learning, I propose a structural evolution from a single-model architecture to a **Heterogeneous Ensemble** powered by **Adversarial Training**.

- **Heterogeneous Backbones**: Instead of relying solely on one architecture, we will construct an ensemble of two distinct "Large" transformer models:
    1.  **DeBERTa-v3-Large**: We retain this as the primary backbone due to its superior performance and disentangled attention mechanism (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016).
    2.  **RoBERTa-Large**: We introduce this as a secondary backbone. While DeBERTa is generally stronger, RoBERTa utilizes a different pre-training objective (MLM vs. RTD) and tokenization approach. Ensembling these distinct architectures exploits their diverse error profiles, providing a more robust decision boundary than simply scaling a single model (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
- **Pooling & Classification**: Both backbones will utilize **Mean Pooling** of the last hidden state (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009) to aggregate semantic signal. The pooled representations will be passed to a **Multi-Sample Dropout** head (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015), which averages predictions across multiple dropout masks to further stabilize the output.

## Data
- **Preprocessing**: We will maintain the unicode un-escaping and quote removal to ensure clean text input.
- **Tokenization**: Each backbone will use its specific tokenizer (DeBERTa-v3 tokenizer and RoBERTa tokenizer, respectively) with a maximum sequence length of 128.
- **Stratification**: We will employ **Stratified K-Fold Cross-Validation (K=5)** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015) to ensure that the class distribution in every training and validation fold mirrors the overall dataset, mitigating the risk of skew due to the small sample size.

## Training
- **Adversarial Weight Perturbation (AWP) (Innovation)**: To address the "overfitting" and "small data" challenges identified in multiple lessons, we will integrate AWP into the training loop. This technique injects a minimax optimization step: after the forward pass, we explicitly perturb the model's weights to maximize the loss (simulating a worst-case scenario), and then update the weights to minimize this adversarial loss. This smooths the loss landscape and significantly improves generalization on small datasets.
- **Optimization**: We will use the **AdamW** optimizer with a linear warmup and cosine decay schedule.
- **Layer-wise Learning Rate Decay (LLRD)**: We will apply LLRD (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015) to both models, assigning lower learning rates to the bottom layers (preserving pre-trained linguistic features) and higher rates to the top layers (adapting to the specific task).
- **Checkpointing**: We will strictly use **`copy.deepcopy(model.state_dict())`** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004) to save the best model weights for each fold based on **Validation AUC** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006), ignoring validation loss.

## Evaluation
- **Inference**: We will generate predictions for the test set using all **10 trained models** (2 Architectures $\times$ 5 Folds).
- **Ensembling**: The final submission will be the **arithmetic mean** of the probability outputs from all 10 models. This combines the variance-reduction benefits of Cross-Validation with the diversity benefits of Heterogeneous Architectures.
