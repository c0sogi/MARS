Based on the lessons learned, I propose a strategy that transitions from a single-model approach to a **High-Capacity K-Fold Cross-Validation Ensemble**. This solution leverages the available computational resources (A100 GPU, 24 hours) to overcome the limitations of previous attempts by combining the capacity of larger models with the stability of ensembling, without the under-training pitfalls observed earlier.

### Model
I propose upgrading the architecture to **ConvNeXt-Small**.
*   **Architecture:** We will use `ConvNeXt-Small` initialized with ImageNet pre-trained weights. This model offers approximately double the parameter count of the successful `ConvNeXt-Tiny` (Lesson 00011), providing the capacity needed to model subtle features.
*   **Correction from Previous Failures:** Unlike the previous attempt with `ConvNeXt-Small` (Lesson 00015), we will **not** increase the input resolution to 384x384. We will stick to the native **224x224** resolution. This ensures the computational cost remains manageable, allowing us to train the model to full convergence, which was the primary failure point of the high-resolution experiment **Cite solution_lesson_node_00015**.

### Data
*   **Preprocessing:** Images will be resized to **224x224** pixels.
*   **Splitting (Structural Innovation):** We will implement **5-Fold Cross-Validation**. The training data will be divided into 5 stratified folds. We will train 5 separate instances of the model, each using 80% of the data for training and 20% for validation. This addresses the variance inherent in single-model training and maximizes the utility of the entire dataset for inference **Cite solution_lesson_node_00009**.
*   **Augmentation:** We will retain the optimized augmentation pipeline:
    *   **RandomResizedCrop:** Strictly with a minimum scale of **0.8** to preserve semantic integrity **Cite solution_lesson_node_00013**.
    *   **Mixup:** Applied with `alpha=0.2` to ensure probability calibration and prevent overconfidence **Cite solution_lesson_node_00011**.
    *   **HorizontalFlip:** For basic invariance.

### Training
*   **Strategy:** We will train each of the 5 folds for **15 epochs**. This extended duration (compared to the previous 5 or 8 epochs) ensures that the larger `ConvNeXt-Small` model fully converges, avoiding the under-fitting observed in previous ensemble attempts **Cite solution_lesson_node_00009**.
*   **Optimization:** We will use **AdamW** with a **Cosine Annealing Learning Rate** scheduler.
*   **Early Stopping:** We will effectively disable aggressive early stopping by setting the patience to the full duration (or simply saving the best checkpoint based on validation Log Loss). This allows the scheduler to fully decay the learning rate and settle into a global minimum without premature termination **Cite solution_lesson_node_00016**.
*   **Checkpoints:** For each fold, we will save the model weights that achieve the lowest validation log loss **Cite solution_lesson_node_00004**.

### Evaluation
*   **Ensemble Inference:** For the test set, we will generate predictions using all 5 trained models.
*   **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):** For each model, we will compute the average prediction of the original image and a horizontally flipped version **Cite solution_lesson_node_00011**.
*   **Aggregation:** The final submission probability will be the arithmetic mean of the TTA-averaged probabilities from all 5 folds. This double-averaging strategy (TTA + Ensemble) is the most robust method for minimizing Log Loss, as it systematically reduces variance and prediction error.
*   **Submission Logic:** We will use a non-strict inequality (or no threshold at all) when validating the final submission to ensure valid results are not discarded **Cite solution_lesson_node_00012**.
