Based on the progression of experiments and the specific empirical lessons learned, I propose a **Heterogeneous High-Capacity Ensemble** strategy. This approach synthesizes the architectural success of ConvNeXt, the regularization benefits of Mixup, and the stability of ensembling, while explicitly avoiding the resolution and convergence pitfalls identified in previous attempts.

### Model
I propose constructing a heterogeneous ensemble comprising two distinct architectures: **ConvNeXt-Small** and **Swin-Small**.
*   **Heterogeneity (Structural Innovation):** By combining a modern CNN (**ConvNeXt**) with a Hierarchical Vision Transformer (**Swin**), we leverage complementary inductive biases—ConvNeXt excels at local feature extraction, while Swin captures long-range global dependencies. This diversity typically decorrelates prediction errors, leading to a lower ensemble Log Loss than homogeneous ensembles.
*   **Capacity:** We will upgrade from the "Tiny" variants to "Small". This increases model capacity to better capture subtle features, addressing the potential under-fitting of smaller models **Cite solution_lesson_node_00009**. We explicitly avoid "Base" or "Large" variants to ensure the ensemble fits within the 24-hour compute budget.

### Data
*   **Resolution:** All images will be resized to **224x224** pixels. We explicitly reject the 384x384 resolution attempted in previous ideas. Lesson 00015 demonstrated that higher resolution drastically increases computational cost and hinders convergence within a fixed budget. Sticking to 224x224 allows us to allocate our compute towards more training epochs and ensemble members **Cite solution_lesson_node_00015**.
*   **Augmentation:** We will strictly maintain the optimized augmentation pipeline:
    *   **RandomResizedCrop:** We will enforce a minimum scale of **0.8**. Previous experiments showed that aggressive cropping (e.g., 0.5) introduces semantic noise that degrades performance **Cite solution_lesson_node_00013**.
    *   **Mixup:** We will apply Mixup with `alpha=0.2`. This is critical for preventing overconfidence and ensuring the probability calibration required for minimizing Log Loss **Cite solution_lesson_node_00011**.
    *   **HorizontalFlip:** Standard geometric invariance.

### Training
*   **Strategy:** We will implement **5-Fold Stratified Cross-Validation** for *each* architecture, resulting in a total of **10 trained models** (5 ConvNeXt-Small + 5 Swin-Small). This ensures every training sample is utilized and provides robust out-of-fold validation **Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**.
*   **Convergence:** Each fold will be trained for **20 epochs**. This extended duration is chosen to correct the under-training observed in previous ensemble attempts **Cite solution_lesson_node_00009** and the larger model experiment **Cite solution_lesson_node_00015**.
*   **Optimization:** We will use the **AdamW** optimizer with a **Cosine Annealing Learning Rate** scheduler.
*   **Patience:** We will effectively disable early stopping (or set patience to the full 20 epochs). This allows the scheduler to fully decay the learning rate and settle into a global minimum, preventing the premature termination observed when using strict patience with cosine schedules **Cite solution_lesson_node_00016**.

### Evaluation
*   **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):** For every test image, each of the 10 models will predict probabilities for both the original and a horizontally flipped version. These two predictions will be averaged to reduce individual model variance **Cite solution_lesson_node_00011**.
*   **Aggregation:** The final submission probability will be the arithmetic mean of the TTA-averaged predictions from all 10 models. This double-averaging strategy (TTA + Heterogeneous Ensemble) maximizes stability and minimizes the Log Loss metric.
