### Model

### Data
*   **Resolution Increase (Structural Innovation):** I will increase the input image resolution from 224x224 to **384x384** pixels. The data analysis revealed that the average image dimensions are approximately 404x360. Resizing to 224x224 discards over 60% of the pixel information, potentially losing fine-grained textures (fur patterns) critical for distinguishing similar breeds. Increasing to 384x384 aligns the input size with the natural distribution of the data.
*   **Augmentation:** We will retain **RandomResizedCrop** but strictly enforce a minimum scale of **0.8**. As learned, aggressive cropping (e.g., 0.5) introduces semantic noise by removing the subject, which degrades performance **Cite solution_lesson_node_00013**.
*   **Regularization:** We will continue to use **Mixup** augmentation. This is essential for preventing overconfidence and ensuring the probability calibration required for minimizing Log Loss **Cite solution_lesson_node_00011**.

### Training
*   **Model Exponential Moving Average (EMA):** I will introduce Model EMA into the training loop. Instead of relying solely on the weights at the end of an epoch, we will maintain a "shadow" model whose parameters are an exponential moving average of the active model's parameters. This technique smoothens the optimization trajectory, leading to flatter minima and better generalization on unseen data.
*   **Strategy:** We will train a single model on the **full training dataset** to maximize data utility **Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**.
*   **Loss Function:** We will use **Soft-Target Cross-Entropy** to accommodate the continuous labels generated by Mixup.
*   **Optimization:** We will use the **AdamW** optimizer with a **Cosine Annealing Learning Rate** scheduler to ensure convergence.

### Evaluation
*   **Inference Weights:** Predictions will be generated using the **EMA model weights** rather than the final training weights, as EMA weights are typically more robust to noise.
*   **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):** We will apply TTA by averaging predictions from the original image and a horizontally flipped version. This technique has proven effective in reducing variance and improving the log loss score **Cite solution_lesson_node_00011**.
