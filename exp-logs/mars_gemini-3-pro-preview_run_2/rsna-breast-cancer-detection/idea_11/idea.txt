Based on the comprehensive analysis of previous experiments and the extracted lessons, I propose the **Multi-Contrast Single-Instance Network (MC-SIN)**.

This strategy synthesizes the robust training dynamics of the "Current Best" solution (Idea 3) with the input engineering attempted in Idea 10, while strictly avoiding the architectural pitfalls (Multi-Task Learning, Mixed Precision Instability) that caused recent failures.

# Model
**Architecture: EfficientNetV2-Small with Simulated Windowing**
1.  **Visual Backbone:** The model utilizes an **EfficientNetV2-Small** architecture initialized with ImageNet weights. This backbone is **completely unfrozen and fine-tuned** end-to-end to adapt to the specific grayscale texture statistics of mammograms, preventing the failure of frozen embeddings **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00002}**. The V2 variant is selected for its superior training speed and parameter efficiency compared to the B-series.
2.  **Input Engineering (Structural Innovation):** To maximize information density without increasing spatial resolution (which triggers the "Resolution Trap" **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00014}**), the input layer is modified to accept 3 distinct representations of the single DICOM image, mimicking a radiologist's "windowing" workflow:
    *   **Channel 1 (Standard):** Linearly normalized grayscale image.
    *   **Channel 2 (Structure):** Processed with **CLAHE** (Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization) to enhance local contrast and highlight microcalcifications.
    *   **Channel 3 (Density):** Processed with **Gamma Correction** ($\gamma > 1$) to suppress background noise and highlight dense tissue masses.
3.  **Head Design:** The model employs a **Single-Task** architecture. The visual embedding (pooled via GeM) is fused with metadata and output through a single sigmoid neuron for cancer probability. We explicitly **reject Multi-Task Learning (MTL)**, as previous experiments demonstrated that auxiliary tasks (BIRADS/Density) dilute the gradient signal for the rare primary class in highly imbalanced settings **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00016}**.

# Data
1.  **Single-Instance Learning (SIL):** The pipeline strictly adheres to the SIL paradigm. Patient-level cancer labels are broadcast to every individual image (view). This **dense supervision** is retained as it outperforms the sparse supervision of Multi-Instance Learning (MIL) in this domain **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00005}**.
2.  **Full Dataset Utilization:** The model will be trained on the **entire training set**. We avoid subsampling the data to fit higher resolutions, as maximizing exposure to the rare positive cases is more critical than spatial detail **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00014}**.
3.  **Resolution:** Images will be resized to **640x640**. This serves as a balanced operating point, allowing for full-dataset training within the 24-hour limit while utilizing the Multi-Contrast input to compensate for not using 1024px resolution.

# Training
1.  **Objective: FP32-Guarded High-Weight Loss:**
    *   The model uses **BCEWithLogitsLoss** with a high `pos_weight` (e.g., 20.0 - 25.0) to aggressively target the minority class.
    *   **Critical Stability Fix:** To prevent the `NaN` divergence observed in previous attempts, the loss calculation is explicitly wrapped in a **Float32 context** (`with torch.amp.autocast(enabled=False):`). This allows us to use the necessary high scalar weights without overflowing the limited dynamic range of Mixed Precision (AMP) **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00008}**, **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00013}**.
2.  **Sampling: Natural Distribution:**
    *   The training uses a **Standard Random Sampler**. We explicitly reject `WeightedRandomSampler` to ensure the model learns the true prior probability of cancer (~2%), preserving the **probability calibration** required for the Probabilistic F1 metric **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00010}**.
3.  **Optimization:** AdamW optimizer with OneCycleLR scheduler.

# Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** The model iterates through the test set, generating a cancer probability for each image using the Multi-Contrast input.
2.  **Aggregation:** Predictions are grouped by `prediction_id`. The final submission score is the **maximum** probability across all images (views) associated with that ID, ensuring that any suspicious view triggers a high risk score **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00005}**.
