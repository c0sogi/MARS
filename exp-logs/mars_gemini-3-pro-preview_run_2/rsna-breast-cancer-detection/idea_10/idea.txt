Based on the comprehensive analysis of previous experiments and the extracted lessons, I propose the **Stabilized Multi-Task Single-Instance Network (SMT-SIN)**.

This strategy is a structural evolution of the "Current Best" (Idea 3). It integrates the feature-enhancing capabilities of Multi-Task Learning (attempted in Ideas 5, 6, 9) but applies specific engineering interventions to resolve the stability, calibration, and data volume issues that caused those attempts to fail.

### Model
**Architecture: Fine-Tuned EfficientNetV2 with Auxiliary Supervision**
1.  **Visual Backbone:** The model employs an **EfficientNetV2-Small** architecture, initialized with ImageNet weights. Crucially, this backbone is **completely unfrozen and fine-tuned** end-to-end. This ensures the feature extractor adapts to the specific grayscale texture statistics of mammograms (e.g., microcalcifications), addressing the root cause of failure in frozen embedding approaches Cite {solution_lesson_node_00002}.
2.  **Input Engineering (Channel Expansion):** To maximize the information extracted from the single-channel grayscale images, the input layer is modified to accept 3 channels. These channels will be populated by the original image and two variants processed with **Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE)** at different clip limits. This "Simulated Windowing" mimics the radiologist's workflow of adjusting contrast to see different tissue densities.
3.  **Pooling & Fusion:** Feature maps are aggregated using **Generalized Mean Pooling (GeM)**, which focuses on high-activation regions (lesions) more effectively than standard average pooling. This visual embedding is concatenated with a normalized metadata vector (Age, Implant, View, Machine ID) processed by a parallel MLP.
4.  **Multi-Task Heads:** The network branches into three output heads. This structural change provides dense gradient signals to prevent feature collapse, a common issue in highly imbalanced binary classification:
    *   **Primary Head:** Binary classification for `cancer`.
    *   **Auxiliary Head 1:** Regression for `BIRADS` scores (0-2), forcing the model to learn a continuous spectrum of suspicion.
    *   **Auxiliary Head 2:** Classification for `density` (A-D), forcing the model to distinguish tissue structure from pathology.

### Data
1.  **Single-Instance Learning (SIL):** The pipeline strictly follows the SIL paradigm. Patient-level labels (Cancer, BIRADS, Density) are broadcast to every individual image (view). This **dense supervision** strategy is retained as it was proven to outperform the sparse supervision of Multi-Instance Learning (MIL) in this domain Cite {solution_lesson_node_00005}.
2.  **Full Dataset Utilization:** We explicitly reject the strategy of subsampling the dataset to fit higher resolutions. The model will be trained on the **entire training set**. As observed in previous failures, reducing the number of positive samples (even for higher resolution) critically harms performance in this imbalanced setting Cite {solution_lesson_node_00014}.
3.  **Resolution Strategy:** Images will be resized to **640x640**. This serves as an optimal geometric mean between the insufficient 224px (Idea 2) and the computationally prohibitive 1024px (Idea 9), allowing for full-dataset training within the 24-hour limit while capturing sufficient detail.

### Training
1.  **Objective: FP32-Guarded Weighted Loss:**
    *   To handle the ~1:50 class imbalance, the Primary Head will use **BCEWithLogitsLoss** with a `pos_weight` of approximately 15.0.
    *   **Critical Stability Fix:** To prevent the `NaN` divergence that caused the failure of previous Multi-Task/Mixed Precision attempts, the loss calculation will be explicitly wrapped in a **Float32 context manager** (`with torch.amp.autocast(enabled=False):`). This ensures that the large scalar gradients generated by `pos_weight` do not overflow the limited dynamic range of `float16` Cite {solution_lesson_node_00008}, Cite {solution_lesson_node_00013}.
2.  **Sampling: Natural Distribution:**
    *   The training will use a **Standard Random Sampler** rather than a Weighted Random Sampler. This ensures the model learns the true prior probability of cancer (~2%), preserving **probability calibration** which is essential for maximizing the Probabilistic F1 metric Cite {solution_lesson_node_00010}.
3.  **Optimization:** The model will be trained using **AdamW** with a **OneCycleLR** scheduler to facilitate rapid convergence.

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** The model iterates through the test set using the metadata provided. Auxiliary heads are ignored, and only the probability from the **Primary Cancer Head** is extracted.
2.  **Aggregation:** Predictions are grouped by `prediction_id`. The final submission score is calculated as the **maximum** probability across all images (views) associated with that ID. This aligns with the empirical finding that robust single-instance predictions aggregated via max-pooling outperform complex learned aggregation mechanisms Cite {solution_lesson_node_00005}.
