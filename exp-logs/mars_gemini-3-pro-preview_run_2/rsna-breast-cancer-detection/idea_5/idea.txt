Based on the failure of frozen embeddings (Idea 2) and the superior performance of dense supervision over bag-level aggregation (Idea 3 vs. Idea 4), I propose a **Multi-Task Single-Instance Network (MT-SIN)**. This strategy evolves the successful Single-Instance Learning (SIL) approach by introducing auxiliary supervision to combat the extreme class imbalance and data scarcity.

### Model
**Architecture: EfficientNetV2-Small with Multi-Task Heads**
1.  **Visual Backbone:** The model utilizes an **EfficientNetV2-Small** architecture initialized with ImageNet weights. This backbone is **completely unfrozen and fine-tuned** end-to-end to adapt to the specific grayscale textures of mammography, addressing the root cause of failure in frozen embedding approaches Cite {solution_lesson_node_00002}. EfficientNetV2 is selected over V1 (used in Idea 3) for its faster training speed and improved parameter efficiency, allowing for higher resolution inputs within the same compute budget.
2.  **Pooling & Fusion:** The feature map is condensed using **Generalized Mean Pooling (GeM)**, which focuses on high-activation regions (lesions) better than standard average pooling. This visual embedding is concatenated with a processed metadata vector (Age, Implant, View, Laterality, Site ID) derived from a parallel MLP branch.
3.  **Multi-Task Heads:** Instead of a single output, the network branches into three distinct heads to leverage the rich auxiliary data provided in the training set:
    *   **Primary Head (Cancer):** Predicts the binary probability of malignancy (Target).
    *   **Auxiliary Head 1 (BIRADS):** Predicts the BIRADS assessment score (0-2 in this dataset). This forces the model to learn a continuous spectrum of "suspicion" rather than just a binary label, providing richer gradients.
    *   **Auxiliary Head 2 (Density):** Predicts the breast density category (A-D). This compels the backbone to understand the underlying tissue structure, which is a key confounder in diagnosis.

### Data
1.  **Single-Instance Ingestion:** The pipeline retains the **Single-Instance Learning (SIL)** format. Images are loaded individually, and the patient-level labels (Cancer, BIRADS, Density) are broadcast to every image of that patient. This ensures **dense supervision** (one loss signal per image), which was proven more effective than sparse bag-level supervision in this domain Cite {solution_lesson_node_00005}.
2.  **Byte-Level Loading:** The robust **Byte-Level Image Extractor** (using `cv2.imdecode` on binary streams) is preserved to handle DICOM files without `pydicom`.
3.  **Resolution & Augmentation:** Input images are resized to **768x768** (or 640x640 depending on VRAM). This higher resolution is critical for detecting microcalcifications. Strong augmentation (Albumentations: ShiftScaleRotate, GridDistortion, CoarseDropout) is applied to prevent the unfrozen backbone from overfitting.

### Training
1.  **Multi-Task Loss Function:** The total loss is a weighted sum: $L_{total} = L_{cancer} + \lambda_1 L_{BIRADS} + \lambda_2 L_{density}$.
    *   $L_{cancer}$: **BCEWithLogitsLoss** with a high `pos_weight` (e.g., 15.0) to handle the ~2% cancer prevalence.
    *   $L_{BIRADS}$: **MSELoss** (treating BIRADS as an ordinal regression target) or CrossEntropy.
    *   $L_{density}$: **CrossEntropyLoss** for the 4 density classes.
2.  **Procedure:** The model is trained end-to-end. The auxiliary tasks act as regularizers, stabilizing the feature extractor and preventing it from collapsing to a trivial "all-negative" solution, a common issue when training solely on the highly imbalanced binary target.
3.  **Sampling:** A **Balanced Batch Sampler** is used to ensure that every batch contains a fixed ratio of positive cancer cases, ensuring the model sees the minority class frequently.

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** The model iterates through the test set using the metadata provided. Since `BIRADS` and `density` are not available in the test set, the auxiliary heads are ignored, and only the probability from the **Cancer Head** is extracted.
2.  **Aggregation:** Predictions are grouped by `prediction_id`. The final submission score is calculated as the **maximum** probability across all images (views) associated with that ID. This aligns with the finding that simple max-pooling of robust single-instance predictions outperforms complex learned aggregation Cite {solution_lesson_node_00005}.
