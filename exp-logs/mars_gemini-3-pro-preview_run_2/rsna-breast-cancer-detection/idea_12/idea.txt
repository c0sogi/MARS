Based on the lessons learned from previous iterations, I propose the **Modality-Regularized Hybrid Network (MR-HN)**.

This strategy addresses the specific dilemma identified in **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00017}**: Hybrid models (Idea 3) outperform image-only models (Idea 11) but suffer from a "Tabular Shortcut," where the network overfits to strong demographic priors (like Age) and fails to learn robust visual features. This solution forces visual learning via **Modality Dropout** while retaining the stability and calibration improvements from previous failures.

### Model
**Architecture: EfficientNetV2-Small with Modality Dropout**
1.  **Visual Backbone:** The model utilizes an **EfficientNetV2-Small** backbone initialized with ImageNet weights. It is **completely unfrozen and fine-tuned** end-to-end to adapt to mammography textures **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00002}**.
2.  **Input Engineering (Simulated Windowing):** To maximize visual information without increasing resolution (avoiding the "Resolution Trap" **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00014}**), the input is a 3-channel tensor representing different radiological views:
    *   Channel 1: Linearly normalized grayscale.
    *   Channel 2: **CLAHE** (Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization) to enhance local contrast.
    *   Channel 3: **Gamma Correction** to highlight density differences.
3.  **Tabular Branch:** A parallel MLP processes patient metadata (Age, Implant, View, etc.).
4.  **Structural Innovation: Modality Dropout:** Before the fusion layer, a custom dropout layer is applied **specifically to the tabular embedding**. During training, the tabular vector is zeroed out with a probability $p=0.5$. This forces the network to rely solely on the visual backbone for prediction in half of the training steps, preventing the "Tabular Shortcut" where the model ignores the image in favor of the easier Age feature **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00017}**.
5.  **Head Design:** A **Single-Task** output head predicts cancer probability. We explicitly reject Multi-Task Learning (Auxiliary Heads), as dense auxiliary gradients have been shown to dilute the sparse signal of the primary minority class **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00016}**.

### Data
1.  **Single-Instance Learning (SIL):** The pipeline strictly follows the SIL paradigm. Patient-level labels are broadcast to every image. This **dense supervision** is retained as it outperforms the sparse supervision of Multi-Instance Learning **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00005}**.
2.  **Full Dataset Utilization:** The model is trained on the **entire training set**. We avoid subsampling to fit higher resolutions, prioritizing the number of positive samples over spatial dimensions **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00014}**.
3.  **Resolution:** Images are resized to **640x640**. This provides a balance between the insufficient 224px (Idea 2) and the computationally expensive 1024px (Idea 8), allowing for full-dataset training within the 24-hour limit.
4.  **Ingestion:** The robust **Byte-Level Image Extractor** (using `cv2.imdecode` on binary streams) is retained to handle DICOM files without `pydicom`.

### Training
1.  **Objective: FP32-Guarded Weighted Loss:**
    *   The model uses **BCEWithLogitsLoss** with a high `pos_weight` (e.g., 20.0) to address the ~1:50 imbalance.
    *   **Stability Mechanism:** The loss calculation is explicitly wrapped in a **Float32 context** (`with torch.amp.autocast(enabled=False):`). This allows the use of high scalar weights without causing the `NaN` divergence observed when such weights interact with the limited dynamic range of Mixed Precision (AMP) **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00008}**, **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00013}**.
2.  **Sampling: Natural Distribution:**
    *   The training uses a **Standard Random Sampler**. We reject `WeightedRandomSampler` to ensure the model learns the true prior probability of cancer (~2%), preserving the **probability calibration** required for the Probabilistic F1 metric **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00010}**.
3.  **Optimization:** AdamW optimizer with a OneCycleLR scheduler is used for rapid convergence.

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** The model iterates through the test set. For every image, the full hybrid model (Image + Tabular) is used (Modality Dropout is disabled during inference).
2.  **Aggregation:** Predictions are grouped by `prediction_id`. The final submission score is the **maximum** probability across all images (views) associated with that ID, ensuring high sensitivity to any suspicious view **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00005}**.
