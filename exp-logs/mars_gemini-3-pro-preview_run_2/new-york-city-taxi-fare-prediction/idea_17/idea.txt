Based on the analysis of the provided lessons and the failure modes of previous experiments, I propose a **Variance-Aware Dual-Hygiene Gradient Boosting** strategy. This approach synthesizes the stability of **Dual-Hygiene** data processing (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00066}), the efficiency of **Vectorized Subtraction** (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00045}), and the structural safety of **Soft Integration** (Cite {solution_lesson_node_00055}).

Crucially, it introduces a **Structural Innovation**: explicitly encoding the **Second Moment (Variance)** of the pricing distribution. Previous solutions provided only the Mean (Expected Value). By providing the Standard Deviation of the route's historical fares, we give the model a direct signal about "Price Risk," allowing it to distinguish between consistently priced routes and those with "Heavy Tail" potential (e.g., traffic-prone airport runs) without overfitting to noise.

# Model
I will utilize the **XGBoost Regressor** as the backbone, leveraging its proven superiority for spatial tabular tasks Cite {solution_lesson_node_00030}.

*   **Structural Innovation (Distributional Priors):** Instead of feeding only the `Mean_Fare` for a route, I will compute and feed the **`Fare_StdDev`** (Standard Deviation).
    *   **Rationale:** The fundamental challenge is predicting "Heavy Tail" outliers without destabilizing the model Cite {solution_lesson_node_00018}. A route with a high Mean could be a long distance trip or a short, expensive trip. A route with high *Variance*, however, explicitly signals the presence of outliers (e.g., variable traffic or surcharges). This feature allows the tree to learn conditional logic like `IF (High_Variance AND Rush_Hour) THEN Predict_High`, effectively capturing the tail behavior safely.
*   **Integration Strategy:** These statistics will be provided as **Input Features** (Soft Integration) rather than Base Margins. This avoids the catastrophic failure modes associated with "Hard Integration" in sparse or noisy regions Cite {solution_lesson_node_00055}, Cite {solution_lesson_node_00070}.

# Data
The data pipeline employs a **Conditional Vectorized Subtraction** strategy to implement Dual-Hygiene without data leakage.

1.  **Input Sanitization (Safety First):**
    *   Strictly clamp all pickup and dropoff coordinates in the **Training, Validation, and Test sets** to the NYC bounding box (e.g., Lat 40-42, Lon -75 to -72). This mitigates the risk of linear extrapolation from corrupt GPS data, which destroyed previous hybrid models Cite {solution_lesson_node_00003}, Cite {solution_lesson_node_00070}.

2.  **Dual-Hygiene Definitions:**
    *   **Wisdom Criteria (Strict):** Rows where `Fare` is between $2.50 and $200, AND `Fare/Km` < $10. Used to generate clean statistics.
    *   **Learner Criteria (Loose):** Rows where `Fare` > $2.50. Retains valid high-fare outliers (e.g., >$500) to ensure the model optimizes the RMSE metric for the tail Cite {solution_lesson_node_00018}.

3.  **Feature Engineering (Conditional Vectorized Subtraction):**
    *   **Global Aggregation:** Compute `Sum_Fare`, `Sum_FareSq` (Squared Fare), and `Count` for every `(Pickup_Geohash7, Dropoff_Geohash7)` route using the **Full 55M Dataset** (filtered by Wisdom Criteria).
    *   **Training Subsample:** Select a stable 5M row subsample (filtered by Learner Criteria).
    *   **Conditional Subtraction:** For each training row:
        *   Check if the row *would have passed* the Wisdom Criteria.
        *   **If Yes:** Subtract its `Fare` and `Fare^2` from the Global Aggregates (LOO).
        *   **If No:** Do not subtract (it wasn't in the Global Aggregate).
        *   Compute `Mean` and `StdDev` from the adjusted sums.
    *   **Significance:** This allows us to use the massive 55M dataset to generate clean Distributional Priors for the 5M training set without data leakage and without the inefficiency of disjoint splitting Cite {solution_lesson_node_00060}.

4.  **Orthogonal Features:**
    *   **Temporal Rate:** Compute `Mean_Fare_Per_Km` for `(Geohash5, Hour)` to capture time-of-day pricing dynamics Cite {solution_lesson_node_00065}.
    *   **Physics:** Haversine, Manhattan, and Rotated Coordinates Cite {solution_lesson_node_00046}.

# Training
1.  **Objective Function:** Use **`reg:squarederror`** (Standard L2 Loss).
    *   **Rationale:** We are training on a stable subsample with robust, clean Distributional Priors. The optimization landscape is stable enough to use L2 loss, which aligns perfectly with the RMSE metric. We avoid robust losses (Huber) as they fail to prioritize the high-fare outliers explicitly retained in the Learner target Cite {solution_lesson_node_00037}.
2.  **Target:** Train on the **raw `fare_amount`**. Do not log-transform, as this biases the RMSE Cite {solution_lesson_node_00025}.

# Evaluation
1.  **Inference:**
    *   **Clamp Test Coordinates** to the NYC bounding box immediately upon loading Cite {solution_lesson_node_00070}.
    *   Map coordinates to Geohashes.
    *   Retrieve `Mean` and `StdDev` priors from the **Global Wisdom Aggregates** (no subtraction needed).
    *   Retrieve `Temporal_Rate` priors.
2.  **Prediction:** Generate predictions using the XGBoost model.
3.  **Post-Processing:** Apply a minimum fare floor ($2.50).
