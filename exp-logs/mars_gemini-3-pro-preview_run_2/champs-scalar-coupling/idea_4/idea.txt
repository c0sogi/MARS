Based on the analysis of the previous results and the identified lessons, I propose an optimized strategy that evolves the **Directional Message Passing** architecture into a **Hybrid Geometric-Attention Network**.

### Model: Hybrid Geometric-Attention Network
The proposed architecture addresses the limitation of local message passing in capturing long-range electronic effects (such as conjugation) while retaining the high-fidelity geometric modeling of the previous best solution.

1.  **Geometric Encoder (Backbone):**
    *   Retain the **Directional Message Passing Neural Network (DMPNN)** structure (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00014`). This component will generate rich local representations by passing messages on directed edges ($j \to i$) and aggregating information from triplets ($k \to j \to i$).
    *   Strictly maintain the use of **Radial Basis Functions (RBF)** for distances (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00004`) and **Spherical Basis Functions (SBF)** for bond angles (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00014`) to ensure geometric completeness (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00002`).
    *   Ensure the update functions within this backbone are deep **Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs)** with non-linear activations (e.g., Swish/SiLU), avoiding the information bottlenecks caused by linear projections (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00006`).

2.  **Global Interaction Module (Innovation):**
    *   Introduce a **Graph Transformer** block on top of the Geometric Encoder.
    *   After $L$ layers of directional message passing, the resulting edge (or atom) embeddings will be projected into a sequence. A stack of **Multi-Head Self-Attention** layers will then process this sequence.
    *   **Purpose:** This allows every atom/edge to attend to every other atom/edge in the molecule, enabling the model to capture global electronic structures and long-range dependencies that extend beyond the fixed radial cutoff of the DMPNN. This is a structural innovation over the purely local aggregation of the previous ideas.

3.  **Pairwise Readout Head:**
    *   For a specific target coupling pair (Atom $u$, Atom $v$), extract the final context-aware embeddings from the Transformer output.
    *   Construct a pair representation (e.g., by concatenating the features of $u$, $v$, and their specific coupling type embedding).
    *   Pass this vector through a **Deterministic MLP** (no Dropout) to predict the scalar coupling constant. This adheres to the finding that stochastic regularization degrades precision in this physical task (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00007`) and avoids the performance penalty of hierarchical readouts (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00013`).

### Data
*   **Preprocessing:**
    *   Generate molecular graphs from `.xyz` files with a spatial cutoff (e.g., 5.0 Å).
    *   Pre-compute **RBF expansions** for edge distances and **SBF expansions** for triplet angles to maximize training throughput.
    *   **Target Normalization:** Standardize the `scalar_coupling_constant` (zero mean, unit variance) individually for each coupling type to handle the varying scales.
*   **Evaluation Data:**
    *   Process the `test_metadata.csv` by loading the corresponding raw `.xyz` files directly to ensure full coverage of the test set.

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** Use **Mean Absolute Error (MAE)**.
*   **Optimizer:** Use **AdamW** with a warm-up phase followed by a Cosine Annealing learning rate schedule.
*   **Scaling:** Leverage the efficiency of the backbone to maximize the **hidden dimension (width)** and **training horizon (epochs)**, as previous experiments indicated the model was underfitting and benefited significantly from increased capacity (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00015`).
*   **Regularization:** Rely on weight decay and the inductive biases of the architecture; strictly avoid Dropout.

### Evaluation
*   **Inference:** Pass the test graphs through the trained Hybrid model.
*   **Post-Processing:** Apply the inverse of the training standardization (per coupling type) to the model's raw output to generate the final predictions.
*   **Submission:** Map the predictions to the `id` column and save to `submission.csv`.
