# Proposed Strategy: Native-Resolution Ensemble (Noisy Student EfficientNet-B0 & DenseNet-121) with GeM Pooling

This strategy introduces a **Heterogeneous Ensemble** of two highly parameter-efficient "legacy" architectures. It integrates the **Noisy Student** pretraining paradigm (which leverages massive external datasets) and introduces **DenseNet** to provide architectural diversity, while strictly adhering to the "Native Resolution" and "Instance Normalization" constraints identified as critical in the lessons.

### Model
We will construct an ensemble of two distinct Convolutional Neural Networks. This maximizes feature diversity while keeping the total parameter count low (approx. 13M total), well within the computational budget.

1.  **Model A: EfficientNet-B0 (Noisy Student)**
    *   **Evolution:** We retain the EfficientNet-B0 backbone but upgrade the initialization from standard ImageNet weights to **Noisy Student weights** (`tf_efficientnet_b0.ns_jft_in1k`). These weights are learned from a massive dataset (JFT-300M) and are generally more robust to noise, but were previously handicapped by poor preprocessing **Cite solution_lesson_node_00033**.
2.  **Model B: DenseNet-121**
    *   **Structural Innovation:** We introduce **DenseNet-121** as the second backbone. DenseNet is a "legacy" architecture **Cite solution_lesson_node_00013** that uses dense connectivity patterns (feature reuse) distinct from the residual additions in ResNet/EfficientNet. This structural difference decorrelates the errors between Model A and Model B, improving ensemble performance.
3.  **Common Components:**
    *   **Pooling:** Both models will utilize **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling** instead of Global Average Pooling. This is non-negotiable for detecting transient whale calls in noise **Cite solution_lesson_node_00017**.
    *   **Input Adaptation:** Both models will be modified to accept **1-channel input**. The weights will be initialized by **averaging the weights of the original pretrained RGB channels** **Cite solution_lesson_node_00001**.

### Data
The data pipeline is rigorously defined to strictly preserve physical signal fidelity, correcting the "Resizing" and "Normalization" errors from Ideas 6 and 7.

1.  **Spectrogram Generation:**
    *   **FFT Parameters:** `n_fft=1024` (High Frequency Resolution) and `hop_length=64` (High Temporal Resolution). This ensures the FFT bins exceed Mel bands (>4:1) to prevent aliasing **Cite solution_lesson_node_00008** and resolves short call contours **Cite solution_lesson_node_00016**.
    *   **Mel Filterbank:** `n_mels=128`, `normalized=False`. We explicitly disable area normalization to preserve the environmental spectral tilt **Cite solution_lesson_node_00022**.
2.  **Preprocessing:**
    *   **Native Resolution:** We will feed the spectrograms into the models at their **native resolution** (approx. $128 \times 63$). We will **NOT** resize them to $224 \times 224$. Upsampling introduces interpolation artifacts that degrade performance **Cite solution_lesson_node_00031**, **Cite solution_lesson_node_00033**.
    *   **Instance Standardization:** We will apply **Zero-Mean, Unit-Variance standardization** to each clip individually. This is critical for model convergence and was the missing key in Idea 6 **Cite solution_lesson_node_00025**.
3.  **Augmentation:**
    *   **SpecAugment** (Time/Freq masking) will be used **Cite solution_lesson_node_00003**. Mixup is explicitly excluded **Cite solution_lesson_node_00018**.

### Training
The training protocol prioritizes stability and full convergence.

1.  **Sampling:** A **WeightedRandomSampler** will be used to balance the batches.
2.  **Batch Size:** We will maximize the batch size to **128** (or hardware limit) to stabilize Batch Normalization statistics **Cite solution_lesson_node_00004**.
3.  **Optimization:** We will use **Binary Cross Entropy with Logits Loss** and the **AdamW** optimizer with a **Cosine Annealing** scheduler.
4.  **Convergence:** We will set a generous patience (e.g., 6-8 epochs) and sufficient max epochs to ensure both models reach their global optimum, avoiding the under-fitting observed in Idea 7 **Cite solution_lesson_node_00026**.

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** Test clips will be processed using the exact same pipeline (1024/64 STFT, Native Resolution, Instance Standardization).
2.  **Ensembling:** We will perform **Soft Voting** by averaging the probability outputs of Model A and Model B. This combines the robust, high-capacity features of the Noisy Student EfficientNet with the dense, texture-focused features of DenseNet.
