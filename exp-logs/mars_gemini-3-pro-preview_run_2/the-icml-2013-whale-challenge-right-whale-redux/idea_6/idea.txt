# Proposed Strategy: Heterogeneous Ensemble (EfficientNet-B0 & ResNet-34) with GeM Pooling

This strategy evolves the solution by combining the proven **EfficientNet-B0** (from Idea 4) with a scaled-up **ResNet-34** backbone. This moves beyond searching for a single "perfect" architecture to exploiting the diverse inductive biases of two robust, legacy CNNs.

### Model
We will construct an ensemble of two distinct convolutional neural networks.
1.  **Model A (Retention):** **EfficientNet-B0**. This preserves the architecture that achieved the current best score (Idea 4), utilizing its Inverted Residual blocks and Squeeze-and-Excitation modules.
2.  **Model B (Evolution):** **ResNet-34**. We introduce a deeper ResNet backbone (scaling up from the ResNet-18 in Idea 1).
    *   **Justification:** Lesson 00013 establishes that "legacy" architectures like ResNet are significantly more robust and easier to train than modern Transformers or ConvNeXt on this dataset. ResNet-34 provides increased capacity over ResNet-18 to capture subtle features without the hyperparameter sensitivity of non-legacy models.
3.  **Structural Components (Both Models):**
    *   **Pooling:** Both models will replace standard Global Average Pooling with **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling**. This is critical for maximizing the detection of transient whale calls against background noise **Cite solution_lesson_node_00017**.
    *   **Input Adaptation:** The first convolutional layer of both models will be modified to accept **1-channel input**. The weights will be initialized by averaging the original pretrained RGB weights to preserve spatial filters **Cite solution_lesson_node_00001**.

### Data
The data pipeline is unified for both models, strictly adhering to the physical signal constraints identified in the lessons.
1.  **Input Representation:** We will use **Single-Channel Log-Mel Spectrograms**. We explicitly reject multi-channel inputs (like Deltas) as CNNs learn these gradients internally and manual engineering degrades performance **Cite solution_lesson_node_00021**.
2.  **Spectrogram Parameters:**
    *   **FFT Window:** `n_fft=1024`. This ensures the number of unique bins (513) exceeds the number of Mel bands (128) by a ratio of >4:1, preventing aliasing **Cite solution_lesson_node_00008**.
    *   **Hop Length:** `hop_length=64`. This provides high temporal resolution ($\approx 62$ frames) to resolve short call contours **Cite solution_lesson_node_00016**.
    *   **Normalization:** We will set `normalized=False` in the Mel filterbank generation. This preserves the spectral tilt of the environmental "Pink" noise, maximizing contrast for the model **Cite solution_lesson_node_00022**.
3.  **Augmentation:** We will apply **SpecAugment** (Time and Frequency Masking). We will explicitly **exclude Mixup**, as interpolation damages the structural integrity of transient signals **Cite solution_lesson_node_00018**.

### Training
Both models will be trained independently using the stabilized procedure developed in previous iterations.
*   **Sampling:** A **WeightedRandomSampler** will be used to oversample the minority class (whale calls) to achieve a 50/50 batch balance.
*   **Batch Size:** We will maximize the batch size to **128**. This is essential to stabilize the gradients from the weighted sampler and ensure robust Batch Normalization statistics **Cite solution_lesson_node_00004**.
*   **Optimization:** We will use **Binary Cross Entropy with Logits Loss** and the **AdamW** optimizer with a Cosine Annealing scheduler.
*   **Regularization:** We rely on SpecAugment **Cite solution_lesson_node_00003** rather than geometric augmentations.

### Evaluation
*   **Inference:** Test clips will be processed into Log-Mel Spectrograms using the exact parameters defined above.
*   **Ensembling:** Both trained models (EfficientNet-B0 and ResNet-34) will perform forward passes on the test data. The final prediction will be the **unweighted average** of the probability outputs from both models. This "Soft Voting" approach reduces the variance of the individual models and typically yields a higher AUC than any single model.
