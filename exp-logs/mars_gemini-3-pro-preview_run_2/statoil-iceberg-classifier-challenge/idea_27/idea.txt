Based on the accumulated lessons and the structural synthesis of previous successful and failed experiments, I propose the **Sustained-Width Delayed-Integration Network (SWDI-Net)**.

This solution represents a structural evolution that resolves the conflict between **Backbone Capacity** (Lesson 77) and **Parameter Efficiency** (Lesson 41) while strictly adhering to the **Dual-Pooling** (Lesson 70) and **Delayed Integration** (Lesson 107) principles. It introduces a **Strided Spatial-Integration Readout** to compress the high-dimensional feature volume without the destructive effects of 1x1 bottlenecks or global pooling.

# Model

The architecture employs the **Split-Branch Topology** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007) with a novel backbone and readout interface.

1.  **Visual Branch (Sustained-Width Backbone)**:
    *   **Sustained Width Strategy**: Maintain a high channel capacity of **128 filters** across all four convolutional stages. This avoids the underfitting observed in narrower architectures (Cite solution_lesson_node_00077) and prevents the "Terminal Bottleneck" failure of reducing channels in the final layer (Cite solution_lesson_node_00055).
    *   **Delayed-Integration Block Topology**:
        *   **Wide Convolution**: 3x3 Convolution (Input Channels $\to$ 128 Filters) $\to$ BatchNorm $\to$ ReLU.
            *   *Note:* For Blocks 2, 3, and 4, the input channels will be **256** (from the previous Dual-Pooling). This convolution layer performs the "Integration" of the previous block's dual streams *at the start* of the new receptive field, satisfying the requirement for **Delayed Integration** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00107).
        *   **Pre-Pooling Attention**: Apply **Convolutional Block Attention Modules (CBAM)** strictly **before** the pooling layer. This refines features at high resolution (Cite solution_lesson_node_00061). Crucially, utilize **Mixed Pooling (Max + Avg)** for the attention logic, explicitly avoiding Min-pooling to ensure stability (Cite solution_lesson_node_00095, solution_lesson_node_00079).
        *   **Dual-Stream Pooling**: Apply **Max Pooling** (Peaks) and **Min Pooling** (Shadows) and concatenate the outputs. This expands the channel depth ($128 \to 256$), preserving the full dynamic range of the signal (Cite solution_lesson_node_00070).
    *   **Structural Innovation: Strided Spatial-Integration Readout**:
        *   The output of Stage 4 is a $4 \times 4 \times 256$ tensor.
        *   Flattening this yields 4,096 parameters (Risk: Overfitting, Cite solution_lesson_node_00041).
        *   Applying a 1x1 bottleneck destroys spatial context (Risk: Context Loss, Cite solution_lesson_node_00101).
        *   **Solution**: Apply a **3x3 Convolution** with **Stride 2** (padding 1) to reduce the channels from 256 to **128**.
        *   **Mechanism**: This layer performs three critical functions simultaneously:
            1.  **Spatial Integration**: Mixes the adjacent "Peak" and "Shadow" channels using a spatial kernel (Cite solution_lesson_node_00101).
            2.  **Downsampling**: Reduces the spatial grid from $4 \times 4$ to $2 \times 2$.
            3.  **Compression**: Reduces channels to 128.
        *   **Result**: The flattened vector is $2 \times 2 \times 128 = 512$ dimensions. This is highly efficient while preserving the wide backbone's feature richness.

2.  **Metadata Branch**:
    *   A dedicated Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) to process the `inc_angle` scalar (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).

3.  **Fusion Head**:
    *   **Concatenation**: Fuse the visual vector (512 dim) and the metadata vector via simple concatenation (Cite solution_lesson_node_00073).
    *   **Classification**: Process through a dense head with Batch Normalization, ReLU, and **High Dropout (0.5)**. The high dropout is essential to regularize the wide backbone (Cite solution_lesson_node_00077, solution_lesson_node_00014).

# Data

1.  **Input Construction**: Construct 3-channel images using `Band_1`, `Band_2`, and the arithmetic mean `(Band_1 + Band_2) / 2`. Do not use inverted inputs (Cite solution_lesson_node_00076).
2.  **Strict Fold-wise Preprocessing**: Implement **Independent Per-Channel Min-Max Scaling** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00025). Crucially, calculate the scaling statistics **exclusively on the training subset** of the current fold and apply them to the validation subset. This eliminates the data leakage present in global scaling approaches (Cite solution_lesson_node_00097).
3.  **Augmentation**:
    *   **Rotational Invariance**: Apply random rotations of **0°, 90°, 180°, or 270°** combined with random **Horizontal Flips** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014).
    *   **Constraints**: Exclude Vertical Flips (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015) and Mixup (Cite solution_lesson_node_00031).

# Training

1.  **Strategy**: Implement **Stratified 5-Fold Cross-Validation**. Train 5 independent instances of the SWDI-Net. This is critical to reduce variance and maximize data utilization (Cite solution_lesson_node_00029, solution_lesson_node_00052).
2.  **Optimization**:
    *   **"Low and Slow"**: Initialize Adam with a conservative learning rate (e.g., **2e-4**) to prevent premature convergence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00023).
    *   **Scheduler**: Use `ReduceLROnPlateau` to decay the learning rate when validation loss stagnates (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
3.  **State Management**: Use `copy.deepcopy(model.state_dict())` in the Early Stopping callback to strictly preserve the best weights (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
4.  **Regularization**: Rely on High Dropout (0.5) and Rotational Augmentation. Only introduce Weight Decay if Validation Loss consistently exceeds Training Loss (Cite solution_lesson_node_00012).

# Evaluation

1.  **Inference**: Process test images into the 3-channel format. Use the **average scaling statistics** derived from the training folds to normalize the test data (or ensemble predictions from models scaled with their respective fold stats).
2.  **Ensemble Prediction**: Pass the test data through all 5 trained SWDI-Net models.
3.  **Aggregation**: Compute the **mean** of the predicted probabilities to generate the final submission. This ensemble approach is the valid metric for performance comparison (Cite solution_lesson_node_00036).
