Based on the accumulated lessons and the specific structural conflicts identified in previous iterations (specifically the tension between Dual-Pooling expansion and Channel Contraction), I propose the **Spatially-Integrated Wide-Body Network (SI-WBN)**.

This solution represents a structural synthesis that resolves the "Spatial Context Loss" identified in Lesson 101. It retains the powerful **Wide Backbone** and **Dual-Pooling** but introduces a **Post-Pooling Spatial Integration Layer** to compress features. This ensures that the spatially adjacent "Peak" and "Shadow" signals are mixed via a convolution kernel *before* channel reduction, preventing the information loss caused by point-wise (1x1) bottlenecks.

# Model

The architecture employs the **Split-Branch Topology** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007) with a specifically engineered visual backbone.

1.  **Visual Branch (Spatially-Integrated Wide Backbone)**:
    *   **Sustained Width Strategy**: Maintain a sustained width of **128 filters** across all four stages. This ensures the network has sufficient capacity to model complex SAR textures and avoids the underfitting observed in narrower architectures (Cite solution_lesson_node_00077).
    *   **Block Topology**:
        1.  **Wide Convolution**: 3x3 Convolution with **128 filters** $\to$ BatchNorm $\to$ ReLU.
        2.  **Attention (Pre-Pooling)**: Apply **Convolutional Block Attention Modules (CBAM)** strictly **before** the pooling layer. This refines features at high resolution (Cite solution_lesson_node_00061). Crucially, utilize **Mixed Pooling (Max + Avg)** for the attention logic, explicitly avoiding Min-pooling to ensure stability (Cite solution_lesson_node_00095).
        3.  **Dual-Stream Pooling**: Apply **Max Pooling** (Peaks) and **Min Pooling** (Shadows) and concatenate the outputs. This expands the channel depth to $128 \times 2 = 256$, preserving the full dynamic range (Cite solution_lesson_node_00070).
        4.  **Structural Innovation: Spatial Integration Compression**: Immediately after the Dual-Pooling expansion, apply a **3x3 Convolution** (padding=1) to compress the 256 channels back to **128**.
            *   **Rationale**: Unlike a 1x1 convolution which mixes channels point-wise (destroying the adjacency info between a peak and its shadow), a 3x3 kernel integrates the spatial context while reducing dimensionality (Cite solution_lesson_node_00101). This respects the "Post-Pooling Compression" principle (Cite solution_lesson_node_00102) without the destructive effects of 1x1 bottlenecks.
    *   **Readout (Quadrant Max Pooling)**:
        *   The final output is $4 \times 4 \times 128$.
        *   Apply **Adaptive Max Pooling** to a **$2 \times 2$ grid (Quadrants)**. This reduces spatial dimensions while strictly preserving the strongest signal peak in each quadrant (Cite solution_lesson_node_00021).
        *   **Result**: The flattened vector is $2 \times 2 \times 128 = 512$ dimensions. This solves the "Parameter Explosion" problem (Cite solution_lesson_node_00041) while maintaining the wide backbone.

2.  **Metadata Branch**:
    *   A dedicated Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) to process the `inc_angle` scalar (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).

3.  **Fusion Head**:
    *   **Concatenation**: Fuse the Quadrant-Pooled visual vector (512 dim) and the metadata vector via simple concatenation (Cite solution_lesson_node_00073).
    *   **Classification**: Process through a dense head with Batch Normalization, ReLU, and **High Dropout (0.5)**. The high dropout is essential to regularize the wide backbone (Cite solution_lesson_node_00077, solution_lesson_node_00014).

# Data

1.  **Input Construction**: Construct 3-channel images using `Band_1`, `Band_2`, and the arithmetic mean `(Band_1 + Band_2) / 2`. Do not use inverted inputs (Cite solution_lesson_node_00076).
2.  **Strict Fold-wise Preprocessing**: Implement **Independent Per-Channel Min-Max Scaling** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00025). Crucially, calculate the scaling statistics **exclusively on the training subset** of the current fold and apply them to the validation subset. This eliminates the data leakage present in global scaling approaches (Cite solution_lesson_node_00097).
3.  **Augmentation**:
    *   **Rotational Invariance**: Apply random rotations of **0°, 90°, 180°, or 270°** combined with random **Horizontal Flips** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014).
    *   **Constraints**: Exclude Vertical Flips (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015) and Mixup (Cite solution_lesson_node_00031).

# Training

1.  **Strategy**: Implement **Stratified 5-Fold Cross-Validation**. Train 5 independent instances of the SI-WBN. This is critical to reduce variance and maximize data utilization (Cite solution_lesson_node_00029, solution_lesson_node_00052).
2.  **Optimization**:
    *   **"Low and Slow"**: Initialize Adam with a conservative learning rate (e.g., **2e-4**) to prevent premature convergence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00023).
    *   **Scheduler**: Use `ReduceLROnPlateau` to decay the learning rate when validation loss stagnates (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
3.  **State Management**: Use `copy.deepcopy(model.state_dict())` in the Early Stopping callback to strictly preserve the best weights (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
4.  **Regularization**: Rely on High Dropout (0.5) and Rotational Augmentation. Only introduce Weight Decay if Validation Loss consistently exceeds Training Loss (Cite solution_lesson_node_00012).

# Evaluation

1.  **Inference**: Process test images into the 3-channel format. Use the **average scaling statistics** derived from the training folds to normalize the test data.
2.  **Ensemble Prediction**: Pass the test data through all 5 trained SI-WBN models.
3.  **Aggregation**: Compute the **mean** of the predicted probabilities to generate the final submission. This ensemble approach is the valid metric for performance comparison (Cite solution_lesson_node_00036).
