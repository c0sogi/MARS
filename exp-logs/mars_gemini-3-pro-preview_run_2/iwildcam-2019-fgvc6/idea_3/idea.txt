Based on the comprehensive analysis of previous experiments and the derived lessons, I propose a **High-Resolution EfficientNet Strategy with Aggressive Alignment**.

This solution introduces a structural shift from the ResNet architecture to the EfficientNet family to better handle scale variance and increases input resolution to detect small animals. It strictly adheres to the successful two-stage training protocol identified in the lessons.

### Model
*   **Backbone Architecture:** Replace the ResNet50 backbone with **EfficientNet-B3**. This architecture is structurally superior for this task due to its compound scaling method, which balances depth, width, and resolution. This is critical for capturing fine-grained features of small animals (e.g., rodents, squirrels) that were likely lost in the 224x224 ResNet features.
*   **Hybrid Pooling Head:** Retain the **Concatenated Global Pooling** mechanism. The output of the backbone will be processed by both Global Average Pooling (GAP) and Global Max Pooling (GMP), and the results concatenated. This ensures the model retains both the scene context (GAP) and the peak activation signals of small, localized subjects (GMP) **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00010}**.
*   **Classifier:** A single Linear layer mapping the concatenated features to the 23 class logits.

### Data
*   **Resolution Upscaling:** Increase the input image resolution to **300x300** pixels (native resolution for EfficientNet-B3). This structural change in the data pipeline directly addresses the difficulty of detecting small animals in wide-angle camera trap images.
*   **Minimal Augmentation:** During training, apply only **Random Horizontal Flip**. We will explicitly **exclude** aggressive photometric augmentations (like ColorJitter) and Label Smoothing, as these were found to degrade performance in this specific domain of imbalanced, low-light surveillance data **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00020}**.
*   **Normalization:** Use standard ImageNet mean and standard deviation.

### Training
We will employ a **Two-Stage Optimization Strategy** to ensure stability and maximum convergence:

1.  **Stage 1: Aggressive Head Alignment (Linear Warmup)**
    *   **Configuration:** The EfficientNet backbone is completely frozen. Only the linear head is trainable.
    *   **Optimizer:** Use **Adam** with a learning rate of `1e-3` and **zero weight decay**.
    *   **Strategy:** Train for an extended duration (e.g., 6-8 epochs). We will prioritize minimizing training loss over validation metrics in this stage to ensuring the head is perfectly aligned with the pre-trained feature space before unfreezing the backbone **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00018}**.

2.  **Stage 2: Partial Fine-Tuning**
    *   **Configuration:** Unfreeze the top blocks of the EfficientNet-B3 backbone (e.g., the final two stages) to allow the model to adapt to the specific background textures of the locations **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00015}**.
    *   **Optimizer:** Switch to **AdamW** with a lower learning rate (e.g., `1e-4`) and standard weight decay (e.g., `1e-4`).
    *   **Scheduler:** Apply a Cosine Annealing scheduler to smoothly decay the learning rate.
    *   **Checkpointing:** Implement a validation loop that saves the best model based on the Macro F1 score. Crucially, use `copy.deepcopy(model.state_dict())` to ensure the saved weights are not implicitly mutated by subsequent optimizer steps **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00003}**.
    *   **Loss Function:** Cross-Entropy Loss weighted inversely by class frequency.

### Evaluation
*   **Inference:** Load the best model weights (restored via the deep-copy checkpoint).
*   **Prediction:** Resize test images to 300x300, normalize, and perform a forward pass. Select the class with the highest probability (`argmax`) for the final submission.
