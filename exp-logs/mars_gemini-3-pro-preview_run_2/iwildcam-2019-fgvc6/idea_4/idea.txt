Based on the progression of experiments and the specific empirical findings documented in the lessons, I propose a **Native-Resolution EfficientNet-B4 Strategy**.

This solution addresses the resolution bottleneck identified in previous iterations. While the move to EfficientNet-B3 (300x300) improved performance over ResNet50 (224x224), the subsequent attempt to use EfficientNet-B4 failed because it did not increase the resolution to match the backbone's capacity. This strategy rectifies that misalignment to maximize the detection of small animals.

### Model
*   **Backbone Architecture:** Upgrade the backbone to **EfficientNet-B4**. This model is deeper and wider than the B3 variant, offering higher representational capacity. Crucially, we will utilize this backbone at its **native resolution** to ensure the increased parameter count translates to better feature extraction. **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00024}**.
*   **Hybrid Pooling Head:** We will retain the **Concatenated Global Pooling** mechanism. The feature maps from the backbone will be processed by both Global Average Pooling (GAP) and Global Max Pooling (GMP), and the resulting vectors will be concatenated. This preserves both the background context (GAP) and the high-intensity features of small, localized animals (GMP) that might otherwise be washed out. **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00010}**.
*   **Classifier:** A single Linear layer mapping the concatenated feature vector (which is larger in B4 than B3) to the 23 class logits.

### Data
*   **Resolution Upscaling:** Process all images at **380x380** pixels. This is the specific native resolution designed for EfficientNet-B4. By strictly aligning the input resolution with the backbone architecture, we leverage the Compound Scaling Law to capture finer details of small subjects that were lost at 300x300. **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00024}** **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00022}**.
*   **Minimal Augmentation:** We will apply only **Random Horizontal Flip** during training. We will explicitly avoid aggressive photometric augmentations (like ColorJitter) and Label Smoothing, as these have been proven to degrade performance in this specific domain of imbalanced, low-light surveillance data. **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00020}**.
*   **Normalization:** Use standard ImageNet mean and standard deviation.

### Training
We will employ the successful **Two-Stage Optimization Strategy** to ensure the new, larger backbone is trained stably:

1.  **Stage 1: Aggressive Head Alignment (Linear Warmup)**
    *   **Configuration:** The EfficientNet-B4 backbone is completely frozen. Only the linear classifier is trainable.
    *   **Optimizer:** Use **Adam** with a learning rate of `1e-3` and **zero weight decay**.
    *   **Duration:** Train for an extended period (e.g., 6-8 epochs) to ensure the head is fully converged and aligned with the pre-trained features before any backbone updates occur. **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00018}**.

2.  **Stage 2: Partial Fine-Tuning**
    *   **Configuration:** Unfreeze the top blocks of the EfficientNet-B4 backbone (e.g., the final 2-3 stages). This allows the model to adapt to the specific background textures of the camera trap locations while preserving the lower-level feature detectors. **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00015}**.
    *   **Optimizer:** Switch to **AdamW** with a reduced learning rate (e.g., `1e-4`) and standard weight decay (`1e-4`). **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00016}**.
    *   **Scheduler:** Apply a Cosine Annealing scheduler.
    *   **Checkpointing:** Save the best model weights based on the validation Macro F1 score using `copy.deepcopy(model.state_dict())` to prevent implicit overwrites. **Cite {solution_lesson_node_00003}**.
    *   **Loss:** Cross-Entropy Loss weighted inversely by class frequency.

### Evaluation
*   **Inference:** Restore the best model from the deep-copied checkpoint.
*   **Prediction:** Resize test images to **380x380** pixels, normalize, and perform a forward pass. Select the class with the highest probability (`argmax`) for the final submission.
