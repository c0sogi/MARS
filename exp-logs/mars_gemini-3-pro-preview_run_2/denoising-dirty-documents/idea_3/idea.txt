To improve upon the current best solution, I propose a structural evolution from the standard ResUNet to a **Deeply Supervised ResUNet++ (Nested U-Net)**. This strategy addresses the semantic gap between encoder and decoder features while leveraging the successful components (SE blocks, SiLU, Global Residuals) identified in previous lessons.

### Model
**Architecture: Deeply Supervised ResUNet++**
Instead of the standard U-Net used previously, I propose the **UNet++** architecture.
*   **Nested Skip Pathways:** Unlike the simple skip connections of a standard U-Net, UNet++ employs dense, nested skip pathways. These pathways consist of convolutional layers that bridge the semantic gap between the encoder (feature extraction) and decoder (reconstruction) sub-networks. This ensures that the decoder receives features that are semantically similar to its own, facilitating easier fusion and more precise reconstruction of fine text details.
*   **Optimized Building Blocks:** We will retain the highly effective **Residual Blocks** integrated with **Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE)** mechanisms and **SiLU (Swish)** activations (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016). The SE blocks allow the network to adaptively recalibrate channel-wise feature responses, while SiLU provides smooth non-linearity.
*   **Deep Supervision:** The model will be designed with multiple output heads (one at each nesting level of the decoder). During training, the loss will be computed for all outputs and averaged. This technique combats the vanishing gradient problem in the deeper encoder layers and forces the intermediate layers to learn meaningful representations.
*   **Global Residual Learning:** All output heads will be trained to predict the **noise residual** rather than the clean image directly (Cite solution_lesson_node_00013).

### Data
**Preprocessing & Augmentation**
*   **High-Density Patch Sampling:** To satisfy the data hunger of this deeper architecture, we will increase the sampling density to **100 random patches per image** per epoch (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014). The patch size will be maintained at $128 \times 128$ or larger to ensure sufficient receptive field (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
*   **Geometric Augmentation:** We will apply random horizontal flips, vertical flips, and 90-degree rotations to the patches (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010). This exploits the rotational symmetry of the noise and effectively multiplies the dataset size.

### Training
**Optimization Strategy**
*   **Multi-Scale Loss:** The objective function will be the weighted sum of **Mean Squared Error (MSE)** losses calculated at each of the Deep Supervision heads. $L_{total} = \sum_{i=1}^{4} \text{MSE}(\text{Noise}_{pred}^{(i)}, \text{Noise}_{gt})$.
*   **Optimizer:** We will use **AdamW** with an aggressive **weight decay of 1e-2** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015). This strong regularization is crucial for preventing the high-capacity UNet++ from overfitting the small training set.
*   **Scheduler:** A **Cosine Annealing** learning rate scheduler will be employed to gradually reduce the learning rate, allowing the model to settle into a sharp minimum (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014).

### Evaluation
**Inference Pipeline**
*   **Branch Selection:** For the final inference, we will use the output from the deepest (most accurate) branch of the UNet++ ($L_4$), which typically has the highest restoration fidelity.
*   **Tiled Inference:** Test images will be processed using overlapping tiles (e.g., 50% overlap) to prevent boundary artifacts (Cite solution_lesson_node_00013).
*   **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):** We will generate predictions for the original image and its geometric transformations (flips/rotates), averaging the results to further reduce variance and error (Cite solution_lesson_node_00013).
