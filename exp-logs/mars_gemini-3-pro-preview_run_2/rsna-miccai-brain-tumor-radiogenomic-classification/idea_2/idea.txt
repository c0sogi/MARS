Based on the empirical findings from the provided lessons, I propose an optimized solution that evolves the **2.5D Multi-Channel** approach into a **Content-Adaptive, Modality-Aware Pipeline**.

### Model: Modality-Aware 2.5D EfficientNet
We will retain the **EfficientNet-B0** backbone due to its proven ability to establish a learning signal (Cite 00001) and its parameter efficiency suitable for the small dataset size. However, we will introduce a structural innovation in the **Input Stem**:

*   **Grouped Convolutional Initialization:** Instead of a standard convolutional layer over the 12-channel input, we will replace the first layer with a **Grouped Convolution** (`groups=4`).
    *   **Rationale:** The 12-channel input consists of 4 distinct MRI modalities (FLAIR, T1w, T1wCE, T2w). By setting `groups=4`, we force the network to learn independent low-level feature extractors for each modality in the first layer (3 slices $\to$ 8 filters per group) before fusing them in subsequent blocks. This structural prior respects the distinct physical properties of each MRI sequence.
*   **Backbone & Head:** The rest of the network remains a standard EfficientNet-B0, terminating in a Global Average Pooling layer and a single linear output unit (Sigmoid activation) for binary classification.

### Data: Anatomically-Guided Slice Sampling
The previous "fixed percentile" sampling (25%, 50%, 75%) is blind to patient positioning and tumor location. We will replace this with a **Region-of-Interest (ROI) Pipeline** that dynamically selects the most informative slices without increasing the input dimensionality (Cite 00002).

1.  **ROI Identification (Pre-scan):** For each subject, we will iterate through the **FLAIR** modality files (which typically show high contrast for tumors/edema). We will load the raw image data and calculate the **Total Signal Intensity** (sum of pixel values) for each slice.
2.  **Anchor Selection:** We identify the slice index with the maximum signal intensity. This index serves as the "Anatomical Anchor," representing the slice with the largest brain cross-section or most significant tissue activity.
3.  **Context Sampling:** We extract 3 slices relative to this anchor: `{Anchor - stride, Anchor, Anchor + stride}` (where stride $\approx$ 5-10). This ensures the model focuses on the center of the brain/tumor mass rather than the neck or skull vertex.
4.  **Multi-Modal Stacking:** We use these same indices to extract the corresponding slices from the T1w, T1wCE, and T2w folders. These are stacked to form the `(12, H, W)` input tensor.

### Training: Signal-Preserving Optimization
We will adopt a training strategy focused on maximizing signal retention for this weak-label task:

*   **Loss & Optimizer:** Binary Cross Entropy with Logits Loss and the **AdamW** optimizer.
*   **Conservative Augmentation:** We will use **only geometric augmentations** (RandomRotate90, HorizontalFlip, VerticalFlip). We will explicitly **exclude** destructive regularization techniques like CoarseDropout or Cutout, as previous experiments showed these extinguish the weak predictive signal in sparse 2.5D inputs (Cite 00003).
*   **Input Parsimony:** We strictly limit the input to **3 slices per modality** (12 channels total). We will not increase the slice count, as previous attempts to "inflate" the input led to severe overfitting (Cite 00002).

### Evaluation
The inference pipeline mirrors the training data process:
1.  **Scan:** Calculate signal intensity profiles for the test subject's FLAIR scans.
2.  **Select:** Identify the Anchor slice index.
3.  **Construct:** Load the 12-channel tensor centered on that anchor.
4.  **Predict:** Pass the tensor through the trained Modality-Aware EfficientNet to generate the final methylation probability.
