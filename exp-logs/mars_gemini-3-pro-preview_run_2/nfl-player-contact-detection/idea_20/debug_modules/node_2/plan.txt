The issue is that `np.vstack` treats 1-dimensional arrays (shape `(N,)`) as row vectors (shape `(1, N)`). When the `DataLoader` produces a final batch with a different size (e.g., 80) compared to previous batches (e.g., 128), `np.vstack` attempts to stack rows of different widths, causing a `ValueError`. The target labels `y` yielded by the loader are 1D tensors, unlike the model probabilities which are 2D `(N, 1)`.

To fix this, I will replace `np.vstack(all_targets).flatten()` with `np.concatenate(all_targets)`. `np.concatenate` correctly joins 1D arrays end-to-end regardless of their individual lengths, handling variable batch sizes robustly. This applies the principle from **debug_lesson_8** (Cite debug_lesson_8) regarding verifying dimensions before aggregation.
