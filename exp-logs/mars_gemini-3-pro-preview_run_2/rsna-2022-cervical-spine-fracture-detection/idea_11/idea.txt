Based on the comprehensive analysis of the previous experiments and the specific lessons learned, I propose the **Calibrated 2.5D Multi-Scale Network with Soft Spatial Attention**.

This solution structurally evolves the successful **Class-Specific Attention** architecture (Idea 4) by integrating **Multi-Scale Feature Fusion** (from Idea 7) and a novel **Unsupervised Spatial Attention Module**. This design directly addresses the signal dilution caused by Global Average Pooling (Cite solution_lesson_node_00035) while avoiding the instability risks associated with sparse auxiliary supervision on small datasets (Cite solution_lesson_node_00047).

# Proposed Solution: Calibrated 2.5D Multi-Scale Network with Soft Spatial Attention

## Model
The architecture is a hierarchical sequence network designed to maximize feature extraction quality from limited data without relying on external, sparse annotations.

1.  **2.5D Multi-Scale Backbone:**
    *   **Input:** The model processes **2.5D stacks** (slices $z-1, z, z+1$) to capture local volumetric continuity (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014).
    *   **Feature Extraction:** An **EfficientNet-B4** backbone is used. To capture both high-level semantic context and fine-grained fracture details, feature maps are extracted from both the **penultimate (P4)** and **final (P5)** blocks.
    *   **Fusion:** These multi-scale maps are resized and concatenated, creating a rich feature representation for every slice (Cite solution_lesson_node_00035).

2.  **Structural Innovation: Soft Spatial Attention Pooling:**
    *   Instead of using Global Average Pooling (which dilutes focal fracture signals with background noise) or Supervised Attention (which risks overfitting to sparse bounding boxes, Cite solution_lesson_node_00047), the model employs a **Learnable Soft Spatial Attention** module.
    *   A lightweight convolutional block ($1\times1$ Conv $\to$ Sigmoid) predicts a spatial attention map from the fused features.
    *   The slice embedding is computed as the spatially weighted sum of the feature maps. This allows the model to dynamically learn *where* to look within a slice (e.g., focusing on the vertebral body) driven solely by the global classification loss, preserving the integrity of the feature vector.

3.  **Sequence Modeling with Positional Injection:**
    *   **Bi-LSTM:** The sequence of spatially-attended embeddings is processed by a Bidirectional LSTM to model anatomical continuity along the Z-axis (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014).
    *   **Positional Embeddings:** Learnable positional vectors are added to the LSTM outputs. This provides the explicit anatomical references (e.g., top vs. bottom of spine) necessary to distinguish between visually similar vertebrae (Cite solution_lesson_node_00029).

4.  **Class-Specific Attention Aggregation:**
    *   **8 Independent Heads:** The model utilizes 8 distinct Attention Heads (one for each vertebra C1-C7 and Patient Overall). Each head computes its own attention weights over the LSTM sequence to aggregate a study-level embedding.
    *   **Disentanglement:** This ensures that the "C1 Head" focuses on the top of the sequence while the "C7 Head" focuses on the bottom, preventing feature crosstalk (Cite solution_lesson_node_00029).

## Data
1.  **Preprocessing:**
    *   **2.5D Stacking:** Inputs are constructed by stacking 3 adjacent slices ($z-1, z, z+1$).
    *   **High-Density Sampling:** The loader samples a sequence of **96 slices**. This prioritizes Z-axis coverage to ensure small fractures are not skipped, adhering to the lesson that sampling density outweighs model complexity (Cite solution_lesson_node_00032).

2.  **Augmentation:**
    *   **Dataset-Encapsulated Volumetric Augmentation:** Geometric transformations (rotation, shift, scale) are sampled once per study and applied identically to every slice in the stack within the `__getitem__` method. This preserves the physical alignment of the spine required for the sequence model (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008, solution_lesson_node_00010).

## Training
1.  **Calibrated Loss Function:**
    *   The model is trained using **Weighted Multi-Label Logarithmic Loss** applied to the study-level predictions.
    *   **Crucial Calibration:** The loss function will use **NO Positive Class Weighting** (i.e., `pos_weight=1.0`). Since the training set is roughly balanced (~46% positive), adding positive weights forces the model to over-predict fractures, destroying probability calibration and degrading the Log Loss metric (Cite solution_lesson_node_00042). The model will rely on the architectural superiority of Class-Specific Attention to achieve sensitivity.

2.  **Optimization:**
    *   **Gradient Accumulation:** Employed to simulate a stable batch size (e.g., 16 studies) despite the memory footprint of the EfficientNet-B4 backbone and dense sampling (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
    *   **Regularization:** Heavy use of **Stochastic Depth** and **Dropout** within the backbone and heads to prevent overfitting on the small dataset (161 samples).

## Evaluation
1.  **Inference:**
    *   Test studies are loaded, preprocessed into 2.5D stacks (96 slices), and passed through the pipeline.
    *   The **Soft Spatial Attention** module implicitly highlights regions of interest.
    *   The **Class-Specific Attention Heads** aggregate the sequence and output the final, well-calibrated probabilities for the 8 target columns.
