# Proposed Strategy: Heterogeneous Ensemble with Cross-Validation

To maximize performance on this subjective regression task with a limited dataset (~7k images), I propose a structural evolution from a single-model approach to a **Heterogeneous Ensemble** comprising two distinct high-capacity backbones (Swin Transformer and ConvNeXt). This strategy leverages the diversity of inductive biases (Transformer global attention vs. CNN local feature extraction) and utilizes **K-Fold Cross-Validation** to robustly estimate the "Pawpularity" score, mitigating the variance and overfitting risks identified in previous experiments.

### Model
I will implement an ensemble of two distinct architectures. The final prediction will be the average of the outputs from all models in the ensemble.

1.  **Backbone 1: Swin Transformer Large**: I will retain this architecture as it has proven highly effective for this task (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007). Its hierarchical attention mechanism captures global semantic context crucial for assessing "cuteness."
2.  **Backbone 2: ConvNeXt Large**: I will introduce ConvNeXt as a second backbone. As a modernized pure CNN, it offers a distinct inductive bias focused on local textures and shapes, providing complementary features to the Transformer.
3.  **Metadata Fusion & Head**: Both backbones will utilize the successful fusion strategy: concatenating the extracted image embeddings with the 12 binary metadata features. This combined vector will feed into a **Narrow MLP Head** (e.g., Hidden Dim 128) rather than a high-capacity one, to prevent the overfitting and "regression to the mean" observed when the head is too wide (Cite solution_lesson_node_00013).
4.  **Output Activation**: The final layer will use a **Sigmoid activation** to constrain predictions strictly to the [0, 1] range, which has been shown to outperform linear outputs for this bounded regression task (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).

### Data
1.  **Preprocessing**: Images will be resized to 224x224 and normalized using ImageNet statistics.
2.  **Augmentation**: I will apply only minimal augmentation (RandomHorizontalFlip). I will explicitly **exclude** heavy augmentations like Mixup or ColorJitter, as these have been shown to delay convergence and degrade performance in this specific regression regime (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
3.  **Target Scaling**: The `Pawpularity` targets will be scaled from [1, 100] to [0, 1] to align with the Sigmoid output and stabilize gradients (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).

### Training
I will replace the single train-validation split with **5-Fold Cross-Validation**. This means training 5 instances of Swin Large and 5 instances of ConvNeXt Large (10 models total).

1.  **Loss Function**: I will use **BCEWithLogitsLoss**, treating the regression problem as a probability estimation. This aligns with the Sigmoid activation and provides stronger gradients at the boundaries compared to MSE (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).
2.  **Optimization**:
    *   **Differential Learning Rates**: I will apply a lower learning rate to the backbones (to preserve pretrained features) and a higher rate to the randomly initialized heads (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
    *   **Scheduler**: Cosine Annealing with Linear Warmup.
3.  **Regularization**: I will strictly employ **Early Stopping** based on validation loss for each fold. This is critical for large backbones on small data to prevent memorization (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference**: For each sample in the test set, I will generate predictions using all 10 trained models (5 Swin + 5 ConvNeXt).
2.  **Aggregation**: The final submission score will be the arithmetic mean of these 10 predictions.
3.  **Post-Processing**: The averaged value (in [0, 1]) will be rescaled back to the original [1, 100] range.

This approach effectively uses the available compute time (24h) to trade runtime for accuracy and stability, addressing the high variance inherent in predicting subjective scores from small data.
