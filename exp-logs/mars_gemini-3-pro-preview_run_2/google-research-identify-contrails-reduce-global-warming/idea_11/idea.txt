Based on the comprehensive analysis of the accumulated lessons, I propose the **Large-Kernel ConvNeXt U-Net**.

This strategy introduces a structural innovation by shifting from the standard 3x3 convolutional paradigm (ResNet) to a **Large-Kernel CNN architecture (ConvNeXt)**. This directly addresses the persistent challenge of capturing long-range linear dependencies in contrails. Previous attempts to solve this with "Strip Pooling" failed due to axis-alignment bias (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00025`), "Deformable Convolutions" failed due to optimization instability (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00032`), and "Transformers" failed due to weak spatial priors (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00034`). ConvNeXt's 7x7 kernels provide the necessary large receptive field isotropically and stably, preserving the spatial inductive bias required for thin feature segmentation.

### Model
The architecture is a **U-Net** variant that fundamentally modernizes both the encoder and decoder to prioritize large-context spatial mixing.

*   **Backbone (Encoder):** We replace the ResNet18 encoder with **ConvNeXt-Tiny**.
    *   **Structural Innovation:** Unlike ResNet's 3x3 kernels, ConvNeXt utilizes **7x7 Depthwise Convolutions**. This significantly expands the effective receptive field at every layer, allowing the network to "see" the continuity of long contrails across larger spatial extents without the complexity of attention mechanisms.
    *   **Efficiency:** Despite the larger kernel size, the depthwise-separable formulation maintains parameter efficiency comparable to ResNet backbones, fitting within the computational budget.
*   **Decoder (Large-Kernel Refinement):** We replace the standard 3x3 convolution blocks in the decoder with **ConvNeXt Blocks**.
    *   **Rationale:** Standard decoders often fragment thin lines because they rely on small 3x3 kernels to reconstruct features. By employing 7x7 kernels in the upsampling path, we enforce a "linearity prior" during reconstruction, helping to bridge gaps in faint contrails.
    *   **Upsampling:** We retain **Bilinear Upsampling** to avoid checkerboard artifacts (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00008`).
*   **Context & Attention:**
    *   We retain the **ASPP (Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling)** bridge to capture multi-scale global context (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00021`).
    *   We retain **SCSE (Spatial and Channel Squeeze & Excitation)** blocks after each concatenation to adaptively suppress noise in the skip connections (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00021`).

### Data
*   **Input Engineering:** We utilize the robust **6-channel input tensor**:
    *   **Channels 1-3:** "Ash" False Color Composite of the labeled frame ($t=4$), explicitly encoding optical depth and temperature (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00001`).
    *   **Channels 4-6:** The temporal difference ($t=4$ minus $t=3$). We explicitly reject symmetric future differencing ($t+1$) as it adds redundancy (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00006`).
*   **Augmentation:** We employ a strict **Affine Transformation** pipeline (Rotation, Scale, Shift, Flip). We explicitly **exclude** elastic, grid, or optical distortions, as these warp the linear morphology of contrails into curve-like shapes that resemble natural clouds, creating adversarial noise (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00004`).
*   **Dataset:** We train on the **full dataset** (18,673 images) to ensure the model learns to suppress the "long tail" of background clutter (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00002`).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** We employ a **Hybrid Loss**: $L_{total} = L_{BCE} + L_{BatchDice}$.
    *   **Binary Cross Entropy (BCE):** Provides smooth, convex gradients for pixel-level classification.
    *   **Batch-Level Dice:** We compute the Dice score over the **entire flattened batch** (treating the batch as a single volume). This stabilizes gradients and aligns with the global metric, avoiding the instability of sample-level averaging (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00014`) and the degradation caused by pure Dice loss (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00018`).
*   **Optimization:** We use **AdamW** with a Cosine Annealing scheduler.
*   **Batch Size:** We utilize a **moderate batch size** (e.g., 32). We avoid excessively large batch sizes (e.g., 64+) to ensure frequent gradient updates, which aids convergence speed (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00023`).
*   **Duration:** We train for **30 epochs**. We explicitly avoid shortening the training horizon to prevent underfitting on fine-grained details (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00007`).

### Evaluation
*   **Inference Strategy:** We implement **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA)**.
    *   For each test image, we generate predictions for the original, horizontally flipped, vertically flipped, and 180-degree rotated versions.
    *   The four probability maps are averaged before thresholding. This ensemble-like approach smooths out prediction noise and boosts the Global Dice score (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00024`).
*   **Post-Processing:** We apply a standard pixel-level threshold of **0.5**. We explicitly reject "Hard Gating" mechanisms, as they introduce single points of failure (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00009`).
