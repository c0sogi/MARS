Based on the accumulated lessons, I propose the **Deformable ResNet18 U-Net with Adaptive Context**.

This strategy introduces **Deformable Convolutions** as the core structural innovation. This directly addresses the failure of "Strip Pooling" (which struggled with non-axis-aligned features, Cite `solution_lesson_node_00025`) and the limitations of standard convolutions in capturing the arbitrary orientation of thin, linear contrails. By allowing the network to dynamically adjust its receptive field, we can capture the "linearity" of contrails regardless of their angle, without the rigidity of fixed kernels.

### Model
The architecture is a **U-Net** variant that modifies the encoder to be geometrically adaptive.

*   **Backbone (Deformable ResNet18):** We retain the **ResNet18** structure as the base, as it offers the optimal balance of capacity and spatial fidelity (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00002`, `solution_lesson_node_00005`).
    *   **Structural Innovation:** We replace the standard $3 \times 3$ convolutions in the **last two stages** (Layer 3 and Layer 4) of the ResNet encoder with **Deformable Convolutions (DCNv2)**.
    *   **Rationale:** Standard convolutions use a fixed square grid. Deformable convolutions learn offset maps that allow the sampling grid to deform. This enables the encoder to "stretch" its receptive field along the length of a contrail, capturing its linear morphology even if it is diagonal or curved, solving the orientation issue identified in `solution_lesson_node_00025`.
*   **Context Bridge (ASPP):** We retain the **Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling (ASPP)** module at the bottleneck. This ensures we capture multi-scale context (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00021`), but it now operates on the shape-adapted feature maps produced by the Deformable encoder.
*   **Decoder:** We utilize **Bilinear Upsampling** followed by standard convolutions, strictly avoiding Transposed Convolutions to prevent artifacts (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00008`).
*   **Attention:** We retain **SCSE (Spatial and Channel Squeeze & Excitation)** blocks after each concatenation in the decoder to suppress noise in the skip connections (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00021`).
*   **Input Head:** The model accepts the **6-channel input tensor** (Ash Composite + Temporal Difference) (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00001`).

### Data
*   **Input Engineering:** We generate the **6-channel input**:
    *   **Channels 1-3:** "Ash" False Color Composite of the labeled frame ($t=4$).
    *   **Channels 4-6:** Temporal difference ($t=4$ minus $t=3$). We reject symmetric future differencing to avoid redundancy (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00006`).
*   **Augmentation:** We employ a strict **Affine Transformation** pipeline (Rotation, Scale, Shift, Flip). We explicitly **exclude** elastic, grid, or optical distortions, as these warp the linear morphology of contrails into curve-like shapes that resemble natural clouds (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00004`).
*   **Dataset:** We train on the **full dataset** to ensure the model learns to suppress the "long tail" of background clutter (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00002`).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** We employ a **Hybrid Loss**: $L_{total} = L_{BCE} + L_{BatchDice}$.
    *   **BCE:** Provides smooth gradients for pixel-level classification.
    *   **Batch-Level Dice:** We compute the Dice score over the **entire flattened batch** (treating the batch as a single volume). This stabilizes gradients and aligns with the global metric, avoiding the instability of sample-level averaging (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00014`) and the degradation caused by pure Dice loss (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00018`).
*   **Optimization:** We use **AdamW** with a Cosine Annealing scheduler.
*   **Batch Size:** We utilize a **moderate batch size** (e.g., 32) to ensure frequent gradient updates while maintaining statistical stability (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00023`).
*   **Duration:** We train for **30 epochs**. We explicitly avoid shortening the training horizon to prevent underfitting on fine-grained details (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00007`).

### Evaluation
*   **Inference Strategy:** We implement **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA)**.
    *   For each test image, we generate predictions for the original, horizontally flipped, vertically flipped, and 180-degree rotated versions.
    *   The four probability maps are averaged to smooth out prediction noise and improve robustness (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00024`).
*   **Post-Processing:** We apply a standard pixel-level threshold of **0.5**. We explicitly reject "Hard Gating" or "Soft Gating" mechanisms (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00009`).
*   **Formatting:** The final binary mask is run-length encoded.
