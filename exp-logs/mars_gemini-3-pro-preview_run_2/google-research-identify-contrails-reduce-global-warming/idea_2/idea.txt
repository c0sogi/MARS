Based on the accumulated lessons, I propose the **Symmetric Temporal-Difference U-Net++**. This strategy evolves the architecture to better handle fine-grained structures while expanding the physics-informed input engineering to capture the full temporal evolution of contrails.

### Model
I propose replacing the standard U-Net with a **U-Net++ (Nested U-Net)** architecture, utilizing a **ResNet18** encoder.

*   **Architecture (U-Net++):** Standard U-Net architectures often struggle to preserve the fine, thin details of contrails during the upsampling process due to the semantic gap between the encoder and decoder feature maps. U-Net++ introduces nested, dense skip pathways that bridge this gap, allowing the model to capture multi-scale features more effectively. This structural innovation is specifically targeted at improving the segmentation of thin, sparse objects.
*   **Backbone (ResNet18):** I will retain the ResNet18 encoder. As established, deeper backbones like ResNet34 increased computational cost without improving performance on these simple geometric features (Cite Lesson 5). ResNet18 provides the necessary capacity to model the background distribution (Cite Lesson 2) without the inefficiency of larger models.
*   **Input Head:** The first layer will be modified to accept a **9-channel input tensor**, expanding the temporal context window.

### Data
The data preprocessing will be enhanced to provide a **Symmetric Temporal Context**, giving the model "stereo vision" in time to distinguish moving contrails from static background features.

*   **Ash Composite Construction:** For any given time step $t$, we generate a 3-channel "Ash" false-color composite (Red: Band 15-14, Green: Band 14-11, Blue: Band 14). This explicitly encodes the physical properties of optical depth and temperature (Cite Lesson 1).
*   **9-Channel Input Stack:**
    1.  **Channels 1-3 (Current):** The Ash composite of the labeled frame ($t=4$).
    2.  **Channels 4-6 (Past Dynamics):** The difference between the current frame and the previous frame: $Ash(t=4) - Ash(t=3)$. This captures the "arrival" gradient of moving objects (Cite Lesson 1).
    3.  **Channels 7-9 (Future Dynamics):** The difference between the current frame and the *next* frame: $Ash(t=4) - Ash(t=5)$. By utilizing the future frame available in the sequence, we provide a symmetric temporal derivative that highlights the "departure" of the contrail, further suppressing static noise.

### Training
*   **Augmentation:** I will employ a strict **Affine Transformation** strategy (Horizontal/Vertical Flip, Shift, Scale, Rotate). I will explicitly **exclude** elastic or grid distortions. Contrails are morphologically defined by their linearity; non-linear warping destroys this signal and creates adversarial training examples (Cite Lesson 4).
*   **Dataset:** The model will be trained on the **full training dataset** (all 18,673 images). Training on subsets is insufficient for learning to suppress the diverse "long tail" of background cloud formations (Cite Lesson 2).
*   **Loss Function:** A combination of **Dice Loss** and **Binary Cross Entropy (BCE)** will be used to balance pixel-level classification accuracy with the global overlap metric.
*   **Deep Supervision:** U-Net++ allows for deep supervision (computing loss at multiple nested output layers). This will be enabled during training to improve gradient flow and convergence speed.

### Evaluation
*   **Inference:** For each test sample, the 9-channel input (Current, Past Diff, Future Diff) will be generated. The model will predict a probability map.
*   **Post-Processing:** If Deep Supervision is used, the output from the final (highest resolution) node will be selected. A threshold of 0.5 will be applied to generate the binary mask, which will then be run-length encoded.
