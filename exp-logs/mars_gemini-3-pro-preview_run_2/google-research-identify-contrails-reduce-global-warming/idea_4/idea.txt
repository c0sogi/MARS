Based on the comprehensive analysis of the accumulated lessons and previous attempts, I propose the **Soft-Gated Multi-Task ResNet18 U-Net with Global Batch Optimization**.

This strategy fundamentally evolves the "Multi-Task" concept from **Idea 3** by replacing the flawed "Hard Gating" post-processing (which caused irreversible false negatives) with a **Differentiable Soft-Gating** mechanism within the architecture itself. It also corrects the critical loss function error identified in **Lesson 14**, ensuring the model optimizes the global metric rather than unstable sample averages.

### Model
The architecture is a **Multi-Task U-Net** designed to explicitly decouple the problem of "detection" (is there a contrail?) from "segmentation" (where is the contrail?).

*   **Backbone:** We utilize a **ResNet18** encoder. As established in **Lesson 2** and **Lesson 5**, this provides the optimal balance of capacity and spatial fidelity for simple geometric features, avoiding the diminishing returns and training overhead of deeper backbones like ResNet34.
*   **Decoder:** The decoder employs **Bilinear Upsampling** followed by standard convolutions, strictly avoiding Transposed Convolutions to prevent checkerboard artifacts and ensure probability calibration (Cite **Lesson 8**).
*   **Attention Mechanism:** We incorporate **SCSE (Spatial and Channel Squeeze & Excitation)** modules at each decoder stage. This allows the model to adaptively re-weight features, suppressing background noise in the skip connections—a crucial capability for sparse targets.
*   **Structural Innovation: Differentiable Soft-Gating:**
    *   **Classification Head:** A parallel branch attached to the encoder's bottleneck performs Global Average Pooling followed by a Fully Connected layer and Sigmoid activation, producing a scalar probability $P_{cls} \in [0, 1]$ representing the likelihood of *any* contrail presence.
    *   **Integration:** Instead of using $P_{cls}$ as a hard threshold during inference (which failed in **Lesson 9**), we multiply the pixel-wise segmentation map $M_{seg}$ by this scalar **during the forward pass**: $M_{final} = M_{seg} \times P_{cls}$.
    *   **Effect:** This makes the gating mechanism fully differentiable. The model learns to continuously suppress the entire mask for false-positive-prone backgrounds (like cirrus clouds) during training, while the segmentation head can focus exclusively on refining the shape of valid contrails.

### Data
We adhere to the optimal input engineering and augmentation strategies identified in the lessons.

*   **Input Engineering:** We generate the **6-channel input tensor** validated in **Lesson 1** and **Lesson 6**:
    *   **Channels 1-3:** The "Ash" False Color Composite of the labeled frame ($t=4$), capturing physical optical depth and temperature.
    *   **Channels 4-6:** The temporal difference ($t=4$ minus $t=3$). We explicitly reject symmetric future differencing to avoid redundancy (Cite **Lesson 6**).
*   **Augmentation:** We employ a strict **Affine Transformation** pipeline (Rotation, Scale, Shift, Flip). We explicitly **exclude** elastic, grid, or optical distortions, as these warp the linear morphology of contrails into curve-like shapes that resemble natural clouds, creating adversarial noise (Cite **Lesson 4**).
*   **Dataset:** We train on the **full dataset** to capture the "long tail" of background variations (Cite **Lesson 2**).

### Training
The training procedure is overhauled to align the optimization objective with the evaluation metric, addressing the failure of Idea 3.

*   **Loss Function:** We employ a **Batch-Level Global Dice Loss** for the segmentation component (Cite **Lesson 14**).
    *   Instead of averaging the Dice score across samples (which causes instability for empty masks), we flatten the predictions and targets of the *entire batch* into single vectors before computing the Dice score. This effectively treats the batch as one large volume, stabilizing gradients and directly optimizing the Global Dice metric.
    *   The total loss is $L_{total} = L_{BatchDice}(M_{final}, Y) + \lambda L_{BCE}(P_{cls}, y_{cls})$.
*   **Optimization:** We use **AdamW** with a Cosine Annealing scheduler.
*   **Duration:** We train for a minimum of **20 epochs** to ensure the model has sufficient time to converge on fine-grained details, avoiding the underfitting observed when reducing epochs (Cite **Lesson 7**).

### Evaluation
*   **Inference:** The model outputs the soft-gated probability map $M_{final}$.
*   **Post-Processing:** Since the "gating" is already applied structurally, we simply apply a pixel-level threshold of **0.5** to $M_{final}$.
*   **Formatting:** The binary mask is flattened and converted to Run-Length Encoding.
