Based on the comprehensive analysis of the accumulated lessons, I propose the **Macro-Context ConvNeXt U-Net with Deep Supervision**.

This strategy represents a structural evolution from the ResNet-based architectures. It fully embraces the "Linearity Prior" identified in **Lesson 36** by extending the Large-Kernel design philosophy from the encoder to the entire network (Decoder and Bridge). It integrates this with the proven efficiency of Contextual Attention modules (**Lesson 21**) and the stability of Batch-Level optimization (**Lesson 14**).

### Model
The architecture is a **U-Net** variant that replaces the standard 3x3 convolutional paradigm with an **All-Large-Kernel** design to maximize the isotropic receptive field for long, thin contrails.

*   **Backbone (Encoder):** We utilize **ConvNeXt-Tiny** (initialized with ImageNet weights).
    *   **Innovation:** Unlike ResNet (3x3), ConvNeXt uses **7x7 Depthwise Convolutions**. This large kernel size allows the encoder to capture the local continuity of linear features in a single operation, enforcing a strong linearity prior without the optimization instability of Deformable Convolutions (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00032`) or the axis-bias of Strip Pooling (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00025`). (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00036`).
*   **Decoder (Large-Kernel Reconstruction):** We replace the standard 3x3 convolution blocks in the U-Net decoder with **ConvNeXt Blocks (7x7)**.
    *   **Rationale:** Standard decoders often fragment thin lines because they rely on small kernels to reconstruct features from skip connections. By using 7x7 kernels in the upsampling path, we preserve the spatial integrity and connectivity of contrails during reconstruction.
    *   **Upsampling:** We use **Bilinear Upsampling** to avoid checkerboard artifacts (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00008`).
*   **Context Bridge:** We retain the **Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling (ASPP)** module at the bottleneck to capture multi-scale global context (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00021`).
*   **Attention:** We integrate **SCSE (Spatial and Channel Squeeze & Excitation)** blocks after every concatenation step in the decoder. This adaptively suppresses noise in the skip connections, improving data efficiency (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00021`).
*   **Deep Supervision:** We attach auxiliary segmentation heads at the decoder's Stride 8 and Stride 4 levels.
    *   **Rationale:** This addresses the "vanishing gradient" problem for fine-grained features, forcing the intermediate layers to learn semantically meaningful representations of the sparse contrails, distinct from the "Cascaded" approach which failed due to sequential instability (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00035`).

### Data
*   **Input Engineering:** We generate the **6-channel input tensor**:
    *   **Channels 1-3:** "Ash" False Color Composite of the labeled frame ($t=4$), explicitly encoding optical depth and temperature (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00001`).
    *   **Channels 4-6:** The temporal difference ($t=4$ minus $t=3$). We reject symmetric future differencing ($t+1$) to avoid redundancy (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00006`).
*   **Augmentation:** We employ a strict **Affine Transformation** pipeline (Rotation, Scale, Shift, Flip). We explicitly **exclude** elastic, grid, or optical distortions, as these warp the linear morphology of contrails into curve-like shapes that resemble natural clouds (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00004`).
*   **Dataset:** We train on the **full dataset** (18,673 images) to ensure the model learns to suppress the "long tail" of background clutter (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00002`).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** We employ a **Deeply Supervised Hybrid Loss**:
    *   $L_{total} = L_{main} + 0.4 \cdot L_{aux1} + 0.2 \cdot L_{aux2}$
    *   Each component is $L = L_{BCE} + L_{BatchDice}$.
    *   **Batch-Level Dice:** We compute the Dice score over the **entire flattened batch** (treating the batch as a single volume). This stabilizes gradients and aligns with the global metric, avoiding the instability of sample-level averaging (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00014`) and the degradation caused by pure Dice loss (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00018`).
*   **Optimization:** We use **AdamW** with a Cosine Annealing scheduler.
*   **Batch Size:** We utilize a **moderate batch size** (e.g., 32). We avoid excessively large batch sizes (e.g., 64+) to ensure frequent gradient updates, which aids convergence speed (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00023`).
*   **Duration:** We train for **30 epochs**. We explicitly avoid shortening the training horizon to prevent underfitting on fine-grained details (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00007`).

### Evaluation
*   **Inference Strategy:** We implement **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA)**.
    *   For each test image, we generate predictions for the original, horizontally flipped, vertically flipped, and 180-degree rotated versions.
    *   The four probability maps are averaged before thresholding. This ensemble-like approach smooths out prediction noise and boosts the Global Dice score (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00024`).
*   **Post-Processing:** We apply a standard pixel-level threshold of **0.5**. We explicitly reject "Hard Gating" mechanisms (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00009`).
