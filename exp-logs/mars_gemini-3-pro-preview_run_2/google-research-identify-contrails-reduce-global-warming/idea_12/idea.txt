Based on the accumulated lessons and the empirical success of large-kernel architectures in early trials, I propose the **Isotropic Large-Kernel ConvNeXt U-Net with Global Context and Attention**.

This strategy structurally evolves the "Current Best" baseline by transitioning from a standard CNN (ResNet) to a modern Large-Kernel CNN (ConvNeXt). It synthesizes the "Linearity Prior" success of Idea 11 (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00036`) with the "Global Context" and "Data Efficiency" successes of Idea 5 (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00021`), while strictly adhering to the robust training protocols established in Idea 6 (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00007`, `solution_lesson_node_00014`).

### Model
The architecture is a **U-Net** variant designed to maximize the receptive field isotropically at every stage, preventing the fragmentation of thin, linear features.

*   **Backbone (Encoder):** We utilize **ConvNeXt-Tiny** as the encoder.
    *   **Structural Innovation:** Unlike ResNet18 (3x3 kernels), ConvNeXt uses **7x7 Depthwise Convolutions**. This large kernel size allows the network to capture the local continuity of contrails in a single operation, enforcing a strong linearity prior without the optimization instability of Deformable Convolutions (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00032`) or the axis-alignment bias of Strip Pooling (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00025`, `solution_lesson_node_00036`).
*   **Context Bridge:** We retain the **Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling (ASPP)** module at the bottleneck. This ensures the model captures multi-scale global context, which is critical for distinguishing contrails from background cirrus (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00021`).
*   **Decoder (Large-Kernel Refinement):** We replace standard convolutional blocks in the decoder with **ConvNeXt Blocks (7x7 kernels)**.
    *   **Rationale:** Standard decoders often rely on small 3x3 kernels, which can break the connectivity of thin lines during upsampling. By maintaining 7x7 kernels in the decoder, we preserve the spatial integrity of the contrail structure during reconstruction (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00036`).
    *   **Upsampling:** We use **Bilinear Upsampling** to avoid checkerboard artifacts (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00008`).
*   **Attention:** We integrate **SCSE (Spatial and Channel Squeeze & Excitation)** blocks after every concatenation step in the decoder. This adaptively suppresses noise in the skip connections, significantly improving data efficiency (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00021`).
*   **Input Head:** The model accepts the **6-channel input tensor** (Ash Composite + Temporal Difference) (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00001`).

### Data
*   **Input Engineering:** We generate the **6-channel input**:
    *   **Channels 1-3:** "Ash" False Color Composite of the labeled frame ($t=4$), explicitly encoding optical depth and temperature.
    *   **Channels 4-6:** The temporal difference ($t=4$ minus $t=3$). We explicitly reject symmetric future differencing ($t+1$) to avoid redundancy (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00006`).
*   **Augmentation:** We employ a strict **Affine Transformation** pipeline (Rotation, Scale, Shift, Flip). We explicitly **exclude** elastic, grid, or optical distortions, as these warp the linear morphology of contrails into curve-like shapes that resemble natural clouds, creating adversarial noise (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00004`).
*   **Dataset:** We train on the **full dataset** (18,673 images) to ensure the model learns to suppress the "long tail" of background clutter (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00002`).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** We employ a **Hybrid Loss**: $L_{total} = L_{BCE} + L_{BatchDice}$.
    *   **Binary Cross Entropy (BCE):** Provides smooth, convex gradients for pixel-level classification.
    *   **Batch-Level Dice:** We compute the Dice score over the **entire flattened batch** (treating the batch as a single volume). This stabilizes gradients and aligns with the global metric, avoiding the instability of sample-level averaging (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00014`) and the degradation caused by pure Dice loss (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00018`).
*   **Optimization:** We use **AdamW** with a Cosine Annealing scheduler.
*   **Batch Size:** We utilize a **moderate batch size** (e.g., 32). We avoid excessively large batch sizes (e.g., 64+) to ensure frequent gradient updates, which aids convergence speed (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00023`).
*   **Duration:** We train for **30 epochs**. We explicitly avoid shortening the training horizon to prevent underfitting on fine-grained details (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00007`).

### Evaluation
*   **Inference Strategy:** We implement **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA)**.
    *   For each test image, we generate predictions for the original, horizontally flipped, vertically flipped, and 180-degree rotated versions.
    *   The four probability maps are averaged before thresholding. This ensemble-like approach smooths out prediction noise and boosts the Global Dice score (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00024`).
*   **Post-Processing:** We apply a standard pixel-level threshold of **0.5**. We explicitly reject "Hard Gating" or "Soft Gating" mechanisms, as they introduce single points of failure (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00009`).
*   **Formatting:** The final binary mask is run-length encoded.
