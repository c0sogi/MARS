Based on the comprehensive analysis of the accumulated lessons, I propose the **Hybrid ResNet18-Transformer U-Net**.

This strategy introduces a **Transformer-based Bottleneck** to the robust ResNet18 U-Net baseline. This structural innovation directly resolves the conflict between the need for global context (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00021`) and the failure of geometric-specific modules like Strip Pooling (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00025`) and Deformable Convolutions (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00032`). By leveraging Self-Attention at the deepest abstraction level, the model can capture the global linearity of contrails regardless of orientation, without the optimization instability of deformable offsets.

### Model
The architecture is a **Hybrid U-Net** that fuses a Convolutional Encoder with a Transformer Bottleneck.

*   **Backbone (Encoder):** We retain the **ResNet18** encoder. As established, this provides the optimal balance of capacity and spatial fidelity, avoiding the diminishing returns of deeper backbones (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00005`) and the weakness of lightweight ones (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00002`).
*   **Structural Innovation: Transformer Bottleneck:** We replace the convolutional bottleneck (or ASPP) with a **Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA) Block**.
    *   The 8x8 feature map from the ResNet18 encoder is flattened into a sequence of 64 tokens.
    *   We add learnable **Positional Embeddings** to preserve spatial information.
    *   We apply a standard Transformer Encoder layer (MHSA + Feed Forward Network).
    *   **Rationale:** Unlike ASPP (fixed dilation) or Strip Pooling (axis-aligned), Self-Attention allows every pixel to attend to every other pixel globally. This enables the model to connect disjoint segments of faint contrails and recognize linear structures at *any* angle, solving the orientation issues of previous attempts (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00025`) while maintaining a stable optimization landscape (unlike Deformable Convolutions, Cite `solution_lesson_node_00032`).
*   **Decoder:** We utilize **Bilinear Upsampling** followed by standard convolutions, strictly avoiding Transposed Convolutions to prevent checkerboard artifacts (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00008`).
*   **Attention:** We retain **SCSE (Spatial and Channel Squeeze & Excitation)** blocks after each concatenation step in the decoder to adaptively suppress noise in the skip connections (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00021`).
*   **Input Head:** The model accepts the **6-channel input tensor** (Ash Composite + Temporal Difference) (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00001`).

### Data
*   **Input Engineering:** We generate the effective 6-channel input:
    *   **Channels 1-3:** "Ash" False Color Composite of the labeled frame ($t=4$), explicitly encoding optical depth and temperature.
    *   **Channels 4-6:** The temporal difference ($t=4$ minus $t=3$). We reject symmetric future differencing ($t+1$) as it adds redundancy (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00006`).
*   **Augmentation:** We employ a strict **Affine Transformation** pipeline (Rotation, Scale, Shift, Flip). We explicitly **exclude** elastic, grid, or optical distortions, as these warp the linear morphology of contrails into curve-like shapes that resemble natural clouds, creating adversarial noise (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00004`).
*   **Dataset:** We train on the **full dataset** (18,673 images) to ensure the model learns to suppress the "long tail" of background clutter (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00002`).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** We employ a **Hybrid Loss**: $L_{total} = L_{BCE} + L_{BatchDice}$.
    *   **Binary Cross Entropy (BCE):** Included to provide smooth, convex gradients for pixel-level classification.
    *   **Batch-Level Dice:** We compute the Dice score over the **entire flattened batch** (treating the batch as a single volume). This stabilizes gradients and aligns with the global metric, avoiding the instability of sample-level averaging (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00014`) and the degradation caused by pure Dice loss (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00018`).
*   **Optimization:** We use **AdamW** with a Cosine Annealing scheduler.
*   **Batch Size:** We utilize a **moderate batch size** (e.g., 32). We avoid excessively large batch sizes (e.g., 64+) to ensure frequent gradient updates, which aids convergence speed (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00023`).
*   **Duration:** We train for **30 epochs**. We explicitly avoid shortening the training horizon to prevent underfitting on fine-grained details (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00007`).

### Evaluation
*   **Inference Strategy:** We implement **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA)**.
    *   For each test image, we generate predictions for the original, horizontally flipped, vertically flipped, and 180-degree rotated versions.
    *   The four probability maps are averaged before thresholding. This ensemble-like approach smooths out prediction noise and boosts the Global Dice score (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00024`).
*   **Post-Processing:** We apply a standard pixel-level threshold of **0.5**. We explicitly reject "Hard Gating" or "Soft Gating" mechanisms, as they introduce single points of failure that irreversibly reduce recall (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00009`).
*   **Formatting:** The final binary mask is run-length encoded.
