Based on the comprehensive analysis of the accumulated lessons, I propose the **Dual-Stream Large-Kernel U-Net with Cross-Modal Fusion**.

This strategy represents a structural evolution of the "Large-Kernel" approach (Idea 11) and the "Decoupled Input" concept (Idea 15/16). It addresses the limitation of early fusion (stacking inputs) where weak temporal signals are often overwhelmed by strong static background textures in the first convolutional layers. By architecturally enforcing the separation of "Appearance" and "Motion" processing, we allow the network to learn specialized features for each domain before fusing them, while retaining the proven benefits of large kernels and sequential refinement.

### Model
The architecture is a **Dual-Encoder U-Net** that processes spectral and temporal data in parallel streams before fusing them for reconstruction.

*   **Backbone (Dual Encoders):** We utilize two parallel **ConvNeXt-Tiny** encoders (initialized with ImageNet weights).
    *   **Stream A (Static/Spectral):** Processes the "Ash" False Color Composite.
    *   **Stream B (Dynamic/Temporal):** Processes the Raw Band Differences.
    *   **Rationale:** This structural decoupling ensures that the "Linearity Prior" of the 7x7 kernels (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00036`) is applied independently to the static cloud structures and the dynamic cooling signatures. This prevents the strong gradients of the background clouds from washing out the subtle, sparse motion signals of contrails in the early layers, a flaw in simple 6-channel stacking (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00045`).
*   **Cross-Modal Feature Fusion:**
    *   At each downsampling stage (Stride 4, 8, 16, 32), the feature maps from the Dynamic Stream are fused into the Static Stream.
    *   **Mechanism:** Concatenation followed by a $1\times1$ convolution to restore the channel dimension. This injects motion context into the semantic appearance features hierarchically.
*   **Context Bridge:** We retain the **Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling (ASPP)** module at the bottleneck of the fused stream to capture multi-scale global context (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00021`).
*   **Decoder (Sequential Refinement):**
    *   We employ a single **Sequential U-Net Decoder** extending from the fused bottleneck.
    *   We utilize **ConvNeXt Blocks (7x7)** in the decoder to preserve the linearity of features during upsampling (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00036`).
    *   We explicitly reject "Pyramid Fusion" heads in favor of sequential refinement to preserve high-frequency spatial details (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00046`).
    *   **Skip Connections:** We connect the *Fused* encoder features to the decoder, ensuring the reconstruction has access to both appearance and motion context.
*   **Attention:** We retain **SCSE (Spatial and Channel Squeeze & Excitation)** blocks after each concatenation in the decoder to adaptively suppress noise (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00021`).

### Data
*   **Input Engineering (Decoupled Streams):**
    *   **Stream A Input (3 Channels):** "Ash" False Color Composite of the labeled frame ($t=4$). This encodes physical properties (optical depth, temperature) using the standard formula (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00001`).
    *   **Stream B Input (3 Channels):** **Raw Band Differences** ($t=4$ minus $t=3$) for Bands 11, 14, and 15. We explicitly use raw values to preserve the dynamic range of the cooling signal, avoiding the information loss caused by differencing processed composites (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00045`).
*   **Augmentation:** We employ a strict **Affine Transformation** pipeline (Rotation, Scale, Shift, Flip). We explicitly **exclude** elastic, grid, or optical distortions (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00004`).
*   **Dataset:** We train on the **full dataset** (18,673 images) to ensure the model learns to suppress the "long tail" of background clutter (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00002`).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** We employ a **Hybrid Loss**: $L_{total} = L_{BCE} + L_{BatchDice}$.
    *   **Binary Cross Entropy (BCE):** Provides smooth gradients for pixel-level classification.
    *   **Batch-Level Dice:** We compute the Dice score over the **entire flattened batch** (treating the batch as a single volume). This stabilizes gradients and aligns with the global metric (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00014`, `solution_lesson_node_00018`).
    *   We apply this loss only to the final output, avoiding Deep Supervision (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00040`).
*   **Optimization:** We use **AdamW** with a Cosine Annealing scheduler.
*   **Batch Size:** We utilize a **Batch Size of 32**. Despite the dual encoder, ConvNeXt-Tiny is efficient enough to fit this batch size on the A100 GPU, ensuring we maintain the statistical stability required for the Batch Dice loss (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00043`).
*   **Duration:** We train for **30 epochs**. We explicitly avoid shortening the training horizon to prevent underfitting (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00007`).

### Evaluation
*   **Inference Strategy:** We implement **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA)**.
    *   Predictions are generated for the original, horizontally flipped, vertically flipped, and 180-degree rotated versions.
    *   The maps are averaged before thresholding to smooth out prediction noise (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00024`).
*   **Post-Processing:** We apply a standard pixel-level threshold of **0.5**. We explicitly reject "Hard Gating" mechanisms (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00009`).
