Based on the analysis of the previous ideas and the empirical lessons learned, I propose the following optimized strategy. This solution structurally evolves the approach by transitioning from a homogeneous single-architecture ensemble to a **Heterogeneous Ensemble** to capture diverse feature representations, while enhancing the classification head with **Multi-Sample Dropout** to accelerate convergence and robustness on the small dataset.

# Model
I propose a **Heterogeneous Ensemble** consisting of two distinct high-capacity backbones.
*   **Structural Innovation (Backbone Diversity):**
    *   **Model A: EfficientNetV2-L:** We will retain this architecture from the previous best iteration to exploit its successful compound scaling and Fused-MBConv blocks `Cite solution_lesson_node_00008`.
    *   **Model B: ConvNeXt-Base:** We will introduce the ConvNeXt architecture. Unlike EfficientNet, ConvNeXt modernizes the standard ResNet design with Transformer-style blocks (large kernels, layer normalization) while remaining purely convolutional. This provides a distinct inductive bias, allowing the ensemble to capture feature patterns that EfficientNet might miss.
*   **Head Design (Multi-Sample Dropout):** Both backbones will utilize the successful **Multi-Label Decomposition** (outputting 2 binary logits: "Is Rust" and "Is Scab") `Cite solution_lesson_node_00020`. However, we will upgrade the regularization within the head by implementing **Multi-Sample Dropout**. Instead of a single dropout layer, the features will pass through multiple parallel dropout layers (e.g., 5) with different masks. The outputs will be classified separately, and the loss will be computed as the average of these branches. This technique accelerates training and improves generalization on small datasets by creating an implicit ensemble within the network itself.

# Training
*   **Strategy:** We will implement **5-Fold Cross-Validation** for *both* architectures, resulting in a total of 10 trained models.
*   **Loss Function:** We will use **Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) Loss** with **Positive Class Weights**. The weights will be calculated based on the inverse frequency of "Rust" and "Scab" in the training set to explicitly handle the class imbalance `Cite solution_lesson_node_00001`.
*   **Optimization:**
    *   **Optimizer:** **AdamW** will be used for both models.
    *   **Batch Size:** We will strictly enforce a batch size of at least **16** for both architectures to ensure stable batch normalization statistics `Cite solution_lesson_node_00014`.
    *   **Reproducibility:** We will use a `worker_init_fn` to seed DataLoaders, ensuring deterministic data presentation `Cite solution_lesson_node_00009`.
    *   **Regularization:** We will apply **Label Smoothing** (e.g., 0.05) to the binary targets to prevent overconfidence in these high-capacity models `Cite solution_lesson_node_00002`.

# Evaluation
*   **Inference:** We will apply **Test Time Augmentation (TTA)** (horizontal flip) for all 10 models (5 EffNetV2-L + 5 ConvNeXt-Base) `Cite solution_lesson_node_00002`.
*   **Ensemble Aggregation:** The final "Rust" and "Scab" probabilities will be the arithmetic mean of the predictions from all 10 models.
*   **Reconstruction:** We will mathematically reconstruct the required 4-class probabilities from the aggregated binary scores ($P_r, P_s$) using the decomposition logic `Cite solution_lesson_node_00020`:
    *   `Healthy`: $(1 - P_r) \times (1 - P_s)$
    *   `Rust`: $P_r \times (1 - P_s)$
    *   `Scab`: $(1 - P_r) \times P_s$
    *   `Multiple`: $P_r \times P_s$
