To address the high stochastic variance and data scarcity identified in the previous experiments, I propose a solution that structurally evolves from a single-model training loop to a **Cross-Validation Ensemble** pipeline, utilizing a more parameter-efficient backbone with specialized pooling for sparse audio events.

# Model
**Architecture: EfficientNet with Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling**
*   **Backbone:** Replace the ResNet18 backbone with **EfficientNet-B0**. EfficientNet scales depth, width, and resolution uniformly and is generally more parameter-efficient and robust for transfer learning on small datasets compared to ResNet.
*   **Pooling Layer Innovation:** Replace the standard Global Average Pooling (GAP) with **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling**.
    *   *Rationale:* Bird calls are often short, sparse events within the 10-second clip. Standard Average Pooling dilutes these high-energy features with the surrounding silence or background noise. GeM pooling introduces a learnable parameter $p$ that interpolates between Max Pooling (focusing on the most prominent feature) and Average Pooling, allowing the model to automatically tune its focus on the active regions of the spectrogram.
*   **Head:** The output layer remains a fully connected layer with 19 units and Sigmoid activation, maintaining the **Binary Relevance** formulation (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006) by treating each class as an independent Bernoulli trial.

# Data
**Preprocessing & Augmentation**
*   **Input:** Continue using **Filtered Spectrograms** as the primary input, as they preserve critical time-frequency topology superior to tabular features (Cite solution_lesson_node_00012).
*   **Augmentation:** Retain **Mixup Augmentation** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00013) to smooth decision boundaries and prevent memorization of the small training set. Additionally, incorporate **SpecAugment** (time and frequency masking) to simulate partial signal loss and improve robustness.
*   **Splitting Strategy:** Abandon the single train/validation split. Instead, implement **Stratified K-Fold Cross-Validation** (e.g., K=5). This ensures that every sample in the limited dataset (206 recordings) is used for both training and validation across different folds, directly addressing the unreliability of single training runs (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).

# Training
**Procedure: K-Fold Ensemble Training**
*   **Loop:** The training process will iterate through 5 folds. For each fold:
    1.  Initialize a fresh EfficientNet-B0 model (pretrained on ImageNet).
    2.  Train on $K-1$ folds and validate on the remaining fold.
    3.  Save the model weights that achieve the highest validation ROC AUC.
*   **Loss Function:** Use **Binary Cross Entropy (BCE) with Logits Loss**, optionally weighted by class frequency to handle imbalance.
*   **Optimizer:** Use **AdamW** with a **Cosine Annealing** learning rate scheduler to converge efficiently.

# Evaluation
**Inference: Bagging Ensemble**
*   **Process:** For the test set, generate predictions using *all* 5 trained models from the cross-validation loop.
*   **Aggregation:** Average the probability outputs of the 5 models.
    *   *Rationale:* Ensembling averages out the stochastic noise inherent in training deep models on small datasets, stabilizing the predictions and improving generalization significantly compared to any single model (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).
*   **Submission:** Flatten the averaged probabilities into the required `Id,Probability` format.
