To establish a robust and highly efficient baseline, I propose a **Sparse N-gram Linear Regression** approach. This method prioritizes computational speed and interpretability, stripping away the complexity of neural architectures and ensemble methods while leveraging the high-dimensional lexical information present in the essays.

# Model
The architecture consists of a streamlined two-stage pipeline:
1.  **Feature Extraction**: A **TF-IDF (Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency) Vectorizer** transforms the raw text into high-dimensional, sparse numerical vectors. Unlike Model 2, which compresses these vectors via SVD, this approach retains the full sparse representation to capture precise lexical usage. The vectorizer will include unigrams, bigrams, and potentially trigrams to capture local context and phraseology.
2.  **Regressor**: A **Ridge Regression** model (Linear Least Squares with L2 regularization) is applied directly to the sparse feature vectors. This linear model learns a weight for each n-gram, effectively determining which words and phrases positively or negatively correlate with higher essay scores. This avoids the non-linearity of Tree-based models (Model 2) and the heavy compute of Transformers (Models 1, 3, 4).

# Data
Preprocessing will be kept minimal to maximize throughput:
1.  **Text Cleaning**: All text will be lowercased to ensure case-insensitivity. No aggressive stemming or lemmatization will be applied, as specific word forms (e.g., "run" vs. "running") can indicate grammatical complexity.
2.  **Tokenization**: The text will be tokenized into n-grams during the TF-IDF vectorization process.
3.  **Target Formatting**: The integer `score` labels (1-6) will be cast to floating-point numbers to allow for regression training.

# Training
The training process focuses on solving a convex optimization problem:
1.  **Objective**: Minimize the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the predicted continuous scores and the ground truth, subject to an L2 penalty on the weights to prevent overfitting to rare n-grams.
2.  **Optimization**: The model will be fitted using a sparse solver (e.g., LSQR or conjugate gradient), which is extremely fast for high-dimensional sparse matrices compared to gradient descent used in neural networks.
3.  **Hyperparameter Tuning**: A simple K-Fold Cross-Validation will be used to select the optimal regularization strength (`alpha`). The selection criterion will be the Quadratic Weighted Kappa (QWK) on the validation fold.

# Evaluation
1.  **Inference**: The pipeline will generate continuous score predictions for the test set.
2.  **Post-processing**:
    *   **Clipping**: Predictions will be constrained to the range [1, 6] to handle potential outliers.
    *   **Rounding**: The continuous values will be rounded to the nearest integer to produce the final discrete class labels required for the QWK metric.
3.  **Submission**: The resulting integer scores will be formatted into the required CSV structure.
