Based on the lessons learned, particularly the critical importance of data volume (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008) and the effectiveness of hierarchical auxiliary tasks (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001), I propose the following optimized strategy.

### Model: Hierarchical Multi-Task ConvNeXt-Tiny

**Architecture Design**
*   **Backbone (Structural Innovation):** I propose replacing the ResNet-18 backbone with **ConvNeXt-Tiny**. While ResNet-18 was efficient, its feature extraction capacity is limited for distinguishing 64,500 fine-grained classes. ConvNeXt modernizes standard CNNs with design choices from Vision Transformers (e.g., larger kernel sizes, layer normalization), offering superior representation learning while remaining purely convolutional and computationally feasible on the A100 GPU.
*   **Heads (Strategic Retention):** I will retain the **Multi-Task Learning** structure with three parallel linear heads: **Species**, **Family**, and **Order** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001). This ensures that the deeper ConvNeXt backbone stabilizes its training by leveraging gradients from the abundant super-category labels, preventing overfitting on the tail classes during the initial feature learning phase.

### Data

**Preprocessing**
*   **Input Pipeline:** I will utilize the **full training dataset** (approx. 2.3M images) rather than a subsample. As demonstrated, maximizing the number of unique training examples is the single most effective driver of performance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004, Cite solution_lesson_node_00008).
*   **Resolution:** Images will be resized to **224x224** to maintain high throughput and allow for large batch sizes (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
*   **Augmentation:** Standard augmentations (RandomResizedCrop, HorizontalFlip) will be applied. Complex augmentations like CutMix will be avoided to prioritize convergence speed (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008).

### Training

**Procedure: Decoupled Two-Stage Learning**
To address the Macro F1 metric (which weighs rare classes equally to frequent ones) without sacrificing feature quality, I propose a **Decoupled Two-Stage Training** strategy.

*   **Stage 1: Representation Learning (Instance-Balanced)**
    *   **Objective:** Train the backbone and all heads to learn robust visual features.
    *   **Sampling:** Use standard **Instance-Balanced Sampling** (random shuffle). This allows the model to learn high-quality features primarily from the "head" (frequent) classes, which provide the most varied visual signals.
    *   **Optimization:** Use **CrossEntropy with Label Smoothing (0.1)** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004) to prevent overconfidence. Enable **Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP)** and maximize the batch size to ensure the model sees the entire dataset within the compute budget (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002). Use a **One-Cycle Learning Rate** scheduler for rapid convergence.
    *   **Duration:** This stage will consume approximately 80% of the training time (e.g., 3-4 epochs).

*   **Stage 2: Classifier Re-balancing (Class-Balanced)**
    *   **Objective:** Adjust the decision boundaries of the classification heads to treat rare species fairly.
    *   **Technique:** **Freeze the ConvNeXt backbone**. Fine-tune *only* the three classification heads.
    *   **Sampling:** Switch to **Class-Balanced Sampling** (oversampling rare classes). Since the backbone is frozen, the risk of overfitting features to repeated rare examples is eliminated. This step explicitly optimizes the Macro F1 score by calibrating the classifier weights for the long tail.
    *   **Duration:** This stage is computationally cheap and will run for a short duration (e.g., 1-2 epochs).

### Evaluation

**Inference Process**
*   **Prediction:** Pass the test images through the network.
*   **Output:** Use the logits from the fine-tuned **Species Head** to determine the predicted `category_id`. The auxiliary Family and Order heads are discarded during inference.
