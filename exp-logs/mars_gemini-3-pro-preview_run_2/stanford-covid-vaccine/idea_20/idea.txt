Based on the comprehensive analysis of the provided lessons and the progression of previous ideas, I propose the **Scale-Partitioned Dense Hybrid Network**.

This strategy optimizes the state-of-the-art "Dense-Context" architecture (Idea 8) by refining the **Latent Interaction Mechanism**. It addresses the "information smashing" flaw of the single-bottleneck design (Idea 8) and the "noise propagation" flaw of the full-gather design (Idea 16) by introducing a **Scale-Partitioned Compression** scheme. This preserves the semantic distinction between the partner's local motifs and global context while strictly adhering to parsimony and regularization constraints.

# Model

1.  **Input Representation (Partner-Aware):**
    *   **Standard Features:** We utilize **One-Hot Encoding** for `sequence`, `structure`, and `predicted_loop_type` (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).
    *   **Explicit Partner Identity:** We **retain** the explicit injection of the **Partner Base Identity** (one-hot vector of the paired base) at the input level. As established in **Lesson 00052**, removing this input cripples the encoder's ability to form basic chemical representations, correcting the flaw of Idea 14.

2.  **Backbone (Dense Dilated TCN):**
    *   **Architecture:** A stack of **Single-Layer Dilated Residual Blocks** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00011) utilizing **Dense Connections** (concatenating outputs of all prior blocks). This preserves the full multi-scale history required for complex interaction modeling (Cite solution_lesson_node_00047).
    *   **Configuration:** We employ exponentially increasing dilation rates ($d=1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32$) to ensure global receptive field coverage (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
    *   **Capacity & Regularization:** We strictly set the **Growth Rate / Channel Width** to **64** to avoid capacity bottlenecks (Cite solution_lesson_node_00037) and apply **Dropout (0.1)** within every block to prevent overfitting (Cite solution_lesson_node_00038).
    *   **Convolution Type:** We use **Standard Convolutions** throughout (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).
    *   **Output:** This produces a high-dimensional tensor $H_{dense}$ containing the concatenated outputs of all 6 dilated layers.

3.  **Innovation: Scale-Partitioned Latent Gather:**
    *   **Concept:** A single compression layer (as in Idea 8) mixes low-dilation (local) and high-dilation (global) features indiscriminately. This makes it difficult for the model to specifically query "What is the *local* motif of my partner?" vs "What is the *global* fold of my partner?".
    *   **Mechanism:**
        *   **Partitioning:** We split the backbone output $H_{dense}$ into two groups: **Local Scale** (Layers with $d=1, 2, 4$) and **Global Scale** (Layers with $d=8, 16, 32$).
        *   **Independent Compression:** We apply separate **1x1 Convolutions** to each group, compressing them to compact vectors $Z_{local}$ and $Z_{global}$ (e.g., 32 channels each). This adheres to **Lesson 00062** (Compress before gather) while preserving scale semantics.
        *   **Gather:** For every position $i$, we gather $Z_{local}$ and $Z_{global}$ from its paired partner $j$ (Cite solution_lesson_node_00031).
        *   **Fusion:** We concatenate the local history $H_{dense}[i]$ with the gathered partner features $[Z_{local}[j], Z_{global}[j]]$. We use simple concatenation (Cite solution_lesson_node_00066) rather than arithmetic operations (Cite solution_lesson_node_00067).

4.  **Global Aggregation (BiGRU):**
    *   The fused features are processed by a **Bidirectional GRU** to capture global constraints (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
    *   **Constraint:** Hidden size is set to `input_dim // 2` to ensure the output dimension matches the input, preventing parameter bloat (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).

5.  **Output Head:**
    *   Linear projection to the 5 target variables.

# Training

1.  **Loss Function (Masked Optimization):**
    *   Use **MCRMSE** (Mean Columnwise Root Mean Squared Error).
    *   **Strategy:** Calculate loss **only on the three scored columns** (`reactivity`, `deg_Mg_pH10`, `deg_Mg_50C`).
    *   **Rationale:** We strictly adhere to **Lesson 00016**, masking out auxiliary targets (`deg_pH10`, `deg_50C`) to prevent negative transfer and align the optimization objective directly with the competition metric.

2.  **Optimizer:**
    *   Use **AdamW** with a learning rate of 1e-3 and a `ReduceLROnPlateau` scheduler.
