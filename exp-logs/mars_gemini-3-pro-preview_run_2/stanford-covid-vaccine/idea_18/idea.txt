Based on the comprehensive analysis of the provided lessons and the progression of previous ideas, I propose the **Interaction-Enriched Dense Hybrid Network**. This strategy optimizes the state-of-the-art "Dense-Context" architecture (Idea 8) by introducing a parameter-free **Interaction Enrichment Module**. This addresses the limitation of simple concatenation (which relies on downstream linear layers to infer non-linear physical relationships) by explicitly injecting element-wise similarity and difference features, while strictly adhering to capacity and parsimony constraints.

# Model

The architecture is a **Dense Dilated TCN** augmented with a **Physically-Motivated Fusion Layer**.

1.  **Input Representation (Partner-Aware):**
    *   **Standard Features:** We utilize **One-Hot Encoding** for `sequence`, `structure`, and `predicted_loop_type` (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).
    *   **Explicit Partner Identity:** We **retain** the explicit injection of the **Partner Base Identity** (one-hot vector of the paired base) at the input level. As established in **Lesson 00052**, removing this input cripples the encoder's ability to form basic chemical representations.

2.  **Backbone (Dense Dilated TCN):**
    *   **Architecture:** A stack of **Single-Layer Dilated Residual Blocks** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00011) utilizing **Dense Connections** (concatenating outputs of all prior blocks). This preserves the full multi-scale history required for complex interaction modeling (Cite solution_lesson_node_00047).
    *   **Configuration:** We employ exponentially increasing dilation rates ($d=1, 2, \dots, 32$) to ensure global receptive field coverage (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
    *   **Capacity & Regularization:** We strictly set the **Growth Rate / Channel Width** to **64** to avoid capacity bottlenecks (Cite solution_lesson_node_00037) and apply **Dropout (0.1)** within every block to prevent overfitting (Cite solution_lesson_node_00038).
    *   **Convolution Type:** We use **Standard Convolutions** throughout (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).

3.  **Innovation: Interaction Enrichment Module:**
    *   **Compression:** The high-dimensional output of the Dense backbone is compressed via a 1x1 convolution to a compact latent dimension of **32**. We choose 32 (vs 64 in Idea 16) to ensure the subsequent expanded representation remains parsimonious (Cite solution_lesson_node_00062, solution_lesson_node_00023).
    *   **Latent Gather:** For every position $i$, we gather the compressed feature vector of its paired partner $j$ (Cite solution_lesson_node_00031). Let $h_i$ be the local features and $h_j$ be the partner features.
    *   **Enriched Fusion:** Instead of simple concatenation ($[h_i, h_j]$) or learnable gating (which failed in Idea 17, Cite solution_lesson_node_00066), we explicitly construct a representation that captures the **compatibility and asymmetry** of the base pair. We compute:
        *   **Element-wise Product:** $h_{mult} = h_i \odot h_j$ (Captures similarity/binding compatibility).
        *   **Absolute Difference:** $h_{diff} = |h_i - h_j|$ (Captures asymmetry/directional context).
    *   **Concatenation:** The final fused vector is $[h_i, h_j, h_{mult}, h_{diff}]$. This results in a $32 \times 4 = 128$ dimensional vector.
    *   **Rationale:** This mimics techniques from Siamese matching networks. It explicitly provides the downstream RNN with non-linear interaction terms that are physically relevant for stability (e.g., complementarity), without adding new learnable parameters or optimization complexity.

4.  **Global Aggregation (BiGRU):**
    *   The enriched features are processed by a **Bidirectional GRU** to capture global constraints (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
    *   **Constraint:** Hidden size is set to `input_dim // 2` (i.e., $128 // 2 = 64$) to ensure the output dimension matches the input, preventing parameter bloat (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).

5.  **Output Head:**
    *   Linear projection to the 5 target variables.

# Training

1.  **Loss Function (Masked Optimization):**
    *   Use **MCRMSE** (Mean Columnwise Root Mean Squared Error).
    *   **Strategy:** Calculate loss **only on the three scored columns** (`reactivity`, `deg_Mg_pH10`, `deg_Mg_50C`).
    *   **Rationale:** We strictly adhere to **Lesson 00016**, masking out auxiliary targets (`deg_pH10`, `deg_50C`) to prevent negative transfer and align the optimization objective directly with the competition metric.

2.  **Optimizer:**
    *   Use **AdamW** with a learning rate of 1e-3 and a `ReduceLROnPlateau` scheduler.
