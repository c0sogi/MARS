This approach synthesizes the modern architectural advantages of ConvNeXt with the specialized pooling and regularization techniques proven effective in earlier iterations.

# Model
I propose a **ConvNeXt-Small** backbone integrated with a **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling** head.

*   **Structural Innovation (Backbone):** We will transition from the EfficientNet architecture (Idea 3) to **ConvNeXt-Small**. While EfficientNet proved effective, its reliance on small kernel sizes (3x3/5x5) can limit the receptive field's ability to capture long, continuous structures like catheters in early layers. ConvNeXt utilizes **7x7 kernels**, providing a larger global context that is structurally better suited for tracing continuous linear features across the chest. We choose the "Small" variant (approx. 50M parameters) to utilize the available A100 compute effectively, offering higher capacity than the previously attempted "Tiny" variant.
*   **Structural Innovation (Head):** We will replace the standard Global Average Pooling (GAP) of ConvNeXt with **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling**. As demonstrated, GAP dilutes the signal of thin, sparse features (catheters) by averaging them with the dominant background. GeM pooling allows the model to dynamically focus on high-activation spatial regions (the tubes) while suppressing the anatomical noise (Cite solution_lesson_node_00012).
*   **Output:** A linear projection from the GeM features to the 11 target classes.

# Data
The preprocessing pipeline is rigorously engineered to resolve the conflict between spatial resolution and optimization stability:

*   **Resolution:** We will standardize on a resolution of **640x640**. This resolution provides the necessary granularity to resolve thin catheter lines (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004) while remaining computationally manageable to ensure a stable batch size, avoiding the optimization collapse observed at 768x768 (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
*   **Preprocessing Sequence:**
    1.  **Aspect-Ratio Preserving Resize:** Resize the input image such that the longest dimension is 640 pixels. This preserves the physical morphology of the catheters, preventing the geometric distortion caused by naive squaring (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
    2.  **Intensity Transformation (CLAHE):** Apply Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization to the resized (unpadded) image. This order is critical: applying it *before* padding prevents boundary artifacts (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007), and applying it *before* geometric augmentation ensures high-frequency edges are enhanced before interpolation blur occurs (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
    3.  **Padding:** Pad the shorter dimension with zeros to achieve the final 640x640 square tensor.

# Training
*   **Batch Size Strategy:** We will strictly enforce a batch size of **$\ge$ 32**. Although ConvNeXt (using LayerNorm) is less sensitive to batch statistics than ResNet, maintaining a sufficient batch size is critical to reduce stochastic gradient noise and prevent the overfitting observed when high resolution forced the batch size to 4 (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
*   **Regularization:** To support the increased capacity of ConvNeXt-Small and prevent overfitting, we will employ **CoarseDropout** (randomly dropping rectangular regions) and **ShiftScaleRotate**. This "destructive augmentation" forces the model to learn robust features rather than memorizing noise (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014).
*   **Stabilization:** We will utilize **Model Exponential Moving Average (EMA)**. Crucially, we will tune the EMA decay rate (e.g., to 0.999 instead of 0.9999) to align with the training schedule, ensuring the averaged weights converge quickly enough given the limited number of epochs (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015).
*   **Optimization:** We will use **AdamW** with a **OneCycleLR** scheduler, optimizing the **Binary Cross-Entropy with Logits Loss**.

# Evaluation
*   **Inference:** Test images will undergo the identical **Resize (Longest Edge) -> CLAHE -> Pad** pipeline. This ensures the model encounters the same geometric proportions and feature distributions as during training.
*   **Prediction:** A single forward pass will be performed using the EMA weights (if available) or final model weights, followed by a Sigmoid activation to generate the submission probabilities.
