Based on the lessons learned from the previous experiments, I propose a strategy that centers on **Hardware-Aware Architectural Optimization** and **Resolution Discrepancy Training**. This approach addresses the computational bottlenecks that caused the high-capacity models to fail (Lesson 00012) while maximizing the data utilization benefits observed in the full-dataset experiments (Lesson 00004).

# Model
I propose replacing the EfficientNet-B0/B4 backbone with **EfficientNetV2-Small**.
*   **Structural Innovation**: Unlike the standard EfficientNet (V1) used in previous ideas, EfficientNetV2 utilizes **Fused-MBConv** blocks in its early stages. This architectural change is specifically designed to maximize training throughput on modern accelerators like the **NVIDIA A100** available in this task.
*   **Rationale**: The previous attempt to scale up to EfficientNet-B4 failed because the model was too computationally heavy, forcing a reduction in training epochs and data (Cite solution_lesson_node_00012). EfficientNetV2-Small offers a higher parameter capacity (~22M) than the B0 baseline (~5.3M) to capture fine-grained details, but its hardware-optimized structure ensures it can still be trained to convergence within the 24-hour limit, unlike the B4.

# Data
*   **Dataset**: We will use the **Full Training Dataset** (~186,000 images) without any downsampling. As established, preserving the natural frequency of "head" classes is critical for global accuracy, and we must avoid the performance degradation caused by artificial balancing or subsetting (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).
*   **Preprocessing (Resolution Discrepancy Strategy)**:
    *   **Training**: Images will be resized and randomly cropped to **256x256**. Keeping the training resolution moderate ensures we can maintain a high batch size and complete the extended training schedule.
    *   **Evaluation**: Images will be resized to a higher resolution (e.g., **384x384**) before center cropping. This "FixRes" strategy allows the model to utilize finer details during inference—crucial for species identification—without incurring the quadratic computational cost of training at that resolution.

# Training
*   **Procedure**: We will train for **20 epochs**. Previous results indicated that the model was not yet saturated at 15 epochs, and extending the training horizon allows the Cosine Annealing scheduler to decay more gradually, refining the decision boundaries for difficult classes (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).
*   **Optimizer & Scheduler**: Use **AdamW** with a **Cosine Annealing** scheduler.
*   **Loss Function**: Use **Cross Entropy Loss** with **Label Smoothing (0.1)**. Label smoothing is retained to prevent the higher-capacity V2 model from overfitting to the noisy labels inherent in fine-grained datasets.
*   **Regularization**: Implement **Stochastic Depth (DropPath)**, a standard regularization technique for EfficientNetV2, to further mitigate overfitting given the increased model size.

# Evaluation
*   **Inference**: Perform a single forward pass on the test images using the higher **384x384** resolution.
*   **Submission**: For each test image, output the top 5 predicted category IDs sorted by confidence, with the highest confidence prediction used for the Top-1 metric.
