Based on the lessons learned from previous iterations, I propose the following optimized strategy. This solution structurally evolves the approach by implementing a **Deep Ensemble of GeM-Pooled ConvNeXt** models. This strategy addresses the limitations of standard pooling for focal tumor detection while correcting the implementation flaws identified in previous attempts.

### Model
I will utilize the **ConvNeXt-Tiny** architecture as the backbone, modified with a **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling** head.
*   **Backbone Selection**: I will strictly adhere to the **ConvNeXt-Tiny** variant. Previous experiments demonstrated that scaling up to "Small" introduced detrimental training inertia that could not be overcome within the runtime constraints (Cite solution_lesson_node_00012). The "Tiny" variant offers the optimal balance of convergence speed and feature extraction capability (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
*   **Structural Innovation (Corrected GeM Pooling)**: I will replace the standard Global Average Pooling (GAP) with **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling**. Since the positive class is defined by the presence of *at least one pixel* of tumor tissue, standard average pooling risks diluting strong focal signals with background noise. GeM pooling (with a learnable parameter $p$) allows the model to focus on high-activation regions (approximating Max Pooling) while retaining differentiability.
*   **Critical Implementation Detail**: Crucially, I will ensure the **LayerNorm** layer from the original ConvNeXt backbone is **retained and applied before the GeM pooling layer**. Previous attempts failed because this normalization was inadvertently stripped when customizing the head, leading to training instability (Cite solution_lesson_node_00013).
*   **Ensemble Architecture**: I will construct a **5-Fold Cross-Validation Ensemble**. Instead of relying on a single model, I will train five independent instances of this architecture on distinct data splits. This reduces generalization error and variance, maximizing the utility of the available compute.

### Data
The data pipeline will strictly enforce the "Augment-then-Crop" paradigm to maximize context usage and minimize artifacts.
1.  **Normalization**: I will use **dataset-specific mean and standard deviation** rather than ImageNet defaults to align with the pathology scans' dynamic range (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).
2.  **Augmentation Pipeline**:
    *   **Global Augmentation**: I will apply geometric (flips, rotations) and intensity (ColorJitter) augmentations to the full **96x96px** source patch. This ensures intensity statistics are derived from the global context (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005) and prevents rotation artifacts (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).
    *   **Contextual Crop**: I will perform a **Center Crop to 64x64px** as the final step. This preserves the 32x32px ROI and a 16px context buffer while removing the irrelevant outer rim, preventing spatial label noise (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).

### Training
The training strategy integrates modern regularization recipes to support the ConvNeXt architecture.
*   **Regularization**: I will apply **Mixup** (alpha=0.2) combined with a high **Weight Decay** (e.g., 0.05). This combination has been proven to significantly outperform legacy recipes like Label Smoothing for this specific architecture (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).
*   **Optimization**:
    *   **Optimizer**: AdamW with a **Cosine Annealing** scheduler.
    *   **Learning Rate**: I will use a **conservative learning rate** (e.g., 2e-4) to preserve pre-trained feature extractors (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
    *   **Duration**: Each fold will be trained for a sufficient duration (e.g., 20 epochs) to ensure full convergence, avoiding the under-fitting observed when compressing the schedule (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).

### Evaluation
The evaluation process is designed to maximize robustness by marginalizing out orientation and model-specific noise.
*   **TTA**: I will implement **8-view Test Time Augmentation (TTA)**, averaging predictions across the original image and its 7 Dihedral variants (rotations and flips) to exploit rotational invariance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
*   **Ensemble Aggregation**: The final prediction for each image will be the average of **40 probabilities** (5 models $\times$ 8 TTA views). This extensive averaging will produce a highly stable and calibrated submission.
