Based on the lessons learned from the previous iterations, I propose the following optimized strategy. This approach structurally evolves the model from the lightweight "Tiny" variant to a higher-capacity **ConvNeXt-Small** backbone and introduces **Model Exponential Moving Average (EMA)** to the training pipeline to stabilize the generalization of this larger network.

### Model
I will upgrade the architecture to **ConvNeXt-Small**.
*   **Structural Innovation**: While the previous solution successfully utilized ConvNeXt-Tiny (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009), the available compute (A100 GPU) allows for a "moderately heavier" backbone. ConvNeXt-Small increases the channel dimensions and depth, enabling the capture of more subtle high-frequency morphological features within the 64x64px context that the Tiny variant might miss.
*   **Model EMA**: I will implement **Exponential Moving Average (EMA)** of the model weights. Instead of using the raw weights from the final epoch or a specific "best" checkpoint, the inference model will be a running average of the weights during training. This acts as a "temporal ensemble," creating a smoother decision boundary that is more robust to the noise introduced by aggressive regularization like Mixup.

### Data
The data pipeline will strictly enforce the "Augment-then-Crop" and "Contextual ROI" paradigms, which have been proven to minimize artifacts and maximize signal.
*   **Normalization**: I will calculate and use **dataset-specific mean and standard deviation** for normalization, ensuring the input distribution matches the pathology scans' dynamic range (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).
*   **Augmentation Pipeline**:
    1.  **Global Augmentation**: Apply geometric (flips, rotations) and intensity (ColorJitter) augmentations to the full **96x96px** source patch. This ensures color statistics are derived from the global context (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005) and prevents rotation artifacts (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).
    2.  **Contextual Crop**: Perform a **Center Crop to 64x64px**. This preserves the 32x32px ROI and a 16px context buffer while removing the irrelevant outer rim (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
*   **Regularization**: I will apply **Mixup** (alpha=0.2) to the training batches. This encourages the larger ConvNeXt-Small model to learn linear interpolations between classes, preventing it from memorizing the training data (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009, solution_lesson_node_00010).

### Training
The training procedure is designed to handle the increased capacity of the Small model without overfitting.
*   **Optimizer & Schedule**: Use **AdamW** with a **Cosine Annealing** scheduler. Crucially, I will maintain a sufficient training duration (e.g., 15-20 epochs) to allow the model to fully converge, avoiding the under-fitting observed when compressing the schedule (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
*   **Hyperparameters**:
    *   **Learning Rate**: Use a conservative learning rate (e.g., 1e-4) to preserve pre-trained feature extraction capabilities (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
    *   **Weight Decay**: Use a high weight decay (e.g., 0.05) to complement the Mixup regularization and prevent weight explosion in the larger backbone (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).
*   **EMA Update**: Update the shadow EMA model weights after every optimizer step.

### Evaluation
The evaluation strategy maximizes the exploitation of the model's learned invariances.
*   **Inference Model**: Predictions will be generated using the **EMA weights**, which typically offer superior generalization compared to the final raw weights.
*   **TTA**: I will implement **8-view Test Time Augmentation (TTA)**. The model will predict on the original image and its 7 Dihedral variants (rotations and flips), averaging the results to marginalize out orientation-specific noise (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
