Based on the lessons learned from previous iterations, I propose the following optimized strategy. This approach evolves the architecture to a modern ConvNet baseline and introduces advanced regularization techniques to handle the specific constraints of the 64x64px patch classification task.

# Model
I will replace the ResNet backbone with **ConvNeXt-Tiny**.
While the ResNet34 model demonstrated that increased capacity improves performance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007), ConvNeXt modernizes the standard CNN design by adopting architectural choices from Vision Transformers (such as larger kernel sizes, inverted bottlenecks, and layer normalization) while retaining the inductive biases of convolutions. This "Modernized CNN" structure is expected to extract more robust morphological features from the limited 64x64px spatial context than the older ResNet architecture.

# Data
The data pipeline will strictly adhere to the "Augment-then-Crop" paradigm to maximize context usage and minimize artifacts:
1.  **Normalization**: Use dataset-specific mean and standard deviation rather than ImageNet defaults to align with the dynamic range of the pathology scans (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).
2.  **Augmentation Pipeline**:
    *   First, apply geometric transformations (flips, rotations) to the full **96x96px** source patch (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).
    *   Second, apply intensity augmentations (ColorJitter) to the full **96x96px** patch. This ensures color statistics are derived from the global context, stabilizing the augmentation (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).
    *   Finally, perform a **Center Crop to 64x64px**. This retains the 32x32px ROI and a 16px context buffer while discarding the noisy outer rim (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).

# Training
I will introduce **Mixup Regularization** as a structural innovation in the training loop.
*   **Strategy**: Instead of standard empirical risk minimization, Mixup trains the network on convex combinations of pairs of examples and their labels. This encourages the model to behave linearly in-between training examples, improving generalization and calibration beyond what was achieved with Label Smoothing alone (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
*   **Optimization**:
    *   Use a **conservative learning rate** (e.g., 1e-4) to prevent disrupting the pre-trained weights of the new ConvNeXt backbone (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
    *   Maintain a sufficient training duration (e.g., 12-15 epochs) to ensure the model has enough iterations to converge, avoiding the under-fitting observed when compressing the schedule (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).

# Evaluation
The evaluation strategy will maximize the exploitation of rotational invariance:
*   **Inference**: Implement **8-view Test Time Augmentation (TTA)**. The model will predict probabilities for the original image and its 7 Dihedral variants (combinations of 90-degree rotations and flips), averaging the results to marginalize out orientation-specific prediction noise (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
