Based on the task description and available resources, I propose a baseline solution using a **Sliding-Window Masked Language Model (SW-MLM)**. This approach leverages the bidirectional context understanding of Transformers, which is ideal for "fill-in-the-blank" tasks, while utilizing a distilled architecture to maintain efficiency.

# Model
I will use **DistilRoBERTa** (specifically `distilroberta-base`), a lightweight Transformer model with approximately 82 million parameters.
*   **Architecture**: The model consists of 6 Transformer layers. It is pre-trained on a large corpus and will be fine-tuned on the provided training data.
*   **Justification**: DistilRoBERTa is significantly faster and smaller than full-sized BERT or RoBERTa models while retaining most of their performance. Its Masked Language Modeling (MLM) objective is mathematically equivalent to the task of predicting a missing word given its context.

# Data
*   **Training Data**: The raw text from `train_v2.txt` will be processed using the RoBERTa tokenizer. To optimize training throughput, text will be concatenated and sliced into fixed-length sequences (e.g., 128 tokens).
*   **Dynamic Masking**: Instead of static masking, I will use a data collator that dynamically masks 15% of tokens in each batch during training. This ensures the model learns robust context representations by seeing different mask patterns for the same text.
*   **Test Data**: The test sentences will be kept in their original form. During inference, they will be dynamically expanded into multiple "hypothesis" versions (see Evaluation).

# Training
*   **Procedure**: The model will be fine-tuned on the `train_v2.txt` dataset. Given the large dataset size (30M sentences), I will train for a single epoch (or a fixed number of steps equivalent to seeing a representative subset of data) to prevent overfitting and stay within the time limit.
*   **Optimization**: I will use the **AdamW** optimizer with a standard learning rate scheduler (linear warmup and decay).
*   **Efficiency**: Training will utilize **Mixed Precision (FP16)** on the NVIDIA A100 GPU to maximize computational speed and reduce memory footprint.

# Evaluation
To predict the missing word and its location, I will use a **Sliding Mask Inference** strategy:
1.  **Candidate Generation**: For a test sentence with $N$ words, I will generate candidate sentences by inserting a `<mask>` token at every possible valid position (indices 1 to $N-1$, as the missing word is never first or last).
2.  **Batch Inference**: These candidate sentences will be batched and fed into the model.
3.  **Scoring**: For each candidate, the model will output the probability distribution for the `<mask>` token. I will extract the maximum probability score and the corresponding token.
4.  **Selection**: The candidate (position + predicted word) with the highest confidence score across all positions will be selected as the final answer.
