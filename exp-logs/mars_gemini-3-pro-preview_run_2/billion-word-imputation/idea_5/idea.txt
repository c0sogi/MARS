Based on the success of the Two-Stage Cascade (Idea 4) and the insights from the provided lessons, I propose a **Probabilistic Beam-Search Cascade with Disentangled Attention**.

This strategy evolves the previous "Greedy Cascade" into a "Joint-Ranked Pipeline." It addresses the critical weakness of the greedy approach—where a single error in the localization step is irreversible—by deferring the final decision until semantic plausibility has been evaluated. Furthermore, it upgrades the structural encoder to an architecture specifically designed for relative positioning.

### Model

The solution employs two specialized Transformer models integrated via a probabilistic beam-search interface:

1.  **Stage 1: The Syntactic Locator (DeBERTa-v3 Backbone)**
    *   **Architecture:** We replace the standard BERT/RoBERTa backbone with **DeBERTa-v3 (Decoding-enhanced BERT with disentangled attention)**.
    *   **Rationale:** DeBERTa uses a disentangled attention mechanism that represents content and relative position separately. This is structurally superior for the "Locator" task, as detecting a missing word often relies on sensing broken syntactic dependencies and relative positional cues rather than just semantic content.
    *   **Head:** A Token Classification head that outputs a probability distribution over the sequence, indicating the likelihood of a gap following each token. This preserves the efficient **sequence tagging** framing **Cite solution_lesson_node_00004**.

2.  **Stage 2: The Semantic Validator (RoBERTa-Large Backbone)**
    *   **Architecture:** A high-capacity **RoBERTa-Large** model.
    *   **Head:** A Masked Language Modeling (MLM) head.
    *   **Rationale:** As established, aligning the in-filling task with the model's native **pre-training objective** is crucial for performance **Cite solution_lesson_node_00007**. We use the "Large" variant to maximize the semantic knowledge available for predicting the exact missing word.

### Data

We will process the data to support this decoupled training, scaling up significantly from the previous 50k sample limit to ensure robust generalization.

1.  **Synthetic Corruption:** We will generate a training set of **1 million samples** from `train_v2.txt`. For each sample, we uniformly remove one word (excluding the first/last).
2.  **Locator Labels:** For Stage 1, we generate binary sequence labels where the token immediately *preceding* the gap is marked as positive (1), and all others are negative (0).
3.  **In-Filler Inputs:** For Stage 2, we use the original sentences but mask the target word with the special `<mask>` token, training the model to reconstruct it.

### Training

Training remains decoupled to maximize the efficiency of each specialist model:

1.  **Locator Training:** Trained using **Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE)** loss on the token classification output. We will implement **Label Smoothing** to prevent the model from becoming overconfident in ambiguous cases, which is essential for the downstream beam search to work effectively.
2.  **In-Filler Training:** Fine-tuned using standard **Cross-Entropy Loss** over the vocabulary (MLM objective). We will use a lower learning rate ($1e-5$) to preserve the pre-trained knowledge while adapting to the specific vocabulary distribution of the dataset.

### Evaluation (Inference)

The core structural innovation lies in the inference pipeline, which moves from a deterministic "Greedy" approach to a **Probabilistic Beam Search**:

1.  **Candidate Generation (Pass 1):** The test sentence is fed into the **Locator**. Instead of taking the single `argmax` index, we extract the **Top-K** (e.g., $K=3$) most probable insertion indices.
2.  **Hypothesis Expansion:** For each of the $K$ indices, we create a candidate sentence by inserting a `<mask>` token at that position.
3.  **Semantic Scoring (Pass 2):** These $K$ masked sentences are batched and fed into the **In-Filler**. The In-Filler predicts the most likely word for the mask in each candidate.
4.  **Joint Ranking:** We select the final answer by maximizing a joint score that combines the structural confidence of the Locator and the semantic confidence of the In-Filler:
    $$ \text{Score} = P_{\text{loc}}(i) \times P_{\text{word}}(w | i) $$
    where $P_{\text{loc}}(i)$ is the probability of the gap being at index $i$, and $P_{\text{word}}(w|i)$ is the probability assigned to the predicted word $w$ by the MLM head.

This approach allows the strong semantic model to "overrule" the locator if the locator's preferred position results in a nonsensical word prediction, significantly improving robustness.
