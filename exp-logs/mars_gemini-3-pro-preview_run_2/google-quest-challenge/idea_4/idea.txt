Based on the comprehensive analysis of previous experiments and lessons learned, I propose the **Unified Context DeBERTa-v3 Siamese Network**.

This strategy represents a structural evolution that combines the superior representational power of the DeBERTa backbone (from Idea 3) with the unified, unrestricted interaction architecture (from Idea 2), explicitly rejecting the "Causal-Aware" split-head constraint that was found to artificially limit model capacity.

# Model

*   **Backbone Architecture**: We will utilize **DeBERTa-v3-base** as the shared encoder. This model's disentangled attention mechanism provides significantly higher fidelity for subjective nuances compared to distilled variants like DistilRoBERTa (Cite Lesson 00018).
*   **Siamese Encoder with Mean Pooling**: The model will process the *Question* (Title + Body) and *Answer* as two independent streams sharing the same DeBERTa weights. We will apply **Mean Pooling** to the final hidden states to generate fixed-size embeddings ($u$ and $v$). We retain Mean Pooling over learnable pooling to maintain simplicity and prevent overfitting on this small dataset (Cite Lesson 00004).
*   **Unified Interaction Fusion**: We will construct a single, rich feature vector by concatenating the embeddings with their explicit interactions: $[u, v, |u - v|, u \cdot v]$. This preserves the critical inductive biases for semantic distance and alignment (Cite Lesson 00005).
*   **Unified Multi-Target Head (Structural Innovation)**: Unlike the previous "Causal-Aware" iteration which split the prediction heads, we will feed the fused interaction vector into a **single Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP)** to predict all 30 target labels simultaneously. This structural change explicitly leverages **Contextual Entanglement**, allowing the model to use the quality of the answer to inform predictions about the question (e.g., the "halo effect"), which was identified as a missing signal in the split-head approach (Cite Lesson 00011).

# Data

*   **Tokenization**: Use the `microsoft/deberta-v3-base` tokenizer (SentencePiece).
*   **Input Formatting**:
    *   Stream 1: `question_title` + ` [SEP] ` + `question_body`
    *   Stream 2: `answer`
    *   Both streams will be truncated to a maximum length of 512 tokens.
*   **Preprocessing**: Data will be pre-tokenized and cached. No "causal masking" or separation of targets will be performed; all 30 labels are treated as dependent on the full $(Q, A)$ context.

# Training

*   **Strategy**: Perform **Full Fine-Tuning** of the DeBERTa backbone. We will not freeze any layers, as fixed representations are insufficient for these specific subjective targets (Cite Lesson 00008).
*   **Optimization**: Use the AdamW optimizer with **Differential Learning Rates**:
    *   Backbone: 1e-5 (to preserve pre-trained knowledge).
    *   Head: 1e-4 (for the randomly initialized MLP).
*   **Extended Convergence Schedule**: We will train for a minimum of 6 to 8 epochs. Previous experiments showed that Siamese architectures with interaction heads require significantly more updates to align the latent spaces compared to standard classification tasks (Cite Lesson 00015).
*   **Checkpointing**: We will implement a custom `Trainer` that saves the model with the best **Mean Column-wise Spearman's Correlation** (not Loss) on the validation set (Cite Lesson 00002). Crucially, we will use `copy.deepcopy()` when saving weights in memory to prevent the "State Dict Reference" bug (Cite Lesson 00001).

# Evaluation

*   **Inference**: The test set will be processed through the Siamese DeBERTa network.
*   **Prediction**: The Unified Head will output a 30-dimensional probability vector for each `qa_id`. These values will be directly mapped to the submission format.
*   **Validation**: We will compute the Spearman correlation on the validation split to confirm that the removal of causal constraints improves ranking performance compared to the split-head baseline.
