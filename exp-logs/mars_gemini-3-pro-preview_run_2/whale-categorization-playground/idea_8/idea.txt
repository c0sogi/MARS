Based on the analysis of the previous experiments and the specific lessons learned, I propose the following **High-Fidelity Dual-Ensemble Strategy**.

This approach challenges the conclusion of Lesson 40 ("Single Model Outperforms Ensembles") by identifying that the previous failure was driven by resource-constrained downscaling (256px) and under-training. With access to an A100 GPU and 24 hours of runtime, we will implement a "No-Compromise" ensemble that trains two distinct, high-capacity architectures to full convergence at high resolution, leveraging the diversity of feature extractors without sacrificing spatial fidelity.

# Model
**Architecture: Heterogeneous Dual-Backbone Ensemble**
We will construct an ensemble of two distinct metric learning models. This ensures feature diversity, as different architectures learn slightly different representations of the data.
1.  **Model A:** **EfficientNet-B5**. We scale up from the successful B4 backbone. With Gradient Checkpointing, the B5 model fits within the A100 memory, offering higher capacity for feature abstraction while maintaining the architectural stability of the EfficientNet family (Cite solution_lesson_node_00017).
2.  **Model B:** **EfficientNet-V2-M**. We re-introduce the V2 architecture. Its use of Fused-MBConv layers provides a structural inductive bias distinct from the standard MBConv layers in B5, which is ideal for ensembling.

**Shared Components:**
*   **Pooling:** Both models will utilize **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling**. This allows the networks to focus on salient features (scars, edges) while suppressing background water noise, which is superior to Global Average Pooling for fine-grained ID (Cite solution_lesson_node_00020).
*   **Head:** Both models will use an **ArcFace** head with a scaling factor of **$s=30.0$** and margin $m=0.50$. We explicitly adhere to the lower scaling factor to ensure stable convergence on this medium-sized dataset (Cite solution_lesson_node_00038).

# Data
**Preprocessing & Curation**
*   **Normalization:** We will strictly use **ImageNet Mean and Standard Deviation** for normalization. We will explicitly avoid computing custom dataset statistics, as this invalidates the pretrained weights and degrades performance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00042).
*   **Resolution:** Both models will be trained targeting a final resolution of **384x384**. We will not downscale to 256px for the ensemble, as high resolution is non-negotiable for resolving minute fluke details (Cite solution_lesson_node_00040, solution_lesson_node_00009).
*   **Filtering:** The training set will strictly **exclude** the `new_whale` class to prevent embedding collapse (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).
*   **Validation Gallery:** We will use a **Deterministic, Known-Only Gallery** for validation. The `new_whale` class will be excluded from the reference set to align the metric with the open-set inference logic (Cite solution_lesson_node_00019, solution_lesson_node_00024).

# Training
**Strategy: Independent Progressive Training**
We will train the two models sequentially or in parallel, treating them as independent "best-effort" runs.
*   **Gradient Checkpointing:** We will enable **Gradient Checkpointing** on both backbones. This is the structural enabler that allows us to train these heavy models at 384px with a stable batch size on a single GPU (Cite solution_lesson_node_00035).
*   **Progressive Resizing:** For each model, we will employ a 2-stage curriculum:
    1.  **Warm-up:** Train at **256x256** for the first ~60% of epochs.
    2.  **Fine-tuning:** Train at **384x384** for the remaining ~40% of epochs.
    This strategy ensures robust convergence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00035) and avoids the under-fitting observed in previous ensemble attempts (Cite solution_lesson_node_00040).
*   **Batch Size:** We will maintain a batch size of **$\ge$ 32**. We will not sacrifice batch stability for model size; Gradient Checkpointing ensures we can satisfy this constraint (Cite solution_lesson_node_00022).
*   **Checkpointing:** We will save the best weights for each model based strictly on the **Validation MAP@5** score (Cite solution_lesson_node_00021).

# Evaluation
**Inference: Similarity Fusion & Open-Set Rejection**
We will employ a robust fusion strategy that aggregates predictions in the similarity space.
1.  **Cache Invalidation:** Explicitly force re-computation of embeddings to ensure no stale data is used (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
2.  **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):** For every image (query and gallery), compute embeddings for the original and horizontally flipped views, averaging them to reduce pose noise.
3.  **Matrix Fusion:** Calculate the Cosine Similarity Matrix for Model A ($S_A$) and Model B ($S_B$) against the **Known-Only** Gallery. Compute the final similarity matrix $S_{final} = 0.5 \cdot S_A + 0.5 \cdot S_B$.
4.  **Open-Set Rejection:** Apply **Distance Thresholding** to $S_{final}$.
    *   Identify the nearest neighbor and its similarity score.
    *   If `Score < Threshold`: Predict `new_whale`.
    *   Else: Predict the top 5 known IDs.
    *   We explicitly **discard k-Reciprocal Re-ranking**, as it degrades performance on this sparse dataset (Cite solution_lesson_node_00033).
