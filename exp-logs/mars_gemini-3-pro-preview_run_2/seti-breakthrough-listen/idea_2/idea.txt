Based on the provided lessons and task description, here is the optimized strategy.

# Model
I propose upgrading the architecture to an **EfficientNet-V2 Small** backbone, structurally modified with a **Generalized Mean Pooling (GeM)** layer.

*   **Backbone (EfficientNet-V2 Small):** This architecture offers a stronger, faster, and more parameter-efficient feature extractor compared to the EfficientNet-B0 used previously. It utilizes Fused-MBConv blocks that are better suited for the high-resolution spatial features found in spectrograms.
*   **Pooling (GeM):** I will replace the standard Global Average Pooling (GAP) or Global Max Pooling (GMP) with **Generalized Mean Pooling (GeM)**.
    *   *Rationale:* **Cite Lesson 00007** demonstrated that Max Pooling drastically outperforms Average Pooling because the target signals are sparse "needles" in a noisy "haystack." GeM is a learnable pooling operation ($f = (\text{mean}(x^p))^{1/p}$) that interpolates between Max ($p \to \infty$) and Average ($p=1$) pooling. By initializing $p$ to a high value (e.g., 3.0 or 4.0), we retain the critical benefit of Max Pooling (focusing on the strongest activation) while allowing the network to fine-tune the pooling sharpness via backpropagation.
*   **Head:** A simple fully connected layer followed by a Sigmoid activation will map the pooled feature vector to the probability score.

# Data
*   **Input Representation (Vertical Stacking):** The 6 cadence panels (ABACAD) will be concatenated vertically to form a single tall image of shape `(1638, 256)` (where 1638 = 6 * 273).
    *   *Rationale:* **Cite Lesson 00004** showed that vertical stacking preserves the geometric continuity of Doppler-drifting signals, transforming them into long diagonal lines that 2D CNNs can easily detect, unlike channel-wise stacking which breaks this spatial coherence.
*   **Preprocessing:**
    *   **Instance Normalization:** Each sample will be independently normalized (subtracted by its mean and divided by its standard deviation) to handle varying noise floors.
    *   **Channel Expansion:** The single-channel spectrogram will be replicated 3 times to create a pseudo-RGB image `(3, 1638, 256)`, allowing the use of pretrained ImageNet weights which facilitate faster convergence.
*   **Augmentation:**
    *   **Horizontal Flip:** Randomly reversing the frequency axis. **Cite Lesson 00009** identified this as critical because Doppler drifts can have positive or negative slopes; removing it causes overfitting.
    *   **Vertical Flip:** Randomly reversing the time axis. This is physically valid for detection (a line is still a line) and increases the diversity of slope orientations.

# Training
*   **Loss Function:** **BCEWithLogitsLoss** will be used.
*   **Optimizer:** **AdamW** with a learning rate of roughly `1e-4` and weight decay for regularization.
*   **Scheduler:** A **Cosine Annealing** scheduler will be employed to smoothly reduce the learning rate, helping the model settle into a robust local minimum.
*   **Regularization:** **Mixup** augmentation will be applied during training. By blending "needle" samples with "noise" samples (and their labels), Mixup forces the model to learn the specific structural features of the signal rather than memorizing high-intensity noise artifacts, addressing the low-SNR nature of the data.

# Evaluation
*   **Inference:** The model will process the test set using the same vertical stacking and normalization pipeline.
*   **TTA (Test Time Augmentation):** Predictions will be averaged across the original image and a horizontally flipped version to improve robustness.
*   **Submission:** Probabilities will be saved to `submission.csv` matching the format required.
