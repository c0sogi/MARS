Based on the accumulated lessons and the available computational resources, I propose a solution that evolves the single-model approach into a **Heterogeneous Ensemble** to maximize feature diversity and robustness against label noise.

# Model
I propose a **Heterogeneous Ensemble** consisting of two distinct architectures. This approach leverages the "wisdom of crowds" to reduce prediction variance caused by noisy annotations.
1.  **ConvNeXt-Small:** I will retain the **ConvNeXt-Small** backbone. This architecture has proven effective in extracting high-quality visual features and scaling well for this task (Cite Lesson 00009).
2.  **Swin Transformer (Small):** I will introduce a **Swin Transformer** (e.g., `swin_small`) as the second backbone. Unlike the CNN-based ConvNeXt, the Swin Transformer utilizes shifted window self-attention mechanisms. This allows it to model long-range global dependencies and semantic context in "scenic" artwork images, providing a complementary signal to the local texture features captured by the CNN.

Both models will utilize a standard linear classification head with Sigmoid activation to output 3,474 independent probabilities.

# Data
- **Resolution:** Input images will be resized to **384x384**. This resolution was found to be superior to 224x224 for capturing fine-grained details in artwork (Cite Lesson 00007).
- **Preprocessing:** Images will be normalized using ImageNet mean and standard deviation.
- **Augmentation:** We will apply light augmentations during training, specifically Random Horizontal Flip and mild Random Resized Crop (scale 0.9–1.0), to prevent overfitting without distorting the artistic composition.

# Training
- **Loss Function:** Both models will be trained independently using **Asymmetric Loss (ASL)**. This is critical to prevent the vast number of "easy negative" labels (zeros) from dominating the gradients, a key issue identified in sparse multi-label tasks (Cite Lesson 00007).
- **Weight Averaging:** I will implement **Exponential Moving Average (EMA)** of the model weights during training. By maintaining a moving average of the parameters, we can produce a final model that is smoother and more robust to the noisy labels described in the dataset, effectively filtering out transient noise from the optimization trajectory.
- **Optimizer:** We will use the **AdamW** optimizer combined with a **OneCycleLR** scheduler for efficient super-convergence.
- **Precision:** Mixed Precision (FP16) will be used to maximize throughput on the A100 GPU.

# Evaluation
- **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):** During inference, we will apply **Test-Time Augmentation** (specifically averaging predictions from the original and horizontally flipped images) for *each* model in the ensemble. This has been proven to improve F1 scores by smoothing out prediction noise (Cite Lesson 00009).
- **Ensemble Aggregation:** The probability outputs from the ConvNeXt-Small and Swin-Small models (after TTA) will be averaged to form the final prediction probabilities.
- **Thresholding:** We will determine a **Single Global Threshold** by optimizing the Micro F1 score on the validation set using the averaged ensemble probabilities. We will strictly avoid per-class thresholding to prevent the explosion of False Positives observed previously (Cite Lesson 00006).
