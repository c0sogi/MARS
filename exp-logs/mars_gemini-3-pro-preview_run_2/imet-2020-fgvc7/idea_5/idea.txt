Based on the accumulated lessons and the "Previous Ideas," I propose a **Calibrated Heterogeneous Distillation Strategy**. This approach structurally evolves the ensemble concept (Idea 3) into a Teacher-Student pipeline (Idea 4), specifically refining the training objectives and model selection criteria to address the critical issues of label noise, probability calibration, and numerical instability identified in the lessons.

# Model
I propose a **Teacher-Student** architecture designed to compress the robust, diverse features of a heterogeneous ensemble into a single, high-capacity student model.
*   **Teachers (Heterogeneous Pair):** The teacher ensemble will consist of a **ConvNeXt-Small** (CNN) and a **Swin-Base** (Transformer). This combination is chosen to capture complementary visual signals: the CNN excels at local texture analysis (brushstrokes), while the Transformer captures global composition and long-range dependencies (Cite Lesson 00011).
*   **Student (Scaled-Up CNN):** The student will be a **ConvNeXt-Large**. We scale the student beyond the size of the CNN teacher (Small -> Large) to ensure it has sufficient capacity to absorb the complex, distilled knowledge from the ensemble (Cite Lesson 00009). We choose a CNN backbone for the student to avoid the numerical instabilities observed with Transformers during mixed-precision training (Cite Lesson 00012).

# Data
*   **Resolution:** All models will be trained and evaluated at **384x384** resolution to capture fine-grained artistic details (Cite Lesson 00007).
*   **Preprocessing:** Images will be normalized using ImageNet mean and standard deviation.
*   **Augmentation:** We will apply **moderate augmentation** (Random Horizontal Flip, Random Resized Crop with scale 0.75–1.0). We will strictly avoid heavy regularization techniques (like strong Color Jitter or high rates of Stochastic Depth) which were found to hinder convergence within the training budget (Cite Lesson 00014).
*   **Soft-Target Generation:** The Teacher Ensemble will generate probability distributions (soft labels) for the entire training set. These soft labels serve as the ground truth for the student, providing information about label ambiguity and correcting noisy annotations.

# Training
The training pipeline is divided into two phases, with a strict focus on probability calibration.

**Phase 1: Teacher Training (Calibration Focus)**
*   Train the **ConvNeXt-Small** and **Swin-Base** independently.
*   **Loss Function:** Use **Asymmetric Loss (ASL)** to handle the extreme class imbalance and prevent easy negatives from dominating gradients (Cite Lesson 00007).
*   **Stability:** Use **Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP)** coupled with **Exponential Moving Average (EMA)**. This combination allows the training to survive numerical instabilities (NaNs) in the Swin Transformer without aborting, as EMA filters out corrupted updates (Cite Lesson 00012).
*   **Checkpoint Selection:** Crucially, select the teacher checkpoints that achieve the **Minimum Validation Loss**, rather than the Maximum F1 score. This ensures the teachers provide well-calibrated probability distributions for distillation, rather than overconfident, overfitted predictions that would degrade the student's performance (Cite Lesson 00015).

**Phase 2: Student Distillation (Noise Reduction)**
*   Train the **ConvNeXt-Large** student.
*   **Loss Function:** Minimize a weighted sum of **Asymmetric Loss** (computed against the original hard labels) and **KL Divergence** (computed against the Teachers' soft labels). The soft labels act as a noise filter, preventing the student from overfitting to incorrect hard annotations.
*   **Optimizer:** Use **AdamW** with a **OneCycleLR** scheduler for efficient convergence.

# Evaluation
*   **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):** During inference, the Student model will predict on both the original and horizontally flipped images, averaging the probabilities. This has been proven to improve robustness and F1 scores (Cite Lesson 00009).
*   **Global Threshold Optimization:** We will optimize a **Single Global Threshold** on the validation set predictions to maximize the Micro F1 score. We will strictly avoid per-class thresholding, which was shown to destroy Micro F1 performance (Cite Lesson 00006).
