Based on the accumulated lessons, I propose a **Knowledge Distillation** strategy. This approach structurally evolves the solution from a simple "Ensemble of Independent Models" to a "Teacher-Student" pipeline. This addresses the critical issues of label noise and calibration identified in the lessons by using the smoothed, calibrated predictions of a strong ensemble to supervise a high-capacity student model.

# Model
The architecture will follow a **Teacher-Student** paradigm designed to compress the robust, diverse features of a heterogeneous ensemble into a single, powerful model.

*   **Teacher Ensemble (Heterogeneous):** We will retain the **Heterogeneous Ensemble** consisting of a **ConvNeXt-Small** (CNN) and a **Swin-Base** (Transformer). This combination is proven to capture complementary local texture and global composition features (Cite Lesson 00011).
*   **Student Model (Scaled-Up CNN):** The student will be a **ConvNeXt-Base**. We choose a CNN backbone for the student to maximize training stability and inference throughput, while scaling up from "Small" to "Base" to provide the necessary capacity to absorb the complex knowledge distilled from the teachers (Cite Lesson 00009).
*   **Heads:** All models will utilize a standard linear classification head with Sigmoid activation to output 3,474 independent probabilities.

# Data
*   **Resolution:** Input images will be resized to **384x384** pixels to capture fine-grained artistic details (Cite Lesson 00007).
*   **Augmentation:** We will apply **moderate augmentation** (Random Horizontal Flip, Random Resized Crop with scale 0.75–1.0). We will explicitly avoid heavy regularization techniques like aggressive Color Jitter or high rates of Stochastic Depth, which were found to cause underfitting within the training budget (Cite Lesson 00014).
*   **Soft-Target Generation:** Before training the student, we will perform inference on the entire training set using the trained Teacher Ensemble. These predictions (probabilities) will serve as "Soft Labels" for the student.

# Training
The training process is divided into two distinct stages:

**Stage 1: Teacher Training (Robust Calibration)**
*   Train the **ConvNeXt-Small** and **Swin-Base** independently.
*   **Loss Function:** Use **Asymmetric Loss (ASL)** to handle the high class imbalance and prevent negative gradient dominance (Cite Lesson 00007).
*   **Stability:** Employ **Exponential Moving Average (EMA)** and **Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP)**. This combination is critical to survive the numerical instabilities (NaN losses) often seen in Swin Transformers without aborting the run (Cite Lesson 00012).
*   **Checkpoint Selection:** Crucially, save the teacher checkpoints that achieve the **Minimum Validation Loss**, not the Maximum F1. This ensures the teachers provide well-calibrated probability distributions rather than overconfident, overfitted predictions (Cite Lesson 00015).

**Stage 2: Student Distillation (Noise Reduction)**
*   Train the **ConvNeXt-Base** student.
*   **Distillation Loss:** The loss function will be a weighted sum of the **Asymmetric Loss** (computed against the original hard ground truth) and **Binary Cross Entropy** (computed against the Teachers' soft labels).
    *   *Rationale:* The soft labels from the ensemble act as a noise filter. If the ground truth label is noisy (incorrect), the ensemble likely assigns it a lower probability, preventing the student from overfitting to the error.
*   **Optimization:** Use **AdamW** with a **OneCycleLR** scheduler for efficient convergence.

# Evaluation
*   **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):** Perform inference with the Student model using TTA (averaging predictions of original and flipped images) to smooth out prediction variance (Cite Lesson 00009).
*   **Global Threshold Optimization:** Instead of per-class thresholding (which destroys Micro F1), we will optimize a **Single Global Threshold** on the validation set predictions to maximize the Micro F1 score (Cite Lesson 00006).
