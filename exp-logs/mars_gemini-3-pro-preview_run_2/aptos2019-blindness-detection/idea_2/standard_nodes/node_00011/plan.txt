To optimize the performance of the Diabetic Retinopathy classifier, I will implement a **5-Fold Cross-Validation (CV) ensemble strategy** combined with **Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE)** augmentation.

**Strategy Outline:**
1.  **Cross-Validation Ensembling (Cite Lesson 00003):** The previous solution relied on a single 80/20 train/val split. To improve robustness and generalization, I will implement 5-Fold Stratified Cross-Validation on the training set. We will train 5 independent models (one for each fold) and ensemble their predictions. This reduces the variance associated with a single split and typically yields a higher metric.
2.  **Adaptive Contrast Augmentation (Cite Lesson 00002):** The failure analysis revealed a negative correlation between prediction error and image standard deviation (contrast). To address this, I will add `CLAHE` (Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization) to the training augmentation pipeline. This will force the model to learn features robust to varying contrast levels.
3.  **Architecture & Resolution (Cite Lesson 00001, 00004, 00010):** I will retain the **EfficientNet-B5** backbone with **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling** and the **Regression** formulation. The input resolution remains **456x456** to match the backbone's capacity and preserve fine-grained retinal details.
4.  **Evaluation Protocol:** I will perform the CV on the `train.csv` metadata and evaluate the ensemble on the fixed `val.csv` hold-out set to ensure a strictly comparable and valid metric.

The implementation involves modifying `config.py` to define fold count, updating `data.py` to support custom dataframes and CLAHE, and rewriting `runfile.py` to execute the CV loop and ensemble inference.
