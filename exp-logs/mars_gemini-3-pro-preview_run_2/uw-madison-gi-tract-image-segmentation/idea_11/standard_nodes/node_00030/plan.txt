The issue is that the `hausdorff_3d` function in `library/utils.py` normalizes the X and Y coordinates to the range $[0, 1]$ but leaves the Z (depth) coordinate as raw integer indices. This creates an inhomogeneous coordinate space where the Z-axis dominates the distance calculation, producing unbounded values (e.g., > 13.0) that break the scoring formula `0.6 * (1 - Hausdorff)` and the model checkpointing logic. The fix is to normalize the Z-coordinates by the volume depth `d`, ensuring all dimensions are scaled to the unit interval $[0, 1]$ (Cite debug_lesson_6).
