Based on the analysis of the previous ideas and the specific lessons learned regarding resolution bottlenecks and cascade failures, I propose **Idea 13**, which implements a **2.5D U-Net++ with Patch-Based Learning**.

### Model
I propose a **2.5D U-Net++ (Nested U-Net)** architecture. This structure evolves the standard U-Net (Idea 1, 11) by introducing nested, dense skip pathways between the encoder and decoder sub-networks.

*   **Structural Innovation:** Unlike the standard U-Net, which connects the encoder and decoder with simple long skip connections, U-Net++ employs a series of nested convolution blocks ($X_{i,j}$) on the skip pathways. This reduces the "semantic gap" between the encoder's feature maps and the decoder's, allowing the model to capture fine-grained boundary details more effectively than the baseline U-Net.
*   **Backbone:** We utilize an **EfficientNet-B4** backbone. This is a higher-capacity encoder than the GhostNet used in Idea 11, providing the necessary feature extraction power to resolve subtle organ boundaries. We rely on the A100 GPU's capacity to handle this heavier backbone within the single-stage framework.
*   **Single-Stage Design:** We explicitly reject the Coarse-to-Fine cascade architecture (Idea 12). By using a single, powerful network that operates on high-resolution data (via the data strategy below), we ensure the model is structurally robust and avoid the "Recall Bottleneck" where a coarse filter permanently discards valid objects. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00031**.
*   **Input:** The model accepts **2.5D inputs** (3 channels: Slice $i-1$, Slice $i$, Slice $i+1$) to encode volumetric context into the channel dimension. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00030**.

### Data
The data strategy undergoes a fundamental shift from "Global Resizing" to "Local Patching" to address the resolution bottleneck identified in Lesson 30.

1.  **2.5D Stack Generation:** For every slice, we construct the 3-channel input stack (Slice $i-1$, Slice $i$, Slice $i+1$). **Cite solution_lesson_node_00030**.
2.  **Patch-Based Training (High Resolution):**
    *   Instead of resizing the entire MRI slice to a small fixed dimension (e.g., 256x256)—which was found to destroy critical details and correlate with error—we maintain the images at their **native resolution** (or a high standardized resolution like 512x512).
    *   During training, we extract **Random Crops (Patches)** of size **320x320** from these high-resolution stacks.
    *   This allows the model to learn from full-fidelity anatomical features, effectively solving the "detail loss" problem without the complexity of a second "Fine" model.
3.  **Normalization:** We apply **Instance Min-Max Normalization** to the crops to handle MRI intensity variance.
4.  **Augmentation:** We apply spatial augmentations (Rotations, Flips) and Elastic Deformations to the patches to encourage shape generalization.

### Training
The training pipeline leverages the unique properties of U-Net++ to improve convergence.

*   **Deep Supervision:** U-Net++ produces segmentation maps at multiple levels of the nested decoder (nodes $X_{0,1}, X_{0,2}, X_{0,3}, X_{0,4}$). We compute the loss for *each* of these outputs and average them. This forces the intermediate layers to learn semantically meaningful features, accelerating convergence and acting as a regularizer.
*   **Loss Function:** We employ a sum of **Binary Cross Entropy (BCE) and Tversky Loss**. Tversky Loss is configured with a higher weight on false negatives ($\beta > \alpha$) to aggressively target high recall for the small intestinal structures.
*   **Optimizer:** **AdamW** with a **Cosine Annealing** scheduler.

### Evaluation
The inference process is adapted to support the patch-based training strategy.

1.  **Sliding Window (Tiled) Inference:**
    *   To process the full-resolution test images, we do not resize them. Instead, we slide a **320x320 window** across the image with a fixed stride (e.g., 160 pixels overlap).
    *   The model predicts probabilities for each window. Overlapping regions are blended using a **Gaussian weighting** scheme (giving higher trust to the center of the window) to reconstruct the full-size segmentation map.
2.  **3D Post-Processing:**
    *   The reconstructed 2D masks are stacked into a 3D volume.
    *   We apply **3D Largest Connected Component** analysis for each class, retaining only the largest object. This removes isolated false positives generated by the tiled inference, optimizing the Hausdorff distance metric.
