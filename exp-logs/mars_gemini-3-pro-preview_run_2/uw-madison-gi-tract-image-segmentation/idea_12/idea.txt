Based on the lessons learned from the development of the 2.5D Ghost U-Net, I propose **Idea 12**, which evolves the architecture into a **2.5D Coarse-to-Fine Cascade Network**.

### Model
The solution addresses the resolution bottleneck identified in the previous iteration by splitting the task into two specialized stages, maintaining the efficient "2.5D" input structure throughout.

1.  **Stage 1: Global Localization (Coarse Model):**
    *   This stage reuses the **2.5D Ghost U-Net** architecture from Idea 11.
    *   **Purpose:** Its sole objective is to identify the Region of Interest (ROI)—the approximate bounding box of the stomach and intestines—within the global context of the abdomen. It prioritizes recall over precision.
    *   **Input:** It accepts the 2.5D stack (slices $i-1, i, i+1$) resized to a low resolution (e.g., 256x256), ensuring it captures the global anatomical structure without heavy computation.

2.  **Stage 2: Local Refinement (Fine Model):**
    *   This stage employs a **2.5D EfficientNet-B1 U-Net**.
    *   **Purpose:** It performs high-fidelity segmentation on the zoomed-in ROI identified by Stage 1. By processing only the relevant sub-region, it effectively increases the pixel density (resolution) of the input without increasing the tensor size, directly addressing the detail loss observed in larger images.
    *   **Input:** It accepts a cropped 2.5D stack centered on the ROI, resized to a standard input size (e.g., 320x320).

### Data
The data preparation strategy exploits the 2.5D stacking technique while introducing ROI-based cropping to handle scale.

1.  **2.5D Channel Stacking:** For both stages, we construct the input volume by stacking the target slice with its superior and inferior neighbors ($i-1, i, i+1$) to encode volumetric context into the channel dimension. **Cite solution_lesson_node_00030**.
2.  **Stage 1 Preprocessing:** Full slice images are resized to **256x256** using Min-Max normalization.
3.  **Stage 2 Preprocessing (Training):** Instead of using full images, we generate training samples by cropping the original high-resolution images around the ground truth masks. We add random margins (jitter) to the bounding boxes to simulate imperfect localization from Stage 1, making the Fine Model robust to localization errors. These crops are resized to **320x320**.
4.  **Augmentation:** We apply spatial augmentations (flips, rotations) to both stages, and mild elastic deformations to Stage 2 to encourage shape generalization.

### Training
The training procedure involves optimizing the two models independently to maximize their specific objectives.

1.  **Stage 1 Training:** Trained on the full dataset with a **Binary Cross Entropy + Dice Loss**. The target masks are the standard ground truth masks.
2.  **Stage 2 Training:** Trained on the dataset of generated crops. We use a **Tversky Loss** to heavily penalize false negatives, ensuring the model captures the fine boundaries of the intestines within the crop.
3.  **Optimization:** Both models use the **AdamW** optimizer with a **Cosine Annealing** scheduler. Since the models are decoupled, they can be trained in parallel to save time.

### Evaluation
The inference pipeline links the two stages sequentially to generate the final predictions.

1.  **Coarse Prediction:** The test slice (2.5D stack) is passed through Stage 1. We threshold the output to generate a coarse binary mask.
2.  **ROI Extraction:** We calculate the bounding box of the coarse mask. If no mask is predicted (background slice), the process terminates with an empty prediction.
3.  **Fine Prediction:** We crop the original high-resolution test slice using the calculated bounding box (plus a fixed safety margin). This crop is resized to 320x320 and passed through Stage 2.
4.  **Reconstruction:** The prediction from Stage 2 is resized back to the dimensions of the crop and "pasted" into a blank mask of the original image size at the correct coordinates.
5.  **3D Post-Processing:** The reconstructed 2D masks are stacked into a 3D volume. We apply **3D Largest Connected Component** analysis to remove isolated noise, optimizing the Hausdorff distance metric.
