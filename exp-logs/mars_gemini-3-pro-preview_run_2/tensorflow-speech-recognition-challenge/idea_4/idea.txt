Based on the comprehensive analysis of previous attempts and the specific lessons learned, here is the proposed optimized strategy.

### Model: ConvNeXt-Tiny with Spectro-Temporal Alignment
I propose re-implementing the **ConvNeXt-Tiny** architecture, but with a critical correction to the signal processing pipeline that caused its previous failure.
*   **Backbone (ConvNeXt-Tiny)**: We will utilize the ConvNeXt-Tiny architecture initialized with ImageNet weights. ConvNeXt modernizes the standard CNN with design choices inspired by Transformers, most notably the use of large **7x7 kernels**. These large kernels provide a significantly wider effective receptive field compared to the 3x3 kernels of EfficientNet or ResNet, allowing the model to capture broader phonetic contexts and temporal dependencies within the 1-second clips without the computational overhead of recurrent layers. The first projection layer will be averaged to accept 1-channel spectrogram inputs **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00004)**.
*   **Head (Attention Pooling)**: We will replace the standard Global Average Pooling with a learnable **Attention Pooling** layer. This mechanism allows the model to dynamically assign high importance weights to the specific time frames containing the spoken command and suppress the surrounding silence or background noise, which is essential for short commands **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00011)**.

### Data: High-Temporal-Resolution Spectrograms
The core innovation in this iteration is the **alignment of the feature resolution with the signal dynamics**, directly addressing the root cause of the previous ConvNeXt failure.
*   **Corrected Spectrogram Generation**: We will generate Log-Mel Spectrograms using a window size of **25ms** (`n_fft=400` at 16kHz) and a hop length of **10ms**.
    *   *Rationale*: Previous attempts with ConvNeXt failed because they used a 64ms window (`n_fft=1024`), which "smeared" rapid phonemic transitions (like 't' vs 'p') across time. By reverting to a ~25ms window, we prioritize **temporal resolution**, ensuring that the strong ConvNeXt backbone receives sharp, distinguishable phonetic features **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00016)**.
*   **Instance Normalization**: We will apply Instance Normalization to each spectrogram to standardize the signal volume and contrast per sample, improving robustness to recording variations **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00011)**.
*   **Weighted Random Sampling**: We will strictly employ a `WeightedRandomSampler` to ensure every training batch contains a balanced distribution of the 12 classes, neutralizing the 17:1 imbalance caused by the "unknown" class **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00001)**.
*   **Calibrated SpecAugment**: We will apply Time and Frequency masking. Crucially, the mask parameters will be calibrated to be a conservative fraction (<20%) of the feature dimensions to act as a regularizer without destroying the semantic signal **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00005)**.

### Training
*   **Loss Function**: We will use Cross-Entropy Loss with **Label Smoothing (0.1)**. This prevents the high-capacity ConvNeXt model from becoming overconfident (pushing probabilities to 1.0) and encourages the learning of more robust, clustered feature representations **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00013)**.
*   **Optimizer & Scheduler**: We will use the **AdamW** optimizer combined with a **Cosine Annealing** learning rate scheduler.
*   **Regularization**: We will **exclude Mixup** augmentation. Given the effectiveness of SpecAugment and the limited training budget, Mixup slows convergence and is unnecessary for this specific task configuration **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00007)**.

### Evaluation
*   **Inference**: Test audio clips will be processed using the exact same high-temporal-resolution parameters (25ms window, 10ms hop) and Instance Normalization.
*   **Prediction**: The model will output class probabilities via Softmax, and the label with the highest probability will be selected.
