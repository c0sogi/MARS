Based on the lessons learned and the analysis of the previous ideas, here is the proposed optimized strategy.

### Model: ConvNeXt-Tiny with Attention Pooling
I propose a structural evolution from the EfficientNet-B0 backbone to a **ConvNeXt-Tiny** architecture. While EfficientNet proved effective **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00011)**, ConvNeXt modernizes the standard Convolutional Network by incorporating design choices from Transformers (e.g., larger 7x7 kernels, Layer Normalization, and GELU activations).
*   **Backbone (ConvNeXt-Tiny)**: The 7x7 kernels in the ConvNeXt stages provide a significantly larger effective receptive field compared to the 3x3 kernels in EfficientNet. This is crucial for capturing longer temporal dependencies and phonetic contexts within the 1-second clips without relying on recurrent layers. We will modify the first projection layer to accept 1-channel input by averaging the pretrained RGB weights **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00004)**.
*   **Head (Attention Pooling)**: We will retain the **Attention Pooling** mechanism instead of standard Global Average Pooling. This allows the model to dynamically weight the relevant time frames (the spoken command) and suppress the silence/noise, which is critical for short commands embedded in 1-second clips **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00011)**.

### Data: High-Resolution Spectrograms & Balanced Sampling
The data pipeline will combine the successful high-resolution feature extraction with robust sampling and augmentation strategies.
*   **High-Resolution Features**: We will generate Log-Mel Spectrograms with a short hop length (e.g., 10ms), resulting in high temporal resolution features (approx. 100 time steps). This prevents the "smearing" of short phonemes observed with coarser strides **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00011)**.
*   **Instance Normalization**: We will apply Instance Normalization (standardizing each spectrogram based on its own mean and variance) rather than global dataset normalization. This improves robustness to varying recording volumes and conditions **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00011)**.
*   **Weighted Random Sampling**: We will strictly use a `WeightedRandomSampler` to balance the batches, ensuring the model sees an equal distribution of the 12 classes despite the 17:1 imbalance caused by the "unknown" class **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00001)**.
*   **Calibrated SpecAugment**: We will apply Time and Frequency masking. Crucially, the mask parameters will be calibrated to be a small fraction (<20%) of the high-resolution dimensions to act as a regularizer without destroying the signal **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00005)**.

### Training
*   **Loss Function**: We will use Cross-Entropy Loss with **Label Smoothing (0.1)**. This prevents the high-capacity ConvNeXt model from becoming overconfident and overfitting to the training data, encouraging more robust feature clusters **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00013)**.
*   **Optimizer & Scheduler**: We will use the **AdamW** optimizer combined with a **Cosine Annealing** scheduler. This setup avoids the complexity of Mixup while ensuring smooth convergence **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00007)**.

### Evaluation
*   **Inference**: Test clips will be processed with the same high-resolution spectrogram parameters and Instance Normalization.
*   **Prediction**: The model will output probabilities via Softmax. The label corresponding to the highest probability will be selected.
