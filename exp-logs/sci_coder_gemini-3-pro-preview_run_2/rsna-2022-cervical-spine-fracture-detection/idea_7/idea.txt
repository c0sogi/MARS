Based on the comprehensive analysis of the previous ideas and the specific lessons learned, here is the proposed solution. This approach evolves the successful **Class-Specific Attention** architecture (Idea 4) by integrating **Multi-Scale Feature Fusion** and **Dense Auxiliary Supervision**, directly addressing the limitations of signal sparsity and resolution trade-offs identified in the lessons.

# Proposed Solution: Multi-Scale 2.5D Network with Dense Bounding-Box Supervision

## Model
The architecture is a **2.5D Multi-Scale Sequence Network** designed to balance high-level semantic understanding with the fine-grained detail required for fracture detection, while leveraging available localization data to stabilize training.

1.  **2.5D Backbone with Multi-Scale Fusion:**
    *   **Input:** The model accepts **2.5D stacks** (slices $z-1, z, z+1$) to capture local volumetric continuity (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014).
    *   **Backbone:** An **EfficientNet-B4** is used as the feature extractor.
    *   **Structural Innovation (Multi-Scale Fusion):** Instead of relying solely on the final convolutional block (which has the lowest spatial resolution, potentially losing hairline fractures), the model extracts features from both the **final block (P5)** and the **penultimate block (P4)**.
    *   Both feature maps undergo Global Average Pooling independently and are **concatenated** to form the slice embedding. This ensures the sequence model receives both abstract semantic context (P5) and higher-resolution texture details (P4) without the memory cost of maintaining full spatial maps.

2.  **Context Modeling:**
    *   **Bi-LSTM with Positional Embeddings:** The sequence of multi-scale embeddings is processed by a Bidirectional LSTM to model Z-axis anatomical continuity. Learnable **Positional Embeddings** are added to the LSTM outputs to provide explicit spatial references (e.g., top vs. bottom of spine) (Cite solution_lesson_node_00029).

3.  **Dual-Head Output Structure:**
    *   **Primary Heads (Class-Specific Attention):** 8 independent Attention Heads (C1-C7, Overall) aggregate the LSTM outputs to predict study-level probabilities. This retains the successful "disentangled" aggregation strategy (Cite solution_lesson_node_00029).
    *   **Auxiliary Head (Dense Supervision):** A simple linear layer projects the LSTM output at *each time step* to a scalar probability, predicting the presence of a fracture on that specific slice. This transforms the problem from pure Multiple Instance Learning (MIL) to **Semi-Supervised Sequence Learning**.

## Data
1.  **Preprocessing & Sampling:**
    *   **2.5D Stacking:** Inputs are constructed by stacking 3 adjacent slices.
    *   **Sampling Density:** The loader samples a sequence of **64 to 96 slices**. This prioritizes **Z-axis coverage** over in-plane resolution, adhering to the finding that reducing slice count to accommodate complex models (like Transformers) is detrimental (Cite solution_lesson_node_00032).
    *   **Volumetric Augmentation:** Geometric transformations (rotation, shift, scale) are applied **identically across the entire volume** within the `__getitem__` method to preserve physical alignment (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008, solution_lesson_node_00010).

2.  **Auxiliary Target Generation:**
    *   The `train_bounding_boxes.csv` file is processed to map fracture bounding boxes to specific Z-indices.
    *   For every training study, a binary target vector (length = sequence length) is generated. Slices falling within a bounding box are labeled `1`, others `0`. Studies without bounding boxes (but labeled positive) retain `0` (or are masked), relying on the MIL loss.

## Training
1.  **Hybrid Loss Function:**
    *   The total loss is a weighted sum: $L_{total} = L_{study} + \lambda L_{slice}$.
    *   **Study Loss ($L_{study}$):** Weighted Multi-Label Logarithmic Loss applied to the Primary Heads. Crucially, a **Positive Class Weight > 1** (e.g., 2.0) is used to maintain sensitivity (Cite solution_lesson_node_00029), avoiding the sensitivity collapse observed with misconfigured Focal Loss (Cite solution_lesson_node_00031).
    *   **Slice Loss ($L_{slice}$):** Binary Cross Entropy applied to the Auxiliary Head's dense predictions. This term is **masked** to only contribute to the gradient for studies that have ground-truth bounding box annotations. This provides strong, localized supervision to the LSTM, preventing it from "drifting" or overfitting to noise in the MIL setting.

2.  **Optimization:**
    *   **Gradient Accumulation:** Employed to maintain a stable effective batch size (e.g., 8-16 studies) despite the memory overhead of the EfficientNet-B4 backbone and multi-scale storage (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).

## Evaluation
1.  **Inference:**
    *   The test studies are processed through the backbone and LSTM.
    *   The **Primary Heads** (Class-Specific Attention) generate the final submission probabilities.
    *   The Auxiliary Head is discarded during inference; its sole purpose is to structure the latent space of the LSTM during training.
