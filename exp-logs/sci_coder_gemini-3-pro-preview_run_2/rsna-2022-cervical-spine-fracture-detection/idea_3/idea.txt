# Proposed Solution: 2.5D Multi-Head Attention Network with Class-Specific Context

## Model
The proposed architecture builds upon the successful sequential modeling of the previous iteration but introduces a **Class-Specific Attention Aggregation** mechanism to resolve the spatial distinctness of the targets (C1-C7).

1.  **2.5D Input & Backbone:**
    The model retains the **2.5D input strategy** (stacking slices $z-1, z, z+1$) to provide local volumetric context to a 2D CNN backbone (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014). To address the bottleneck in resolving subtle fracture features, the backbone capacity will be increased (e.g., from EfficientNet-B0 to EfficientNet-B3) and the input resolution raised (e.g., to 384x384) (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015).

2.  **Sequential Encoding:**
    The sequence of feature vectors extracted by the CNN will be processed by a **Bidirectional LSTM**. This preserves the successful modeling of the spine's anatomical continuity along the Z-axis (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014).

3.  **Structural Innovation: Class-Specific Attention Heads:**
    Instead of aggregating the LSTM outputs into a single global study embedding (which forces one vector to encode the state of all 7 vertebrae), the model will employ **7 independent Attention Heads**, one for each target vertebra (C1 through C7).
    *   Each head learns a unique query vector to calculate attention weights over the slice sequence.
    *   This allows the "C1 Head" to attend heavily to the top slices of the scan, while the "C7 Head" attends to the bottom, effectively performing weakly-supervised localization.
    *   The "Patient Overall" probability will be predicted by a separate head that aggregates the 7 vertebra-specific feature vectors, ensuring the global diagnosis is consistent with the local findings.

## Data
1.  **Volumetric-Consistent Augmentation:**
    The data loading pipeline will strictly enforce **Dataset-Encapsulated Augmentation**. Geometric transformations (rotation, scale, shift) will be sampled once per study and applied identically to every slice in the 2.5D stack within the `__getitem__` method. This ensures the physical alignment of the spine is preserved during training (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008, solution_lesson_node_00010).

2.  **Resolution Upgrade:**
    Given that increasing sequence length yielded diminishing returns, the computational budget will instead be allocated to higher spatial resolution (e.g., 384x384 or 512x512) to better capture fine fracture lines (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015).

## Training
1.  **Loss with Positive Weighting:**
    The model will be trained using the competition's Weighted Multi-Label Logarithmic Loss. Crucially, a **positive class weight** (e.g., `pos_weight > 1` in the loss function) will be applied to fracture targets. This directly addresses the previous model's tendency to minimize loss by predicting the majority "healthy" class, forcing it to prioritize sensitivity to fractures (Addressing failure in solution_lesson_node_00015).

2.  **Gradient Accumulation:**
    To support the larger memory footprint of the EfficientNet-B3 backbone and higher resolution without reducing the effective bag-level batch size, **Gradient Accumulation** will be employed. This ensures the optimizer sees a stable representative batch (e.g., 8-16 studies) even if the GPU can only fit 1-2 studies per forward pass (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).

## Evaluation
The inference pipeline remains consistent with the training setup:
1.  Load the test study and sample the fixed sequence of slices.
2.  Generate 2.5D stacks and pass them through the CNN-LSTM backbone.
3.  The 7 Class-Specific Attention Heads will independently extract features and predict probabilities for C1-C7.
4.  The fusion head will output the `patient_overall` probability.
