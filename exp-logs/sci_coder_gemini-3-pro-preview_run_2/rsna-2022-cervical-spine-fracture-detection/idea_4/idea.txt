# Proposed Solution: 2.5D Anatomically-Aware Multi-Head Attention Network

## Model
The proposed architecture introduces a **Class-Specific Multi-Head Attention** mechanism fortified with **Positional Embeddings** to explicitly disentangle the anatomical structure of the cervical spine.

1.  **2.5D High-Resolution Backbone:**
    The input layer constructs **2.5D stacks** (slices $z-1, z, z+1$) to provide local volumetric context to the 2D CNN (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014). Addressing the findings that sequence length is not the bottleneck, the computational budget is shifted to **increasing image resolution** (e.g., 384x384) and **backbone capacity** (e.g., EfficientNet-B4) to resolve subtle fracture lines that were previously missed (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015).

2.  **Sequential Encoding with Positional Injection:**
    The feature sequence extracted by the CNN is processed by a **Bidirectional LSTM** to model the Z-axis continuity (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014).
    *   **Structural Innovation:** A learnable **Positional Embedding** vector is added to the output features of the LSTM at each step. Since the spine has a fixed anatomical order (C1 at the top, C7 at the bottom), these embeddings provide explicit spatial cues, allowing the subsequent attention mechanism to distinguish between vertebrae even if their visual features are similar.

3.  **Anatomically-Aware Multi-Head Aggregation:**
    Instead of a single global pooling layer, the model employs **8 Independent Attention Heads** (one for each vertebra C1-C7 and one for `patient_overall`).
    *   Each head calculates its own attention weights over the sequence. Guided by the positional embeddings, the "C1 Head" learns to attend to the beginning of the sequence, while the "C7 Head" attends to the end.
    *   This utilizes **Embedding-Space Aggregation**, summing weighted feature vectors rather than probabilities, to preserve high-dimensional information for the final classifiers (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).

## Data
1.  **Dataset-Encapsulated Volumetric Augmentation:**
    To prevent overfitting while maintaining physical realism, geometric augmentations (rotation, shift, scale) are applied **consistently across the entire volume**. The transformation parameters are sampled once per study within the `Dataset.__getitem__` method and applied identically to every slice in the 2.5D stack (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008, solution_lesson_node_00010).

2.  **Sampling:**
    The loader uniformly samples a fixed sequence (e.g., 64 slices). As established, this coverage is sufficient for the Z-axis; the focus remains on the resolution of individual slices (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015).

## Training
1.  **Loss with Positive Class Weighting:**
    The model is trained using Weighted Multi-Label Logarithmic Loss. Crucially, a **Positive Class Weight** (e.g., > 2.0) is applied to the fracture targets. This forces the model to prioritize sensitivity to the minority fracture class, directly addressing the previous failure where the model minimized loss by defaulting to "healthy" predictions (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015).

2.  **Gradient Accumulation:**
    To support the memory-intensive EfficientNet-B4 backbone and higher resolution without sacrificing optimization stability, **Gradient Accumulation** is used. This ensures the effective bag-level batch size remains robust (e.g., 8-16 studies), preventing the noisy convergence observed with small physical batches (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).

## Evaluation
1.  **Inference Pipeline:**
    For each test study, the 2.5D inputs are generated and passed through the backbone and LSTM.
2.  **Disentangled Prediction:**
    The 8 specific heads independently aggregate features and output probabilities. This ensures that a fracture in C7 does not confuse the prediction for C1, as their feature extraction is spatially and attentionally decoupled.
