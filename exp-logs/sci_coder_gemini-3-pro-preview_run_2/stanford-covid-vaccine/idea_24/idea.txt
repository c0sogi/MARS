Based on the comprehensive analysis of the provided lessons and the progression of previous ideas, I propose the **Regularized Non-Linear Dense-Context Network**.

This strategy optimizes the state-of-the-art "Dense-Context" architecture (Idea 8) by addressing the "Capacity-Regularization Mismatch" (Lesson 00038) and the "Linear Compression Bottleneck." By upgrading the latent projection to a **Deep Non-Linear Bottleneck** with increased capacity (64 channels) and strict regularization, we enable the model to compress the dense history into a richer semantic message without overfitting, while adhering to all established structural constraints.

# Model

1.  **Input Representation (Partner-Aware):**
    *   **Standard Features:** We utilize **One-Hot Encoding** for `sequence`, `structure`, and `predicted_loop_type` (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).
    *   **Explicit Partner Identity:** We **retain** the explicit injection of the **Partner Base Identity** (one-hot vector of the paired base) at the input level. As established in **Lesson 00052**, removing this input cripples the encoder's ability to form basic chemical representations.

2.  **Backbone (Dense Dilated TCN):**
    *   **Architecture:** A stack of **Single-Layer Dilated Residual Blocks** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00011) utilizing **Dense Connections** (concatenating outputs of all prior blocks). This preserves the full multi-scale history required for complex interaction modeling (Cite solution_lesson_node_00047).
    *   **Configuration:** We employ exponentially increasing dilation rates ($d=1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32$) to ensure global receptive field coverage (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
    *   **Capacity & Regularization:** We strictly set the **Growth Rate / Channel Width** to **64** to avoid capacity bottlenecks (Cite solution_lesson_node_00037) and apply **Dropout (0.1)** within every block to prevent overfitting (Cite solution_lesson_node_00038).
    *   **Convolution Type:** We use **Standard Convolutions** throughout (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).

3.  **Innovation: Deep Non-Linear Latent Bottleneck:**
    *   **Critique:** Previous successful models used a simple linear 1x1 convolution to compress the dense history (~400 channels) to a latent vector (32 channels). This likely discards non-linear correlations required for the interaction step. Attempts to simply widen this (Idea 9) failed due to lack of regularization.
    *   **New Mechanism:** We replace the linear projection with a **Deep Non-Linear Bottleneck**:
        *   `Conv1d(In -> 128) -> BN -> GELU -> Dropout(0.1)`
        *   `Conv1d(128 -> 64)`
    *   **Rationale:** This allows the model to perform non-linear dimensionality reduction, distilling a richer 64-dimensional "message" from the backbone history. The added depth and dropout ensure this increased capacity does not lead to overfitting.
    *   **Unified Projection:** We apply this to the full concatenated history, avoiding manual scale partitioning (Cite solution_lesson_node_00070).

4.  **Symmetric Structural Interaction:**
    *   **Gather:** For every position $i$, we gather the 64-dimensional latent vector of its paired partner $j$ (Cite solution_lesson_node_00031).
    *   **Null-Masking:** We explicitly apply a **Zero-Mask** to the gathered features for unpaired bases, ensuring orthogonal representation for missing links (Cite solution_lesson_node_00071).
    *   **Fusion:** We concatenate the local latent vector (64 dim) with the gathered partner vector (64 dim). This maintains **Symmetry** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00073) and uses simple **Concatenation** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00066).

5.  **Global Aggregation (BiGRU):**
    *   The fused features (128 dim) are processed by a **Bidirectional GRU** to capture global constraints (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
    *   **Constraint:** Hidden size is set to `input_dim // 2` (64) to ensure the output dimension matches the input (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).

6.  **Output Head:**
    *   Linear projection to the 5 target variables.

    *   Use **MCRMSE** (Mean Columnwise Root Mean Squared Error).
    *   **Strategy:** Calculate loss **only on the three scored columns** (`reactivity`, `deg_Mg_pH10`, `deg_Mg_50C`).
    *   **Rationale:** We strictly adhere to **Lesson 00016**, masking out auxiliary targets (`deg_pH10`, `deg_50C`) to prevent negative transfer and align the optimization objective directly with the competition metric.
