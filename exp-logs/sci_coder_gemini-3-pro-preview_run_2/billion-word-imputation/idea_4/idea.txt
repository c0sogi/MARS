# Optimized Strategy: Two-Stage Cascade with Sequence Tagging and Masked In-Filling

Based on the empirical success of the dual-head architecture in decoupling localization and prediction, and addressing the limitations of fixed-vocabulary generation, I propose a **Two-Stage Cascade** strategy. This approach evolves the "Previous Ideas" by splitting the problem into two specialized sub-tasks: high-speed structural localization and high-capacity semantic in-filling.

### Model
The solution utilizes two distinct Transformer models arranged in a pipeline:

1.  **Stage 1: The Locator (Structural Encoder)**
    *   **Architecture:** A lightweight Transformer encoder (e.g., `distilroberta-base`) designed for efficiency.
    *   **Head:** A **Token Classification Head** (Linear + Sigmoid) that outputs a scalar probability for each token.
    *   **Function:** This component implements the **sequence tagging** strategy (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004). Instead of predicting the word directly, it solely predicts the *probability that a gap exists immediately after the current token*. This preserves the $O(1)$ search efficiency of the tagging approach while using a Transformer backbone to capture better syntactic dependencies than the Bi-LSTM.

2.  **Stage 2: The In-Filler (Semantic Decoder)**
    *   **Architecture:** A larger, higher-capacity Transformer (e.g., `roberta-base` or `deberta-v3-base`) capable of deep semantic understanding.
    *   **Head:** The standard **Masked Language Modeling (MLM) Head**.
    *   **Function:** This component takes the sentence with a `<mask>` token inserted at the coordinates found by the Locator. It uses the full pre-trained vocabulary and context-aware representations to predict the missing token. This overcomes the "fixed vocabulary" and "sub-word alignment" limitations of the previous dual-head designs.

### Data
The training data will be processed to create two distinct datasets, maximizing the utility of the provided `train_v2.txt`:

1.  **For the Locator:**
    *   **Synthetic Corruption:** We uniformly remove one word from each training sentence.
    *   **Label Generation:** We generate a binary label sequence where the token *immediately preceding* the removed word is labeled `1` (positive), and all others are `0` (negative). This aligns with the **sequence tagging** lesson (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).

2.  **For the In-Filler:**
    *   **Domain Adaptation:** We use the original sentences and apply dynamic masking (masking random words) to fine-tune the model on the specific linguistic style of the corpus.
    *   **Target:** The model learns to reconstruct the masked word, minimizing the perplexity on the training set.

### Training
We will employ a decoupled training strategy to optimize each stage:

1.  **Locator Training:**
    *   **Loss Function:** Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) Loss applied to the per-token classification outputs.
    *   **Strategy:** We train this model to maximize the F1-score of gap detection. Since the classes are heavily imbalanced (one gap vs. many non-gaps), we will use weighted BCE or Focal Loss.

2.  **In-Filler Training:**
    *   **Loss Function:** Standard Cross-Entropy Loss over the vocabulary (MLM Objective).
    *   **Strategy:** We fine-tune the pre-trained model on the training data for 1-2 epochs. This ensures the model favors words and phrasings common in the dataset (e.g., specific entities or formalisms) over generic pre-training knowledge.

### Evaluation
Inference is performed in a deterministic two-step cascade for each test sentence:

1.  **Localization (Pass 1):**
    *   Feed the raw test sentence into the **Locator**.
    *   Identify the token index $i$ with the maximum probability from the output head. This is the predicted insertion point (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).

2.  **Prediction (Pass 2):**
    *   Construct a new sentence by inserting a `<mask>` token immediately after token $i$.
    *   Feed this masked sentence into the **In-Filler**.
    *   Extract the probability distribution for the `<mask>` token.
    *   **Decoding:** Select the highest-probability token. To ensure the output is a valid "word" (as per the task description), we filter the predictions to exclude special tokens or sub-word fragments that do not form complete words (e.g., requiring the token to start with the tokenizer's "start-of-word" marker).
    *   Format the final sentence with the predicted word inserted at the correct location.
