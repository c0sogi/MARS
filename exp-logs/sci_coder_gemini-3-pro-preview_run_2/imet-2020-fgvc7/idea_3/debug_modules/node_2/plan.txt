The issue stems from the `AsymmetricLoss` function in `library/loss.py`, which manually sets `torch.set_grad_enabled(True)` at the end of its forward pass. This side effect overrides the `torch.no_grad()` context used during validation, causing subsequent forward passes to track gradients. Consequently, attempting to convert the resulting tensors to NumPy arrays in `library/models.py` raises a `RuntimeError`. The fix involves detaching the tensors in `library/models.py` before conversion (as the primary defense) and correcting `library/loss.py` to restore the previous gradient state instead of forcing it to `True`.
