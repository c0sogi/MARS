Based on the comprehensive analysis of the previous ideas and the empirical lessons learned, I propose the following optimized strategy. This solution structurally evolves the successful EfficientNet-B4 baseline by applying the principle of **Compound Scaling** to the next tier (EfficientNet-B5) while rigorously correcting the regularization strategy that caused the failure of the EfficientNetV2-M attempt.

### Model
I propose upgrading the backbone to **EfficientNet-B5**.
*   **Structural Innovation:** We will advance the **Compound Scaling** strategy `Cite solution_lesson_node_00008` by moving from the B4 to the B5 architecture. While the previous attempt at a larger model (EfficientNetV2-M) failed, the analysis suggests this was due to the incompatibility of CutMix with localized pathologies `Cite solution_lesson_node_00013`, rather than model capacity itself. By pairing the B5 backbone (which offers increased width, depth, and receptive field) with the correct regularization, we aim to capture the fine-grained features of the "multiple_diseases" class that smaller models miss.
*   **Head Design:** The classification head will consist of a Global Average Pooling layer, followed by a Dropout layer (rate 0.4), and a final Linear layer with 4 outputs.

### Data
*   **Preprocessing:**
    *   **Resolution:** Input images will be resized to **456x456 pixels**. This is the native resolution for EfficientNet-B5, ensuring we match the input detail to the model's increased capacity `Cite solution_lesson_node_00008`.
*   **Augmentation:**
    *   **CoarseDropout (Restored):** We will explicitly reject CutMix and revert to **CoarseDropout**, as area-based mixing introduces label noise for small, localized disease spots `Cite solution_lesson_node_00013`.
    *   **Scaling Occlusion:** We will scale the occlusion parameters to match the new 456x456 resolution. We will use large hole sizes (e.g., max 100x100) to force distributed feature learning `Cite solution_lesson_node_00006` and strictly enforce **minimum hole dimensions** (e.g., 16x16) to prevent trivial occlusions `Cite solution_lesson_node_00005`.
    *   **Exclusions:** We will continue to exclude global photometric distortions to preserve color semantics `Cite solution_lesson_node_00004`.

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** We will implement a **Weighted Label-Smoothed Cross-Entropy Loss**.
    *   **Combination:** We will combine **Inverse Class Frequency Weights** `Cite solution_lesson_node_00001` to handle the severe class imbalance with **Label Smoothing (0.1)** `Cite solution_lesson_node_00002` to prevent overconfidence in the larger B5 model. This addresses the metric requirement (Mean Column-wise ROC AUC) while regularizing the increased model capacity.
*   **Optimizer:** We will use the **AdamW** optimizer.
*   **Scheduler:** A **Cosine Annealing** scheduler will be used to decay the learning rate over the training duration.
*   **Stability:** We will strictly maintain a batch size of at least **16** to ensure optimization stability `Cite solution_lesson_node_00014`.
*   **Reproducibility:** We will enforce determinism by passing a `worker_init_fn` to the DataLoaders to seed workers individually, eliminating stochastic noise from the evaluation `Cite solution_lesson_node_00009`.

### Evaluation
*   **Inference:** We will apply **Test Time Augmentation (TTA)** by averaging predictions from the original image and a horizontally flipped version `Cite solution_lesson_node_00002`.
*   **Metric:** The model will be evaluated using the Mean Column-wise ROC AUC.
