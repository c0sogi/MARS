Based on the accumulated empirical lessons, I propose the following optimized strategy. This solution introduces a **Heterogeneous Ensemble with Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling** to specifically target the localized nature of the disease artifacts, while implementing a **Rank-Calibrated Stacking** pipeline to rigorously solve the calibration drift identified in previous cross-validation attempts.

# Model
I propose a **Heterogeneous Ensemble** consisting of **EfficientNetV2-L** and **ConvNeXt-Base**, enhanced with **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling**.

*   **Structural Innovation (GeM Pooling):** I will replace the standard Global Average Pooling (GAP) layer in both backbones with **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling**.
    *   **Rationale:** Previous lessons established that the disease indicators are often small, localized spots (rust pustules, scab lesions) rather than global textures `Cite solution_lesson_node_00006`. Standard GAP averages the entire feature map, diluting the strong signal of a small lesion with the large background of the healthy leaf. GeM Pooling ($f(x) = (\frac{1}{N}\sum x^p)^{1/p}$) introduces a learnable parameter $p$. As $p \to \infty$, it approaches Max Pooling. This allows the network to automatically focus on high-activation regions (the lesions) while retaining more context than pure Max Pooling, structurally aligning the architecture with the fine-grained nature of the task.
*   **Backbones:**
    *   **EfficientNetV2-L:** Selected for its superior compound scaling and training efficiency `Cite solution_lesson_node_00008`.
    *   **ConvNeXt-Base:** Selected to provide a distinct inductive bias (large-kernel convolutions) complementary to EfficientNet.
*   **Head Design:** Both models will utilize the **Multi-Label Decomposition** head, outputting 2 binary logits ("Is Rust" and "Is Scab"). This enables the model to learn shared representations for the "Multiple Diseases" class, which is semantically the intersection of the two `Cite solution_lesson_node_00020`.

# Data
*   **Preprocessing:**
    *   **Resolution:** We will match the input resolution to the native design of each backbone to maximize feature fidelity `Cite solution_lesson_node_00008`: **480x480** for EfficientNetV2-L and **384x384** for ConvNeXt-Base.
*   **Augmentation:**
    *   **CoarseDropout:** We will apply scaled CoarseDropout (e.g., max hole 100x100) to force distributed feature learning `Cite solution_lesson_node_00006`.
    *   **Constraints:** We will strictly enforce **minimum hole dimensions** (e.g., 16x16) to prevent the generation of trivial, pixel-level occlusions that fail to regularize the model `Cite solution_lesson_node_00005`.
    *   **Exclusions:** We will exclude CutMix due to the risk of label noise on localized pathologies `Cite solution_lesson_node_00013` and global photometric distortions to preserve color semantics `Cite solution_lesson_node_00004`.

# Training
*   **Strategy:** We will implement **5-Fold Cross-Validation** for both architectures.
*   **Loss Function:** We will use **Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) Loss** combined with:
    *   **Inverse Class Frequency Weights:** To explicitly handle the imbalance between positive and negative samples for Rust and Scab `Cite solution_lesson_node_00001`.
    *   **Label Smoothing (0.05):** To prevent overconfidence in the high-capacity backbones `Cite solution_lesson_node_00002`.
*   **Optimization:**
    *   **Optimizer:** **AdamW** for stable convergence.
    *   **Stability:** We will enforce a batch size of at least **16** to ensure stable Batch Normalization statistics `Cite solution_lesson_node_00014`.
    *   **Reproducibility:** A `worker_init_fn` will be used to seed DataLoaders, ensuring deterministic data presentation `Cite solution_lesson_node_00009`.

# Evaluation
*   **Structural Innovation (Rank-Calibrated Stacking):** To address the **calibration drift** observed when concatenating Out-Of-Fold (OOF) predictions `Cite solution_lesson_node_00030`, we will implement a rank-based stacking meta-learner.
    1.  **Rank Normalization:** For each fold, the OOF predictions (probabilities) will be converted to **Percentile Ranks** (0 to 1). This removes fold-specific calibration biases while preserving the discriminative ordering.
    2.  **Meta-Learner:** We will train two **Logistic Regression** models (one for "Rust", one for "Scab") using the *ranked* OOF predictions from both backbones as input features. This allows the ensemble to learn the optimal weighting between EfficientNet and ConvNeXt based on their relative ranking reliability.
    3.  **Inference:**
        *   Generate TTA predictions (Horizontal Flip `Cite solution_lesson_node_00002`) for the test set using all 10 base models.
        *   Convert test predictions to percentile ranks.
        *   Feed ranks to the Logistic Regression meta-learners to obtain calibrated $P(Rust)$ and $P(Scab)$.
        *   **Reconstruction:** Calculate the final 4-class probabilities:
            *   `Healthy`: $(1 - P_r)(1 - P_s)$
            *   `Rust`: $P_r(1 - P_s)$
            *   `Scab`: $(1 - P_r)P_s$
            *   `Multiple`: $P_r P_s$
