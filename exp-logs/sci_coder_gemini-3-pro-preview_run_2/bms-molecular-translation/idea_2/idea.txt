To establish a reliable and high-speed baseline, I propose a **Global Context Image-to-Sequence Network**.

This approach simplifies the "Show and Tell" paradigm (Model 3) by removing the computationally expensive attention mechanism. It differs fundamentally from the CRNN+CTC approach (Idea 1) because it compresses the entire image into a single semantic vector rather than processing it as a spatial sequence of columns. This architecture treats the problem as a pure image captioning task, prioritizing inference speed and architectural simplicity.

### Model
The architecture consists of a lightweight encoder-decoder structure:
1.  **Global Feature Encoder:** A highly efficient CNN backbone, such as **MobileNetV2**, is used to extract features. Unlike the CRNN approach which preserves spatial dimensions, this encoder utilizes **Global Average Pooling** at the final convolutional layer. This operation condenses the entire visual content of the molecule into a single, fixed-length 1D feature vector, effectively creating a "fingerprint" of the image.
2.  **State Projector:** A simple linear layer projects this 1D feature vector to match the hidden state dimension of the decoder.
3.  **Autoregressive Decoder:** A single-layer **LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory)** network serves as the decoder. The projected image vector is used to initialize the LSTM's hidden and cell states (the "thought vector"). The LSTM then generates the InChI string character-by-character based solely on this initial visual context and the sequence of previously generated characters.

### Data
*   **Preprocessing:** Images will be resized to a standard fixed square resolution (e.g., 224x224 pixels) to enable efficient batching and compatibility with the pre-trained encoder. Pixel values will be normalized to the standard range expected by the backbone (typically 0-1 or standardized using ImageNet statistics).
*   **Tokenization:** The InChI strings will be tokenized at the character level. The vocabulary will include all unique characters found in the training labels plus special tokens: `<SOS>` (Start of Sequence), `<EOS>` (End of Sequence), and `<PAD>` (Padding).
*   **Formatting:** Target sequences will be padded to a fixed maximum length to facilitate tensor operations during training.

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** The model will be trained using **Cross-Entropy Loss**, comparing the predicted probability distribution of characters at each time step against the ground truth character.
*   **Strategy:** **Teacher Forcing** will be employed. During training, the input to the LSTM at time step $t$ will be the *actual* ground truth character from time step $t-1$, rather than the model's own prediction. This stabilizes training and helps the model converge faster.
*   **Optimization:** A standard optimizer like Adam will be used. Since the encoder is pre-trained, a lower learning rate may be applied to the CNN backbone compared to the LSTM decoder to preserve learned visual features.

### Evaluation
*   **Prediction:** During inference, the test image is passed through the encoder to initialize the LSTM. The generation process starts by feeding the `<SOS>` token into the LSTM.
*   **Decoding:** A **Greedy Decoding** method will be used for maximum efficiency. At each time step, the model selects the character with the highest softmax probability. This predicted character is then fed back into the LSTM as the input for the next time step.
*   **Termination:** Generation continues until the model outputs the `<EOS>` token or reaches a predefined maximum sequence length. The resulting list of characters is concatenated to form the predicted InChI string.
