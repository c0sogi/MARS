Based on the lessons learned, particularly the critical failure of the CTC-based approach due to insufficient encoder output resolution (Lesson ID: `solution_lesson_node_00012`), I propose a **High-Resolution Hybrid CTC-Attention Network**.

This solution evolves the previous "CNN-Transformer" ideas by introducing a **Dual-Head** architecture. It addresses the "density" problem by structurally enforcing high-resolution feature extraction and combines the alignment-learning strengths of CTC with the syntactic robustness of an Autoregressive Transformer.

### Model
The architecture is designed as a multi-task Encoder-Decoder network:
1.  **Anisotropic Visual Backbone:** A **ResNet-34** or **EfficientNet-B2** serves as the feature extractor. To prevent the "Capacity Constraint Violation" identified in the lessons, the network's downsampling operations are modified to be **anisotropic**. Specifically, while the vertical dimension ($H$) is downsampled normally (e.g., 32x), the horizontal dimension ($W$) downsampling is strictly limited (e.g., max 4x or 8x). This guarantees that the output feature sequence length $T$ strictly exceeds the target label length $L$ for all samples (Cite solution_lesson_node_00012).
2.  **Transformer Encoder:** The resulting high-resolution feature map is collapsed vertically and projected into a sequence of vectors. A stack of Transformer Encoder layers processes this sequence to model global interactions between distant molecular parts.
3.  **Hybrid Decoding Heads:**
    *   **CTC Head:** A linear layer attached directly to the Encoder output. It predicts the character sequence based on local features. Its primary role is to force the Encoder to learn strict alignment between image columns and characters.
    *   **Attention Decoder:** A standard Transformer Decoder that attends to the Encoder outputs (Cross-Attention) and generates the InChI string autoregressively. This head handles the complex syntax and grammar of InChI strings which CTC often struggles with.

### Data
1.  **Fixed-Height Preprocessing:** Instead of resizing to a fixed square (which distorts aspect ratios and reduces width resolution), images are resized to a **fixed height** (e.g., 256 pixels) while maintaining their aspect ratio. The width is padded to the nearest multiple of the backbone's stride, up to a defined maximum (e.g., 2048 pixels). This ensures the horizontal pixel density remains high enough to distinguish atomic subscripts.
2.  **Tokenization:** A unified character-level vocabulary is created, including the CTC "blank" token and the Autoregressive special tokens (`<SOS>`, `<EOS>`, `<PAD>`).

### Training
1.  **Joint Loss Optimization:** The model is trained using a weighted sum of two loss functions: $\mathcal{L} = \lambda \mathcal{L}_{CTC} + (1-\lambda) \mathcal{L}_{CE}$.
    *   $\mathcal{L}_{CTC}$ is applied to the Encoder's CTC Head output. This acts as a strong regularizer, ensuring the encoder features are aligned with the text (Cite solution_lesson_node_00012).
    *   $\mathcal{L}_{CE}$ (Cross-Entropy) is applied to the Attention Decoder's output with Teacher Forcing.
2.  **Optimizer:** An **AdamW** optimizer is used with a "One Cycle" or "Cosine Annealing" learning rate schedule to stabilize the training of the Transformer components.

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** Test images are preprocessed (fixed-height resize) and passed through the Encoder.
2.  **Decoding:** The prediction is generated using the **Attention Decoder** with **Beam Search**. The CTC head is primarily used for feature alignment during training, but it can optionally be used to rescore beam hypotheses during inference if needed. This ensures the final output respects the strict syntax of InChI strings while benefiting from the robust visual features learned via the hybrid objective.
