# Model: Hybrid ConvNeXt-1D with Cadence-Aware Gating

I propose a structural evolution that combines a modern large-kernel CNN backbone with a specialized 1D temporal head. This addresses the need to capture long diagonal lines (spatial geometry) and the specific "ON-OFF" periodicity (temporal logic) simultaneously.

*   **Backbone (ConvNeXt-Tiny):** I will replace the EfficientNet backbone with **ConvNeXt-Tiny**.
    *   *Rationale:* ConvNeXt utilizes **7x7 kernels** (vs. 3x3 in EfficientNet). This larger receptive field is structurally superior for detecting the continuous **diagonal lines** caused by Doppler drifts, which span long spatial distances across the spectrogram **Cite Lesson 00004**.
*   **Input Representation:** The 6 cadence panels will be vertically stacked to form a `(1638, 256)` input tensor. This preserves the geometric continuity of drifting signals across time steps **Cite Lesson 00004**.
*   **Frequency Pooling (Neck):** Instead of applying Global Pooling immediately to the 2D feature map, I will apply **Global Average Pooling only along the Frequency (Width) axis**.
    *   *Mechanism:* This collapses the `(C, H, W)` feature map into a `(C, H)` sequence, where `H` represents the vertical time axis (spanning all 6 cadence panels).
*   **Cadence-Aware Head:** This 1D sequence will be processed by a lightweight **1D Convolutional block** (kernel size 3, stride 1).
    *   *Purpose:* This layer explicitly models the temporal structure, allowing the network to learn the specific "Active-Inactive-Active" (A-B-A) periodicity of technosignatures, distinguishing them from RFI which often persists across all panels.
*   **Global Pooling:** The final aggregation will use **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling** on the time dimension.
    *   *Rationale:* GeM Pooling (`p=3.0`) is proven to isolate sparse "needle" signals from the noise floor better than Average Pooling, while being more robust to outliers than Max Pooling **Cite Lesson 00013**.

# Data

*   **Preprocessing:**
    *   **Vertical Stacking:** Concatenate the 6 panels of the `.npy` file vertically.
    *   **Instance Normalization:** Normalize each sample to zero mean and unit variance to handle varying noise floors.
    *   **Channel Expansion:** Replicate the single channel 3 times to utilize pretrained weights.
*   **Augmentation:**
    *   **Horizontal Flip:** Randomly reverse the frequency axis. This is critical to capture both positive and negative Doppler drift slopes **Cite Lesson 00009**.
    *   **CoarseDropout (Cutout):** Apply random rectangular masking with **moderate intensity** (probability ~0.25). This forces the model to learn distributed signal features **Cite Lesson 00016**, but the probability is capped to avoid destroying the sparse "needle" signals entirely **Cite Lesson 00017**.
    *   **Mixup:** Apply Mixup regularization (alpha=1.0) to blend samples, preventing the model from memorizing specific background noise patterns **Cite Lesson 00013**.

# Training

*   **Loss Function:** **BCEWithLogitsLoss** to optimize binary classification.
*   **Optimizer:** **AdamW** with a starting learning rate of `1e-4` and weight decay.
*   **Scheduler:** **Cosine Annealing Warm Restarts** to navigate the loss landscape effectively.
*   **Precision:** Mixed Precision (FP16) training on the NVIDIA A100 to maximize batch size and throughput.

# Evaluation

*   **Test Time Augmentation (TTA):** For each test sample, predictions will be generated for both the original and a **horizontally flipped** version. The final probability will be the average of these two, leveraging the domain symmetry for robustness **Cite Lesson 00009**.
