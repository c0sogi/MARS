Based on the lessons learned from the previous experiments, I propose the following optimized strategy. This solution structurally evolves the ensemble approach by replacing the numerically unstable MaxViT architecture with a robust Swin Transformer V2, while maintaining the high capacity and resolution necessary for fine-grained disease detection.

### Model
I propose a **Heterogeneous Large-Scale Ensemble** comprising **ConvNeXt-Large** and **Swin Transformer V2-Large**.
*   **Architecture 1 (ConvNeXt-Large):** I will retain the **ConvNeXt-Large** backbone from Idea 4. Its Layer Normalization design makes it robust to batch size variations (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006), and its increased capacity allows it to capture the fine-grained texture details of diseases like "rust" and "powdery_mildew" better than smaller variants (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
*   **Architecture 2 (SwinV2-Large):** I will replace the MaxViT architecture (which caused the NaN failures in Idea 4) with **Swin Transformer V2-Large**. Swin V2 is explicitly designed for stability at scale and high resolution. Unlike MaxViT, it has a proven track record of numerical stability in mixed-precision training while providing the necessary global attention mechanism to complement the CNN's local features.
*   **Head:** Both backbones will utilize **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling** followed by a linear classifier, preserving the design that effectively captures salient lesion features.

### Data
*   **Resolution:** The input resolution will be standardized to **384x384** pixels. While 512x512 was attempted in Idea 4, it likely contributed to the numerical instability. 384x384 strikes the optimal balance, significantly outperforming 256x256 (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003) while remaining computationally safe for "Large" models on a single A100.
*   **Augmentation:** The pipeline will incorporate **CoarseDropout** (randomly masking rectangular regions). This occlusion-based regularization is critical for large models, forcing them to learn distributed feature representations and preventing overfitting to specific visual cues (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008).

### Training
*   **Gradient Accumulation:** To train these "Large" architectures without reducing the physical batch size to unstable levels, I will strictly employ Gradient Accumulation. This ensures the **effective batch size remains at 32**, preventing the noisy gradients and Batch Norm degradation associated with small physical batches (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
*   **Checkpointing:** I will implement the **`copy.deepcopy(model.state_dict())`** method for saving the best model. This guarantees that the stored weights are an immutable snapshot of peak performance, preventing the "reference vs. value" bug where the best model is overwritten by subsequent updates (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
*   **Loss Function:** I will revert to **BCEWithLogitsLoss**. While ASL was proposed in Idea 4, the primary failure mode was numerical instability. Given the high capacity of the new ensemble, standard BCE is a safer, more stable objective that has proven effective in previous successful runs (Idea 2).

### Evaluation
*   **NaN-Safe Ensemble Aggregation:** I will implement a "validity gate" during the ensemble process. Instead of naive averaging, the inference loop will check for `NaN` or `Inf` values in the predictions of each model. If a model outputs invalid values (as MaxViT did in Idea 4), it will be excluded from the voting for that sample. This ensures the system degrades gracefully rather than failing catastrophically (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015).
*   **Test Time Augmentation (TTA):** Inference will be performed on both the original and horizontally flipped versions of each image. The predictions will be averaged (subject to the NaN check) to leverage the "free" accuracy boost provided by invariant feature averaging (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
