### Model
I propose a **Heterogeneous Dual-Backbone Ensemble** to maximize feature diversity and classification performance.
*   **Architecture 1 (Evolution):** **ConvNeXt-Base**. This scales up the successful `ConvNeXt-Small` architecture from the previous idea. By retaining the Layer Normalization design, we preserve the model's stability under smaller physical batch sizes (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006), while the increased capacity ("Base" vs "Small") allows for capturing more intricate disease patterns (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
*   **Architecture 2 (Innovation):** **Swin Transformer V2-Base**. I introduce a Hierarchical Vision Transformer as a structural complement to the ConvNeXt model. Swin V2 utilizes shifted window attention, providing a distinct inductive bias compared to the convolutional approach of ConvNeXt. This structural diversity ensures that the models make uncorrelated errors, which is critical for effective ensembling.
*   **Head Design:** Both backbones will be equipped with a **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling** layer followed by a linear classifier. GeM pooling is a learnable operation that focuses on salient features (like specific lesions) more effectively than standard Global Average Pooling, which is beneficial for fine-grained disease detection.

### Data
*   **Resolution:** Input images will be resized to **384x384** pixels. This resolution has been empirically proven to outperform 256x256 by resolving the fine-grained details necessary for distinguishing specific leaf diseases (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
*   **Augmentation:** The pipeline will include **CoarseDropout** (randomly masking rectangular regions). Although this increases training difficulty, it forces the model to learn distributed feature representations and prevents overfitting to specific visual cues, significantly improving generalization (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008).

### Training
*   **Gradient Accumulation:** To accommodate the larger "Base" variants of ConvNeXt and Swin Transformer on a single GPU without compromising training stability, I will use Gradient Accumulation. This allows us to use a small physical batch size (to fit in VRAM) while optimizing on an effective batch size of 32, avoiding the noisy gradients associated with small batches (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
*   **Checkpointing:** I will implement a custom callback that uses `copy.deepcopy(model.state_dict())` when saving the best model. This ensures that the stored weights are an immutable snapshot of the peak performance, preventing the "reference vs. value" bug where the best model is overwritten by subsequent updates (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
*   **Schedule:** The training duration will be extended to **20-25 epochs**. This extension is necessary to allow the higher-capacity "Base" models to converge, especially given the increased difficulty introduced by the CoarseDropout regularization (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
*   **Loss:** The models will be optimized using **BCEWithLogitsLoss** to handle the multi-label nature of the target variables.

### Evaluation
*   **Test Time Augmentation (TTA):** Inference will be performed on both the original and horizontally flipped versions of each test image. The predictions will be averaged to smooth out invariance errors (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
*   **Ensemble Strategy:** The final predictions will be generated using **Soft Voting**. The sigmoid probabilities from the ConvNeXt-Base and Swin V2-Base models (after TTA) will be averaged. A fixed threshold of 0.5 will then be applied to the averaged probabilities to generate the final space-delimited labels.
