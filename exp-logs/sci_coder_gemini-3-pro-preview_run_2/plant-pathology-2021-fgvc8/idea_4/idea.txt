Based on the progression of previous ideas and the specific empirical lessons learned, I propose the following optimized strategy. This approach evolves the ensemble concept from Idea 3 by integrating a more efficient high-resolution architecture (MaxViT) and upgrading the optimization objective to better handle the multi-label distribution.

### Model
I propose a **High-Resolution Hybrid Ensemble** consisting of **ConvNeXt-Large** and **MaxViT-Base**.
*   **Structural Innovation (MaxViT):** I will replace the Swin Transformer with **MaxViT (Multi-Axis Vision Transformer)**. While Swin (used in Idea 3) relies on shifted windows to approximate global context, MaxViT utilizes a novel **Multi-Axis Attention** mechanism (combining Block Attention for local interactions and Grid Attention for global sparse interactions). This allows the model to achieve a global receptive field with linear complexity, which is critical for connecting distant disease lesions on high-resolution leaf images without the computational quadratic explosion of standard ViTs.
*   **Scaling (ConvNeXt-Large):** I will scale the Convolutional branch from ConvNeXt-Base to **ConvNeXt-Large**. Since the "Computational Budget" allows for heavier solutions and previous runs were well under the time limit, this increase in capacity allows the model to capture even finer-grained texture details of diseases like "rust" and "powdery_mildew" (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
*   **Head:** Both models will utilize a **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling** layer followed by a linear classifier, preserving the successful head design from Idea 3.

### Data
*   **Resolution:** The input resolution will be increased to **512x512** pixels. Previous experiments showed that resolution is the primary bottleneck for this fine-grained task, and 384x384 yielded significant gains over 256x256 (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003). Moving to 512x512 exploits the A100's capacity to resolve minute lesion details that distinguish "complex" cases.
*   **Augmentation:** The pipeline will retain **CoarseDropout** (randomly masking regions). This occlusion-based regularization is essential for forcing the larger models to learn distributed feature representations rather than overfitting to specific visual cues (Cite solution_lesson_node_00008).

### Training
*   **Loss Function (Structural Innovation):** I will replace the standard Binary Cross-Entropy with **Asymmetric Loss (ASL)**. The data analysis revealed a class imbalance (e.g., "scab" is 3x more frequent than "rust") and a multi-label setting where negatives vastly outnumber positives. ASL dynamically down-weights "easy negative" samples (background or irrelevant diseases), focusing the gradient on hard misclassifications and the minority classes, which provides a better optimization landscape for this specific dataset distribution.
*   **Gradient Accumulation:** To enable the training of Large/Base models at 512x512 resolution without reducing the batch size to unstable levels, I will use Gradient Accumulation. This ensures the effective batch size remains at **32**, preventing the Batch Normalization instability observed when physical batch sizes drop below 16 (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
*   **Checkpointing:** I will strictly implement the **`copy.deepcopy(model.state_dict())`** method for saving the best model. This ensures the stored weights are a true snapshot of peak performance and are not mutated by subsequent optimizer steps (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
*   **Schedule:** The training duration will be set to **20 epochs** with a Cosine Annealing scheduler. The combination of heavier models, higher resolution, and CoarseDropout regularization necessitates this extended convergence time (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).

### Evaluation
*   **Test Time Augmentation (TTA):** Inference will be performed on the original and horizontally flipped versions of each image, with predictions averaged. This leverages the LayerNorm-based architectures' stability to provide a "free" accuracy boost (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
*   **Ensemble Strategy:** The final predictions will be generated via **Soft Voting**, averaging the sigmoid probabilities from the ConvNeXt-Large and MaxViT-Base models before applying a fixed threshold of 0.5.
