Based on the comprehensive empirical analysis and the derived lessons, I propose the implementation of a **Dual-View Hybrid ResFunnel Network**.

This strategy represents a structural evolution of the successful "Hybrid Attention-ResFunnel" (Idea 9). It synthesizes the sequence modeling power of Transformers (Cite Lesson 29) with the robust, position-specific signal preservation of shallow embeddings (Cite Lesson 1, Lesson 2). The core innovation is a **Dual-View Categorical Encoder** that fuses a "Contextual View" (Transformer output) with a "Local View" (Raw Embeddings) before entering the deep backbone, ensuring that the model captures both complex inter-character dependencies and simple, high-precision character identities without information loss.

# Model

The architecture is a multi-stream network that fuses dual categorical representations with raw continuous signals into a deep, compressive residual backbone.

1.  **Categorical Processing: The Dual-View Encoder**
    *   **Input**: The `f_27` feature is decomposed into 10 distinct character tokens (Cite Lesson 1).
    *   **Shared Embedding**: Tokens are passed through a shared embedding layer (dimension 32).
    *   **View 1: Contextual Representation (Deep)**
        *   **Positional Encoding**: Learnable Positional Embeddings are added to the token vectors to explicitly encode sequence order (Cite Lesson 30).
        *   **Transformer**: The sequence is processed by a **Transformer Encoder** (2 layers). This captures the "grammar" and non-linear dependencies between characters (e.g., "A" at pos 1 modifies "B" at pos 5).
        *   **Output**: The resulting sequence is flattened.
    *   **View 2: Local Representation (Shallow)**
        *   **Identity**: The raw output of the shared embedding layer is taken directly (without positional addition or Transformer processing) and flattened.
        *   **Rationale**: Flattening the raw embeddings implicitly encodes position (via feature index) and preserves the exact identity of the characters. This provides a "linear shortcut" for the backbone to access simple, strong signals that might be over-smoothed or diluted by the Transformer's mixing (Synthesizing Lesson 2 and Lesson 29).

2.  **Continuous Processing**
    *   **Input**: The 30 numerical features are standardized.
    *   **Preservation**: These features are maintained in their raw, normalized form. No early projection is applied to avoid signal dilution (Cite Lesson 38).

3.  **Fusion Layer**
    *   **Concatenation**: The **Flattened Contextual View**, the **Flattened Local View**, and the **Raw Continuous Features** are concatenated into a single dense vector.
    *   **Rationale**: This "Late Fusion" strategy (Cite Lesson 32) via concatenation (Cite Lesson 35) ensures the backbone has access to the full spectrum of information: global context, local identity, and machine state.

4.  **Backbone: Residual Funnel Gated Network (ResFunnel-GLU)**
    *   **Projection**: The fused vector is projected to an initial width of 512.
    *   **Structure**: The backbone follows the **ResFunnel** topology (Cite Lesson 15), consisting of 3 stages with progressively decreasing widths (512 $\to$ 256 $\to$ 128).
    *   **Blocks**: Each stage employs stacked **Residual Gated Blocks** ($x_{out} = x + \text{Block}(x)$) to enable depth (Cite Lesson 17).
    *   **Components**:
        *   **Gating**: Blocks use **Gated Linear Units (GLU)** with **Sigmoid** activation for the gate branch to ensure bounded feature selection (Cite Lesson 13).
        *   **Regularization**: Aggressive **Dropout** (rate 0.35) is applied within blocks (Cite Lesson 16) alongside **Batch Normalization** (Cite Lesson 7).
    *   **Downsampling**: Transitions between stages use **Projected Residual Connections** (linear projection on the skip path) to strictly preserve gradient flow (Cite Lesson 24).

5.  **Output Head**
    *   The final stage output (width 128) is passed through a Linear layer with Sigmoid activation.

# Data

1.  **Decomposition**: The string feature `f_27` is strictly split into 10 integer-encoded columns.
2.  **Normalization**: Continuous features (`f_00` - `f_30`) are Z-score normalized.
3.  **Splitting**: Use the metadata-defined stratified validation split.

# Training

1.  **Optimizer**: Use **AdamW** with a high weight decay of `1e-2`. This aggressive regularization is critical to constrain the parameters of the Transformer, the expanded fusion layer, and the deep backbone (Cite Lesson 8).
2.  **Loss & Metric**: Optimize Binary Cross-Entropy. Crucially, the training loop must **decouple the stopping criterion from the loss function**. Training should continue as long as the **Validation AUC** improves, even if the validation loss plateaus, to fully refine the ranking of difficult examples (Cite Lesson 3).
3.  **Schedule**: Use a **Step Learning Rate Scheduler** (e.g., decay by 0.1 every 10 epochs). This is strictly necessary to achieve high-precision convergence in hybrid architectures; constant learning rates lead to suboptimal plateaus (Cite Lesson 33).
