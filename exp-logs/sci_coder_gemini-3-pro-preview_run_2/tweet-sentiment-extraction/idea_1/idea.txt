Based on the provided dataset analysis and architectural constraints, I propose the following baseline approach. This solution synthesizes the Question-Answering formulation of Model 1 with the lightweight recurrent processing of Model 2, creating a distinct and efficient **Bi-Directional GRU Pointer Network**.

### Model
The architecture is designed to predict the start and end indices of the sentiment-bearing phrase directly, conditioned on the tweet's sentiment.

1.  **Conditional Embeddings**: The input text is converted into dense vectors using a trainable word embedding layer. To explicitly condition the model on the `sentiment`, the sentiment label (positive, negative, neutral) is mapped to a low-dimensional embedding vector. This sentiment vector is concatenated to **every** word embedding in the sequence, ensuring the model is constantly aware of the target sentiment context at every time step.
2.  **Recurrent Encoder**: The sequence of concatenated embeddings is passed through a **Bidirectional Gated Recurrent Unit (Bi-GRU)**. The Bi-GRU is chosen over an LSTM (Model 2) for its simpler architecture (fewer gates), which results in fewer parameters and faster computation while still effectively capturing the forward and backward context required to understand phrase boundaries.
3.  **Pointer Heads**: Unlike the sequence tagging approach (BIO/CRF) of Model 2, this model uses a "Pointer" approach similar to Question Answering. The hidden states from the Bi-GRU are fed into two separate linear layers (classification heads):
    *   **Start Head**: Outputs a score for each token representing the likelihood of it being the *start* of the selected text.
    *   **End Head**: Outputs a score for each token representing the likelihood of it being the *end* of the selected text.
    *   A Softmax activation is applied across the sequence dimension for both heads to produce probability distributions.

### Data
1.  **Tokenization**: To maximize alignment with the Jaccard metric, the text is tokenized simply by splitting on **whitespace**. This preserves punctuation attached to words (e.g., "good!" remains "good!"), ensuring that the reconstructed output matches the raw text format required for submission.
2.  **Target Generation**: For the training set, the `selected_text` is located within the full `text`. The indices of the first and last tokens of this substring are extracted to serve as the ground truth `start_index` and `end_index` labels.
3.  **Padding**: Sequences are padded to a fixed length (derived from the 95th percentile of training lengths) to facilitate efficient mini-batch processing.

### Training
1.  **Objective Function**: The model is trained to minimize the **Sum of Cross-Entropy Losses** for the start and end predictions.
    *   `Loss = CrossEntropy(Pred_Start, True_Start) + CrossEntropy(Pred_End, True_End)`
2.  **Optimization**: The Adam optimizer is utilized for stable convergence.
3.  **Regularization**: Dropout is applied after the embedding layer and the GRU layer to prevent overfitting, particularly given the high overlap between neutral inputs and targets.

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference**: For each test sample, the model outputs probability distributions for the start and end positions.
2.  **Decoding**: The final span is selected by finding the pair of indices `(i, j)` that maximizes the joint probability `P(start=i) * P(end=j)`, subject to the constraint that `i <= j` (the start cannot be after the end).
3.  **Reconstruction**: The tokens corresponding to the indices from `i` to `j` (inclusive) are joined by whitespace to form the final predicted string.
