Based on the comprehensive analysis of the "Lessons" and "Previous Ideas," I propose a **Homogeneous Ensemble of Stochastic Weight Averaged (SWA) EfficientNet-B0s**.

This strategy represents a structural evolution from searching for a "better" single architecture to **optimizing the generalization landscape** of the proven best architecture. It explicitly corrects the implementation errors that caused the failure of the Noisy Student backbone (Idea 8) and the heterogeneous ensembles (Idea 6, 7), while strictly adhering to the signal processing constraints identified in the successful Idea 4.

### Model
We will construct a **Homogeneous Ensemble** using the **EfficientNet-B0** architecture.
*   **Backbone:** We will utilize **EfficientNet-B0** initialized with **Noisy Student weights** (`tf_efficientnet_b0.ns_jft_in1k`).
    *   *Justification:* Previous attempts with these weights (Idea 8) failed due to aggressive regularization (`1e-2` weight decay) and resizing artifacts. We will correct this by applying the "Legacy" fine-tuning recipe (low weight decay) to leverage the superior feature extractors learned from the massive JFT-300M dataset **Cite solution_lesson_node_00039**.
*   **Pooling:** We will replace Global Average Pooling with **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling**. This is structurally non-negotiable for detecting transient whale calls in noise **Cite solution_lesson_node_00017**.
*   **Input Adaptation:** The first convolutional layer will be modified to accept **1-channel input**, initialized by averaging the original RGB weights to preserve spatial filters **Cite solution_lesson_node_00001**.

### Data
The data pipeline is strictly defined to respect physical signal fidelity, combining the successful elements of Idea 4 with the necessary corrections for the Noisy Student backbone.
*   **Spectrogram Generation:**
    *   **FFT Parameters:** `n_fft=1024` (High Frequency Resolution) and `hop_length=64` (High Temporal Resolution). This ensures the FFT bins exceed Mel bands (>4:1) to prevent aliasing **Cite solution_lesson_node_00008** and resolves short call contours **Cite solution_lesson_node_00016**.
    *   **Mel Filterbank:** `n_mels=128`, `normalized=False`. We explicitly disable area normalization to preserve the environmental "Pink" noise spectral tilt **Cite solution_lesson_node_00022**.
*   **Preprocessing:**
    *   **Native Resolution:** Inputs will be fed to the model at their **native resolution** (approx. $128 \times 63$). We will **NOT** resize to $224 \times 224$, as this introduces interpolation artifacts that degraded the Noisy Student model previously **Cite solution_lesson_node_00031**, **Cite solution_lesson_node_00033**.
    *   **Instance Standardization:** We will apply **Zero-Mean, Unit-Variance standardization** to each clip individually. This is critical for convergence **Cite solution_lesson_node_00025**.
*   **Augmentation:** We will use **SpecAugment** (Time/Freq masking) **Cite solution_lesson_node_00003**. We will explicitly exclude Mixup **Cite solution_lesson_node_00018**.

### Training
We introduce **Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA)** and **Stratified K-Fold Cross-Validation** as the core structural innovations in the training process.
*   **Optimizer Configuration:** We will use **AdamW** with a **Low Weight Decay of `1e-4`**. This corrects the error in Idea 8 (which used `1e-2`) and allows the Noisy Student backbone to adapt to the audio domain without being over-constrained **Cite solution_lesson_node_00039**.
*   **Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA):** Instead of using the weights from the final epoch, we will employ SWA. After the model converges (e.g., epoch 5), we will capture a snapshot of the weights at the end of each subsequent epoch and average them. This finds a flatter minimum in the loss landscape, improving generalization and robustness to the test set distribution.
*   **Sampling:** A **WeightedRandomSampler** will be used with a **Batch Size of 128** to stabilize Batch Normalization statistics **Cite solution_lesson_node_00004**.

### Evaluation
*   **Ensembling Strategy:** We will train **5 separate instances** of the SWA-EfficientNet-B0 model using **5-Fold Stratified Cross-Validation**.
    *   *Justification:* Previous ensembles (Idea 6, 7, 8) failed because they combined a strong model with a weaker one (DenseNet/ResNet). Lesson 00037 dictates that effective ensembling requires base learners of **comparable strength** **Cite solution_lesson_node_00037**. A K-Fold ensemble ensures all members are the "Best" architecture, reducing variance and maximizing AUC.
*   **Inference:** Test clips will be processed at native resolution. The final prediction will be the average probability of the 5 SWA models.
