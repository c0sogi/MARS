To optimize the performance of the Right Whale call detection model, I will focus on improving the quality of the input data fed into the ResNet18 model. The previous solution used a relatively coarse temporal resolution for the spectrograms (`HOP_LENGTH=128`), which might obscure the fine-grained "up-sweep" characteristic of the whale calls. I will significantly increase the temporal resolution and focus the frequency analysis on the relevant band.

**Strategy:**
1.  **High-Resolution Spectrograms**: In `library/config.py`, I will decrease `HOP_LENGTH` from 128 to 20. This increases the number of time frames from ~32 to ~200 for a 2-second clip. This ensures that the resizing to `(224, 224)` for the ResNet input preserves real temporal details rather than relying on interpolation.
2.  **Focused Frequency Band**: I will adjust `F_MIN` to 40Hz and `F_MAX` to 600Hz. Right whale calls are typically 50-250Hz. Narrowing the range from 0-1000Hz to 40-600Hz allows the 128 Mel bins to capture more detailed spectral features of the call, while still retaining sufficient context.
3.  **Adapted Regularization**: Since the spectrogram now has more time steps, I will increase `TIME_MASK_PARAM` from 10 to 40 in `library/config.py`. This ensures that SpecAugment remains an effective regularizer by masking a meaningful portion of the time axis (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
4.  **Training Optimization**: I will update `BATCH_SIZE` to 128 and `NUM_EPOCHS` to 12 in `library/config.py` to efficiently use the A100 GPU and allow for better convergence.
5.  **Conditional Submission**: I will modify `runfile.py` to strictly enforce the requirement of generating a submission only if the validation AUC exceeds the previous best score of 0.99426.
