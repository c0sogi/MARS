Based on the comprehensive analysis of the accumulated lessons and the structural synthesis of previous successful and failed experiments, I propose the **Integrated Dual-Stream Wide Network (IDSW-Net)**.

This solution represents a structural evolution that resolves the "Spatial Context Loss" (Cite solution_lesson_node_00101) and "Parameter Explosion" (Cite solution_lesson_node_00041) issues while maintaining the critical "Wide Backbone" (Cite solution_lesson_node_00077) and "Shadow Preservation" (Cite solution_lesson_node_00070) capabilities.

# Model

The architecture employs the **Split-Branch Topology** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007) with a novel **Spatially-Integrated Block** structure.

1.  **Visual Branch (Integrated Wide Backbone)**:
    *   **Sustained Width Strategy**: Maintain a high channel capacity (128 filters) in the deeper layers to capture complex SAR textures, avoiding the underfitting observed in narrower architectures (Cite solution_lesson_node_00077).
    *   **Structural Innovation: Spatially-Integrated Block**:
        *   **Wide Convolution**: 3x3 Convolution (e.g., 128 filters) $\to$ BatchNorm $\to$ ReLU.
        *   **Pre-Pooling Attention**: Apply **Convolutional Block Attention Modules (CBAM)** strictly **before** the pooling layer. This refines features at high resolution (Cite solution_lesson_node_00061). Crucially, utilize **Mixed Pooling (Max + Avg)** for the attention logic, explicitly avoiding Min-pooling to ensure optimization stability (Cite solution_lesson_node_00095, solution_lesson_node_00079).
        *   **Dual-Stream Pooling**: Apply **Max Pooling** (Peaks) and **Min Pooling** (Shadows) and concatenate the outputs. This expands the channel depth (e.g., $128 \to 256$), preserving the full dynamic range of the signal (Cite solution_lesson_node_00070).
        *   **Post-Pooling Spatial Integration**: Immediately after the Dual-Pooling expansion, apply a **3x3 Convolution** to compress the channels back to the base width (e.g., $256 \to 128$).
            *   **Rationale**: Unlike 1x1 convolutions used in previous failures, a **3x3 kernel** integrates the spatial context while reducing dimensionality. This allows the network to learn the spatial adjacency between a "Peak" and its "Shadow" before compression, satisfying the requirement for spatial integration (Cite solution_lesson_node_00101) and channel contraction (Cite solution_lesson_node_00102).
    *   **Hybrid Readout Interface**:
        *   The final output is $4 \times 4 \times 128$.
        *   **Path A (Spatial)**: Apply **Adaptive Max Pooling** to a **$2 \times 2$ grid (Quadrants)**. This preserves coarse spatial geometry (Cite solution_lesson_node_00021) while reducing parameters ($2 \times 2 \times 128 = 512$).
        *   **Path B (Intensity)**: Apply **Global Max Pooling** to extract the absolute strongest signal peak ($1 \times 1 \times 128 = 128$).
        *   **Fusion**: Concatenate Path A and Path B vectors ($512 + 128 = 640$). This is significantly more efficient than the 4,096 parameters of previous wide models (Cite solution_lesson_node_00041).

2.  **Metadata Branch**:
    *   A dedicated Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) to process the `inc_angle` scalar (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).

3.  **Fusion Head**:
    *   **Concatenation**: Fuse the visual vector (640 dim) and the metadata vector via simple concatenation (Cite solution_lesson_node_00073).
    *   **Classification**: Process through a dense head with Batch Normalization, ReLU, and **High Dropout (0.5)**. The high dropout is essential to regularize the wide backbone (Cite solution_lesson_node_00077, solution_lesson_node_00014).

# Data

1.  **Input Construction**: Construct 3-channel images using `Band_1`, `Band_2`, and the arithmetic mean `(Band_1 + Band_2) / 2`. Do not use inverted inputs (Cite solution_lesson_node_00076).
2.  **Strict Fold-wise Preprocessing**: Implement **Independent Per-Channel Min-Max Scaling** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00025). Crucially, calculate the scaling statistics **exclusively on the training subset** of the current fold and apply them to the validation subset. This eliminates the data leakage present in global scaling approaches (Cite solution_lesson_node_00097).
3.  **Augmentation**:
    *   **Rotational Invariance**: Apply random rotations of **0°, 90°, 180°, or 270°** combined with random **Horizontal Flips** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014).
    *   **Constraints**: Exclude Vertical Flips (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015) and Mixup (Cite solution_lesson_node_00031).

# Training

1.  **Strategy**: Implement **Stratified 5-Fold Cross-Validation**. Train 5 independent instances of the IDSW-Net. This is critical to reduce variance and maximize data utilization (Cite solution_lesson_node_00029, solution_lesson_node_00052).
2.  **Optimization**:
    *   **"Low and Slow"**: Initialize Adam with a conservative learning rate (e.g., **2e-4**) to prevent premature convergence (Cite solution_lesson_node_00023).
    *   **Scheduler**: Use `ReduceLROnPlateau` to decay the learning rate when validation loss stagnates (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).
3.  **State Management**: Use `copy.deepcopy(model.state_dict())` in the Early Stopping callback to strictly preserve the best weights (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
4.  **Regularization**: Rely on High Dropout (0.5) and Rotational Augmentation. Only introduce Weight Decay if Validation Loss consistently exceeds Training Loss (Cite solution_lesson_node_00012).

# Evaluation

1.  **Inference**: Process test images into the 3-channel format. Use the **average scaling statistics** derived from the training folds to normalize the test data (or ensemble predictions from models scaled with their respective fold stats).
2.  **Ensemble Prediction**: Pass the test data through all 5 trained IDSW-Net models.
3.  **Aggregation**: Compute the **mean** of the predicted probabilities to generate the final submission. This ensemble approach is the valid metric for performance comparison (Cite solution_lesson_node_00036).
