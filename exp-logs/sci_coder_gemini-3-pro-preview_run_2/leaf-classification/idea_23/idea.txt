Based on the comprehensive analysis of the previous experiments and the distilled lessons, I propose the **Diverse-Covariance Generative Library (DCGL) Ensemble**.

This strategy structurally evolves the "Library of Experts" concept (Lesson 81) and the "Precision-Gaussianized" baseline (Lesson 70, 73). It addresses the remaining bottleneck—reliance on a single covariance estimation method (Ledoit-Wolf)—by introducing **Algorithmic Covariance Diversity**. By generating a pool of experts that span the spectrum from Diagonal Covariance (Naive Bayes) to various Regularized Full Covariance estimators (OAS, Ledoit-Wolf, Fixed Shrinkage), and applying them to both provided and orthogonal morphometric views, we allow the Greedy Selector to mathematically construct the optimal density estimation surface for the 99-class manifold.

# Model
The solution utilizes a **Dynamic Ensemble Selection** architecture. We generate a diverse library of probabilistic experts and use Greedy Forward Selection to optimize the ensemble weights.

### 1. Expert Library Generation
All experts operate in **double precision (`float64`)** (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00073`) and utilize **PowerTransformer** (Yeo-Johnson) preprocessing (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00070`) to strictly satisfy the normality assumptions.

We instantiate a library of experts that vary in their **Inductive Bias regarding Feature Covariance**:

*   **Group A: The Ledoit-Wolf Experts (Baseline)**
    *   **Algorithm:** **Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA)** with `shrinkage='auto'` (Ledoit-Wolf).
    *   **Role:** The proven baseline that balances bias and variance using an analytical shrinkage formula (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00063`).
    *   **Views:** Applied to *Global* (Provided Features) and *Combined* (Provided + Morphometric) views.

*   **Group B: The OAS Experts (Innovation)**
    *   **Algorithm:** **LDA** with **Oracle Approximating Shrinkage (OAS)**.
    *   **Rationale:** While Ledoit-Wolf is robust, OAS often yields a lower Mean Squared Error for the covariance matrix when the data is truly Gaussian (which `PowerTransformer` enforces). Including OAS experts allows the ensemble to exploit this alternative estimation if it aligns better with the Log Loss surface.
    *   **Views:** Applied to *Global* and *Combined* views.

*   **Group C: The Fixed-Shrinkage Experts (Exploitation)**
    *   **Algorithm:** **LDA** with fixed shrinkage values (`[0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 0.5]`).
    *   **Rationale:** Analytical methods (LW/OAS) optimize statistical properties, not Log Loss. Lesson 81 proved that a fixed shrinkage (e.g., 0.01) can outperform analytical defaults. We treat each setting as a distinct expert (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00081`).
    *   **Views:** Applied to *Global* view.

*   **Group D: The Diagonal Covariance Experts (Boundary Case)**
    *   **Algorithm:** **Gaussian Naive Bayes (GNB)** with a grid of `var_smoothing` parameters.
    *   **Rationale:** GNB assumes feature independence (diagonal covariance). While likely too simple for the full dataset, it acts as a high-bias regularizer. If specific feature subsets (like Morphometrics) are largely independent, GNB provides a stable, low-variance density estimate that can stabilize the ensemble.

### 2. Selection Mechanism
*   **Greedy Forward Selection:** We employ the selection strategy from Lesson 56. We iteratively add experts from the library to the ensemble *if and only if* they maximize the improvement in Validation Log Loss. This ensures that the final model is a mathematically optimal mix of covariance estimators (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00056`).

# Data
1.  **Feature Engineering (Dual-Source):**
    *   **Source A:** Load the provided 192 features (Margin, Shape, Texture).
    *   **Source B:** Extract **Orthogonal Morphometrics** from raw binary images: **Hu Moments** (7 invariant descriptors) and **Geometric Scalars** (Aspect Ratio, Solidity, Extent, Eccentricity). (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00050`).
    *   **View Construction:** Create `Global` (Source A) and `Combined` (Source A + Source B) matrices.
2.  **Preprocessing:**
    *   **Precision:** Cast all data to `float64` to prevent numerical noise in exponential density calculations (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00073`).
    *   **Gaussianization:** Apply `PowerTransformer(method='yeo-johnson')` to all views. This is the critical enabler for the Generative Library (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00070`).
    *   **Leakage Prevention:** Transformers are fit on the training split and applied to the test split.

# Training
The training procedure rigorously separates the Selection phase from the Final Retraining phase to maximize data utilization (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00010`).

1.  **Phase 1: Selection (Train/Val Split)**
    *   Split training data (80/20).
    *   **Train Library:** Train all experts (LW, OAS, Fixed-LDA, GNB) on the 80% split.
    *   **Generate Predictions:** Compute `float64` probability matrices on the 20% hold-out set.
    *   **Run Selection:** Apply Greedy Forward Selection (with replacement) to determine the optimal subset of experts and their integer weights.

2.  **Phase 2: Final Retraining (Full Data)**
    *   Retrain *only* the selected experts on the **Combined Training + Validation Set**.
    *   Use the exact hyperparameters (e.g., specific shrinkage value) identified in Phase 1 to prevent configuration drift (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00028`).

# Evaluation
1.  **Inference:**
    *   Extract Morphometric features for the Test set.
    *   Process the test set through the retrained pipelines of the selected experts (applying `PowerTransformer` and `float64` casting).
2.  **Aggregation:**
    *   Compute the final submission probabilities using the **Weighted Average** determined in Phase 1.
    *   This strategy combines the strengths of diverse covariance estimators—from diagonal to regularized full matrices—tailoring the density estimation to the specific geometry of the Gaussianized feature manifold.
