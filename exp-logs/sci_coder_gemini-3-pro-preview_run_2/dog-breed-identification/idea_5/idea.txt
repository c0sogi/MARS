Based on the analysis of the previous attempts and the provided lessons, I propose the **Heterogeneous Multi-View Stacking** strategy. This approach synthesizes the successful "Multi-View" data strategy with a "Dual-Backbone" architectural strategy, strictly adhering to the integration protocols that failed in previous iterations (e.g., avoiding early fusion for heterogeneous models).

# Model
- **Dual-Stream Backbone Architecture**: We will utilize two distinct, State-of-the-Art (SOTA) architectures to capture complementary visual features.
    1.  **Stream A (CNN)**: **`ConvNeXt-Large`** initialized with `torchvision` "New Recipe" weights (`IMAGENET1K_V1`). This retains the high-performance local feature extraction from the current best solution (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
    2.  **Stream B (Transformer)**: **`ViT-Large-16`** initialized with **`SWAG` weights** (`IMAGENET1K_SWAG_E2E_V1`). This introduces a massive upgrade in base estimator quality over previous transformer attempts (Cite solution_lesson_node_00011). The SWAG (Supervised Weakly from AGgregated hashtags) pre-training provides superior fine-grained classification capabilities compared to standard ImageNet-1k weights.
- **Independent Classifiers**: We will attach a separate **Logistic Regression** head to each stream. This avoids the "Early Fusion" pitfall where concatenating CNN and Transformer embeddings degraded performance due to feature space mismatches (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009).

# Data
- **Multi-View Preprocessing**: For *each* backbone, we will generate three geometric views of every image to maximize signal extraction (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016):
    1.  **Standard View**: Resize and Center Crop (Balances context and detail).
    2.  **Global View**: Resize without cropping ("Squish") to capture the complete object shape.
    3.  **Local View**: Resize to a larger scale and Center Crop ("Zoom") to capture high-frequency texture details.
- **Model-Specific Pipelines**: Crucially, Stream A and Stream B will use independent data pipelines. Stream A will apply the specific transforms (mean/std/interpolation) required by ConvNeXt, while Stream B will apply the distinct transforms required by the SWAG ViT. This ensures perfect alignment with pre-training distributions (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).
- **Feature-Level TTA**: We will apply horizontal flipping to every view and average the embeddings at the feature level before classification (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).

# Training
- **Early Fusion of Views (Per Stream)**:
    - For Stream A, we will concatenate the embeddings from its three views (Standard, Global, Local) into a single feature vector (approx. 4608 dimensions).
    - For Stream B, we will similarly concatenate the embeddings from its three views (approx. 3072 dimensions).
    - We will **not** apply L2 normalization to these vectors, preserving magnitude information (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015). This "Early Fusion" of homogeneous views allows the linear classifier to learn correlations between shape and texture (Cite solution_lesson_node_00019).
- **Parallel Optimization**: We will train the two Logistic Regression classifiers independently on their respective concatenated feature sets using the L-BFGS solver.
- **Ensemble Weight Optimization**: We will learn optimal scalar weights $(w_A, w_B)$ on the validation set to combine the predictions. Since both base learners are high-capacity SOTA models, this weighted average will leverage their diversity without the risk of a weak model diluting the strong one (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014).

# Evaluation
- **Weighted Late Fusion**:
    - Generate probability predictions using the trained Stream A pipeline.
    - Generate probability predictions using the trained Stream B pipeline.
    - Combine the predictions using the optimized weights: $P_{final} = w_A \cdot P_A + w_B \cdot P_B$.
