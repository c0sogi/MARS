Based on the accumulated lessons, I propose the **Stabilized High-Resolution Multi-Task Network (SHR-MTN)**. This strategy synthesizes the architectural benefits of Multi-Task Learning (for robust feature extraction) with the proven calibration of Single-Instance Learning, while introducing specific engineering interventions to resolve the numerical instabilities that caused previous high-resolution/mixed-precision attempts to fail.

### Model
**Architecture: Fine-Tuned EfficientNetV2 with Multi-Task Heads**
1.  **Visual Backbone:** The model will employ an **EfficientNetV2-Small** architecture. Crucially, this backbone will be **completely unfrozen and fine-tuned** end-to-end to adapt to the specific grayscale textures of mammography (e.g., microcalcifications), correcting the root cause of failure in frozen embedding approaches Cite {solution_lesson_node_00002}.
2.  **Pooling & Fusion:** Feature maps will be aggregated using **Generalized Mean Pooling (GeM)**, which focuses on high-activation regions (lesions) more effectively than standard average pooling. This visual embedding will be concatenated with a processed metadata vector (Age, Implant, View, etc.) derived from a parallel MLP branch.
3.  **Multi-Task Output:** The network will branch into three distinct heads to leverage all available training signals, which helps prevent the feature extractor from collapsing to a trivial "all-negative" solution:
    *   **Primary Head:** Binary classification for `cancer`.
    *   **Auxiliary Head 1:** Regression for `BIRADS` scores (providing a continuous gradient of suspicion).
    *   **Auxiliary Head 2:** Classification for `density` (forcing the model to understand tissue structure).

### Data
1.  **Single-Instance Learning (SIL):** The pipeline will strictly adhere to the SIL paradigm. Patient-level labels (Cancer, BIRADS, Density) will be broadcast to every individual image (view). This **dense supervision** strategy is retained as it was proven to outperform the sparse supervision of Multi-Instance Learning (MIL) in this domain Cite {solution_lesson_node_00005}.
2.  **Resolution Upgrade:** Input images will be resized to **1024x1024**. This high resolution is critical for detecting subtle microcalcifications that are lost at lower resolutions (e.g., 224px or 512px).
3.  **Ingestion:** The robust **Byte-Level Image Extractor** (scanning binary streams for JPEG/JPEG2000 headers and decoding via `cv2.imdecode`) will be used to handle DICOM files without `pydicom`.

### Training
1.  **Objective: Stabilized Standard Loss:**
    *   To address the class imbalance, the model will use the standard **BCEWithLogitsLoss** with a `pos_weight` (e.g., 15.0).
    *   **Crucial Stability Fix:** To prevent the `NaN` divergence observed in previous Mixed Precision attempts Cite {solution_lesson_node_00008}, the loss calculation will be explicitly wrapped in a **Float32 context** (`with torch.cuda.amp.autocast(enabled=False):`). This prioritizes the numerical stability of optimized standard library losses over custom implementations like Focal Loss, which previously failed due to manual probability math Cite {solution_lesson_node_00013}.
2.  **Initialization Strategy:**
    *   The final classification layer bias will be initialized to $b = \log(\frac{0.02}{1-0.02}) \approx -3.9$. This aligns the model's initial predictions with the dataset's natural imbalance, preventing the massive initial loss spike that often destabilizes gradients in the first few iterations.
3.  **Sampling: Natural Distribution:**
    *   The training will use a **Standard Random Sampler** rather than a Weighted Random Sampler. This ensures the model learns the true prior probability of cancer (~2%), preserving **probability calibration** which is critical for the Probabilistic F1 metric Cite {solution_lesson_node_00010}.

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:** The model iterates through the test set. Auxiliary heads are ignored, and only the probability from the **Primary Cancer Head** is extracted.
2.  **Aggregation:** Predictions are grouped by `prediction_id`. The final submission score is the **maximum** probability across all images (views) associated with that ID. This aligns with the empirical finding that robust single-instance predictions aggregated via max-pooling outperform complex learned aggregation mechanisms Cite {solution_lesson_node_00005}.
