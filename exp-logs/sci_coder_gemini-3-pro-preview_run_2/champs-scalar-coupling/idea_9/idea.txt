Based on the comprehensive analysis of the provided lessons and previous experiments, I propose the **Scaled Hybrid Geometric-Attention Network (HGA-Net)**. This strategy synthesizes the high-fidelity local geometric modeling of the Directional Message Passing backbone with the rapid convergence and long-range modeling capabilities of a Global Transformer, while strictly adhering to the scaling and regularization protocols identified as critical for this physical task.

### Model: Scaled Hybrid Geometric-Attention Network (HGA-Net)

The architecture represents a structural evolution that fuses a deep local geometric encoder with a global attention mechanism, connected via a residual geometric readout to preserve precision.

1.  **Local Geometric Encoder (Backbone):**
    *   **Architecture:** The foundation is a **Directional Message Passing Neural Network (DMPNN)** operating on directed edges ($u \to v$) rather than atoms. This is essential to capture the angular dependencies of scalar coupling (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00014`).
    *   **Geometric Basis:** We strictly employ **Radial Basis Functions (RBF)** for expanding inter-atomic distances (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00004`) and **Spherical Basis Functions (SBF)** for encoding triplet bond angles (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00014`). This ensures "Geometric Completeness" (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00002`).
    *   **Non-Linear Updates:** The update functions within the message passing blocks will be deep **Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs)** with Swish/SiLU activations. We explicitly avoid reducing these to linear projections to prevent information bottlenecks (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00006`).
    *   **Capacity:** The backbone will be scaled to a hidden dimension of **192 or 256** with **6 interaction layers**. Previous scaling experiments demonstrated that smaller models significantly underfit the complex geometric landscape (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00015`).

2.  **Global Interaction Module:**
    *   **Transition:** After the local message passing layers, edge embeddings will be aggregated (via scatter sum) to form atom-level representations.
    *   **Transformer Encoder:** These atom embeddings will be processed by a **Graph Transformer** (Multi-Head Self-Attention). This component acts as a "wormhole," allowing every atom to attend to every other atom regardless of distance. This addresses the limitation of local GNNs in capturing long-range electronic effects (e.g., conjugation) and has been proven to drastically accelerate convergence (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00026`).

3.  **Residual Geometric Readout:**
    *   **Feature Fusion:** For a target coupling pair (Atom $u$, Atom $v$), the readout input will be constructed by concatenating:
        1.  The **Global** node embedding of Atom $u$ (from the Transformer).
        2.  The **Global** node embedding of Atom $v$ (from the Transformer).
        3.  The **Local** directed edge embedding $e_{u \to v}$ from the final layer of the DMPNN backbone.
        *Note: Explicitly retaining the local edge embedding is a critical structural choice. It ensures that the precise, high-frequency geometric information (exact distances and angles) modeled by the SBFs is not smoothed out by the global pooling of the Transformer (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00002`).*
    *   **Prediction Head:** This fused vector will pass through a single **Deterministic MLP** (no Dropout) to predict the scalar coupling constant. We strictly avoid Dropout to maintain regression precision (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00007`) and use a single final readout to avoid the noise associated with hierarchical summation (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00013`).

### Data

*   **Source Integrity:** We will generate molecular graphs directly from the raw `.xyz` files rather than aggregated CSVs to ensure complete data coverage, particularly for the test set (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00002`).
*   **Graph Construction:** We will use a spatial cutoff (e.g., 5.0 Å) for the DMPNN connectivity.
*   **Basis Pre-computation:** RBF expansions for distances and SBF expansions for triplet angles will be pre-computed and cached during data loading to maximize training throughput (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00014`).
*   **Target Normalization:** The `scalar_coupling_constant` targets will be standardized (zero mean, unit variance) individually for each coupling type to handle the varying dynamic ranges.

### Training

*   **Loss Function:** We will minimize the **Mean Absolute Error (MAE)**.
*   **Optimizer:** We will use **AdamW** with a linear warm-up phase followed by a Cosine Annealing learning rate schedule.
*   **Extended Horizon:** We will train for **50-60 epochs**. While the Hybrid architecture accelerates early learning (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00026`), high-capacity geometric models require extended training schedules to fully converge to the loss floor (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00015`).
*   **Regularization:** We will rely on weight decay and the inductive biases of the architecture, strictly avoiding Dropout (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00007`).

### Evaluation

*   **Inference Pipeline:** The evaluation will explicitly load test geometries from the raw `.xyz` files (via `test_metadata.csv`) to ensure 100% coverage (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00002`).
*   **Post-Processing:** The model's predictions will be inversely transformed using the training set's mean and standard deviation (per coupling type) to produce the final values.
