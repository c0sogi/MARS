Based on the provided lessons and the limitations of the purely local geometric models evaluated so far, I propose the **Hybrid Geometric-Attention Network (HGA-Net)**.

### Model
The architecture represents a structural evolution from the purely local **Directional Message Passing** (Idea 3) to a **Local-Global Hybrid** system. It addresses the limitation that local GNNs (even with directional passing) struggle to capture long-range electronic effects (like conjugation or aromaticity) which significantly influence scalar coupling constants.

1.  **Local Geometric Encoder (Backbone):**
    *   **Structure:** We retain the **Directional Message Passing Neural Network (DMPNN)** as the initial feature extractor. This component operates on **directed edges** ($u \to v$) rather than nodes.
    *   **Geometric Basis:** We strictly preserve the use of **Radial Basis Functions (RBF)** for distances (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00004`) and **Spherical Basis Functions (SBF)** for triplet angles (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00014`). This ensures the model captures the precise local geometry and angular dependencies required for quantum mechanical predictions (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00002`).
    *   **Non-Linear Updates:** The update functions within this backbone will be deep **MLPs** with Swish/SiLU activations, avoiding the information bottlenecks of linear projections (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00006`).

2.  **Global Context Module (Transformer):**
    *   **Transition:** After $L$ layers of DMPNN, the learned *edge* embeddings will be aggregated (via scatter sum) to form *node* (atom) embeddings.
    *   **Attention Mechanism:** These node embeddings will be processed by a **Transformer Encoder** (Multi-Head Self-Attention). This allows every atom to attend to every other atom in the molecule, creating a global receptive field that bypasses the local spatial cutoff. This captures the through-space electronic environment.

3.  **Feature Fusion & Readout:**
    *   **Hybrid Representation:** For a target coupling pair (Atom $u$, Atom $v$), the readout will construct a feature vector by concatenating three elements:
        1.  The **Global** node embedding of Atom $u$ (from the Transformer).
        2.  The **Global** node embedding of Atom $v$ (from the Transformer).
        3.  The **Local** directed edge embedding $e_{u \to v}$ (from the DMPNN backbone). *Note: This residual connection of the local edge feature is critical to preserve the high-frequency geometric precision (exact distance/angle) that might be smoothed out by the global attention.*
    *   **Deterministic Regression:** This concatenated vector (plus the coupling type embedding) is passed to a single **Deterministic MLP** (no Dropout) to predict the constant (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00007`). We use a single final readout rather than hierarchical summation (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00013`).

### Data
*   **Input Processing:** Parse raw `.xyz` files to construct molecular graphs. Use a spatial cutoff (e.g., 5.0 Å) for the DMPNN graph connectivity, while the Transformer treats the molecule as a fully connected graph.
*   **Basis Pre-computation:** Pre-compute RBF and SBF expansions during data loading to maximize training throughput (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00014`).
*   **Target Normalization:** Standardize the `scalar_coupling_constant` (zero mean, unit variance) individually for each coupling type.
*   **Test Set Coverage:** Explicitly load test structures from the raw `.xyz` files to ensure 100% data coverage (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00002`).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** Mean Absolute Error (MAE).
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with a linear warm-up phase followed by a Cosine Annealing learning rate schedule.
*   **Scaling Strategy:** We will utilize the "Scaled" configuration identified as optimal: a hidden dimension of at least **192** and a training duration of **45-60 epochs**. Previous experiments proved that high-capacity geometric models significantly underfit with smaller widths or shorter schedules (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00015`).
*   **Regularization:** Rely on weight decay and the architecture's geometric constraints; strictly avoid Dropout (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00007`).

### Evaluation
*   **Inference:** Pass the test set graphs through the trained HGA-Net.
*   **Inverse Transform:** Apply the inverse of the per-type standardization to the model outputs.
*   **Submission:** Map the predictions to the `id` column and save to `submission.csv`.
