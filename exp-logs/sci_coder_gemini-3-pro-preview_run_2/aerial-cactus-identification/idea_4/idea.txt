Based on the comprehensive analysis of the previous experiments and the specific lessons learned, I propose an optimized strategy that evolves the architecture from a standard Residual Network to a modern **Custom Micro-ConvNeXt**, while strictly adhering to the successful data and training paradigms identified in prior runs.

### Model
I propose a **Custom Micro-ConvNeXt Architecture**. This represents a structural evolution from the "Custom ResNet" (Idea 2/3) by adopting the design principles of modern Vision Transformers and ConvNeXts (inverted bottlenecks, depthwise convolutions, layer normalization) but scaling them down specifically for the 32x32 input domain.

*   **Micro-ConvNeXt Blocks:** Instead of standard Residual Blocks (Convolution $\rightarrow$ BatchNorm $\rightarrow$ ReLU), we will implement custom **Inverted Bottleneck Blocks**. Each block will consist of:
    1.  A **Depthwise Convolution** (e.g., kernel size $5 \times 5$ or $7 \times 7$) to capture spatial context with a larger receptive field than the previous $3 \times 3$ kernels.
    2.  **Layer Normalization** (instead of Batch Normalization) for more stable feature statistics.
    3.  A **Pointwise Convolution** ($1 \times 1$) to expand the channel dimension (expansion ratio of 4).
    4.  **GELU Activation**.
    5.  A final **Pointwise Convolution** ($1 \times 1$) to project back to the original channel dimension, followed by a residual connection.
*   **Spatial Preservation:** We will strictly maintain the shallow downsampling schedule identified as critical in **Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**. The network will only downsample 2 or 3 times (via strided convolutions between stages) to ensure the final feature map resolution remains **8x8**. This avoids the performance degradation caused by reducing low-resolution inputs to $1 \times 1$ too early (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00001**).
*   **Head:** The network will conclude with a **Global Average Pooling (GAP)** layer followed by a single linear classification layer, ensuring translation invariance and parameter efficiency (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**).

### Data
*   **Preprocessing:** Images will be used in their native **32x32** resolution with pixel values normalized to [0, 1].
*   **Augmentation:** We will strictly adhere to the "light augmentation" strategy. We will apply only **Random Horizontal Flips** and **Random Vertical Flips**. We will explicitly avoid geometric rotations, color jittering, or cutout, as these have been proven to degrade performance on this high-signal dataset (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**).

### Training
*   **Ensembling Strategy:** We will employ **Homogeneous Seed Averaging**. We will train multiple independent instances (e.g., 5 seeds) of the Custom Micro-ConvNeXt. This leverages the stability of a single high-performing architecture and eliminates stochastic ranking errors (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00007**), while avoiding the instability risks associated with heterogeneous ensembles like the DenseNet mix (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00008**).
*   **Optimizer & Scheduler:** We will use **AdamW** combined with a **Cosine Annealing** learning rate scheduler. This combination has proven effective for converging to perfect validation scores by refining weights in the final epochs (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00007**).
*   **Loss Function:** Binary Cross-Entropy with Logits Loss.

### Evaluation
*   **Test Time Augmentation (TTA):** During inference, we will predict on the original image, a horizontally flipped version, and a vertically flipped version for each model in the ensemble (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**).
*   **Aggregation:** The final submission probabilities will be the arithmetic mean of all TTA predictions across all trained seeds.
