Based on the comprehensive analysis of the provided lessons and the progression of previous experiments—specifically the dominance of "Wide" architectures (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00064**), the efficiency of "Multi-Scale Aggregation" (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00016**), and the insight that "3x3 strided convolutions accelerate convergence" (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00060**)—I propose the **Custom Wide RepVGG with Multi-Scale Aggregation**.

This strategy introduces **Structural Re-parameterization** (RepVGG) to the solution space. It synthesizes the high representational capacity of the "Wide" backbone with the inference efficiency of a plain CNN. By decoupling the training architecture (multi-branch) from the inference architecture (single-branch), we can leverage the convergence benefits of residual-like structures without the computational overhead of skip connections or attention blocks at test time (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00013**). Furthermore, the RepVGG downsampling block naturally employs 3x3 strided convolutions, aligning with the convergence insights from **Cite solution_lesson_node_00060**.

# Model
I propose a **Custom Wide RepVGG Architecture**.

*   **Backbone (Wide RepVGG):** We will implement a 3-stage backbone using **RepVGG Blocks**.
    *   **Training Topology:** Each block will consist of three parallel branches: a $3 \times 3$ convolution, a $1 \times 1$ convolution, and an identity mapping (where dimensions match). This multi-branch structure facilitates gradient flow and rapid convergence, similar to ResNet.
    *   **Inference Topology:** After training, we will perform **structural re-parameterization** to fuse the parallel branches into a single $3 \times 3$ convolution. This results in a strictly feed-forward VGG-style network, eliminating the memory access cost of residual addition and the latency of attention blocks (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00013**).
    *   **Width:** We will strictly adhere to the **"Super-Wide" Channel Configuration** (`[64, 128, 256]`) identified as optimal in **Cite solution_lesson_node_00064**. The re-parameterized inference model is highly efficient, allowing us to afford this massive capacity.
    *   **Downsampling:** We will use the RepVGG downsampling block (parallel stride-2 $3 \times 3$ and stride-2 $1 \times 1$). This inherently applies the **3x3 strided convolution** strategy proven to accelerate convergence on low-resolution data (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00060**).
    *   **Spatial Preservation:** The network will downsample 32x32 $\to$ 16x16 $\to$ 8x8. We strictly preserve the 8x8 resolution to avoid the loss of fine-grained spatial details (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**).

*   **Head (Multi-Scale Aggregation):** We will retain the concatenation-based aggregation head.
    *   We will extract feature maps from the end of **Stage 2** (16x16) and **Stage 3** (8x8).
    *   We will apply **Global Average Pooling (GAP)** to both. We explicitly reject Covariance Pooling as GAP is sufficient for this high-signal task and avoids statistical instability (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00053**).
    *   The concatenated vector will be passed to a single dense classification layer. This ensures the classifier leverages both the texture-rich mid-level features and the semantic high-level features (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00016**).

# Data
*   **Preprocessing:** Images will be loaded in their native **32x32** resolution and normalized to the [0, 1] range. We will strictly avoid resizing or upscaling to prevent interpolation artifacts (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00001**).
*   **Augmentation:** We will strictly adhere to the "light augmentation" strategy, applying only **Random Horizontal Flips** and **Random Vertical Flips**. We will explicitly avoid geometric rotations, color jittering, or cutout, as these techniques act as noise on this high-signal dataset (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**).

# Training
*   **Ensembling Strategy:** We will employ **Homogeneous Seed Averaging**. We will train **5 independent instances** of this architecture using different random seeds. This strategy eliminates stochastic residual ranking errors (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00007**) while avoiding the instability risks of heterogeneous ensembles (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00008**).
*   **Optimizer & Scheduler:** We will use **AdamW** combined with a **Cosine Annealing** learning rate scheduler. This combination ensures robust convergence and generalization (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00007**).
*   **Training Duration:** We will utilize a reduced training schedule (e.g., 20 epochs). The combination of the "Wide" capacity (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00064**) and the gradient flow of the multi-branch RepVGG block facilitates rapid convergence.
*   **Loss Function:** Binary Cross-Entropy with Logits Loss.

# Evaluation
*   **Re-parameterization:** Before inference, we will fuse the batch normalization and parallel convolutional branches of every RepVGG block into a single $3 \times 3$ convolution kernel and bias. This transforms the model into a simple stack of convolutions.
*   **Test Time Augmentation (TTA):** During inference, we will generate predictions for the original image, a horizontally flipped version, and a vertically flipped version for each of the 5 model instances (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**).
*   **Aggregation:** The final submission probability for each image will be the arithmetic mean of all TTA predictions across all 5 seeds. This maximizes robustness and minimizes stochastic noise.
