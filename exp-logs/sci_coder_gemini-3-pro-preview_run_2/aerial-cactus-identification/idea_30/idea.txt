Based on the comprehensive analysis of the provided lessons and the progression of previous experiments—specifically the dominance of the "Wide SE-ResNeXt" (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00064**), the efficiency of "Multi-Scale Aggregation" (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00016**), and the critical need to preserve spatial fidelity in low-resolution imagery (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**)—I propose the **Custom Wide Anti-Aliased ResNeXt-ECA with Multi-Scale GeM Aggregation**.

This strategy represents a structural synthesis and optimization of the most effective components identified in the lessons. It retains the massive **Representational Capacity** of the Wide ResNeXt but refines the **Attention Mechanism** (replacing SE with Efficient Channel Attention for better cross-channel interaction), the **Pooling Layer** (replacing GAP with Generalized Mean Pooling for adaptive texture capture), and the **Downsampling Operation** (introducing BlurPool for shift invariance).

# Model
I propose a **Custom Wide Anti-Aliased ResNeXt-ECA Architecture**.

*   **Backbone (Wide ResNeXt):** We will implement a 3-stage backbone using **Grouped Convolutions** to maximize width efficiency.
    *   **Channel Configuration:** We will strictly adhere to the **"Super-Wide" Channel Configuration** (`[64, 128, 256]`) identified as optimal in **Cite solution_lesson_node_00064**.
    *   **Cardinality:** We will use a high cardinality (e.g., 32 groups) to enable this width without parameter explosion.
    *   **Kernels:** We will exclusively use **3x3 convolutions** (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00011**).
    *   **Shortcuts:** We will strictly utilize **1x1 convolutions** for projection shortcuts, rejecting dense 3x3 shortcuts (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00063**).

*   **Structural Innovation 1 (ECA Attention):** We will replace Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) blocks with **Efficient Channel Attention (ECA)** blocks.
    *   **Rationale:** While SE blocks accelerate convergence (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00012**), they involve dimensionality reduction (two fully connected layers) which destroys the direct correspondence between channels and their weights. ECA replaces this with a local 1D convolution, capturing local cross-channel interactions *without* dimensionality reduction. This is particularly effective for ResNeXt architectures where channel grouping already isolates features, making interaction modeling critical.

*   **Structural Innovation 2 (Anti-Aliased Downsampling):** We will replace standard stride-2 convolutions with a **Stride-1 Convolution followed by BlurPool**.
    *   **Rationale:** Downsampling on small 32x32 grids violates the sampling theorem, causing aliasing and loss of shift invariance. By applying a low-pass filter (BlurPool) before subsampling, we ensure the features remain robust to small shifts, addressing the spatial preservation needs of aerial imagery (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**).

*   **Head (Multi-Scale GeM Aggregation):** We will evolve the aggregation head.
    *   **Multi-Scale:** We will extract feature maps from **Stage 2** (16x16) and **Stage 3** (8x8) (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00016**).
    *   **GeM Pooling:** Instead of fixed Global Average Pooling (GAP), we will apply **Generalized Mean Pooling (GeM)** with a learnable parameter $p$.
    *   **Rationale:** GAP (Average Pooling) captures global context, while Max Pooling captures salient features (spines). GeM mathematically interpolates between the two ($f = (\text{mean}(x^p))^{1/p}$), allowing the network to learn the optimal pooling strategy for each channel. This offers superior expressivity to GAP without the computational cost or instability of Second-Order Covariance Pooling (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00053**).
    *   **Fusion:** The GeM vectors from both stages will be concatenated and passed to a single dense classification layer.

# Data
*   **Preprocessing:** Images will be loaded in their native **32x32** resolution and normalized to the [0, 1] range. We will strictly avoid resizing or upscaling to prevent interpolation artifacts (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00001**).
*   **Augmentation:** We will strictly adhere to the "light augmentation" strategy, applying only **Random Horizontal Flips** and **Random Vertical Flips**. We will explicitly avoid geometric rotations, color jittering, or cutout, as these techniques act as noise on this high-signal dataset (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**).

# Training
*   **Ensembling Strategy:** We will employ **Homogeneous Seed Averaging**. We will train **5 independent instances** of this architecture using different random seeds. This strategy eliminates stochastic residual ranking errors (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00007**) while avoiding the instability risks of heterogeneous ensembles (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00008**).
*   **Optimizer & Scheduler:** We will use **AdamW** combined with a **Cosine Annealing** learning rate scheduler. This combination ensures robust convergence and generalization (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00007**).
*   **Training Duration:** We will utilize a reduced training schedule (e.g., 20 epochs). The combination of the "Super-Wide" capacity (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00064**) and the enhanced ECA attention facilitates rapid convergence.
*   **Loss Function:** Binary Cross-Entropy with Logits Loss.

# Evaluation
*   **Test Time Augmentation (TTA):** During inference, we will generate predictions for the original image, a horizontally flipped version, and a vertically flipped version for each of the 5 model instances (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**).
*   **Aggregation:** The final submission probability for each image will be the arithmetic mean of all TTA predictions across all 5 seeds. This maximizes robustness and minimizes stochastic noise.
