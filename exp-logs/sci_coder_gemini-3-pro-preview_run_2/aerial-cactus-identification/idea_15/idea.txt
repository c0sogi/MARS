Based on the progression of experiments and the specific lessons learned—particularly the statistical limitations of second-order pooling identified in the most recent trial—I propose the **Custom Narrow SE-ResNet with Selective Texture-Context Aggregation**.

This strategy resolves the instability observed in Idea 14 by strictly applying pooling mechanisms where they are mathematically robust. It synthesizes the efficiency of the "Narrow" backbone, the convergence speed of "SE" blocks, and a hybrid aggregation head that assigns **Global Covariance Pooling (GCP)** to high-resolution layers (for texture) and **Global Average Pooling (GAP)** to low-resolution layers (for semantics).

### Model
I propose a **Custom Narrow SE-ResNet with Selective Texture-Context Aggregation**.

*   **Backbone (Narrow SE-ResNet):** We will utilize the highly efficient backbone validated in previous runs.
    *   **Channel Configuration:** We will strictly adhere to the "Narrow" width (`[16, 32, 64]`). This reduced capacity is sufficient for the dataset and minimizes computational cost **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00019)**.
    *   **Kernel Selection:** We will exclusively use **3x3 convolutions**, avoiding large kernels which are inefficient for 32x32 inputs **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00011)**.
    *   **Spatial Preservation:** The network will downsample the input from 32x32 $\to$ 16x16 $\to$ 8x8. We strictly preserve the 8x8 resolution to avoid losing fine-grained details **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00005)**.
    *   **Attention:** Each residual block will include a **Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) module** to accelerate convergence **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00012)**.

*   **Structural Innovation (Selective Texture-Context Aggregation):** We will implement a bifurcated aggregation head that respects the dimensionality constraints of feature statistics **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00044)**.
    *   **Texture Stream (Stage 2):** We will extract the feature map from **Stage 2** ($16 \times 16$ resolution, 32 channels). Here, the number of spatial pixels ($N=256$) is significantly larger than the number of channels ($C=32$), satisfying the condition $N \gg C$. We will apply **Global Covariance Pooling (GCP)** with Newton-Schulz normalization here to explicitly capture texture correlations (e.g., spines) **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00039)**.
    *   **Context Stream (Stage 3):** We will extract the feature map from **Stage 3** ($8 \times 8$ resolution, 64 channels). Here, $N=64$ and $C=64$, making covariance estimation unstable. Therefore, we will apply **Global Average Pooling (GAP)** here to capture high-level semantic context, ensuring statistical stability **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00044)**.
    *   **Fusion:** We will concatenate the flattened upper-triangular covariance vector from Stage 2 and the average pooled vector from Stage 3.
    *   **Classifier:** This fused vector will be passed to a single dense classification layer. This design leverages the "Multi-Scale" insight **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00016)** while correcting the flaw of applying second-order pooling at deep layers.

### Data
*   **Preprocessing:** Images will be loaded in their native **32x32** resolution and normalized to the [0, 1] range. We will strictly avoid resizing or upscaling to prevent interpolation artifacts **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00001)**.
*   **Augmentation:** We will apply only **Random Horizontal Flips** and **Random Vertical Flips**. We will explicitly avoid geometric rotations, color jittering, or cutout, as these act as noise on this high-signal dataset **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00002)**.

### Training
*   **Ensembling Strategy:** We will employ **Homogeneous Seed Averaging**. We will train **5 independent instances** of this architecture using different random seeds. This strategy eliminates stochastic residual ranking errors **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00007)** while avoiding the instability of heterogeneous ensembles **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00008)**.
*   **Optimizer & Scheduler:** We will use **AdamW** combined with a **Cosine Annealing** learning rate scheduler. This combination ensures robust convergence **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00007)**.
*   **Training Duration:** We will utilize a reduced training schedule (e.g., 15 epochs). The combination of the narrow architecture **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00019)** and SE blocks **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00012)** facilitates rapid convergence.
*   **Loss Function:** Binary Cross-Entropy with Logits Loss.

### Evaluation
*   **Test Time Augmentation (TTA):** During inference, we will generate predictions for the original image, a horizontally flipped version, and a vertically flipped version for each of the 5 model instances **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00005)**.
*   **Aggregation:** The final submission probability for each image will be the arithmetic mean of all TTA predictions across all 5 seeds. This maximizes robustness and minimizes stochastic noise.
