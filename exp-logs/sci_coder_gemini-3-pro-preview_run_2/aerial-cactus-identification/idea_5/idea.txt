Based on the lessons learned, I propose a strategy that evolves the architecture into a **Custom Lightweight SE-ResNet**. This approach introduces channel-wise attention mechanisms to enhance feature selectivity while strictly adhering to the spatial and kernel constraints proven effective for this specific dataset.

### Model
I propose replacing the standard ResNet backbone with a **Custom Lightweight SE-ResNet (Squeeze-and-Excitation ResNet)**.
*   **Structural Innovation (SE Blocks):** We will integrate **Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) modules** within each residual block. Unlike the standard ResNet which treats all feature channels equally, the SE block explicitly models inter-dependencies between channels. It adaptively recalibrates channel-wise feature responses by compressing the spatial dimension (Squeeze) and learning a non-linear interaction between channels (Excitation). This allows the network to focus on the most informative feature maps (e.g., specific texture patterns of cacti) while suppressing less relevant ones, providing a structural advantage over the previous baseline without the computational penalty of large kernels found in the failed ConvNeXt experiment (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00011**).
*   **Spatial Preservation:** We will strictly maintain the shallow downsampling schedule identified as critical in previous runs. The network will process the 32x32 input through stages that preserve resolution, ensuring the final feature map size is **8x8** before the classification head. This avoids the loss of fine-grained details caused by aggressive downsampling (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**).
*   **Kernel Selection:** We will exclusively use **3x3 convolutions**. We will explicitly avoid the large 7x7 kernels used in the Micro-ConvNeXt, as empirical evidence showed that large receptive fields are redundant and computationally inefficient for these small 32x32 images (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00011**).
*   **Head:** The architecture will conclude with a **Global Average Pooling (GAP)** layer followed by a single dense classification layer. This minimizes parameter count and enforces translation invariance (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**).

### Data
*   **Preprocessing:** Images will be loaded in their native **32x32** resolution and normalized to the [0, 1] range.
*   **Augmentation:** We will strictly adhere to the "light augmentation" strategy, applying only **Random Horizontal Flips** and **Random Vertical Flips**. We will explicitly avoid geometric rotations, color jittering, or cutout, as these techniques have been proven to act as noise and degrade performance on this high-signal dataset (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**).

### Training
*   **Ensembling Strategy:** We will employ **Homogeneous Seed Averaging**. We will train 5 independent instances of the Custom SE-ResNet using different random seeds. This strategy is chosen over heterogeneous ensembling because the SE-ResNet is expected to be a stable, high-performing architecture, and previous lessons demonstrated that mixing in less stable architectures (like DenseNet) introduces risk without performance gains (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00008**).
*   **Optimizer & Scheduler:** We will use the **AdamW** optimizer combined with a **Cosine Annealing** learning rate scheduler. This combination has been proven to eliminate residual ranking errors and ensure robust convergence across multiple seeds (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00007**).
*   **Loss Function:** Binary Cross-Entropy with Logits Loss.

### Evaluation
*   **Test Time Augmentation (TTA):** During inference, we will generate predictions for the original image, a horizontally flipped version, and a vertically flipped version for each of the 5 model instances.
*   **Aggregation:** The final submission probability for each image will be the arithmetic mean of all TTA predictions across all 5 seeds. This combines the noise-reduction benefits of TTA with the stability of seed averaging (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**, **Cite solution_lesson_node_00007**).
