Based on the comprehensive analysis of the previous experiments and the specific lessons learned—specifically the dominance of "Wide" architectures (Lesson 49, 64), the efficiency of Multi-Scale Aggregation (Lesson 16), and the convergence benefits of Squeeze-and-Excitation (Lesson 12)—I propose the **Custom Wide SE-Res2Net with Multi-Stage Feature Fusion**.

This strategy represents a structural evolution from the "Wide SE-ResNeXt" (Idea 20) and "Split-Attention" (Idea 21) concepts. While those architectures successfully utilized grouped convolutions to increase width, they processed features at a single scale within each block. The **Res2Net** architecture evolves this by introducing hierarchical connections *within* a single residual block, effectively creating a multi-scale receptive field at a granular level. This allows the model to simultaneously capture fine-grained texture details (spines) and global shapes (plant body) without the inefficiency of large kernels or separate branches.

# Model
I propose a **Custom Wide SE-Res2Net Architecture**.

*   **Backbone (Wide Res2Net):** We will implement a 3-stage backbone using **Res2Net Bottleneck Blocks**.
    *   **Intra-Block Multi-Scale Processing:** Unlike standard ResNet or ResNeXt blocks, the Res2Net block splits the input feature map into $s$ subsets (scales). Each subset is processed by a $3 \times 3$ convolution and then added to the input of the next subset. This creates a hierarchy of receptive fields ($3 \times 3, 5 \times 5, 7 \times 7, \dots$) within a single block, explicitly modeling the multi-scale nature of the cactus textures (spines vs. vegetation) without using computationally expensive large kernels **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00011)**.
    *   **Width:** We will strictly adhere to the **"Super-Wide" Channel Configuration** (`[64, 128, 256]`) identified as optimal in **Cite solution_lesson_node_00064**. The hierarchical structure of Res2Net is parameter-efficient, allowing us to maintain this massive capacity.
    *   **Attention:** We will integrate **Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) Blocks** at the output of each Res2Net residual unit. This ensures that the diverse multi-scale features generated by the block are adaptively recalibrated, accelerating convergence **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00012)**.
    *   **Shortcuts:** We will strictly utilize **1x1 convolutions** for projection shortcuts during downsampling, rejecting 3x3 shortcuts **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00063)**.
    *   **Spatial Preservation:** The network will downsample 32x32 $\to$ 16x16 $\to$ 8x8. We strictly preserve the 8x8 resolution to avoid losing fine-grained spatial details **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00005)**.

*   **Head (Multi-Stage Feature Fusion):** We will implement the concatenation-based aggregation head that proved effective for wide models.
    *   We will extract feature maps from the end of **Stage 2** (16x16) and **Stage 3** (8x8).
    *   We will apply **Global Average Pooling (GAP)** to both. We explicitly reject Covariance Pooling as the high channel count makes second-order statistics computationally expensive and potentially unstable **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00053)**.
    *   The concatenated vector will be passed to a single dense classification layer. This ensures the classifier leverages both the texture-rich mid-level features and the semantic high-level features **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00016)**.

# Data
*   **Preprocessing:** Images will be loaded in their native **32x32** resolution and normalized to the [0, 1] range. We will strictly avoid resizing or upscaling to prevent interpolation artifacts **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00001)**.
*   **Augmentation:** We will strictly adhere to the "light augmentation" strategy, applying only **Random Horizontal Flips** and **Random Vertical Flips**. We will explicitly avoid geometric rotations, color jittering, or cutout, as these techniques act as noise on this high-signal dataset **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00002)**.

# Training
*   **Ensembling Strategy:** We will employ **Homogeneous Seed Averaging**. We will train **5 independent instances** of this architecture using different random seeds. This strategy eliminates stochastic residual ranking errors **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00007)** while avoiding the instability risks of heterogeneous ensembles **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00008)**.
*   **Optimizer & Scheduler:** We will use **AdamW** combined with a **Cosine Annealing** learning rate scheduler. This combination ensures robust convergence and generalization **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00007)**.
*   **Training Duration:** We will utilize a reduced training schedule (e.g., 20 epochs). The combination of the "Wide" capacity **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00049)** and the convergence acceleration of SE blocks **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00012)** ensures the model will saturate the metric rapidly.
*   **Loss Function:** Binary Cross-Entropy with Logits Loss.

# Evaluation
*   **Test Time Augmentation (TTA):** During inference, we will generate predictions for the original image, a horizontally flipped version, and a vertically flipped version for each of the 5 model instances **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00005)**.
*   **Aggregation:** The final submission probability for each image will be the arithmetic mean of all TTA predictions across all 5 seeds. This maximizes robustness and minimizes stochastic noise.
