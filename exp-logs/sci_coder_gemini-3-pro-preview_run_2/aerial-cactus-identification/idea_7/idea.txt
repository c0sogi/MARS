Based on the comprehensive analysis of previous experiments and the specific lessons learned, I propose an optimized strategy that evolves the architecture into a **Custom ResNet-UNet Classifier**. This approach maximizes the preservation of spatial details—identified as critical for this low-resolution dataset—by adapting a segmentation-style Encoder-Decoder architecture for the classification task.

### Model
I propose a **Custom ResNet-UNet Architecture**. This represents a structural evolution from the "Multi-Scale ResNet" (Idea 6) by replacing the simple concatenation of multi-scale features with a full decoder pathway that progressively reconstructs high-resolution semantic features.

*   **Encoder (Backbone):** We will utilize the **Custom Lightweight ResNet** backbone that proved highly effective in previous runs (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**). It will feature 3 stages of residual blocks using **3x3 convolutions** (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00011**) and a channel configuration of `[16, 32, 64]` to maintain efficiency (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00016**). This encoder will downsample the input from 32x32 to an 8x8 bottleneck.
*   **Decoder (Structural Innovation):** Instead of classifying directly from the low-resolution bottleneck or a concatenated vector, we will introduce a lightweight **Decoder Pathway**. This pathway will upsample the features back from 8x8 $\to$ 16x16 $\to$ 32x32.
*   **Skip Connections:** We will implement lateral skip connections between the Encoder and Decoder at the 16x16 and 32x32 resolutions. This allows the network to recover fine-grained morphological details (e.g., spines) lost during downsampling and fuse them with the deep semantic context from the bottleneck, effectively generalizing the "Multi-Scale Aggregation" concept (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00016**).
*   **Head:** The final output of the decoder will be a **32x32 feature map**. We will apply **Global Average Pooling (GAP)** to this high-resolution map, followed by a single dense classification layer. This ensures the classifier has access to a representation that is both semantically deep and spatially preserved, addressing the resolution limitations identified in **Cite solution_lesson_node_00001**.

### Data
*   **Preprocessing:** Images will be loaded in their native **32x32** resolution and normalized to the [0, 1] range. We will strictly avoid resizing or upscaling, as this introduces interpolation artifacts (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00001**).
*   **Augmentation:** We will strictly adhere to the "light augmentation" strategy. We will apply only **Random Horizontal Flips** and **Random Vertical Flips**. We will explicitly avoid geometric rotations, color jittering, or cutout, as these techniques have been proven to degrade performance on this high-signal dataset (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**).

### Training
*   **Ensembling Strategy:** We will employ **Homogeneous Seed Averaging**. We will train **5 independent instances** of the Custom ResNet-UNet using different random seeds. This strategy is chosen to eliminate stochastic residual ranking errors (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00007**) while avoiding the instability observed with heterogeneous ensembles (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00008**).
*   **Optimizer & Scheduler:** We will use the **AdamW** optimizer combined with a **Cosine Annealing** learning rate scheduler. This combination has been demonstrated to ensure robust convergence and maximize validation metrics across multiple seeds (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00007**).
*   **Loss Function:** Binary Cross-Entropy with Logits Loss.

### Evaluation
*   **Test Time Augmentation (TTA):** During inference, we will generate predictions for the original image, a horizontally flipped version, and a vertically flipped version for each of the 5 model instances (**Cite solution_lesson_node_00005**).
*   **Aggregation:** The final submission probability for each image will be the arithmetic mean of all TTA predictions across all 5 seeds. This combines the noise-reduction benefits of TTA with the stability of seed averaging.
