Based on the lessons learned from the previous experiments, I propose a solution that evolves the architecture from a simple sequential CNN to a **Custom Residual Network (ResNet)** tailored specifically for 32x32 inputs, while maintaining the minimal regularization strategy that proved effective.

### Model
I propose replacing the sequential "ShallowCNN" with a **Custom Lightweight ResNet**.
*   **Architecture Design:** The network will begin with an initial 3x3 convolution to expand the channel dimension. This will be followed by a series of **Residual Blocks** grouped into three stages. Each Residual Block will consist of two 3x3 convolutions with Batch Normalization and ReLU, featuring a skip connection (identity shortcut) that adds the input of the block to its output.
*   **Spatial Preservation:** Unlike standard ResNet-50 backbones that aggressively downsample inputs to $1 \times 1$ too quickly, this custom architecture will be designed for the 32x32 input size. It will perform downsampling (via strided convolutions) only between stages, ensuring the feature maps remain large enough (e.g., 32x32 $\to$ 16x16 $\to$ 8x8) to capture fine-grained details. This directly addresses the finding that preserving spatial hierarchy is critical for low-resolution images **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00001)**.
*   **Global Average Pooling:** Instead of flattening the feature map into a dense layer (which can be parameter-heavy and prone to overfitting), the network will conclude with a **Global Average Pooling** layer. This structurally enforces spatial invariance and significantly reduces the parameter count before the final classification head.

### Data
*   **Preprocessing:** Images will be loaded in their native 32x32 resolution and normalized (pixel values scaled to [0, 1]). No resizing or upscaling will be performed.
*   **Augmentation:** We will strictly adhere to the "light augmentation" strategy. Only **Random Horizontal Flips** and **Random Vertical Flips** will be applied during training. We will explicitly avoid geometric rotations, color jittering, or cutout, as empirical evidence showed these aggressive techniques degrade performance on this high-signal dataset **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00002)**.

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** We will use **Binary Cross-Entropy with Logits Loss**.
*   **Optimizer:** The **Adam** optimizer will be used for its robust convergence properties.
*   **Regularization:** In line with previous findings, we will **remove Dropout** from the architecture. The structural regularization provided by the Residual connections and Global Average Pooling, combined with the early stopping mechanism, will be sufficient. This respects the lesson that adding unnecessary regularization to a high-performing baseline acts as noise **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00002)**.

### Evaluation
*   **Test Time Augmentation (TTA):** To maximize the Area Under the ROC Curve (AUC) without altering training dynamics, we will implement TTA during the inference phase. For each test image, the model will predict probabilities for the original image, a horizontally flipped version, and a vertically flipped version. The final prediction will be the average of these probabilities. This exploits the aerial nature of the data (where orientation is arbitrary) to smooth out random prediction noise.
