Based on the lessons learned from previous experiments, I propose an optimized strategy that evolves the successful ResNet-UNet hybrid into a **Custom Deeply Supervised Narrow ResNet-UNet**. This approach synthesizes the efficiency of narrow channel widths, the spatial precision of segmentation-style architectures, and the robustness of multi-scale feature learning.

### Model
I propose a **Custom Deeply Supervised Narrow ResNet-UNet**. This architecture builds upon the efficiency of the narrow ResNet-UNet but introduces a structural innovation—**Deep Supervision**—to explicitly enforce discriminative power at multiple resolutions.

*   **Backbone (Narrow ResNet):** We will utilize the lightweight ResNet encoder with a channel configuration of `[16, 32, 64]`. This specific width was proven to be sufficient for the dataset, drastically reducing computational cost without sacrificing accuracy **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00019)**. We will strictly use **3x3 convolutions** to match the receptive field to the 32x32 input resolution **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00011)**.
*   **Decoder (Spatial Recovery):** The network will feature a decoder pathway that upsamples features from the 8x8 bottleneck back to 16x16 and 32x32, utilizing lateral skip connections from the encoder. This preserves the fine-grained morphological details (e.g., spines) lost during downsampling **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00016)**.
*   **Structural Innovation (Dual Heads):** Instead of a single output, we will attach two classification heads:
    1.  **Semantic Head:** Attached to the 8x8 bottleneck feature map (after Global Average Pooling).
    2.  **Detail Head:** Attached to the final 32x32 decoder output (after Global Average Pooling).
    *   **Rationale:** This structure enforces a "Deep Supervision" paradigm. The Semantic Head ensures the encoder learns robust high-level abstractions (existence of object), while the Detail Head ensures the decoder successfully recovers and utilizes high-frequency texture information. This explicitly leverages the multi-scale aggregation insight **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00016)** within a unified training objective.

### Data
*   **Preprocessing:** Images will be loaded in their native **32x32** resolution and normalized to [0, 1]. We will strictly avoid resizing or upscaling to prevent interpolation artifacts **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00001)**.
*   **Augmentation:** We will apply only **Random Horizontal Flips** and **Random Vertical Flips**. We will explicitly avoid geometric rotations, color jittering, or cutout, as these have been proven to act as noise on this high-signal dataset **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00002)**.

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** We will use a joint loss function: $L_{total} = 0.5 \cdot L_{semantic} + 0.5 \cdot L_{detail}$, where both terms are Binary Cross-Entropy with Logits Loss. This ensures both pathways are optimized simultaneously.
*   **Optimizer & Scheduler:** We will use **AdamW** combined with a **Cosine Annealing** learning rate scheduler. This combination is critical for eliminating residual ranking errors and ensuring robust convergence **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00007)**.
*   **Ensembling Strategy:** We will employ **Homogeneous Seed Averaging**. We will train **5 independent instances** of this architecture using different random seeds. This is preferred over heterogeneous ensembles because the architecture is expected to saturate the metric, and mixing in less stable models introduces risk **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00008)**.

### Evaluation
*   **Test Time Augmentation (TTA):** During inference, we will generate predictions for the original image, a horizontally flipped version, and a vertically flipped version for each model instance **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00005)**.
*   **Aggregation:** For each image, we will compute the final probability by averaging the predictions from *both* the Semantic Head and the Detail Head, across all TTA versions and all 5 ensemble seeds. This maximizes the utilization of learned features from all scales.
