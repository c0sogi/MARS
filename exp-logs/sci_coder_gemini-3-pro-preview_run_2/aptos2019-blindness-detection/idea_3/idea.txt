Based on the empirical insights gathered from the previous experiments, I propose the following optimized strategy. This solution evolves the approach from a single-architecture ensemble to a **Heterogeneous Ensemble of Regressors**, leveraging architectural diversity to maximize generalization on the noisy ordinal labels.

# Model
I propose a **Dual-Backbone Ensemble** strategy to capture complementary feature representations of the retinal images.
*   **Heterogeneous Backbones:** Instead of relying solely on EfficientNet, we will train two distinct architectures:
    1.  **EfficientNet-B5:** Retained for its proven ability to resolve fine-grained details at high resolutions (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).
    2.  **ConvNeXt-Base:** Introduced as the structural innovation. ConvNeXt is a modern "Transformer-style" CNN that often learns different texture and shape biases compared to standard EfficientNets. Ensembling these distinct inductive biases will reduce the variance of the final predictions more effectively than ensembling identical models.
*   **Pooling (GeM):** Both backbones will utilize **Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling** instead of standard Global Average Pooling. This ensures that the models remain sensitive to sparse, high-activation lesions (like microaneurysms) within the large retinal background (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).
*   **Regression Head:** Both models will terminate in a single linear neuron outputting a continuous scalar, maintaining the regression formulation that aligns mathematically with the Quadratic Weighted Kappa metric (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).

# Data
The data pipeline prioritizes effective resolution and robust, non-deterministic augmentation.
*   **Preprocessing (Circle Crop & Scale):** Apply the **Circle Crop** technique to remove uninformative black borders, then resize images to **456x456**. This ensures the model capacity is matched with sufficient input detail to resolve small pathologies (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004, Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).
*   **Augmentation:**
    *   **Geometric:** Apply aggressive geometric augmentations (Random Horizontal/Vertical Flip, Rotation up to 30°) to prevent overfitting (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
    *   **Photometric (Stochastic CLAHE):** Apply Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE) with a probability of **$p=0.5$**. We explicitly avoid setting $p=1.0$ to ensure the model learns to handle both enhanced and raw contrast distributions, preventing the train-inference distribution mismatch observed previously (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014).

# Training
The training procedure focuses on stability and maximizing the area under the optimization curve.
*   **Cross-Validation:** Implement **5-Fold Cross-Validation** for *both* architectures. This results in a total of 10 trained models (5 EfficientNet-B5 + 5 ConvNeXt-Base), ensuring robust coverage of the data distribution (Cite solution_lesson_node_00012).
*   **Loss Function:** Use **Mean Squared Error (MSE)** to directly optimize the proxy for the Quadratic Weighted Kappa metric (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
*   **Scheduler:** Use the **Cosine Annealing Learning Rate Scheduler** to avoid premature stagnation and allow the models to settle into better global minima (Cite solution_lesson_node_00003).
*   **Checkpointing:** Save model weights based on the best Validation Kappa score rather than the final epoch, safeguarding against optimization noise (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).

# Evaluation
*   **Ensemble Aggregation:** Generate predictions using all 10 models. Crucially, average the **continuous regression scores** from all models *before* rounding. This "soft voting" stabilizes the predictions against the quantization noise inherent in ordinal classification (Cite solution_lesson_node_00012).
*   **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):** To further boost stability, average the predictions of the original image and its horizontal flip for each model.
*   **Post-processing:** Round the final averaged continuous score to the nearest integer to produce the diagnosis labels (0-4).
