Based on the accumulated lessons and the analysis of previous attempts, I propose a **Large-Scale Heterogeneous Ensemble with Layer-Wise Optimization**.

This strategy evolves the architecture by upgrading the semantic backbones to their "Large" variants (DeBERTa-v3-Large and RoBERTa-Large) to maximize model capacity. It addresses the failure of previous "Task-Adaptive Pretraining" attempts (Lesson 00015) by reallocating the computational budget entirely to **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**, ensuring full convergence over 3 epochs (Lesson 00011). To manage the increased memory footprint of Large models on the A100 GPU, we will utilize **Gradient Accumulation**.

### Model
The solution employs a tri-branch ensemble designed to maximize architectural diversity [Cite solution_lesson_node_00007]:

1.  **Branch A: DeBERTa-v3-Large (Primary Semantic Backbone)**
    *   **Architecture:** `microsoft/deberta-v3-large`. This model utilizes disentangled attention and a larger parameter space to capture subtle toxicity nuances and long-range dependencies better than the "Base" variants used previously.
    *   **Head:** A **Multi-Sample Dropout** classification head (averaging outputs from 5 parallel dropout masks) to smooth the loss landscape and accelerate convergence [Cite solution_lesson_node_00009].
2.  **Branch B: RoBERTa-Large (Secondary Semantic Backbone)**
    *   **Architecture:** `roberta-large`. Retained to provide ensemble diversity. Its standard self-attention mechanism produces error modes uncorrelated with DeBERTa's disentangled attention, which is critical for ensemble gain [Cite solution_lesson_node_00007].
    *   **Head:** Multi-Sample Dropout.
3.  **Branch C: Linear Baseline (Surface/Lexical)**
    *   **Architecture:** One-Vs-Rest Logistic Regression.
    *   **Features:** Concatenated **Word N-grams (1-2)** and **Character N-grams (2-6)**. This branch is explicitly preserved to handle "out-of-vocabulary" noise, misspellings, and obfuscations (e.g., "f**k") that subword tokenizers might fragment or misinterpret [Cite solution_lesson_node_00001, solution_lesson_node_00006].

### Data
1.  **Preprocessing:**
    *   **Branch C:** Compute TF-IDF sparse matrices once and **cache them to disk**. This eliminates vectorization overhead during iterative runs [Cite solution_lesson_node_00005].
    *   **Branches A & B:** Use **Dynamic Padding** with a maximum sequence length of **256 tokens**. This increase (from 128/160) ensures that toxic signals appearing late in "rant-style" comments are captured [Cite solution_lesson_node_00008].
2.  **Augmentation:** None. We rely on the model's capacity and the natural noise of the dataset.

### Training
1.  **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):**
    *   **Epochs:** Strictly train for **3 Epochs**. Previous attempts failed when reducing SFT to 1 epoch; multi-pass learning is essential for convergence [Cite solution_lesson_node_00011].
    *   **Priority:** Allocate all GPU time to SFT. Do not perform Task-Adaptive Pretraining (TAPT), as it previously led to under-fitting when it encroached on the SFT budget [Cite solution_lesson_node_00015].
2.  **Optimization:**
    *   **Layer-wise Learning Rate Decay (LLRD):** Apply multiplicative decay (e.g., 0.9x per layer) to the learning rates. This stabilizes the fine-tuning of Large models by preserving their pre-trained general knowledge while allowing the top layers to adapt aggressively [Cite solution_lesson_node_00009].
    *   **Scheduler:** Use a **Cosine Schedule with Warmup** to ensure better convergence compared to linear decay [Cite solution_lesson_node_00008].
    *   **Gradient Accumulation:** Accumulate gradients over multiple steps to simulate a batch size of 32 or 64, allowing the "Large" models to fit within the 40GB GPU memory.
    *   **Loss:** Binary Cross-Entropy with Logits.

### Evaluation
1.  **Inference:**
    *   Generate probability predictions for the test set using DeBERTa-Large, RoBERTa-Large, and the Linear model.
2.  **Optimized Blending:**
    *   Perform a **validation-driven scalar minimization** (using `scipy.optimize`) to determine the optimal weights $w_1, w_2, w_3$ for the ensemble ($P_{final} = w_1 P_{DeBERTa} + w_2 P_{RoBERTa} + w_3 P_{Linear}$). This ensures the linear baseline's contribution is mathematically calibrated to complement the high-capacity deep models [Cite solution_lesson_node_00007].
