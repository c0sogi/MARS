Based on the progression of previous ideas and the empirical lessons learned, I propose a **Heterogeneous High-Capacity Ensemble** strategy. This approach evolves the solution from a single-architecture focus to a diverse ensemble that leverages complementary inductive biases (Convolutional vs. Transformer) while strictly adhering to the convergence and calibration principles established in the lessons.

### Model
I propose constructing a heterogeneous ensemble consisting of two distinct high-capacity architectures: **ConvNeXt-Small** and **Swin-Small**.
*   **Structural Innovation (Heterogeneity):** Previous attempts relied on scaling a single architecture (ResNet $\to$ EfficientNet $\to$ ConvNeXt). By combining **ConvNeXt** (which excels at local feature extraction via convolutions) and **Swin Transformer** (which captures long-range global dependencies via shifted-window attention), we create an ensemble where the errors are less correlated, leading to superior log loss reduction compared to homogeneous ensembles.
*   **Capacity Retention:** We retain the **ConvNeXt-Small** capacity level, which is superior to the "Tiny" variants used in earlier successful iterations, ensuring the model has sufficient parameter depth to model complex features **Cite solution_lesson_node_00015**.

### Data
*   **Preprocessing:** All images will be resized to **224x224** pixels. This resolution balances computational efficiency with feature preservation, avoiding the training throughput bottlenecks observed with 384x384 resolution **Cite solution_lesson_node_00015**.
*   **Splitting:** We will employ **5-Fold Stratified Cross-Validation**. This ensures that every training image is utilized for learning (in 4 out of 5 folds) and provides out-of-fold predictions for robust validation, addressing the data utility limitations of single-split training **Cite solution_lesson_node_00002**.
*   **Augmentation:** We will strictly maintain the optimized augmentation pipeline:
    *   **RandomResizedCrop:** With a minimum scale of **0.8**. This prevents the semantic noise introduced by aggressive cropping (e.g., 0.5) while still forcing feature invariance **Cite solution_lesson_node_00013**.
    *   **Mixup:** Applied with `alpha=0.2` to enforce soft decision boundaries and probability calibration, which is critical for minimizing Log Loss **Cite solution_lesson_node_00011**.
    *   **HorizontalFlip:** For basic geometric invariance.

### Training
*   **Strategy:** We will train a total of **10 models** (5 folds $\times$ 2 architectures). This fits within the 24-hour computational budget on the A100 GPU (estimated ~12-14 hours total).
*   **Optimization:** Each model will be trained using the **AdamW** optimizer with a **Cosine Annealing Learning Rate** scheduler.
*   **Convergence & Duration:** Each fold will be trained for **15 epochs** without aggressive early stopping. We will disable early stopping (or set patience to the full duration) to ensure the learning rate fully decays, allowing the models to settle into the global minimum. This addresses the "under-trained large model" failure mode **Cite solution_lesson_node_00015** and aligns with the scheduler's dynamics **Cite solution_lesson_node_00016**.
*   **Checkpointing:** We will save the weights with the lowest validation log loss for each fold **Cite solution_lesson_node_00004**.

### Evaluation
*   **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):** For every test image, each of the 10 models will predict probabilities for both the original and a horizontally flipped version. These two predictions will be averaged to reduce individual model variance **Cite solution_lesson_node_00011**.
*   **Ensemble Aggregation:** The final submission probability will be the arithmetic mean of the TTA-averaged predictions from all 10 models (5 ConvNeXt-Small + 5 Swin-Small). This double-averaging strategy maximizes stability and leverages the diversity of the heterogeneous architectures to minimize Log Loss.
