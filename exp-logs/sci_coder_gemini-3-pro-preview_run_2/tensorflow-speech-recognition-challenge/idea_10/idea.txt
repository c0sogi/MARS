Based on the comprehensive analysis of previous attempts and the synthesized lessons, I propose the following optimized strategy.

### 1. Model: EfficientNetV2-B0 with Single-Head Attention Pooling
I propose a structural evolution that combines the efficiency of the **EfficientNetV2** backbone with the proven localization capability of **Single-Head Attention Pooling**, explicitly correcting the architectural over-engineering (Multi-Head Attention) that caused the failure of Idea 9.

*   **Backbone (EfficientNetV2-B0)**: We will utilize **EfficientNetV2-B0** initialized with ImageNet weights.
    *   *Structural Innovation*: Unlike V1 (used in Idea 8), V2 employs **Fused-MBConv** blocks in the early stages. This replaces the depthwise-separable convolutions with standard convolutions, which are computationally more efficient on GPUs and better suited for the dense, continuous nature of spectrograms **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00035)**.
    *   *Input Adaptation*: We will modify the first projection layer to accept 1-channel input. Crucially, we will initialize the weights by **summing** the original RGB weights to preserve activation magnitudes, preventing signal collapse **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00019)**.
*   **Head (Single-Head Attention Pooling)**: We will strictly employ a **Single-Head Attention Pooling** layer.
    *   *Correction*: We reject the Multi-Head approach from Idea 9. For single-event detection (one command per clip), a single attention map acts as a learned "Voice Activity Detector," effectively localizing the speech and suppressing noise. Multi-head attention introduces redundant parameters that dilute this signal **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00035)**.

### 2. Data: Dual-Domain Augmentation & Spectral Oversampling
The data pipeline will be restructured to perform augmentations in both the **Waveform** and **Spectrogram** domains, maximizing robustness while maintaining the high signal fidelity identified as critical in Idea 8.

*   **Waveform Domain (Noise Injection)**: Before spectrogram generation, we will apply **Background Noise Mixing**. We will dynamically mix random segments from the `_background_noise_` folder into the training clips with a high Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR > 10dB). This forces the model to learn features invariant to the specific noise profiles found in the dataset, addressing the "Silence/Unknown" confusion.
*   **Spectral Oversampling**: We will generate Log-Mel Spectrograms on-the-fly with **Spectral Oversampling**. We will use a window size of **25ms** (400 samples) but an FFT size (`n_fft`) of **1024**. This zero-padding interpolates the frequency spectrum, increasing the density of the Mel-filterbank inputs and reducing quantization noise **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00034)**.
*   **Spectrogram Domain (SpecAugment)**: We will apply Time and Frequency masking. The mask parameters will be calibrated to be a conservative fraction (<20%) of the input dimensions to act as a regularizer without destroying the signal **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00005)**.
*   **Sampling**: We will strictly employ a `WeightedRandomSampler` to balance the 12 classes in every batch **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00001)**.

### 3. Training: Model EMA & Robust Optimization
We will introduce **Model Exponential Moving Average (EMA)** to stabilize training against the increased variance from the Noise Injection.

*   **Model EMA**: We will maintain a shadow copy of the model weights that is updated as a moving average of the training weights. This smooths the optimization trajectory and typically yields a model that generalizes better to unseen test data.
*   **Loss Function**: We will use Cross-Entropy Loss with **Label Smoothing (0.1)** to prevent overconfidence and encourage robust clustering **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00013)**.
*   **Batch Size**: We will use a small batch size of **32**. This ensures a high frequency of gradient updates (~14k updates), which is critical for convergence on this dataset size **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00031)**.
*   **Optimizer**: We will use **AdamW** with a **Cosine Annealing** learning rate scheduler.

### 4. Evaluation
*   **Inference**: Test audio clips will be processed using the **EMA weights** of the trained model. The preprocessing will mirror the training pipeline (Spectral Oversampling: 1024 FFT, 400 Window, 160 Hop) but without Noise Injection or SpecAugment.
*   **Prediction**: The model will output class probabilities via Softmax. The label corresponding to the highest probability will be selected.
