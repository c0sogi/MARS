### Proposed Baseline: Hierarchical Sentence-Factorized Network

This approach introduces a **Sentence-Level Siamese** architecture. It diverges from previous ideas by changing the fundamental unit of analysis from "tokens" (Ideas 3, 4, 7, 9) or "whole candidates" (Idea 1) to **sentences**. By modeling the document as a bag-of-sentences, this approach drastically reduces the sequence length the model must process, offering a highly efficient baseline that aligns well with the structure of short answers (which are often complete sentences).

#### **Model**
*   **Embedding Layer:** The model uses pre-trained static word embeddings (e.g., GloVe) to convert tokens into dense vectors. These are frozen to ensure maximum training speed.
*   **Shared Encoder (Deep Averaging Network):** A shared **Deep Averaging Network (DAN)** processes the Question and each Sentence within a candidate independently.
    *   **Averaging:** It computes the unweighted average of the word embeddings in the sequence.
    *   **Non-Linearity:** The averaged vector is passed through a shallow Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) with ReLU activations to generate a dense semantic embedding for the question ($V_q$) and the sentence ($V_s$).
*   **Interaction Layer:** The relevance of a sentence is calculated using the **Cosine Similarity** between $V_q$ and $V_s$.
*   **Ranking Head (Long Answer):** The score for a Long Answer Candidate is derived via **Max-Pooling** over the scores of its constituent sentences. The logic is that if a candidate contains at least one highly relevant sentence, the candidate itself is likely relevant.
*   **Span Prediction (Short Answer):** This model does not use a separate span prediction head. Instead, it implicitly predicts the short answer as the **Sentence** that produced the maximum similarity score within the selected long answer.
*   **Yes/No Head:** The encoded question vector $V_q$ and the vector of the best-scoring sentence $V_{best}$ are concatenated and passed through a small classifier to predict `YES`, `NO`, or `NONE`.

#### **Data**
*   **Preprocessing:**
    *   **Segmentation:** The `document_text` is segmented into individual sentences using a fast rule-based splitter (e.g., splitting on periods and newlines).
    *   **Mapping:** Sentences are grouped by the `long_answer_candidates` they fall into.
    *   **Tokenization:** Sentences are tokenized by whitespace.
    *   **Vocabulary:** Tokens are mapped to indices in the pre-trained embedding matrix.
*   **Labeling:**
    *   **Sentence Labels:** A sentence is labeled positive (`1`) if it overlaps significantly (e.g., Jaccard similarity > 0.5) with the ground truth short answer span. All other sentences are negative (`0`).
    *   **Yes/No:** Categorical labels for the three classes.

#### **Training**
*   **Strategy:** The model is trained primarily as a **Sentence Classifier**. It learns to distinguish sentences that answer the question from those that do not.
*   **Sampling:** **Random Negative Sampling** is applied at the sentence level. Each training batch consists of positive sentence pairs (Question, Answer Sentence) and a ratio of negative pairs (Question, Irrelevant Sentence) sampled from the same document.
*   **Loss Function:**
    *   **Binary Cross-Entropy** is used to optimize the sentence similarity scores.
    *   **Categorical Cross-Entropy** is used for the Yes/No head.
*   **Optimization:** The Adam optimizer is used with a standard learning rate.

#### **Evaluation**
1.  **Inference:** For a test document, the model encodes the question and every sentence within every long answer candidate.
2.  **Scoring:** Cosine similarity scores are computed for all Question-Sentence pairs.
3.  **Long Answer Selection:** Each candidate is assigned a score equal to the maximum score of its sentences. The candidate with the highest aggregate score is selected. If this score is below a **confidence threshold**, the prediction is `BLANK`.
4.  **Short Answer Extraction:** The specific sentence responsible for the candidate's maximum score is selected as the short answer span.
5.  **Yes/No Prediction:** The label is predicted using the Yes/No head based on the best matching sentence.
