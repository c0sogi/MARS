Based on the accumulated lessons, I propose the **High-Efficiency Disentangled Transformer**. This strategy evolves the approach by combining the superior linguistic capabilities of pre-trained Transformers (proven in Lesson 12) with the specialized architectural and efficiency optimizations developed during the LSTM experiments (Lessons 2, 6, 7), allowing us to train a high-capacity model on the full dataset within the time constraints.

### Model
We will construct a **Multi-Task RoBERTa with Attention Pooling**.
*   **Backbone:** We will use **RoBERTa-base** as the feature extractor. Lesson 12 demonstrated that this pre-trained architecture, even on a small data subset, outperforms RNNs trained on the full dataset due to its superior ability to disentangle semantic context.
*   **Aggregation (Structural Innovation):** Standard Transformer implementations rely on the `[CLS]` token for classification. We will instead discard the `[CLS]` token and apply **Attention Pooling** to the full sequence of hidden states. Lesson 2 showed that Attention Pooling significantly outperforms static pooling by dynamically weighting relevant tokens, which is crucial for distinguishing toxic intent from identity mentions.
*   **Regularization:** We will apply **Spatial Dropout** to the Transformer's hidden states before the pooling layer. This technique, validated in Lesson 2, drops entire feature channels across the sequence, forcing the model to learn robust, distributed representations and preventing overfitting to specific identity keywords.
*   **Multi-Task Heads:** The pooled output will feed into two separate classification heads: a **Toxicity Head** (primary) and an **Identity Head** (auxiliary). This structure forces the backbone to explicitly model identities as distinct concepts, preventing spurious correlations (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).
*   **Capacity Scaling:** The combination of the Transformer backbone and the Attention mechanism provides the necessary model capacity to support **Aggressive Multi-Task Weighting** without underfitting the primary task (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).

### Data
We will process the **Full Dataset** using the **"Pad-then-Trim"** strategy to maximize GPU throughput.
*   **Preprocessing:** Text will be tokenized using the RoBERTa subword tokenizer and pre-padded to a fixed length (e.g., 512) into contiguous `int32` arrays.
*   **Device-Side Trimming:** To avoid the CPU bottlenecks observed with custom collation, we will load fixed-length batches to the GPU and dynamically slice them to the maximum non-padding length of the specific batch before the forward pass (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007). This efficiency enables us to train the heavy Transformer on the full dataset, evolving beyond the subsampling strategy used in Lesson 12.

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** We will employ an **Aggressive Multi-Task Loss**. The total loss will be a weighted sum of the Toxicity BCE and Identity BCE, with a high weight (e.g., $\lambda=0.8$) assigned to the Identity task. This forces the model to decouple identity recognition from toxicity prediction (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004).
*   **Optimizer & Scheduler:** We will use the **AdamW** optimizer combined with the **OneCycleLR** scheduler. This scheduling policy accelerates convergence, allowing the complex model to fit the data effectively within the 24-hour runtime limit (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).
*   **Dynamic Padding:** By implementing dynamic padding via Device-Side Trimming, we ensure that compute is not wasted on padding tokens, further optimizing the training loop (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).

### Evaluation
*   **Inference:** The test set will be processed using the same tokenizer and Device-Side Trimming pipeline.
*   **Prediction:** We will generate predictions using solely the **Toxicity Head**, discarding the auxiliary Identity Head outputs.
