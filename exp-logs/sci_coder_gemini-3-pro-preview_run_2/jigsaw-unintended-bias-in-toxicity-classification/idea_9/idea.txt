Based on the accumulated lessons and the analysis of previous experiments, I propose the **RoBERTa-Large with Identity-Centric Sample Weighting and Multi-Sample Dropout**.

This strategy represents a structural evolution that synthesizes the superior linguistic capabilities of "Large" Transformers (Lesson 12) with a novel optimization approach to resolve the conflict between primary task performance and bias mitigation (Lesson 15). It further integrates advanced regularization to stabilize the training of this high-capacity model.

### Model
We will construct a **Multi-Task RoBERTa-Large** architecture designed for maximum capacity and robust generalization.

*   **Backbone:** We will upgrade the feature extractor to **RoBERTa-Large**. Lesson 12 proved that pre-trained Transformers significantly outperform RNNs, and Lesson 4 demonstrated that **Capacity Scaling** is critical when disentangling complex confounders (identities) from targets (toxicity). The "Large" variant provides the necessary depth to model these distinct concepts without underfitting (Cite solution_lesson_node_00012, solution_lesson_node_00004).
*   **Aggregation:** We will replace the standard `[CLS]` token classification with **Attention Pooling**. This allows the model to dynamically weight tokens (e.g., emphasizing the context around an identity term rather than the term itself), which Lesson 2 showed is superior to static pooling for capturing nuance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
*   **Regularization (Structural Innovation):** We will implement **Multi-Sample Dropout** after the pooling layer. Instead of a single dropout mask followed by a classifier, we will generate multiple different dropout masks in parallel, pass the features through the shared classifier weights, and average the outputs. This technique accelerates convergence and acts as an internal ensemble, smoothing the decision boundary and reducing variance more effectively than the standard Spatial Dropout alone (though we will retain Spatial Dropout at the embedding level, Cite solution_lesson_node_00002).
*   **Multi-Task Heads:** We retain the auxiliary **Identity Head** to explicitly supervise the learning of identity features, ensuring they are not conflated with toxicity (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001).

### Data
We will process the **Full Dataset** using the efficiency optimizations required to train the "Large" model within the runtime limit.

*   **Preprocessing:** Text will be tokenized using the RoBERTa-Large tokenizer and pre-padded to a fixed length (e.g., 512) into contiguous `int32` arrays.
*   **Device-Side Trimming:** We will strictly employ the **"Pad-then-Trim"** strategy. Fixed-length batches are loaded to the GPU and dynamically sliced to the maximum non-padding length of that specific batch before the forward pass. This maximizes GPU throughput and avoids the CPU bottlenecks associated with custom collation (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).

### Training
The training procedure introduces a structural shift in how bias is mitigated to resolve the conflict identified in Lesson 15.

*   **Identity-Centric Sample Weighting (Structural Innovation):** Lesson 15 warned that aggressive auxiliary weights ($\lambda > 1.0$) cause the model to overfit to identities, degrading primary performance. To mitigate bias *without* this side effect, we will shift the pressure to the **Primary Toxicity Loss**. We will pre-calculate a sample weight vector where any example mentioning an identity is assigned a significantly higher weight (e.g., 5.0). This forces the model to prioritize the "hard" examples (BPSN/BNSP regions) directly in the main task.
*   **Loss Function:** The total loss will be a weighted sum where the auxiliary task acts only as a regularizer:
    $$L_{total} = L_{BCE}(\text{Tox}, \text{weight}=w_{sample}) + \lambda_{aux} L_{BCE}(\text{Ident})$$
    We will keep $\lambda_{aux}$ moderate (e.g., 0.4) to guide feature learning without hijacking gradients (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015).
*   **Optimizer:** We will use **AdamW** with **OneCycleLR** scheduling to achieve super-convergence, enabling the heavy model to fit the data efficiently within the time constraints (Cite solution_lesson_node_00006).

### Evaluation
*   **Metric Stability:** When calculating the Generalized Mean of Bias AUCs ($p=-5$) for internal validation, we will **clip the predictions** to a safe range (e.g., `[1e-6, 1-1e-6]`). This prevents the divide-by-zero errors that caused previous high-capacity attempts to fail (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).
*   **Prediction:** We will generate predictions using solely the **Toxicity Head** (averaged across the Multi-Sample Dropout masks), discarding the auxiliary Identity outputs.
