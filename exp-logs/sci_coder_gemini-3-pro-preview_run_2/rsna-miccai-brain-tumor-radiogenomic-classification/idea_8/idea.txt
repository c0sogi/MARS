Based on the extensive empirical evidence gathered from previous experiments, I propose the **Asymmetric Grouped EfficientNet with Consensus-Based Anatomical Anchoring**.

This solution structurally evolves the "Content-Adaptive" approach by resolving its two critical failure modes: the loss of feature diversity during model initialization (Cite 00023) and the brittleness of single-metric ROI heuristics (Cite 00005, Cite 00018).

### Model: Asymmetric Grouped EfficientNet-B0
We will utilize **EfficientNet-B0** as the backbone due to its proven efficiency (Cite 00001), but we will implement a specific **Asymmetric Initialization Strategy** to maximize feature transfer.

1.  **Grouped Convolutional Stem:** The first convolutional layer is modified to accept **12 channels** (4 modalities $\times$ 3 slices) using **Grouped Convolutions** with `groups=4`. This enforces the learning of independent feature representations for FLAIR, T1w, T1wCE, and T2w in the initial layer (Cite 00007).
2.  **Structural Innovation: Asymmetric Filter Distribution:** Unlike previous iterations that replicated a small subset of weights (Symmetric Initialization), we will explicitly **distribute the full bank of 32 pre-trained ImageNet filters** across the 4 modality groups (Cite 00023).
    *   *Mechanism:* We assign pre-trained filters `0-7` to the FLAIR group, `8-15` to T1w, `16-23` to T1wCE, and `24-31` to T2w.
    *   *Rationale:* This preserves the complete diversity of edge, texture, and color detectors learned from ImageNet, providing a richer starting point than repeating the same 8 edge detectors four times.
3.  **Regularized Head:** We explicitly retain the **Dropout layer** in the final classification head to prevent overfitting, avoiding the common pitfall of attaching raw linear layers to backbone features (Cite 00017).

### Data: Consensus-Based Anatomical Anchoring
To address the instability of the "Max Intensity" heuristic (which often selects bright artifacts, Cite 00005), we introduce a **Multi-Objective Consensus** pipeline for Region of Interest (ROI) selection.

1.  **Robust Loading:** We implement a **Raw Binary Fallback** to ensure valid pixel data is loaded even if standard DICOM parsers fail (Cite 00014).
2.  **Boundary Exclusion:** We strictly **exclude the top and bottom 15%** of the slice volume from the search space to remove coil noise and non-brain anatomy (Cite 00018).
3.  **Consensus Scoring:** For the FLAIR modality, we calculate two Z-axis profiles:
    *   **Mean Intensity:** To capture hyper-intense regions (edema/tumor) (Cite 00019).
    *   **Pixel Variance:** To capture heterogeneous textures characteristic of glioblastoma (unlike uniform artifacts).
    *   *Selection:* We normalize both profiles to $[0, 1]$ and sum them. The slice index with the maximum **Combined Score** is selected as the Anchor.
4.  **Strided Stacking:** We extract the Anchor slice and its neighbors with a **Stride of 5** (Anchor-5, Anchor, Anchor+5) to maximize Z-axis context without input inflation (Cite 00010).
5.  **Absolute Alignment:** We use **Absolute Indexing** to retrieve the corresponding slices from T1w, T1wCE, and T2w, ensuring anatomical alignment across modalities with varying Fields of View (Cite 00024).
6.  **Parsimony:** The final input is a **12-channel tensor** (3 slices $\times$ 4 modalities) (Cite 00002).

### Training: Signal-Preserving Optimization
The training strategy is designed to force the model to rely on the robust, pre-trained features while preventing memorization of the small dataset.

*   **Augmentation:** We employ **Geometric Augmentations** (HorizontalFlip, VerticalFlip) which are critical for generalization (Cite 00015). We include **Random Rotation**, but strictly limit it to **$\pm 15^{\circ}$** to respect the canonical anatomical orientation (Cite 00020). We explicitly **exclude** destructive techniques like Cutout or CoarseDropout (Cite 00003).
*   **Regularization:** We maintain **Aggressive Weight Decay (`1e-2`)** to constrain model complexity (Cite 00011).
*   **Loss & Optimizer:** Binary Cross Entropy with Logits Loss and the **AdamW** optimizer.

### Evaluation
*   **Inference:** For each test subject, we generate the input tensor using the **Consensus-Based Anchoring** pipeline on the FLAIR scan.
*   **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):** We generate predictions for the original tensor, a horizontally flipped version, and a vertically flipped version. We average these probabilities to produce the final `MGMT_value` (Cite 00015).
