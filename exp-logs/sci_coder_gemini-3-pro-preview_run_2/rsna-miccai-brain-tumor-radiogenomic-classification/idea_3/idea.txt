Based on the empirical findings from the provided lessons, I propose a **Siamese Hybrid-Sampling 2.5D Network**. This solution addresses the instability of the content-adaptive approach and the weak signal of the geometric approach by structurally fusing both strategies into a unified, end-to-end trainable pipeline.

### Model
The core architecture is a **Siamese Neural Network** that processes two distinct "views" of the patient's brain volume simultaneously using a shared feature extractor.

*   **Backbone:** We will use **EfficientNet-B0** as the shared feature extractor. This preserves the parameter efficiency required for this small dataset (Cite 00001).
*   **Input Stem (Structural Innovation):** The first convolutional layer of the backbone will be modified to accept **12 channels** (3 slices $\times$ 4 modalities). Crucially, we will employ **Grouped Convolutions** with `groups=4` to force the network to learn modality-specific features (FLAIR, T1w, T1wCE, T2w) in the initial layer (Cite 00007). We will initialize these weights by **replicating the pre-trained RGB weights** four times to ensure robust convergence (Cite 00006).
*   **Dual-Stream Architecture:**
    1.  **Stream A (ROI View):** Processes a 12-channel tensor focused on the tumor region (Content-Adaptive).
    2.  **Stream B (Global View):** Processes a 12-channel tensor focused on the overall brain structure (Geometric).
    *   Both streams share the exact same weights (Siamese configuration).
*   **Fusion Head:** The Global Average Pooled feature vectors from both streams are concatenated. This combined vector is passed through a lightweight fully connected layer to produce the final `MGMT_value` probability. This allows the model to leverage the high signal strength of the ROI view while using the Global view as a stabilizer against heuristic failure.

### Data
We will generate two distinct input tensors for every subject, avoiding the "input inflation" trap of stacking too many slices into a single tensor (Cite 00002).

1.  **View 1: Content-Adaptive (ROI) Stack**
    *   Iterate through the **FLAIR** MRI slices to identify the index of the slice with the **maximum signal intensity** (Cite 00005).
    *   Extract 3 slices centered on this index (Index - 1, Index, Index + 1).
    *   Extract the corresponding slices from T1w, T1wCE, and T2w.
    *   Stack to form a `(12, H, W)` tensor.

2.  **View 2: Geometric (Uniform) Stack**
    *   Calculate slice indices at **25%, 50%, and 75%** of the total volume depth (Cite 00001).
    *   Extract these slices from all 4 modalities.
    *   Stack to form a second `(12, H, W)` tensor.

3.  **Preprocessing:** All images will be resized to 256x256 and normalized to the [0, 1] range.

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** Binary Cross Entropy with Logits Loss.
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with a standard learning rate (e.g., 1e-4).
*   **Augmentation:** We will strictly limit augmentation to **geometric transformations** (RandomRotate90, HorizontalFlip, VerticalFlip). We will explicitly **exclude** destructive regularization techniques like CoarseDropout or Cutout, as these have been proven to extinguish the weak predictive signal in sparse 2.5D inputs (Cite 00003).
*   **Strategy:** The model is trained end-to-end. The Siamese constraint ensures the backbone learns features that are robust across different slice selection strategies, effectively acting as a spatial ensemble.

### Evaluation
For each subject in the test set:
1.  Generate the **ROI Stack** (using the max-intensity heuristic on the test FLAIR scan).
2.  Generate the **Geometric Stack** (using fixed depth percentiles).
3.  Pass both tensors through the trained Siamese Network.
4.  The network will output a single fused probability for the `MGMT_value`.
