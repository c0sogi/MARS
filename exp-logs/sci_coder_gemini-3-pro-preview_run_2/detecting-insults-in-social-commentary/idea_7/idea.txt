Based on the comprehensive analysis of the previous ideas and the specific lessons learned, I propose a **Three-Stage Semi-Supervised Pipeline** that integrates Task-Adaptive Pre-Training (TAPT), Self-Training (Pseudo-Labeling), and Adversarial Weight Perturbation (AWP).

This strategy synthesizes the domain adaptation of Idea 6, the data augmentation of Idea 5, and the robust optimization of Idea 4, while explicitly correcting the engineering failures (under-convergence, dilution) identified in Lesson 00022.

# Model
We will utilize a **Homogeneous Ensemble** based on the **`microsoft/deberta-v3-large`** architecture.
- **Backbone**: We retain the "Large" variant as it is the only backbone proven to capture the necessary semantic nuances for high-performance ranking (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016). We explicitly reject mixing in weaker architectures like RoBERTa to avoid ensemble dilution (Cite solution_lesson_node_00022).
- **Head**: The classification head will employ **Mean Pooling** of the last hidden state (Cite solution_lesson_node_00009) followed by a **Multi-Sample Dropout** mechanism (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015). This structure maximizes signal aggregation and provides internal regularization.

# Data
We will implement a progressive data processing pipeline to leverage both the labeled and unlabeled data provided.
1.  **Preprocessing**: Apply the standard cleaning (unicode un-escaping, quote removal) to all text.
2.  **TAPT Corpus**: Concatenate the text from `train.csv` and `test.csv` to create a domain-specific corpus.
3.  **Pseudo-Labeling (Augmentation)**:
    *   After training the "Teacher" model (Stage 2), we will generate probability predictions for the entire `test.csv`.
    *   Samples with high confidence (e.g., $P(Insult) > 0.9$ or $P(Insult) < 0.1$) will be assigned their predicted class as a hard label.
    *   These pseudo-labeled samples will be merged with the original `train.csv` to create an **Augmented Training Set** for the "Student" model.

# Training
The training will execute in three sequential stages to maximize the utility of the 24-hour budget.

**Stage 1: Task-Adaptive Pre-Training (TAPT)**
- We will fine-tune the `deberta-v3-large` backbone on the TAPT Corpus using a **Masked Language Modeling (MLM)** objective. This adapts the model's internal representations to the specific vocabulary and linguistic patterns of the forum comments (e.g., slang, insults) before any supervised learning occurs.

**Stage 2: Teacher Training (Standard Fine-Tuning)**
- **Objective**: Train a robust 5-Fold Cross-Validation ensemble to generate high-quality pseudo-labels.
- **Initialization**: Initialize with weights from Stage 1 (TAPT).
- **Optimization**: Use **AdamW** with **Layer-wise Learning Rate Decay (LLRD)** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015) to preserve pre-trained features.
- **Checkpointing**: Save the best model state using **`copy.deepcopy`** based strictly on **Validation AUC** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004, solution_lesson_node_00006).

**Stage 3: Student Training (Adversarial Self-Training)**
- **Objective**: Train the final 5-Fold ensemble on the **Augmented Training Set**.
- **Initialization**: Initialize with weights from Stage 1 (TAPT) to ensure a stable starting point for the new data manifold.
- **Adversarial Weight Perturbation (AWP)**: We will inject AWP into the training loop to minimize the sharpness of the loss landscape.
- **Correction of Previous Failure**: Unlike the failed Idea 4, we will run this stage for a sufficient number of epochs (e.g., 5-6) to ensure full convergence. The computational budget accommodates this (~2x cost) because we are not training a second distinct architecture, avoiding the resource contention that led to under-training previously (Cite solution_lesson_node_00022).

# Evaluation
- **Inference**: Generate predictions on `test.csv` using the **Stage 3 (Student) Ensemble** (5 models).
- **Aggregation**: Compute the **arithmetic mean** of the probabilities. This ensures the final submission relies solely on high-capacity, domain-adapted, and adversarially robust models.
