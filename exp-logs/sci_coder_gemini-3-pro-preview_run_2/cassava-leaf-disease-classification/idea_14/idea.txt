Based on the comprehensive analysis of the provided lessons and the trajectory of previous experiments, I propose the following optimized strategy. This approach structurally evolves the solution by scaling up to a **High-Capacity Backbone (ConvNeXt Base)** and training it within a **Robust 5-Fold Progressive Framework** that explicitly corrects the regularization scheduling errors identified in Lesson 00036.

# Model
I propose upgrading the architecture to **ConvNeXt Base**, initialized with pre-trained ImageNet-1k weights.
*   **Capacity Upgrade**: We will scale from the "Small" variant (approx. 50M params) to "Base" (approx. 88M params). The 24-hour compute budget and the A100 GPU allow for this capacity increase, which is necessary to capture the subtle, fine-grained features of cassava diseases that smaller models may miss **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00027)**.
*   **Regularization**: To prevent overfitting with this larger model, we will increase the **Stochastic Depth (Drop Path)** rate to **0.5**.
*   **Weight Averaging**: We will utilize **Model EMA (Exponential Moving Average)** to stabilize the weights of this high-capacity model during the noisy training process, ensuring the schedule is sufficiently long to avoid the "EMA Lag" issue **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00030)**.

# Data
*   **Preprocessing**: We will strictly utilize **PIL (Python Imaging Library)** for image loading to ensure native alignment with the augmentation pipeline **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00010)**.
*   **Augmentation Pipeline**:
    1.  **Geometric**: **RandomResizedCrop** is mandatory. It forces the model to learn scale-invariant features and prevents the high-capacity backbone from memorizing global image patterns **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00013)**.
    2.  **Photometric**: **RandAugment** will be used for automated, diverse distortions **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00005)**.
    3.  **Mixing**: **MixUp and CutMix** will be applied with a probability of **0.5**. This probability is chosen to balance regularization with the need for the model to see "clean" data for convergence **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00023)**.

# Training
We will implement a **5-Fold Stratified Cross-Validation** loop. Unlike previous attempts that were either truncated **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00022)** or misconfigured **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00028)**, we will utilize the full 24-hour budget to train all 5 folds to convergence using a **Consistent Progressive Curriculum**:

*   **Phase 1: Coarse Feature Learning (12 Epochs)**
    *   **Resolution**: Input images are resized to **$224 \times 224$**.
    *   **Regularization**: **MixUp/CutMix** is active (p=0.5).
    *   **Objective**: Establish robust global features at a low computational cost **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00036)**.

*   **Phase 2: High-Fidelity Fine-Tuning (8 Epochs)**
    *   **Resolution**: Input images are increased to **$384 \times 384$**.
    *   **Regularization Correction**: Crucially, we will **maintain MixUp/CutMix (p=0.5)** during this phase. This corrects the critical error in Idea 13, where disabling regularization during fine-tuning led to overfitting and degraded performance. As identified in **Lesson 00036**, maintaining strong regularization is essential when exposing the model to high-frequency spatial details.
    *   **Batch Size**: We will use **Gradient Accumulation** to maintain an effective Batch Size of 32.

*   **Loss Function**: **Soft Target Cross-Entropy Loss** throughout both phases to handle the probabilistic labels from MixUp/CutMix.
*   **Optimizer**: **AdamW** with a **Cosine Annealing** scheduler.

# Evaluation
*   **Ensemble Inference**: The final submission will be generated by averaging the softmax probabilities from the **EMA weights** of all **5 Fold Models**. We will ensure strict experiment isolation (fresh directories) to avoid the "Silent Stale Artifact" issue **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00028)**.
*   **Test Time Augmentation (TTA)**: For each fold, we will average predictions from the original image and a horizontally flipped version to leverage orientation invariance **(Cite solution_lesson_node_00013)**.
