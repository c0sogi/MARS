My implementation strategy focuses on refining the training process and loss function to improve the Levenshtein error rate, building upon the success of the CRCN architecture.

1.  **Truncated MSE (T-MSE)**: I will modify the temporal smoothing loss to use a **truncated** Mean Squared Error. As identified in Lesson 00029, standard MSE penalizes all temporal changes, which can blur genuine gesture boundaries. By clamping the loss at a threshold (`tmse_threshold=0.1`), the model can learn sharp transitions without incurring a large penalty, while still smoothing out small-magnitude jitter. I will increase the `tmse_weight` to 5.0 to compensate for the reduced gradient magnitude due to truncation.
2.  **Cosine Annealing Scheduler**: I will replace `ReduceLROnPlateau` with `CosineAnnealingLR`. The previous run showed that the validation metric continued to improve even as the validation loss plateaued or slightly rose. A cosine schedule provides a more predictable and optimal learning rate decay for fine-tuning, ensuring the model settles into a robust minimum by the end of training.
3.  **Checkpointing Strategy**: I will update the checkpointing logic to save the model based on the **Levenshtein score** rather than the validation loss. While Lesson 00010 warns about metric volatility, the use of Cosine Annealing (ending with a low LR) mitigates this risk, and Lesson 00008 highlights that the sequence metric is the true objective.
4.  **Operational Improvements**: I will increase the `batch_size` to 32 to better utilize the A100 GPU and stabilize gradients, and increase `num_epochs` to 40 to allow the cosine schedule sufficient time to converge.
