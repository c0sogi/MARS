Based on the comprehensive analysis of the provided lessons, I propose a **Multi-Resolution Heterogeneous Ensemble with Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA)**.

This strategy structurally evolves the "Heterogeneous Ensemble" (Idea 8) by introducing a **Multi-Resolution Input Topology** and integrating **Stochastic Weight Averaging**. These innovations directly address the "saturation" observed when adding more architectures (Cite solution_lesson_node_00042) and the generalization benefits of lower resolutions (Cite solution_lesson_node_00035), while strictly adhering to the optimization protocols proven effective for this micro-dataset.

### Model
**Architecture: Dual-Branch Multi-Resolution Ensemble**
The solution will utilize two distinct CNN backbones trained in parallel, but unlike previous iterations, they will ingest data at different resolutions to force feature decorrelation.
1.  **Branch A (High-Fidelity Temporal):** **ResNet18** (Pretrained on ImageNet).
    *   **Input:** **224 (Freq) x 448 (Time)**.
    *   *Role:* Retained from the Current Best solution (Cite solution_lesson_node_00034), this branch captures fine-grained spectral textures and rapid temporal transients.
2.  **Branch B (Structural Regularization):** **DenseNet121** (Pretrained on ImageNet).
    *   **Input (Structural Innovation):** **160 (Freq) x 320 (Time)**.
    *   *Role:* By reducing the input resolution while maintaining the critical 1:2 aspect ratio (Cite solution_lesson_node_00034), this branch is forced to learn coarser, macro-scale signal patterns. This leverages the finding that lower resolutions act as architectural regularizers in data-scarce regimes (Cite solution_lesson_node_00035), preventing the deeper DenseNet from overfitting to high-frequency noise.

**Head Design:**
Both backbones will terminate in a **Global Average Pooling (GAP)** layer (Cite solution_lesson_node_00024) followed by a single fully connected layer with 19 outputs and **Sigmoid** activation.

### Data
**Preprocessing & Splitting**
*   **Input Source:** Use **Filtered Spectrograms** to ensure training on denoised signals (Cite solution_lesson_node_00028).
*   **Adaptation:**
    *   **Pseudo-RGB:** Replicate the single-channel grayscale spectrogram 3 times (Cite solution_lesson_node_00030).
    *   **Resizing:** Resize inputs to 224x448 for ResNet18 and 160x320 for DenseNet121.
*   **Splitting:** Implement **Iterative Stratified K-Fold (K=5)** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018).
*   **Augmentation:**
    *   **Mixup:** Apply **Mild Mixup ($\alpha=0.4$)**. This is critical for regularization (Cite solution_lesson_node_00013) but must not be aggressive (Cite solution_lesson_node_00022).
    *   **Photometric:** Apply **RandomBrightnessContrast** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00022).
    *   **Geometric:** Apply **SpecAugment**.
*   **Loader:** Implement a `worker_init_fn` to explicitly seed worker processes, ensuring true stochastic diversity (Cite solution_lesson_node_00045).

### Training
**Procedure: SWA-Enhanced Optimization**
*   **Loss Function:** Use **Binary Cross Entropy (BCE) with Logits Loss** combined with **`pos_weight`**. Explicitly weighting positive classes is non-negotiable (Cite solution_lesson_node_00020).
*   **Optimizer:** **AdamW** with **Learning Rate `1e-3`** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00040) and **Weight Decay `1e-4`** (Cite solution_lesson_node_00033).
*   **Batch Size:** **16**. Small batches provide beneficial gradient noise for micro-datasets (Cite solution_lesson_node_00042).
*   **Schedule:** Use a **Constant Learning Rate**. This is required to escape local minima in the noisy loss landscape of small datasets (Cite solution_lesson_node_00036).
*   **Duration:** Target **1000 Total Update Steps** per fold (Cite solution_lesson_node_00031).
*   **Technique (Innovation): Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA).**
    *   Instead of saving only the single best checkpoint, cache the model weights at the end of each epoch during the final 25% of training.
    *   Average these weights to create the final model for inference.
    *   *Rationale:* SWA finds a flatter minimum in the loss surface, which significantly improves generalization and robustness to domain shift compared to standard SGD/Adam convergence, especially when training data is insufficient to pinpoint a stable global optimum.

### Evaluation
**Inference: Multi-Resolution Bagging**
*   **Process:** For each test recording:
    1.  Generate a 224x448 input and pass it through the 5 trained ResNet18 models.
    2.  Generate a 160x320 input and pass it through the 5 trained DenseNet121 models.
*   **Aggregation:** Average the probability outputs of all 10 models. This combines the stability of bagging (Cite solution_lesson_node_00018) with the feature diversity of the multi-resolution, heterogeneous architecture (Cite solution_lesson_node_00034).
