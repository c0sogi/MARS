To solve the task of categorizing radiographs and detecting COVID-19 opacities, I propose a **Multi-Task Faster R-CNN with Global Context Guidance**. This strategy evolves the previous "Class-Aware" baseline by structurally decoupling the study-level diagnosis from the object detection success, directly addressing the identified bottlenecks regarding object density and diffuse opacities.

### Model
**Architecture:** Multi-Task Faster R-CNN with ResNet-101-FPN.
*   **Backbone:** I will upgrade the backbone from ResNet-50 to **ResNet-101-FPN**. This deeper feature extractor is better suited for capturing the subtle, diffuse patterns of "Indeterminate" or "Atypical" COVID-19 variants, which the failure analysis suggested were problematic.
*   **Structural Innovation (Auxiliary Head):** I will introduce a **Global Classification Head** parallel to the existing Region Proposal Network (RPN). This head will perform Global Average Pooling (GAP) on the high-level feature maps (e.g., the P5 layer of the FPN) followed by a fully connected layer to predict the 4 study-level classes directly.
    *   *Rationale:* Previous experiments revealed a high correlation between loss and object count, indicating that the detector fails in dense scenes (Cite solution_lesson_node_00004). By adding this global head, the model can correctly classify a study as "Typical" or "Atypical" based on global context, even if the detector's NMS suppresses individual bounding boxes in crowded regions.
*   **RPN Configuration:** I will explicitly retain the increased proposal capacity (3000 pre-NMS / 200 post-NMS) to ensure the network is not bottlenecked by the default proposal limits in dense images (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).

### Data
**Preprocessing:**
*   **Contrast Enhancement:** I will apply **CLAHE** (Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization) to all images during training and inference. This was proven to enhance feature visibility and improve performance in monochromatic medical imaging (Cite solution_lesson_node_00005).
*   **Labeling:**
    *   **Instance Level:** Bounding boxes will be assigned class IDs corresponding to their study label (Typical, Indeterminate, Atypical), preserving the "Unified" approach of the baseline.
    *   **Study Level:** The study-level labels will be used to supervise the new Global Classification Head.

### Training
**Procedure:**
*   **Loss Function:** The model will be trained using a composite loss: $L_{total} = L_{detection} + \lambda L_{global\_classification}$. This ensures the backbone learns features that are discriminative for both localizing opacities and diagnosing the overall patient condition.
*   **Checkpointing:** I will save model checkpoints based on **Validation Loss** rather than calculating mAP at every epoch. Previous lessons demonstrated that loss is a sufficiently aligned and far more efficient proxy for performance in this specific setup (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
*   **Strategy:** I will use a standard SGD optimizer with momentum, training on the stratified group split to prevent patient leakage.

### Evaluation
**Inference Strategy:**
*   **Study-Level Prediction:** The final study label will be determined by an ensemble of the **Global Classification Head's output** and the **maximum confidence score** of the detected bounding boxes. This prevents the "default to Negative" error mode when the detector misses boxes in a positive study.
*   **Image-Level Prediction:** Bounding box predictions will be generated solely by the detection head. If the study-level prediction is "Negative", all boxes will be suppressed to ensure consistency.
