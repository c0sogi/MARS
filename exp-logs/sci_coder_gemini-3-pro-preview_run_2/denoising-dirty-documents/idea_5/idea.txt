Based on the comprehensive analysis of the previous experiments and the specific nature of the task (denoising scanned text), I propose the **Context-Aware Coordinate ResUNet (CAC-ResUNet)**.

This strategy addresses the primary limitation identified in Lesson 00001 and 00007 (insufficient receptive field/context) and refines the attention mechanism used in the current best solution (Lesson 00016) to be specifically optimized for the geometric structure of text.

### Model: Context-Aware Coordinate ResUNet (CAC-ResUNet)
I propose a structural evolution of the ResUNet that integrates **Coordinate Attention** and **Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling (ASPP)**.

*   **Backbone:** The core architecture remains a U-Net with **Residual Blocks** to facilitate deep gradient flow. We explicitly retain the **64-filter base capacity** and **Transposed Convolutions** for upsampling, as reducing capacity or using fixed interpolation was proven detrimental (Cite solution_lesson_node_00021, solution_lesson_node_00020).
*   **Coordinate Attention (CA):** I will replace the Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) blocks with **Coordinate Attention** modules within the residual blocks.
    *   *Rationale:* SE blocks globally average-pool spatial information, effectively destroying positional details. Text, however, is highly structured (horizontal lines, vertical strokes). Coordinate Attention pools features along the horizontal and vertical directions separately. This allows the network to capture long-range dependencies along one spatial direction (e.g., a line of text) while preserving precise positional information along the other, significantly improving the distinction between random noise specks and structural text components.
*   **Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling (ASPP):** I will insert an ASPP module at the bottleneck of the U-Net.
    *   *Rationale:* To address the receptive field limitations (Cite solution_lesson_node_00001), ASPP employs multiple parallel convolutional layers with different dilation rates. This captures multi-scale context—allowing the model to "see" both fine-grained noise and the broader background texture—without the loss of resolution associated with standard downsampling (Cite solution_lesson_node_00007).
*   **Activation:** We will retain **SiLU (Swish)** activations throughout the network (Cite solution_lesson_node_00016).

### Data: High-Density Contextual Patching
*   **Patch Size:** We will strictly use **128x128** (or larger) patches. We reject the use of smaller patches (e.g., 64x64) even if it allows for larger batch sizes, as preserving the local context is critical for the model to infer text structure (Cite solution_lesson_node_00022).
*   **Sampling Density:** We will employ **High-Density Sampling**, extracting **100 random patches per image per epoch**. This ensures the model is exposed to the full diversity of noise patterns and text alignments (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014).
*   **Augmentation:** We will apply aggressive geometric augmentations (random horizontal/vertical flips and 90-degree rotations) to exploit the rotational symmetry of the noise (Cite solution_lesson_node_00010).

### Training: Residual Optimization with Aggressive Regularization
*   **Objective:** The model will be trained to predict the **noise residual** ($Clean = Input - Prediction$) rather than the clean image directly. This simplifies the optimization landscape by focusing on the sparse, high-frequency noise signal (Cite solution_lesson_node_00013).
*   **Optimizer:** We will use **AdamW** with a high **weight decay of 1e-2**. This aggressive regularization is necessary to prevent the increased capacity of the CAC-ResUNet from overfitting the small dataset (Cite solution_lesson_node_00015).
*   **Scheduler:** A **Cosine Annealing** learning rate scheduler will be used to ensure convergence to a sharp minimum (Cite solution_lesson_node_00014).
*   **Duration:** Training will run for a sufficient number of epochs (e.g., 100) to avoid the "convergence tax" associated with complex architectures (Cite solution_lesson_node_00019).

### Evaluation: Robust Tiled Inference
*   **Tiled Inference with Overlap:** Test images will be processed using a sliding window approach with significant overlap (e.g., 50%). Predictions in overlapping regions will be averaged to eliminate boundary artifacts and seamless reconstruction (Cite solution_lesson_node_00023).
*   **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA):** We will generate predictions for the original image and its geometric transformations (flips/rotations) and average the results to reduce variance (Cite solution_lesson_node_00013).
