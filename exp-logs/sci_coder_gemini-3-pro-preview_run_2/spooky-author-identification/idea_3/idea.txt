# Solution Strategy: Hybrid Classical-Neural Stacking Ensemble

## Model
I propose a structural evolution from the purely classical stacking ensemble to a **Hybrid Classical-Neural Stacking Ensemble**. This architecture integrates the statistical rigor of n-gram models with the contextual understanding of deep learning, creating two distinct "views" of the data that are aggregated by a meta-learner.

*   **Level 1: Base Estimators (Dual-Branch)**
    *   **Branch A: Statistical & Sparse (Retained):** We will retain the successful components from the previous iteration (Cite Lesson 00004). This includes:
        1.  **Logistic Regression** and **Multinomial Naive Bayes** operating on high-dimensional sparse TF-IDF features.
        2.  **XGBoost** operating on SVD-projected dense features.
    *   **Branch B: Sequential & Semantic (Innovation):** We will introduce a **Fine-Tuned Transformer** (e.g., `RoBERTa-base` or `DeBERTa-v3-small`) as a new base estimator. Unlike bag-of-words models, this component captures long-range dependencies, syntactic structures, and semantic context, which are critical for distinguishing complex writing styles but are missed by n-gram approaches.

*   **Level 2: Meta-Learner**
    *   A **Logistic Regression** model will serve as the blender. It will take the class probability vectors from all Level 1 models (both Classical and Neural) as input features. This allows the ensemble to dynamically weigh the explicit stylistic signals of Branch A against the implicit semantic signals of Branch B.

## Data
The data processing pipeline will be bifurcated to suit the distinct needs of the two branches.

*   **For Branch A (Classical):**
    *   We will construct the **Hybrid N-Gram** feature space (Word N-grams 1-3 + Character N-grams 2-5) to preserve the character-level stylistic signals identified as crucial (Cite Lesson 00001).
    *   We will strictly enforce **`min_df=2`** during vectorization to prune singleton noise and improve generalization (Cite Lesson 00002).
    *   **Dimensionality Reduction:** Truncated SVD will be applied specifically for the XGBoost component to enable tree-based learning on dense representations (Cite Lesson 00004).

*   **For Branch B (Neural):**
    *   Raw text will be processed using the specific **Tokenizer** associated with the chosen pre-trained Transformer model. This preserves the original sequence order and utilizes sub-word tokenization (e.g., Byte-Pair Encoding) to handle rare words effectively.

## Training
The training strategy prioritizes robustness and computational efficiency within the 24-hour limit.

*   **Stratified K-Fold Cross-Validation:** We will employ a consistent 5-fold split across *all* models (Classical and Neural). This ensures that the Out-Of-Fold (OOF) predictions generated for the Meta-Learner are aligned and statistically valid.
*   **Configuration-Aware Caching:** We will implement the hash-based caching mechanism for all intermediate artifacts (TF-IDF matrices, SVD projections). This ensures that any hyperparameter adjustment triggers a re-computation, preventing the "stale cache" errors observed previously (Cite Lesson 00003).
*   **Neural Fine-Tuning:** The Transformer will be fine-tuned using a standard optimization loop (AdamW optimizer) with a low learning rate and **Early Stopping** based on validation loss to prevent overfitting on this relatively small dataset.

## Evaluation
*   **Inference:** The test data will be processed through both parallel pipelines. The Classical models will generate predictions based on the TF-IDF/SVD features, while the Transformer will generate predictions from the tokenized sequences.
*   **Aggregation:** These diverse probability distributions will be concatenated and fed into the trained Level 2 Meta-Learner to produce the final posterior probabilities.
*   **Post-Processing:** Final predictions will be clipped to the range \\([10^{-15}, 1-10^{-15}]\\) to satisfy the metric stability requirements.
