Based on the comprehensive analysis of the accumulated lessons, I propose the **Strip-Pooling Enhanced ResNet18 U-Net**.

This strategy evolves the "Context-Enhanced" architecture (Idea 5) by replacing the generic context aggregation (ASPP) with a **Strip Pooling** mechanism. This structural innovation directly targets the specific morphology of contrails—long, narrow, linear features—addressing the limitation where standard square kernels or pooling layers struggle to capture long-range dependencies in isolated linear structures.

### Model
The architecture is a **U-Net** variant designed to enforce a strong "linearity prior" at the deepest feature level.

*   **Backbone (Encoder):** We retain the **ResNet18** encoder. As established, this provides the optimal balance of capacity and spatial fidelity, avoiding the diminishing returns of deeper backbones (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00005`) and the weakness of lightweight ones (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00002`).
*   **Structural Innovation: Strip Pooling Bottleneck:** We replace the standard bottleneck (or ASPP) with a **Strip Pooling Module (SPM)**.
    *   Standard pooling or dilated convolutions (like in ASPP) use square windows, which inevitably incorporate irrelevant background noise when processing long, thin objects like contrails.
    *   The SPM performs pooling along two distinct axes: horizontal ($1 \times W$) and vertical ($H \times 1$). This allows the model to capture global relationships along the length of a contrail—regardless of its span across the image—without diluting the signal with the surrounding "cirrus" noise found in square neighborhoods.
*   **Decoder:** We utilize **Bilinear Upsampling** followed by standard convolutions, strictly avoiding Transposed Convolutions to prevent checkerboard artifacts (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00008`).
*   **Attention:** We retain **SCSE (Spatial and Channel Squeeze & Excitation)** blocks after each concatenation step in the decoder to adaptively suppress noise in the skip connections, a technique proven to improve data efficiency (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00021`).
*   **Input Head:** The model accepts the **6-channel input tensor** (Ash Composite + Temporal Difference) (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00001`).

### Data
*   **Input Engineering:** We generate the effective 6-channel input:
    *   **Channels 1-3:** "Ash" False Color Composite of the labeled frame ($t=4$).
    *   **Channels 4-6:** Temporal difference ($t=4$ minus $t=3$). We reject symmetric future differencing ($t+1$) as it adds redundancy (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00006`).
*   **Augmentation:** We employ a strict **Affine Transformation** pipeline (Rotation, Scale, Shift, Flip). We explicitly **exclude** elastic, grid, or optical distortions, as these warp the linear morphology of contrails into curve-like shapes that resemble natural clouds, creating adversarial noise (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00004`).
*   **Dataset:** We train on the **full dataset** to ensure the model learns to suppress the "long tail" of background clutter (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00002`).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** We employ a **Hybrid Loss**: $L_{total} = L_{BCE} + L_{BatchDice}$.
    *   **BCE:** Provides smooth gradients for pixel-level classification.
    *   **Batch-Level Dice:** We compute the Dice score over the **entire flattened batch** (treating the batch as a single volume). This stabilizes gradients and aligns with the global metric, avoiding the instability of sample-level averaging and the degradation caused by pure Dice loss (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00014`, `solution_lesson_node_00018`).
*   **Optimization:** We use **AdamW** with a Cosine Annealing scheduler.
*   **Batch Size:** We utilize a **moderate batch size** (e.g., 32). We avoid excessively large batch sizes (e.g., 64+) to ensure frequent gradient updates, which aids convergence speed (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00023`).
*   **Duration:** We extend training to **30 epochs**. Since we are using a robust backbone and augmentation, we must avoid the underfitting caused by short training horizons (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00007`).

### Evaluation
*   **Inference Strategy:** We implement **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA)**.
    *   For each test image, we generate predictions for the original, horizontally flipped, vertically flipped, and 180-degree rotated versions.
    *   The four probability maps are averaged before thresholding. This ensemble-like approach smooths out prediction noise and has been proven to boost the Global Dice score (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00024`).
*   **Post-Processing:** We apply a standard pixel-level threshold of **0.5**. We explicitly reject "Hard Gating" or "Soft Gating" mechanisms, as they introduce single points of failure (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00009`).
*   **Formatting:** The final binary mask is run-length encoded.
