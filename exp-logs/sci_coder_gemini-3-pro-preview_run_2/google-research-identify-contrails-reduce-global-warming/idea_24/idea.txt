Based on the comprehensive analysis of the accumulated lessons, I propose the **Progressive Large-Kernel ConvNeXt U-Net**.

This strategy represents a structural evolution of the "Large-Kernel" approach (Idea 11) and the "Extended-Kernel" decoder (Idea 23). It introduces **Progressive Kernel Expansion** in the decoder to solve the "receptive field shrinkage" problem during upsampling. It combines this with the rigorous "Homogeneous Feature Engineering" protocol established in **Lesson 58** and **Lesson 45**, ensuring that the model's linearity prior is applied effectively to physically consistent data.

### Model
The architecture is a **U-Net** variant designed to maintain a consistent *physical* receptive field size across all resolution levels, preventing the fragmentation of thin features during reconstruction.

*   **Backbone (Encoder):** We utilize **ConvNeXt-Tiny** (initialized with ImageNet weights).
    *   **Retention:** We retain the **7x7 Depthwise Convolutions**. As established, this large isotropic receptive field enforces a strong linearity prior, which is superior for contrail segmentation compared to standard 3x3 CNNs (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00036`) or dilated backbones (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00033`).
*   **Context Bridge:** We retain the **Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling (ASPP)** module at the bottleneck. This ensures the model captures multi-scale global context, which is critical for distinguishing contrails from background cirrus (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00021`).
*   **Decoder (Structural Innovation: Progressive Kernel Expansion):**
    *   We employ a standard **Sequential U-Net Decoder** (Stride 32 $\to$ 16 $\to$ ... $\to$ 1) to ensure pixel-level refinement (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00046`).
    *   **Innovation:** Instead of using a fixed kernel size (e.g., 3x3 or 7x7) throughout the decoder, we **progressively increase the kernel size** of the Depthwise Convolutions as the resolution increases.
        *   Example progression: Stride 16 (7x7) $\to$ Stride 8 (9x9) $\to$ Stride 4 (11x11) $\to$ Stride 2 (13x13) $\to$ Stride 1 (15x15).
    *   **Rationale:** In a standard decoder, a fixed kernel size (e.g., 7x7) covers a progressively smaller *physical* area of the earth as the feature map resolution grows. By scaling the kernel size inversely with the stride, we maintain a massive, consistent physical receptive field all the way to the final pixel-level prediction. This allows the model to bridge gaps between disjoint contrail segments even at high resolution, directly addressing the fragmentation issue identified in **Lesson 59**.
    *   **Upsampling:** We use **Bilinear Upsampling** followed by convolutions, strictly avoiding Transposed Convolutions (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00008`) and interpolation bottlenecks (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00039`).
    *   **Attention:** We retain **SCSE (Spatial and Channel Squeeze & Excitation)** blocks after each concatenation to adaptively suppress noise in the skip connections (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00021`). We explicitly reject Attention Gates (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00055`).

### Data
*   **Input Engineering (Homogeneous Decoupled Features):** We generate a **6-channel input tensor** that explicitly decouples spectral and temporal signals while ensuring numerical consistency.
    *   **Channels 1-3 (Spectral):** "Ash" False Color Composite of the labeled frame ($t=4$). Normalized to $[0, 1]$.
    *   **Channels 4-6 (Temporal):** **Raw Band Differences** ($t=4$ minus $t=3$) for Bands 11, 14, and 15. We explicitly use raw values to preserve the dynamic range of the cooling signal (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00045`).
    *   **Preprocessing:** We apply **Homogeneous Scaling**. The raw differences are clipped to a fixed physical range (e.g., $\pm 5$ Kelvin) and linearly scaled to $[0, 1]$. This ensures that all 6 channels share the same distribution, preventing the convergence failure observed when mixing normalized and raw units (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00058`).
*   **Augmentation:** We employ a strict **Affine Transformation** pipeline (Rotation, Scale, Shift, Flip). We explicitly **exclude** elastic, grid, or optical distortions, as these warp the linear morphology of contrails into curve-like shapes that resemble natural clouds (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00004`).
*   **Dataset:** We train on the **full dataset** (18,673 images) to ensure the model learns to suppress the "long tail" of background clutter (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00002`).

### Training
*   **Loss Function:** We employ a **Hybrid Loss**: $L_{total} = L_{BCE} + L_{BatchDice}$.
    *   **Binary Cross Entropy (BCE):** Included to provide smooth, convex gradients for pixel-level classification.
    *   **Batch-Level Dice:** We compute the Dice score over the **entire flattened batch** (treating the batch as a single volume). This stabilizes gradients and aligns with the global metric, avoiding the instability of sample-level averaging (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00014`) and the degradation caused by pure Dice loss (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00018`).
    *   We explicitly avoid Deep Supervision (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00040`).
*   **Optimization:** We use **AdamW** with a Cosine Annealing scheduler.
*   **Batch Size:** We utilize a **Batch Size of 32**. This is critical to ensure the statistical stability of the Batch Dice loss (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00043`).
*   **Duration:** We train for **30 epochs**. We explicitly avoid shortening the training horizon to prevent underfitting on fine-grained details (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00007`).

### Evaluation
*   **Inference Strategy:** We implement **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA)**.
    *   For each test image, we generate predictions for the original, horizontally flipped, vertically flipped, and 180-degree rotated versions.
    *   The four probability maps are averaged before thresholding. This ensemble-like approach smooths out prediction noise and boosts the Global Dice score (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00024`).
*   **Post-Processing:** We apply a standard pixel-level threshold of **0.5**. We explicitly reject "Hard Gating" mechanisms (Cite `solution_lesson_node_00009`).
