# Solution Proposal: Hierarchical Multi-Task Learning with EfficientNet-B4

To address the fine-grained nature of the dataset and the extreme class imbalance, I propose a solution that leverages the biological taxonomy (Family, Genus, Species) inherent in the data. By structurally enforcing the model to learn these hierarchical relationships, we facilitate knowledge transfer from frequent classes to rare ones that share the same Genus or Family.

### Model
I propose an **EfficientNet-B4** architecture modified for **Hierarchical Multi-Task Learning**.
*   **Backbone**: The EfficientNet-B4 serves as the feature extractor. It is significantly deeper and wider than the B0 used previously, providing the capacity needed to discriminate between 32,000+ species while fitting comfortably within the A100's memory.
*   **Multi-Task Heads**: Instead of a single output layer, the network will branch into three separate fully connected heads after the global pooling layer:
    1.  **Family Head**: Predicts the plant family (coarse-grained).
    2.  **Genus Head**: Predicts the plant genus (medium-grained).
    3.  **Species Head**: Predicts the specific species (fine-grained, the target).
*   **Rationale**: This structure forces the shared backbone to learn robust features that are biologically meaningful. Since rare species often share visual characteristics with common species in the same Genus or Family, this shared representation helps regularize the learning for the long-tail classes.

### Data
*   **Taxonomy Extraction**: The `categories` table in the metadata will be parsed to create a mapping from `Species ID` to `Genus ID` and `Family ID`. These auxiliary labels will be assigned to every training image.
*   **Preprocessing**: Images will be resized to **380x380** pixels (the native resolution for EfficientNet-B4). This higher resolution is critical for capturing the subtle texture and shape details required for fine-grained identification.
*   **Augmentation**:
    *   **Training**: In addition to Random Resized Crop and Horizontal Flip, I will introduce **Mixup** augmentation. Mixup creates synthetic training examples by linearly interpolating between images and labels, which encourages smoother decision boundaries and improves robustness for tail classes.
    *   **Evaluation**: Standard Resize and Center Crop to 380x380.

### Training
*   **Loss Function**: A compound loss function will be used:
    *   **Species Loss**: **Focal Loss** will be applied to the species head. This explicitly retains the successful strategy of dynamically down-weighting easy examples to prevent majority class dominance `Cite solution_lesson_node_00004`.
    *   **Auxiliary Loss**: Standard Cross-Entropy Loss will be applied to the Family and Genus heads.
    *   **Total Loss**: $L_{total} = L_{Focal}(Species) + \lambda_1 L_{CE}(Genus) + \lambda_2 L_{CE}(Family)$.
*   **Optimizer**: **AdamW** with a Cosine Annealing scheduler.
*   **Strategy**: The auxiliary heads provide "hints" during training, stabilizing the gradient updates for the shared backbone.

### Evaluation
*   **Inference**: During the test phase, the auxiliary heads (Family and Genus) are discarded.
*   **Prediction**: The model performs a forward pass, and the final prediction is determined solely by the Argmax of the **Species Head** logits. This ensures the output format matches the submission requirement while benefiting from the structured feature learning acquired during training.
