Based on the empirical evidence gathered from previous experiments, I propose a solution that structurally evolves the baseline by integrating **Hierarchical Multi-Task Learning** while strictly adhering to the data density and convergence principles established in the lessons.

# Model
I propose using an **EfficientNet-B3** architecture modified for **Hierarchical Multi-Task Learning**.
*   **Backbone**: The EfficientNet-B3 will serve as the shared feature extractor. This choice balances the need for higher resolution (300x300) to capture fine-grained details—an improvement over the B0 used in the baseline—while maintaining the strong inductive bias and faster convergence speed of CNNs compared to Transformers `Cite solution_lesson_node_00009`.
*   **Hierarchical Heads**: Instead of a single output layer, the network will branch into three parallel fully connected heads after the global pooling layer:
    1.  **Family Head**: Predicts the plant family (Coarse).
    2.  **Genus Head**: Predicts the plant genus (Medium).
    3.  **Species Head**: Predicts the specific species (Fine - Target).
*   **Rationale**: This structure leverages the biological taxonomy provided in the metadata. By training the backbone to recognize families and genera simultaneously, we enforce the learning of robust, biologically meaningful features that transfer well to the fine-grained species classification, particularly for rare classes that share visual traits with common relatives.

# Data
*   **Dataset Strategy**: We will train on the **Full Training Dataset** (approx. 650,000 images). We explicitly reject the subsetting strategy that caused the failure of the previous hierarchical experiment, ensuring we maintain the necessary samples-per-class density to support the 32,093-way classification `Cite solution_lesson_node_00007`.
*   **Preprocessing**: Images will be resized to **300x300** pixels, the native resolution for EfficientNet-B3. This resolution increase over the baseline (224x224) provides the model with more visual information to discriminate between subtle species differences.
*   **Augmentation**: We will utilize standard augmentations (Random Resized Crop, Horizontal Flip). Crucially, we will **avoid** heavy regularization techniques like Mixup or CutMix. While these were used in previous failed attempts, lessons indicate they slow down convergence in time-constrained settings `Cite solution_lesson_node_00009`.

# Training
*   **Loss Function**: A weighted multi-task loss will be employed:
    *   **Species Loss**: **Focal Loss** will be applied to the species head. This retains the critical success factor of the baseline, dynamically focusing updates on hard, misclassified examples to maximize the Macro F1 score `Cite solution_lesson_node_00004`.
    *   **Auxiliary Loss**: Standard Cross-Entropy Loss will be applied to the Family and Genus heads to provide stable gradient signals.
*   **Optimizer**: **AdamW** with a Cosine Annealing scheduler will be used to optimize the network.
*   **Strategy**: The auxiliary heads act as regularizers and accelerators, helping the shared backbone converge to useful representations faster than learning from the sparse species labels alone.

# Evaluation
*   **Inference**: For the test set, images will be resized to 300x300 pixels.
*   **Prediction**: The model will perform a forward pass, and the final prediction will be determined solely by the **Species Head**. The auxiliary heads are discarded at inference time, ensuring the output matches the required submission format.
